\begin{abstract}
We study online control of a scalar storage system with nonnegative adversarial
resource arrivals and state-dependent action constraints. The adversarial,
time-varying cost depends on both the state and the action. At each time, the
controller selects a feasible action before the current resource arrival and cost
are revealed. To handle the resulting coupling
between feasibility and online learning, we introduce \emph{Simplex Disturbance-Action
Control} (SDAC), a policy class specified by $H$ nonnegative parameters
whose sum is at most one. The key feature of SDAC is that every fixed policy in the
class is feasible by construction. Our online SDAC controller updates these
parameters using entropic online mirror descent while preserving feasibility along
the time-varying trajectory. We prove a regret bound of
$O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log(H+1)}\right)$ relative to the best fixed SDAC policy. We
also derive a minimax lower bound showing that the dependence on $T$ is tight. We
then show that SDAC
geometrically approximates a decreasing-allocation policy class that spreads each
arrival over time according to a common decreasing allocation sequence, a structure
that arises in stochastic energy-harvesting control. With
$H=\Theta(\log T)$, the resulting regret against the decreasing-allocation class is
$O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log\log T}\right)$. The framework applies to energy-harvesting
batteries and other storage-constrained resource systems.
\end{abstract}

Online control, disturbance-action policies, storage systems, energy harvesting,

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{Motivation and Problem Setting}
\IEEEPARstart{M}{any} resource-allocation systems have a storage state that changes
over time and limits the control action at each time. An action that lowers the
current cost may leave less resource for future decisions, while conserving the
resource may increase the current cost. The controller must balance these effects
while satisfying this constraint at every step.

We study this problem for a scalar storage system that loses a fixed fraction of
its stored resource each period. At each time, the controller observes the current
state and all past arrivals and cost functions, chooses a control action, and then
observes the current arrival and cost function. The arrival and cost sequences may
be chosen adversarially, so we do not assume a known
probability model. Our goal is to construct an online SDAC controller whose
cumulative cost is close to that of the best fixed policy, chosen in hindsight,
from a specified benchmark class.

One difficulty is that the current action affects the next state. In online control,
\emph{Disturbance-Action Control} (DAC) parameterizes actions using a linear filter
of past disturbances, thereby yielding a tractable algorithm with bounded regret.
In our storage model, however, some DAC policies can produce negative actions or
actions larger than the available resource.

We address this issue by introducing \emph{Simplex Disturbance-Action Control}
(SDAC). SDAC restricts the filter parameters to a subprobability simplex and includes
powers of the retention coefficient in the filter. These choices ensure that
every fixed SDAC policy satisfies the action constraint. The state and action
components of each fixed-policy trajectory are also affine in the policy parameter.
Thus, a convex state--action cost is convex as a function of that parameter. This
structure permits the parameter to be updated using online convex optimization.

\subsection{Model and Information Pattern}

The storage state $x_t\ge0$ evolves according to
\[
x_{t+1}=\alpha x_t-u_t+w_t,
\]
where $\alpha\in(0,1)$ is a fixed retention coefficient, $u_t$ is the control
action, and $w_t\in[0,1]$ is a nonnegative resource arrival. The action must satisfy
\[
0\le u_t\le\alpha x_t.
\]
Thus, the controller cannot use more than the amount remaining after storage loss.

At time $t$, the controller chooses $u_t$ using the current state and the
information available through time $t-1$. The arrival $w_t$ and the convex cost
function $c_t$ are revealed after the action is selected, and the controller incurs
the cost $c_t(x_t,u_t)$. Both the online trajectory and every fixed-policy
trajectory used as a benchmark must satisfy the same dynamics and action
constraint. The formal model and assumptions are given in
Section~\ref{sec:model-setup}.

Energy-harvesting battery management is our main application. In this case, $x_t$
is the battery state of charge, $w_t$ is the harvested energy, $u_t$ is the energy
used, and $1-\alpha$ is the fraction of stored energy lost during one period.
Similar dynamics arise in perishable inventory systems, water reservoirs with
storage loss, and queues with abandonment. In many standard queueing models,
$\alpha=1$; that case is outside our main results and is discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:extensions}.


\subsection{Contributions}

Our contributions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Policy class.}
    We introduce SDAC, a finite-memory policy class parameterized by a
    subprobability simplex. We prove that every fixed SDAC policy is feasible
    for every arrival sequence with entries in $[0,1]$.

    We further show that SDAC approximates the decreasing-allocation policy
    class defined in
    Section~\ref{subsec:allocation-sequences}. This class allocates each arrival
    over future periods according to the same decreasing sequence. It contains
    every feasible fixed-fraction policy
    $u_t=kx_t$ for some fixed $k\in[0,\alpha]$, and the approximation error decreases
    geometrically with the memory horizon $H$.

\item \textbf{Online control and regret.}
    We develop an online SDAC controller that updates its parameter using
    entropic online mirror descent (OMD) over the subprobability simplex.
    We prove that the controller remains feasible and achieves
    \[
    O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log(H+1)}\right)
    \]
    regret against the best fixed SDAC policy.
    For $H=\Theta(\log T)$, the regret against the decreasing-allocation class is
    $O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log\log T}\right)$. The same bound
    holds against the best fixed-fraction policy.

\item \textbf{Minimax lower bound.}
    We prove a lower bound of order $\sqrt{T}$ for the expected regret of every
    causal feasible controller, including randomized controllers; see
    Theorem~\ref{thm:sdac-lower-bound}. When $T$ is at least on the order of
    $(1-\alpha)^{-1}$, the lower bound scales as
    $\Omega((1-\alpha)^{-1}\sqrt{T})$ near $\alpha=1$.
    The upper and lower bounds differ by a factor of $(1-\alpha)^{-1/2}$ in their
    dependence on $1-\alpha$. Closing this gap is left for future work.
\end{itemize}


\subsection{Related Work}

\paragraph{Online control}
Online control is the main basis for this work. Agarwal et al.\ use
DAC policies to obtain $\widetilde O(\sqrt{T})$ regret against strongly
stable linear controllers \cite{agarwal2019online}. More closely related to
our simplex parameterization, Golowich et al.\ adapt DAC to population
dynamics and search over simplex-structured DAC policy parameters
\cite{golowich2024population}. Their state lies in a probability simplex,
and their feasible action set is independent of the current state. Here,
the constraint $0\le u_t\le\alpha x_t$ couples each action to the available
resource.

Li et al.\ consider affine constraints on the state and action
\cite{li2021onlineConstrained}. Their action constraint is independent of
the current state. They enforce feasibility by restricting and projecting
their policy updates onto a tightened parameter set. In our work, the
action produced by every fixed SDAC policy is feasible by construction.
When the parameter changes online, we clip the nominal action to the
current admissible interval as in \eqref{eq:alg-feasible-action}.

Liu et al.\ consider general convex constraint functions
\cite{liu2023constrainedControl}. For their adversarial constraints, the
guarantees bound cumulative violation and may permit violations at individual
times. Such violations are not permitted here, since
\eqref{eq:model-feasibility} must hold at every time.

Kumar et al.\ introduce online convex optimization with unbounded memory
\cite{kumar2023unbounded}. For their discounted infinite-memory model, the
lower bound scales as $\sqrt{T}$ divided by one minus the discount factor. This
parallels our $\Omega((1-\alpha)^{-1}\sqrt{T})$ lower bound beyond the mixing
time, although ours applies directly to causal feasible
controllers under the state-dependent action constraint.



\paragraph{Energy harvesting}
Fixed-fraction and related allocation policies for stochastic energy arrivals
motivate our \emph{decreasing-allocation policy class}. Shaviv and \"Ozg\"ur
\cite{shaviv2016universally} allocate a fixed fraction of the available energy
under i.i.d.\ arrivals. Arafa et al.\ \cite{arafa2018online} extend fixed-fraction
policies to general concave utilities under i.i.d.\ arrivals and, for Bernoulli
arrivals, characterize a non-increasing allocation sequence following a full
recharge. Zibaeenejad and Chen \cite{zibaeenejad2019lookahead} derive an optimal
decreasing allocation sequence under Bernoulli arrivals when the controller
observes a fixed look-ahead window. Together, these works motivate allocating each
arrival over future periods according to a decreasing sequence. Our allocation
begins one period after the arrival because the controller acts before observing
the current arrival.

Yu and Neely \cite{Yu2019} study expected utility maximization under i.i.d.\ system
states, while Asgari and Neely \cite{10.1145/3428337} obtain an
$O(\sqrt{T})$ regret--battery-capacity tradeoff for arbitrary loss sequences under
i.i.d.\ energy arrivals. Neither objective depends directly on the battery state.
Yu and Neely optimize expected time-average utility, whereas Asgari and Neely
measure regret against a fixed action. Lin et al.\ \cite{lin2025optimal} allow
arbitrary energy arrivals and time-varying age-of-information
objectives. They observe the current arrival before allocation and compare with an
unrestricted offline optimum through a competitive ratio. In our setting, the
controller acts before observing the current arrival and cost, the arrival and cost
sequences may be chosen adversarially, the cost depends on the storage state and
action, and the benchmark is a fixed SDAC policy chosen in hindsight.

\paragraph{Queueing and network optimization}
Classical queueing control studies throughput and queue stability through dynamic
service and scheduling decisions
\cite{tassiulas1990stability,tassiulas1993dynamic,mckeown1999achieving}, while
stochastic network optimization studies tradeoffs between time-average performance
and average queue backlog \cite{neely2010stochastic}. Our performance criterion is
finite-horizon regret against the best fixed SDAC policy in hindsight.

For their time-invariant class, Khojastepour and Sabharwal
\cite{khojastepour2004delay} represent schedulers satisfying a maximum-delay
constraint as fixed linear filters of the current and past arrivals, with
nonnegative coefficients summing to one rather than at most one as in SDAC.
Under i.i.d.\ arrivals, they identify the uniform moving-average filter as the
minimizer of long-run average transmit power over a Gaussian channel. The
connection is limited to the simplex-like filter structure: they solve a
stochastic average-power problem with a fixed cost, whereas our algorithm
updates the SDAC parameters online under adversarial arrival and cost sequences
with general convex state--action costs.

\paragraph{Inventory and reservoir management}
Yang et al.\ \cite{yang2020online} study online procurement under inventory
constraints and obtain optimal competitive ratios relative to an offline optimum.
They observe the current demand and price before acting, whereas our controller
acts before the current arrival and cost are revealed. Hihat and Fermanian
\cite{hihat2024online} learn a base-stock policy for more general inventory
dynamics but do not prove a regret bound for their method. For our scalar linear
model, we prove feasibility, approximation, and sublinear regret guarantees.
Classical stochastic reservoir models study storage under random inflows and fixed
release rules \cite{moran1954dams}, with an emphasis on steady-state behavior rather
than finite-horizon online regret.

\section{Model and Regret Benchmark}
\label{sec:model-setup}

For every positive integer $n$, we write
$[n]\triangleq\{1,\ldots,n\}$. For every $z\in\mathbb{R}$, we define
$[z]_+\triangleq\max\{z,0\}$. For
$\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^n$, the $i$-th coordinate is denoted by $v_i$. If the
vector already carries a subscript, the coordinate is appended after a comma: the
$i$-th coordinate of $\mathbf{v}_t$ is $v_{t,i}$. We write
$\mathbf{1}$ for the all-ones vector, with its dimension clear from context, and
$\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ for the Euclidean inner product. We use $\log$ for the
natural logarithm.

Fix a positive integer $T$ and a known retention coefficient
$\alpha\in(0,1)$. The state $x_t\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}$ is the resource level at the
beginning of time $t$. The system starts empty:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-init}
x_1=0.
\end{equation}
At each time $t\in[T]$, the controller chooses an action
$u_t\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-feasibility}
0\le u_t\le\alpha x_t.
\end{equation}
The system then receives an arrival $w_t\in[0,1]$ and evolves according to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-dynamics}
x_{t+1}=\alpha x_t-u_t+w_t.
\end{equation}
Here, $1-\alpha$ is the fraction of the stored resource lost during one period.
The constraint \eqref{eq:model-feasibility} ensures that the action does not exceed
the amount remaining after this loss.

In the online-control literature, $w_t$ is commonly called a disturbance. Here, we
call it an \emph{arrival} because it is nonnegative, while retaining the standard
names Disturbance-Action Control. The
nonnegativity of $w_t$ is important for both the storage interpretation and the
feasibility properties of SDAC.

At time $t$, the controller chooses $u_t$ before observing $w_t$ or $c_t$. After the
action is selected, the arrival and the entire cost function are revealed, the
controller incurs $c_t(x_t,u_t)$, and the state is updated according to
\eqref{eq:model-dynamics}.

For every feasible trajectory, \eqref{eq:model-dynamics} and $w_t\in[0,1]$ give
\[
0\le x_{t+1}\le\alpha x_t+1.
\]
Since $x_1=0$, induction gives
\[
0\le x_t
\le\frac{1-\alpha^{t-1}}{1-\alpha}
\le\frac{1}{1-\alpha},
\qquad t\in[T+1].
\]
Together with \eqref{eq:model-feasibility}, this bound places every feasible
state--action pair in the compact set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-domain}
\mathcal{D}_\alpha
\triangleq
\left\{
(x,u):
0\le x\le\frac{1}{1-\alpha},
0\le u\le\alpha x
\right\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{remark}[Storage capacity]
A storage capacity of $1/(1-\alpha)$ suffices to avoid overflow.
\end{remark}

Each revealed cost function has the form
\[
c_t:\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^2\to\mathbb{R}
\]
and is convex in $(x,u)$. We also assume that each $c_t$ satisfies the following
Lipschitz condition on $\mathcal{D}_\alpha$: there are constants $L_x,L_u>0$, such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-lipschitz}
|c_t(x,u)-c_t(x',u')|
\le
L_x|x-x'|+L_u|u-u'|
\end{equation}
for all $(x,u),(x',u')\in\mathcal{D}_\alpha$.
We also assume access, at every $(x,u)\in\mathcal{D}_\alpha$, to a cost subgradient
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-cost-subgradient}
\boldsymbol{\zeta}_t(x,u)
=
\bigl(\zeta_t^x(x,u),\zeta_t^u(x,u)\bigr)
\in\partial c_t(x,u)
\end{equation}
satisfying
\[
|\zeta_t^x(x,u)|\le L_x,
\qquad
|\zeta_t^u(x,u)|\le L_u.
\]
These subgradient bounds hold, for example, when
\eqref{eq:model-lipschitz} extends to an open neighborhood of
$\mathcal{D}_\alpha$; see
\cite[Thm.~9.13]{rockafellar1998variational}.

We use $x_t$ and $u_t$ without parameter arguments for the trajectory generated by
the online controller. Its cumulative cost is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-cumulative-cost}
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}
\triangleq
\sum_{t=1}^T c_t(x_t,u_t).
\end{equation}



For a fixed positive integer \(H\), let \(\pi_{\mathbf{m}}\) denote the fixed SDAC
policy indexed by \(\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H\), where \(\mathcal{M}_H\) is defined in Section~\ref{sec:sdac}.
The \emph{fixed-policy trajectory associated with $\mathbf{m}$} is the unique
state--action sequence $\{(x_t(\mathbf{m}),u_t(\mathbf{m}))\}_{t=1}^T$ obtained by
applying $\pi_{\mathbf{m}}$ with $\mathbf{m}$ held
constant at every time.
Define the cumulative cost of this fixed-policy trajectory by
\[
J_T(\mathbf{m})
\triangleq
\sum_{t=1}^T
c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{m}),u_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr).
\]
The regret against the best fixed SDAC policy is defined by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-regret}
\RegSDAC{T}
\triangleq
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}
-
\min_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H}J_T(\mathbf{m}).
\end{equation}
Our objective is to satisfy \eqref{eq:model-feasibility} at every time and achieve
sublinear regret.

For the other comparator classes considered below, $x_t(k)$ and $u_t(k)$ denote
the state and action induced by the feasible fixed-fraction policy
$u_t(k)=kx_t(k)$ for $k\in[0,\alpha]$, while $x_t(\mathbf{q})$ and
$u_t(\mathbf{q})$ denote those
induced by a decreasing allocation sequence $\mathbf{q}$.



\section{Simplex Disturbance-Action Control (SDAC)}
\label{sec:sdac}

\subsection{From Disturbance-Action Control to SDAC}
DAC uses a
finite linear filter of past disturbances. Under standard conditions, this
parameterization yields convex surrogate costs and efficient online updates. In
our scalar storage model, DAC has the following form:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dac-param}
u_t = \sum_{i=1}^{H} \theta_i w_{t-i},
\end{equation}
where $H$ is a positive integer (the memory horizon) and
$\boldsymbol{\theta}=(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_H)^\top\in\mathbb{R}^H$ is a linear
filter.

For our constrained model, an unrestricted filter does not ensure feasibility: its
action can exceed $\alpha x_t$ or become negative.
We therefore introduce \emph{Simplex Disturbance-Action Control} (SDAC), which uses a
simplex-constrained filter tailored to the storage dynamics. This restriction ensures
that every fixed SDAC policy is feasible while preserving the convexity of the surrogate costs and
enabling us to achieve a tractable online algorithm.


\subsection{Policy Definition and Structural Properties}
Fix a memory horizon $H\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}$ and define the subprobability simplex
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-simplex}
\mathcal{M}_H
\triangleq
\left\{
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^H:
\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\le1
\right\}.
\end{equation}
With the convention $w_\tau\equiv0$ for $\tau\le0$, each
$\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$ defines an SDAC policy $\pi_{\mathbf{m}}$ through
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-sdac-policy}
u_t(\mathbf{m})
\;\triangleq\;
\sum_{i=1}^{H} m_i\alpha^i w_{t-i}
=
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}\rangle,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-phi-u}
\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u
\triangleq
\bigl(\alpha w_{t-1},\alpha^2 w_{t-2},\ldots,\alpha^H w_{t-H}\bigr)^\top
\in\mathbb{R}^H.
\end{equation}
The resulting SDAC policy class is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-sdac-class}
\Pi_H^{\mathrm{SDAC}}
\triangleq
\{\pi_{\mathbf{m}}:\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H\}.
\end{equation}

We next establish two structural properties of this policy class. Every
$\pi_{\mathbf{m}}$ satisfies the feasibility constraint
\eqref{eq:model-feasibility}, and its induced cost is convex and Lipschitz in
$\mathbf{m}$. Proofs omitted from this section are collected in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-sdac-proofs}.

\begin{lemma}[State decomposition under SDAC]
\label{lem:sdac-state-decomp}
Fix a positive integer $H$.
For $t\in[T]$ and $i\in[H]$, define
\[
r_t(i)
\triangleq
\sum_{j=1}^{i}\alpha^{j-1}w_{t-j},
\qquad
r_t
\triangleq
\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}\alpha^{j-1}w_{t-j},
\]
where the empty sum defining $r_1$ is zero. Then, for every
$\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$ and $t\in[T]$, the fixed-policy state associated
with $\mathbf{m}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-state-decomp}
x_t(\mathbf{m})
=
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i r_t(i)
+\bigl(1-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\bigr)r_t.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, with
\[
\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x
\triangleq
\bigl(r_t(1)-r_t,\ldots,r_t(H)-r_t\bigr)^\top,
\]
we have
\[
x_t(\mathbf{m})
=
r_t+\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x,\mathbf{m}\rangle.
\]
Thus, $\mathbf{m}\mapsto x_t(\mathbf{m})$ is affine.
\end{lemma}


\begin{lemma}[Feasibility of SDAC policies]
\label{lem:sdac-feasibility}
For every $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, the fixed SDAC policy
$\pi_{\mathbf{m}}$ is feasible:
\[
0\le u_t(\mathbf{m})\le \alpha x_t(\mathbf{m})
\qquad \text{for every }t\in[T].
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $t\in[T]$. For every $i\in[H]$,
\[
\alpha r_t(i)-\alpha^i w_{t-i}
=\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha^j w_{t-j}
\ge0.
\]
Since $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, we have $m_i\ge0$ for every $i\in[H]$ and
$1-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\ge0$. Also, $w_{t-i}\ge0$ for every $i\in[H]$. By the
definition of $r_t$ and the nonnegativity of the arrivals,
\[
r_t=\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}\alpha^{j-1}w_{t-j}\ge0.
\]
Therefore,
\[
\begin{aligned}
0\le u_t(\mathbf{m})
&=\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\alpha^i w_{t-i} \\
&\le\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\alpha r_t(i) \\
&\le\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\alpha r_t(i)
+\alpha\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\right)r_t \\
&=\alpha x_t(\mathbf{m}),
\end{aligned}
\]
where the last equality follows from \eqref{eq:sdac-state-decomp}.
\end{proof}



\begin{lemma}[Convexity and Lipschitz continuity of surrogate cost]
\label{lem:sdac-surrogate}
For $t\in[T]$, define
\[
\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
\triangleq
c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{m}),u_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr),
\qquad
\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H,
\]
and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-surrogate-G}
G_\alpha
\triangleq
\alpha\left(\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}+L_u\right).
\end{equation}
Then $\ell_t$ is convex on $\mathcal{M}_H$, and, for all
$\mathbf{m},\mathbf{m}'\in\mathcal{M}_H$,
\[
|\ell_t(\mathbf{m})-\ell_t(\mathbf{m}')|
\le
G_\alpha\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\|_1.
\]
For each $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, use the cost subgradient from
\eqref{eq:model-cost-subgradient} to write
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-subgradient}
\begin{aligned}
(\zeta_t^x,\zeta_t^u)
&=
\boldsymbol{\zeta}_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{m}),u_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr), \\
\mathbf{g}_t
&\triangleq
\zeta_t^x\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x
+\zeta_t^u\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The resulting vector satisfies
\[
\mathbf{g}_t\in\partial\ell_t(\mathbf{m}),
\qquad
\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\le G_\alpha.
\]
\end{lemma}

\subsection{Approximation of Decreasing-Allocation Policies}
\label{subsec:allocation-sequences}

Define the set of feasible decreasing allocation sequences
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-allocation-sequences}
\mathcal{Q}_\alpha
\triangleq
\left\{
\mathbf{q}=(q_i)_{i\ge1}:
q_1\ge q_2\ge\cdots\ge0,
\quad
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{q_i}{\alpha^i}\le1
\right\}.
\end{equation}
For $\mathbf{q}\in\mathcal{Q}_\alpha$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-allocation-policy}
\begin{aligned}
u_t(\mathbf{q})
&\triangleq
\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}q_iw_{t-i}, \\
x_{t+1}(\mathbf{q})
&=
\alpha x_t(\mathbf{q})-u_t(\mathbf{q})+w_t,
\qquad x_1(\mathbf{q})=0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here, $q_iw_s$ is the amount from arrival $w_s$ allocated $i$ periods later.
The restriction $i\ge1$ gives a one-period delay because $w_s$ is observed only
after $u_s$ is chosen. Policies of this form have been studied for Bernoulli
recharges \cite{arafa2018online,zibaeenejad2019lookahead}. Let
\[
\Pi_\alpha^{\mathrm{alloc}}
\triangleq
\{\pi_{\mathbf{q}}:\mathbf{q}\in\mathcal{Q}_\alpha\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Finite-memory approximation of decreasing-allocation policies]
\label{lem:sdac-allocation-approx}
Every policy in $\Pi_\alpha^{\mathrm{alloc}}$ is feasible for every arrival
sequence with entries in $[0,1]$. Fix
$\mathbf{q}\in\mathcal{Q}_\alpha$ and an integer $H\ge1$, and define
\[
m_{H,i}(\mathbf{q})
\triangleq
\frac{q_i}{\alpha^i},
\qquad i\in[H].
\]
Then $\mathbf{m}_H(\mathbf{q})\in\mathcal{M}_H$. Let
\[
\operatorname{Tail}_H(\mathbf{q})
\triangleq
\sum_{i=H+1}^{\infty}q_i,
\]
so $\operatorname{Tail}_H(\mathbf{q})\le\alpha^{H+1}$. If each $c_t$
satisfies \eqref{eq:model-lipschitz}, then, for every such arrival sequence,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:allocation-cost-gap}
\begin{aligned}
&\left|
\sum_{t=1}^T
\left[
c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{m}_H(\mathbf{q})),u_t(\mathbf{m}_H(\mathbf{q}))\bigr)
-c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{q}),u_t(\mathbf{q})\bigr)
\right]
\right|\\
&\qquad\le
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)
\operatorname{Tail}_H(\mathbf{q})T \\
&\qquad\le
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)\alpha^{H+1}T.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{corollary}[Fixed-fraction policies as geometric allocations]
\label{cor:fraction-as-allocation}
Fix $k\in[0,\alpha]$, set $\lambda_k\triangleq\alpha-k$, and define
\[
q_i(k)=k\lambda_k^{i-1},
\qquad i\ge1,
\]
where $\lambda_k^0\triangleq1$. Then $\mathbf{q}(k)\in\mathcal{Q}_\alpha$
and, for every arrival sequence with entries in $[0,1]$,
\[
\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{q}(k)),u_t(\mathbf{q}(k))\bigr)
=
\bigl(x_t(k),u_t(k)\bigr),
\qquad t\in[T].
\]
Thus, $\pi_{\mathbf{q}(k)}$ realizes the feasible fixed-fraction policy
$u_t(k)=k x_t(k)$. For every integer $H\ge1$, define its SDAC truncation by
\[
\mathbf{m}_H^{\mathrm{frac}}(k)
\triangleq
\mathbf{m}_H\bigl(\mathbf{q}(k)\bigr)
\in\mathcal{M}_H,
\]
whose omitted tail is
\[
\operatorname{Tail}_H\bigl(\mathbf{q}(k)\bigr)
=
\frac{k\lambda_k^H}{1-\lambda_k}.
\]
If each $c_t$ satisfies \eqref{eq:model-lipschitz}, then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-fraction-approx-cost}
\begin{aligned}
&\left|
\sum_{t=1}^T
\left[
c_t\!\bigl(
x_t(\mathbf{m}_H^{\mathrm{frac}}(k)),
u_t(\mathbf{m}_H^{\mathrm{frac}}(k))
\bigr)
-c_t\bigl(x_t(k),u_t(k)\bigr)
\right]
\right| \\
&\qquad\le
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)
\frac{k\lambda_k^H}{1-\lambda_k}\,T.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}







\section{Online SDAC Controller}
\label{sec:online-algorithm}

The controller maintains a parameter $\mathbf{m}_t\in\mathcal{M}_H$ and updates
it by OMD with unnormalized negentropy and step size $\eta>0$
\cite{hazan2016introduction}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-negentropy}
\Psi(\mathbf{m}) \triangleq \sum_{i=1}^H\bigl(m_i\log m_i-m_i\bigr), \qquad
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^H,
\end{equation}
where $0\log0\triangleq0$. For $\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^H$ and
$\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$, its Bregman divergence is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-bregman}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{v})
\triangleq
\sum_{i=1}^H\Bigl(m_i\log\tfrac{m_i}{v_i}-m_i+v_i\Bigr).
\end{equation}
Initialize the online parameter as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-init}
\mathbf{m}_1\triangleq\frac{1}{H+1}\mathbf{1}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Action selection}
At the start of round $t$, the controller has the online parameter
$\mathbf{m}_t$. For $t=1$, this parameter is initialized according to
\eqref{eq:alg-init}; for $t\ge2$, it is the output of the OMD update from round
$t-1$.
Because $\mathbf{m}_t$ varies with $t$, the nominal action
$\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\rangle$ may exceed the current upper
bound $\alpha x_t$. The controller therefore selects
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-feasible-action}
u_t
\triangleq
\min\!\left\{
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\rangle,
\alpha x_t
\right\}.
\end{equation}
The state then evolves according to \eqref{eq:model-dynamics}.
Since $\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u$ and $\mathbf{m}_t$ are componentwise nonnegative,
$\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\rangle\ge0$. If $x_t\ge0$, then
\eqref{eq:alg-feasible-action} gives $0\le u_t\le\alpha x_t$, and
\eqref{eq:model-dynamics} gives $x_{t+1}\ge w_t\ge0$. Since $x_1=0$, induction
shows that the online trajectory is feasible.

The clipping step also has an interesting interpretation. Lemma~\ref{lem:availability-projection} shows that the $D_\Psi$-projection of
$\mathbf{m}_t$ onto the parameters compatible with $x_t$ induces exactly the
clipped action $u_t$.
\paragraph{Surrogate cost and OMD update}
After $w_t$ and $c_t$ are revealed, the controller evaluates the subgradient
$\mathbf{g}_t$ in \eqref{eq:surr-subgradient} at $\mathbf{m}_t$ and sets
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-omd-argmin}
\mathbf{m}_{t+1}
\triangleq
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H}
\left\{
\eta\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}\rangle
+D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_t)
\right\}.
\end{equation}
Lemma~\ref{lem:omd-closed-form} gives a two-step implementation. First, take the
coordinatewise multiplicative step
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-omd-mult}
\widetilde{m}_{t+1,i}
\triangleq
m_{t,i}\exp\!\bigl(-\eta g_{t,i}\bigr),
\qquad
i\in[H].
\end{equation}
Next, project onto $\mathcal{M}_H$, which here amounts to rescaling if needed:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-omd-proj}
\mathbf{m}_{t+1}
\triangleq
\operatorname{Proj}_{\mathcal{M}_H}^{\Psi}(\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1})
=
\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}}{\max\{1,\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}\|_1\}},
\end{equation}
where the equality follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:bregman-projection}.


\section{Regret Upper Bounds}
\label{sec:regret-analysis}
The following theorem states that the online SDAC controller achieves sublinear
regret relative to every fixed SDAC policy
$\pi_{\mathbf{m}}\in\Pi_H^{\mathrm{SDAC}}$. Proofs omitted from this section are
collected in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-regret-proofs}.

\begin{theorem}[Regret against fixed SDAC policies]
\label{thm:regret-bound}
Suppose the cost functions satisfy the regularity and bounded-subgradient
assumptions of Section~\ref{sec:model-setup}. Then the controller from
Section~\ref{sec:online-algorithm}, initialized as in \eqref{eq:alg-init} and using
step size $\eta>0$, satisfies, for the regret defined in \eqref{eq:def-regret},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:regret-theorem-bound}
\RegSDAC{T}
\le
\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta}
+
\eta T\Gamma_\alpha,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-Gamma-alpha}
\Gamma_\alpha
\triangleq
\frac{G_\alpha^2}{2}
+
\frac{\alpha G_\alpha(L_x+\alpha L_u)}{(1-\alpha)^2},
\end{equation}
and $G_\alpha=\alpha\bigl(\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}+L_u\bigr)$ as in
\eqref{eq:def-surrogate-G}.
In particular, the choice
\[
\eta^*
=
\sqrt{\frac{\log(H+1)}{T\Gamma_\alpha}}
\]
gives
\[
\RegSDAC{T}
\le
2\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha T\log(H+1)}.
\]
For fixed $L_x,L_u>0$, the leading factor obeys
\[
\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha}=\Theta(\alpha)
\quad\text{as }\alpha\downarrow0,
\]
and
\[
\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha}
=\Theta\!\left((1-\alpha)^{-3/2}\right)
\quad\text{as }\alpha\uparrow1.
\]
\end{theorem}

The nonstandard step is to control the difference between the realized online
trajectory and the fixed-policy trajectory associated with the current online
parameter $\mathbf{m}_t$.

\begin{lemma}[Stability under online parameter updates]
\label{lem:contractive-deviation}
For the online trajectory and the fixed-policy trajectory associated with the
current online parameter $\mathbf{m}_t$, one has, for every $t\in[T]$,
\begin{align}
\left|x_t-x_t(\mathbf{m}_t)\right|
&\le
\frac{\alpha\eta G_\alpha}{(1-\alpha)^2},
\label{eq:contractive-state-bound}\\
0\le u_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-u_t
&\le
\frac{\alpha^2\eta G_\alpha}{(1-\alpha)^2}.
\label{eq:contractive-action-bound}
\end{align}
By definition of the fixed-policy trajectory, $x_t(\mathbf{m}_t)$ is generated by
holding $\mathbf{m}_t$ constant from time $1$ through time $t$. Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stability-sum-bound}
\sum_{t=1}^T \Bigl(c_t(x_t,u_t)-\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)\Bigr)
\le
\frac{\alpha\eta T G_\alpha(L_x+\alpha L_u)}{(1-\alpha)^2}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound} has three steps. First, the OMD bound
for the surrogate costs gives
\[
\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)
-\min_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H}\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
\le
\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta}+\frac{\eta G_\alpha^2T}{2}.
\]
Second, \eqref{eq:stability-sum-bound} controls the difference between the
realized and surrogate costs. Third, adding the two bounds and using
\eqref{eq:def-Gamma-alpha} gives \eqref{eq:regret-theorem-bound}; optimizing over
$\eta$ gives the stated choice $\eta^*$.

Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-allocation-approx} give the following comparator guarantee.

\begin{corollary}[No-regret against decreasing-allocation policies]
\label{cor:regret-vs-allocation}
Set
\[
H_T
\triangleq
\max\!\left\{1,\left\lceil\frac{\log T}{\log(1/\alpha)}\right\rceil\right\},
\]
and run the online SDAC controller with action clipping, memory horizon $H=H_T$,
and the step size $\eta^*$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound}. For
$\mathbf{q}\in\mathcal{Q}_\alpha$, let
\[
J_T(\mathbf{q})
\triangleq
\sum_{t=1}^T c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{q}),u_t(\mathbf{q})\bigr)
\]
and
\[
\RegAlloc{T}
\triangleq
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}
-
\inf_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathcal{Q}_\alpha}J_T(\mathbf{q}).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:allocation-regret-bound}
\begin{aligned}
\RegAlloc{T}
&\le
2\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha T\log(H_T+1)}\\
&\quad+
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)\alpha^{H_T+1}T.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $\Gamma_\alpha$ is defined in \eqref{eq:def-Gamma-alpha}. Since
$\alpha^{H_T}\le1/T$ and
$\log(H_T+1)=O(\log\log T)$ as $T\to\infty$,
the approximation term is $O(1)$. Consequently,
\[
\RegAlloc{T}
=
O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log\log T}\right)
=o(T).
\]
\end{corollary}

Because geometric allocation sequences induce fixed-fraction policies, the
controller also satisfies a corresponding bound for that policy class.

\begin{corollary}[No-regret against fixed-fraction policies]
\label{cor:regret-vs-fraction}
Run the online SDAC controller in Corollary~\ref{cor:regret-vs-allocation}. For
$k\in[0,\alpha]$, let
$J_T(k)\triangleq\sum_{t=1}^T c_t\bigl(x_t(k),u_t(k)\bigr)$, where
$u_t(k)=k x_t(k)$, and define
\[
\RegFrac{T}
\triangleq
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}-\min_{k\in[0,\alpha]}J_T(k).
\]
For every $k\in[0,\alpha]$, with $\lambda_k=\alpha-k$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fraction-regret-decomposition}
\begin{aligned}
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}-J_T(k)
&\le
2\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha T\log(H_T+1)} \\
&\quad+
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)
\frac{k\lambda_k^{H_T}}{1-\lambda_k}\,T.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\RegFrac{T}
&\le
2\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha T\log(H_T+1)}
\\
&\quad+
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)\alpha^{H_T+1}T
\\
&=
O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log\log T}\right)
=o(T).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{corollary}

\section{Minimax Regret Lower Bound}
\label{sec:minimax-lower-bound}

The next result shows that the $\sqrt{T}$ dependence is unavoidable and quantifies
the necessary dependence on $1-\alpha$ for any causal feasible controller. Proofs
omitted from this section are collected in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-minimax-proofs}.

\begin{theorem}[Minimax SDAC regret lower bound]
\label{thm:sdac-lower-bound}
Fix $H\ge1$ and $T\ge2$. For every causal feasible online controller
$\mathcal{A}$, possibly randomized, there exists a deterministic oblivious sequence
of arrivals and costs satisfying the assumptions of
Section~\ref{sec:model-setup} such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-lower-exact}
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\!\left[\RegSDAC{T}\right]
\ge
\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}
\sqrt{\sum_{t=2}^T \beta_t^2},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-lower-beta}
\beta_t
\triangleq
\alpha\left(
L_u
+
\frac{L_x(1-\alpha^{t-2})}{1-\alpha}
\right).
\end{equation}
The expectation is only over the internal randomization of $\mathcal{A}$; the
adversarial sequence is deterministic and fixed before the interaction.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Lower bound beyond the mixing time]
\label{cor:sdac-lower-mixing}
Define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-mixing-time}
t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)
\triangleq
\left\lceil
\frac{\log 2}{\log(1/\alpha)}
\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
If $T\ge2t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)+2$, then every causal feasible controller $\mathcal{A}$
admits a deterministic oblivious sequence for which
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-lower-mixing}
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\!\left[\RegSDAC{T}\right]
\ge
\frac{G_\alpha}{8}\sqrt{T},
\end{equation}
where $G_\alpha=\alpha(L_x/(1-\alpha)+L_u)$ as in
\eqref{eq:def-surrogate-G}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Large-$\alpha$ regime]
\label{rem:large-alpha-regime}
As $\alpha\uparrow1$, stored arrivals persist longer and
$t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)=\Theta((1-\alpha)^{-1})$. Hence,
Corollary~\ref{cor:sdac-lower-mixing} yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-lower-large-alpha}
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\!\left[\RegSDAC{T}\right]
=
\Omega\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{T}}{1-\alpha}\right)
\end{equation}
whenever $T\ge2t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)+2$. The upper bound in
Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound} is
$O((1-\alpha)^{-3/2}\sqrt{T\log(H+1)})$, leaving a factor of
$(1-\alpha)^{-1/2}$ between the two bounds.

The condition $T\ge2t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)+2$ is essential for interpreting the
large-$\alpha$ asymptotic. If $T$ is fixed while $\alpha\uparrow1$, then
\[
\frac{1-\alpha^{t-2}}{1-\alpha}\longrightarrow t-2,
\]
and the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:sdac-lower-exact} converges to the finite
quantity
\[
\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}
\sqrt{\sum_{t=2}^T\bigl(L_u+L_x(t-2)\bigr)^2}.
\]
Thus, for fixed $T$, the exact finite-horizon bound in
\eqref{eq:sdac-lower-exact} applies instead of the long-horizon scaling in
\eqref{eq:sdac-lower-large-alpha}.
\end{remark}

\section{Discussion and Extensions}
\label{sec:extensions}

We briefly discuss three extensions. In each case, the feasibility, convexity, and
stability arguments used above continue to apply after the corresponding changes
to the policy domain and the Lipschitz constants. We state these changes but omit
the resulting regret bounds, whose form is unchanged up to the modified constants
and, in the vector settings, the chosen product norm.

\paragraph{Retention $\alpha\ge 1$}
For $\alpha\ge1$, fix $k$ such that
$\bar\alpha\triangleq\alpha-k\in(0,1)$ and write $u_t=kx_t+v_t$. Then
\[
x_{t+1}=\bar\alpha x_t-v_t+w_t.
\]
An SDAC filter for $v_t$ uses the powers $\bar\alpha^i$. If
$0\le v_t\le\bar\alpha x_t$, then
\[
kx_t\le u_t=kx_t+v_t
\le(k+\bar\alpha)x_t
=\alpha x_t,
\]
so the original feasibility constraint \eqref{eq:model-feasibility} is satisfied.
Since
\[
x_{t+1}\le\bar\alpha x_t+1,
\qquad x_1=0,
\]
induction gives $x_t\le1/(1-\bar\alpha)$. The transformed cost
$c_t(x,kx+v)$ remains convex in $(x,v)$. Thus the analysis applies when the costs
satisfy the corresponding Lipschitz and bounded-subgradient assumptions on the
bounded domain
\[
0\le x\le\frac{1}{1-\bar\alpha},
\qquad
kx\le u\le\alpha x.
\]
For a fixed $k$, the comparator class is correspondingly restricted to policies
whose actions satisfy $u_t\ge kx_t$. Selecting $k$, either in advance or online,
remains an interesting direction for future work.

\paragraph{Vector actions and multi-task allocation}
Consider a single storage state $x_t$ with a vector action
$\mathbf{u}_t\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^n$ that allocates resources across $n$ tasks,
subject to $\sum_{a=1}^n u_{t,a}\le\alpha x_t$. Thus, the total drain from storage
is the sum of the task allocations. The dynamics remain
$x_{t+1}=\alpha x_t-\sum_{a=1}^n u_{t,a}+w_t$, and the cost
$c_t(x_t,\mathbf{u}_t)$ is convex in $(x,\mathbf{u})$. Replace
$\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$ with a matrix
$\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^{n\times H}$ satisfying
\[
\sum_{a=1}^n\sum_{i=1}^H M_{a,i}\le1,
\qquad
u_{t,a}(\mathbf{M})
=\sum_{i=1}^H M_{a,i}\alpha^i w_{t-i}.
\]
Set $m_i=\sum_{a=1}^n M_{a,i}$. Then
\[
m_i\ge0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i
=\sum_{a=1}^n\sum_{i=1}^H M_{a,i}
\le1,
\]
so $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$. Moreover,
\[
\sum_{a=1}^n u_{t,a}(\mathbf{M})
=\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\alpha^i w_{t-i}
\le\alpha x_t(\mathbf{M}),
\]
where the inequality is the scalar SDAC feasibility bound.
Both the state and vector action are affine in $\mathbf{M}$. Therefore,
\[
c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{M}),\mathbf{u}_t(\mathbf{M})\bigr)
\]
is convex in $\mathbf{M}$. For the
online SDAC controller, a nominal vector that exceeds the available total resource
can be scaled proportionally so that its entries remain nonnegative and sum to
$\alpha x_t$. Under an $\ell_1$ Lipschitz bound in the action, the size of this
correction equals the excess total action, and the stability proof proceeds with
the corresponding matrix norm. OMD then runs over the matrix subprobability
simplex above.

\paragraph{Multiple storage states with diagonal dynamics}
Suppose the state is $\mathbf{x}_t\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^d$ and evolves according to
$\mathbf{x}_{t+1}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_t-\mathbf{u}_t+\mathbf{w}_t$, where
$\mathbf{A}=\operatorname{diag}(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_d)$,
$\alpha_s\in(0,1)$ for $s\in[d]$, the arrivals satisfy
$\mathbf{w}_t\in[0,1]^d$, and the per-storage constraints are
$0\le u_{t,s}\le\alpha_s x_{t,s}$. Assign one SDAC parameter in $\mathcal{M}_H$
to each storage and use the powers $\alpha_s^i$ in coordinate $s$. The dynamics
and feasibility constraints then decouple by storage, and the SDAC parameter domain
is a product of subprobability simplices, with
$x_{t,s}\le1/(1-\alpha_s)$ for every $s\in[d]$. The online SDAC controller clips
each nominal action to $\alpha_s x_{t,s}$. If the cost is jointly convex and
satisfies compatible Lipschitz and bounded-subgradient assumptions on the resulting bounded product
domain, the surrogate cost is jointly convex in all SDAC parameters and OMD applies
on the product set. The update separates by storage only when the cost is
coordinate-separable; otherwise it is a joint update.
Cross-coupled storage systems (nondiagonal $\mathbf{A}$) require additional
analysis and remain an interesting direction for future work.



\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied adversarial online control of a storage system under a state-dependent
action constraint. We introduced SDAC so that every fixed-policy trajectory is
feasible and developed an online SDAC controller with
$O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log(H+1)}\right)$ regret against the best fixed SDAC policy.
Once $T$ is at least on the order of $(1-\alpha)^{-1}$, the
minimax lower bound gives
$\Omega((1-\alpha)^{-1}\sqrt{T})$ regret as $\alpha\uparrow1$; hence, a factor of
$(1-\alpha)^{-1/2}$ between the upper and lower bounds remains open. The
SDAC approximation results further give
$O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log\log T}\right)$ regret against the best decreasing-allocation
policy when $H=\Theta(\log T)$. The model applies directly to
energy-harvesting battery management and related settings in which storage limits
must be respected in real time.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-state-decomp}]
For $t\in[T]$ and $i\in[H]$, the definitions give
\begin{align*}
r_{t+1}
&=\sum_{j=1}^{t}\alpha^{j-1}w_{t+1-j} \\
&=w_t+\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}\alpha^j w_{t-j} 
=w_t+\alpha r_t, \\
r_{t+1}(i)
&=\sum_{j=1}^{i}\alpha^{j-1}w_{t+1-j} \\
&=w_t+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha^j w_{t-j} 
=w_t+\alpha r_t(i)-\alpha^i w_{t-i}.
\end{align*}
The sum from $j=1$ to $s-1$ is empty when $s=1$.

At $t=1$, \eqref{eq:model-init} gives $x_1(\mathbf{m})=0$, while
$r_1(i)=r_1=0$ by the arrival and empty-sum conventions. Thus,
\eqref{eq:sdac-state-decomp} holds at $t=1$. Suppose it holds at some
$t$ with $1\le t<T$. By \eqref{eq:model-dynamics} and
\eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy},
\begin{align*}
x_{t+1}(\mathbf{m})
&=\alpha x_t(\mathbf{m})-u_t(\mathbf{m})+w_t \\
&=\alpha\left[
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i r_t(i)
+\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\right)r_t
\right] \\
&\quad
-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\alpha^i w_{t-i}+w_t \\
&=\sum_{i=1}^H m_i
\bigl(\alpha r_t(i)-\alpha^i w_{t-i}\bigr) \\
&\quad
+\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\right)\alpha r_t+w_t \\
&=\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\bigl(r_{t+1}(i)-w_t\bigr) \\
&\quad
+\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\right)(r_{t+1}-w_t)+w_t \\
&=\sum_{i=1}^H m_i r_{t+1}(i)
+\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\right)r_{t+1}.
\end{align*}
Thus, \eqref{eq:sdac-state-decomp} follows by induction.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-surrogate}]
Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-state-decomp} and \eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy} give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-affine-pair}
x_t(\mathbf{m})=r_t+\langle \boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x,\mathbf{m}\rangle,
\qquad
u_t(\mathbf{m})=\langle \boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}\rangle.
\end{equation}
For $i\in[H]$, Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-state-decomp} and the definitions of
$r_t(i)$ and $r_t$ give
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\phi_{t,i}^x|&=r_t-r_t(i)
=\sum_{j=i+1}^{t-1}\alpha^{j-1}w_{t-j} \\
&\le\sum_{j=i+1}^{\infty}\alpha^{j-1}
=\frac{\alpha^i}{1-\alpha}
\le\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}, \\
0\le\phi_{t,i}^u&=\alpha^i w_{t-i}\le\alpha^i\le\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\]
The sum is empty when $i\ge t-1$. Taking the maximum over $i\in[H]$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-coefficient-norms}
\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x\|_\infty\le\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha},
\qquad
\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u\|_\infty\le\alpha.
\end{equation}
Since the state--action pair in \eqref{eq:surr-affine-pair} is affine in
$\mathbf{m}$ and $c_t$ is convex, $\ell_t$ is convex.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-feasibility}, the fixed-policy state--action pairs
associated with $\mathbf{m}$ and $\mathbf{m}'$ belong to $\mathcal{D}_\alpha$.
Thus, \eqref{eq:model-lipschitz}, \eqref{eq:surr-affine-pair},
Hölder's inequality, and \eqref{eq:surr-coefficient-norms} give
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\ell_t(\mathbf{m})-\ell_t(\mathbf{m}')|
&\le
L_x|x_t(\mathbf{m})-x_t(\mathbf{m}')| \\
&\quad{}
+L_u|u_t(\mathbf{m})-u_t(\mathbf{m}')| \\
&=
L_x\bigl|\langle \boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x,\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\rangle\bigr| \\
&\quad{}
+
L_u\bigl|\langle \boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\rangle\bigr| \\
&\le
\left(\frac{\alpha L_x}{1-\alpha}+\alpha L_u\right)
\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\|_1 \\
&=
G_\alpha\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\]

Fix $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, and let $(\zeta_t^x,\zeta_t^u)$ and
$\mathbf{g}_t$ be defined by \eqref{eq:surr-subgradient}. For every
$\mathbf{m}'\in\mathcal{M}_H$, the subgradient inequality gives
\begin{align*}
\ell_t(\mathbf{m}')
&=c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{m}'),u_t(\mathbf{m}')\bigr) \\
&\ge c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{m}),u_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr) \\
&\quad
+\zeta_t^x\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{m}')-x_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr)
+\zeta_t^u\bigl(u_t(\mathbf{m}')-u_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr) \\
&=\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
+\zeta_t^x\langle \boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x,\mathbf{m}'-\mathbf{m}\rangle
+\zeta_t^u\langle \boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}'-\mathbf{m}\rangle \\
&=
\ell_t(\mathbf{m})+\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}'-\mathbf{m}\rangle.
\end{align*}
Thus $\mathbf{g}_t\in\partial\ell_t(\mathbf{m})$. The bounded-subgradient
assumption and \eqref{eq:surr-coefficient-norms} give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty
&\le
|\zeta_t^x|\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x\|_\infty
+|\zeta_t^u|\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u\|_\infty \\
&\le
\frac{\alpha L_x}{1-\alpha}+\alpha L_u \\
&=G_\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-allocation-approx}]
Since $\mathbf{q}\in\mathcal{Q}_\alpha$,
\[
m_{H,i}(\mathbf{q})=\frac{q_i}{\alpha^i}\ge0,
\qquad
\|\mathbf{m}_H(\mathbf{q})\|_1
=\sum_{i=1}^H\frac{q_i}{\alpha^i}\le1.
\]
Thus $\mathbf{m}_H(\mathbf{q})\in\mathcal{M}_H$.

We next prove that $\pi_{\mathbf{q}}$ is feasible. For this purpose, take the
truncation length to be $T$. Using
$w_\tau=0$ for $\tau\le0$, for every $t\in[T]$,
\[
u_t\bigl(\mathbf{m}_T(\mathbf{q})\bigr)
=\sum_{i=1}^T\frac{q_i}{\alpha^i}\alpha^i w_{t-i}
=\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}q_iw_{t-i}=u_t(\mathbf{q}).
\]
The two policies have the same initial state and state recursion, so their
state trajectories coincide. Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-feasibility} therefore gives
\[
0\le u_t(\mathbf{q})\le\alpha x_t(\mathbf{q}),
\qquad t\in[T].
\]

For the approximation error, define
\[
e_t^u\triangleq
u_t(\mathbf{q})-u_t\bigl(\mathbf{m}_H(\mathbf{q})\bigr),
\qquad
e_t^x\triangleq
x_t\bigl(\mathbf{m}_H(\mathbf{q})\bigr)-x_t(\mathbf{q}).
\]
Again using $w_\tau=0$ for $\tau\le0$,
\[
e_t^u
=
\sum_{i=H+1}^{\infty}q_iw_{t-i},
\qquad
0\le e_t^u\le\operatorname{Tail}_H(\mathbf{q}).
\]
Subtracting the two state recursions gives
\[
e_{t+1}^x=\alpha e_t^x+e_t^u,
\qquad e_1^x=0.
\]
Hence, with the sum understood to be zero at $t=1$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
0\le e_t^x
&=\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\alpha^{t-1-s}e_s^u
\le\operatorname{Tail}_H(\mathbf{q})
\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\alpha^{t-1-s}\\
&=\operatorname{Tail}_H(\mathbf{q})
\frac{1-\alpha^{t-1}}{1-\alpha}
\le\frac{\operatorname{Tail}_H(\mathbf{q})}{1-\alpha}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Both policies are feasible, so their state--action pairs belong to
$\mathcal{D}_\alpha$. By \eqref{eq:model-lipschitz} and the triangle
inequality,
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\left|
\sum_{t=1}^T
\left[
c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{m}_H(\mathbf{q})),u_t(\mathbf{m}_H(\mathbf{q}))\bigr)
-c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{q}),u_t(\mathbf{q})\bigr)
\right]
\right|\\
&\qquad\le
\sum_{t=1}^T\left(L_xe_t^x+L_ue_t^u\right)
\le
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)
\operatorname{Tail}_H(\mathbf{q})T.
\end{aligned}
\]
Finally, since $\alpha\in(0,1)$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Tail}_H(\mathbf{q})
&=\sum_{i=H+1}^{\infty}\alpha^i\frac{q_i}{\alpha^i}
\le\alpha^{H+1}
\sum_{i=H+1}^{\infty}\frac{q_i}{\alpha^i}\le\alpha^{H+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:allocation-cost-gap}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:fraction-as-allocation}]
Since $0\le\lambda_k\le\alpha<1$,
\[
q_i(k)\ge0,\qquad
q_{i+1}(k)=\lambda_k q_i(k)\le q_i(k),\qquad i\ge1.
\]
If $k=0$, then $\mathbf{q}(k)=\mathbf{0}\in\mathcal{Q}_\alpha$. If $k>0$,
then $0\le\lambda_k/\alpha<1$ and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{q_i(k)}{\alpha^i}
&=\frac{k}{\alpha}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}
\left(\frac{\lambda_k}{\alpha}\right)^j
=\frac{k}{\alpha}\frac{1}{1-\lambda_k/\alpha}\\
&=\frac{k}{\alpha-\lambda_k}=1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus, $\mathbf{q}(k)\in\mathcal{Q}_\alpha$.

Under the fixed-fraction policy, \eqref{eq:model-dynamics} becomes
\[
x_{t+1}(k)=\lambda_k x_t(k)+w_t.
\]
Iterating this recursion from $x_1(k)=0$ gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
x_t(k)&=\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\lambda_k^{i-1}w_{t-i},\\
u_t(k)&=k x_t(k)=\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}q_i(k)w_{t-i}
=u_t\bigl(\mathbf{q}(k)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
The two policies have the same actions, initial state, and dynamics. Hence,
their state trajectories coincide. Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-allocation-approx}
gives $\mathbf{m}_H^{\mathrm{frac}}(k)\in\mathcal{M}_H$. Moreover,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Tail}_H\bigl(\mathbf{q}(k)\bigr)
&=k\sum_{i=H+1}^{\infty}\lambda_k^{i-1}
=k\lambda_k^H\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\lambda_k^j\\
&=\frac{k\lambda_k^H}{1-\lambda_k}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Substituting the trajectory and tail identities into
\eqref{eq:allocation-cost-gap} proves \eqref{eq:sdac-fraction-approx-cost}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Action clipping as a Bregman projection]
\label{lem:availability-projection}
For $t\in[T]$, define the set of parameters compatible with the current state by
\[
\mathcal{B}_t(x_t)
\triangleq
\left\{
\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H:
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}\rangle
\le \alpha x_t
\right\}.
\]
Let $\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}$ be the Bregman projection of
$\mathbf{m}_t$ onto $\mathcal{B}_t(x_t)$:
\[
\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}
\in
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{B}_t(x_t)}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_t).
\]
Then
\[
\left\langle
\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}
\right\rangle
=
\min\!\left\{
\left\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\right\rangle,
\alpha x_t
\right\}
=u_t.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The online state satisfies $x_t\ge0$, so
$\mathbf{0}\in\mathcal{B}_t(x_t)$. This set is compact and convex because it is
the intersection of $\mathcal{M}_H$ and a closed halfspace. By
\eqref{eq:alg-init} and the update
\eqref{eq:alg-omd-mult}--\eqref{eq:alg-omd-proj}, every coordinate of
$\mathbf{m}_t$ is positive. Thus,
$D_\Psi(\cdot\|\mathbf{m}_t)$ is continuous and strictly convex on
$\mathcal{M}_H$, so the projection exists and is unique. Moreover,
$D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_t)\ge0$, with equality only at
$\mathbf{m}=\mathbf{m}_t$.

If
\[
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\rangle
\le \alpha x_t,
\]
then $\mathbf{m}_t\in\mathcal{B}_t(x_t)$ and
$\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}=\mathbf{m}_t$.

Now suppose
\[
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\rangle
>\alpha x_t.
\]
Since $\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\in\mathcal{B}_t(x_t)$,
\[
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\rangle
\le\alpha x_t
<\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\rangle.
\]
Suppose, for contradiction, that the action constraint is slack at
$\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}$:
\[
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\rangle
<\alpha x_t
<\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\rangle.
\]
We move a small distance from $\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}$ toward
$\mathbf{m}_t$. The step is chosen to remain in $\mathcal{B}_t(x_t)$; strict
convexity will then give a smaller Bregman divergence, contradicting the
optimality of $\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}$. Choose $0<\delta<1$ such that
\[
\delta\left(
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\rangle-
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\rangle
\right)
<
\alpha x_t-
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\rangle.
\]
Such a $\delta$ exists because both differences are positive. Set
\[
\mathbf{m}_\delta
\triangleq
(1-\delta)\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}+\delta\mathbf{m}_t.
\]
By convexity, $\mathbf{m}_\delta\in\mathcal{M}_H$, and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_\delta\rangle
&=
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\rangle
+\delta\left(
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\rangle-
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\rangle
\right) <\alpha x_t.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus $\mathbf{m}_\delta\in\mathcal{B}_t(x_t)$. Since
$\mathbf{m}_t\notin\mathcal{B}_t(x_t)$,
$\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\ne\mathbf{m}_t$, and hence
$D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\|\mathbf{m}_t)>0$. Strict convexity and
$D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t\|\mathbf{m}_t)=0$ give
\begin{align*}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_\delta\|\mathbf{m}_t)
&<
(1-\delta)D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\|\mathbf{m}_t)
+\delta D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t\|\mathbf{m}_t) \\
&=
(1-\delta)D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\|\mathbf{m}_t) \\
&<
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\|\mathbf{m}_t),
\end{align*}
contradicting the definition of $\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}$. Therefore, the
constraint is active:
\[
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\rangle
=\alpha x_t.
\]
Combining the two cases with \eqref{eq:alg-feasible-action} proves the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Bregman projection onto $\mathcal{M}_H$]
\label{lem:bregman-projection}
For the Bregman divergence $D_\Psi$ in \eqref{eq:def-bregman} and every
$\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$,
\[
\operatorname{Proj}_{\mathcal{M}_H}^{\Psi}(\widetilde{\mathbf{m}})
\triangleq
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}})
=
\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}}{\max\{1,\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|_1\}}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The function $r\mapsto r\log r-r+1$ is nonnegative for $r\ge0$ and vanishes
only at $r=1$. Writing the $i$th summand in \eqref{eq:def-bregman} as
$v_i(r\log r-r+1)$, where $r=m_i/v_i$, therefore gives
\[
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{v})\ge0,
\qquad
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{v})=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathbf{m}=\mathbf{v}.
\]
If $\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|_1\le1$, then
$\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, so the equality condition shows that
it is the unique minimizer.

Now suppose $\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|_1>1$ and set
\[
\mathbf{m}^\star
\triangleq
\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}}{\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|_1}.
\]
Then $\mathbf{m}^\star\in\mathcal{M}_H\cap\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$ and
\[
\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}
=\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|_1\mathbf{m}^\star,
\qquad
\|\mathbf{m}^\star\|_1=1.
\]
For every $i\in[H]$, the first identity gives
\[
\begin{split}
m_i\log\frac{m_i}{\widetilde{m}_i}
&=m_i\log\frac{m_i}{m_i^\star}
-m_i\log\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|_1, \\
m_i^\star\log\frac{m_i^\star}{\widetilde{m}_i}
&=-m_i^\star\log\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|_1.
\end{split}
\]
Using these identities in \eqref{eq:def-bregman}, for every
$\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
&D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}})
-D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}^\star\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}) \\
&=\sum_{i=1}^H
\left(
m_i\log\frac{m_i}{m_i^\star}-m_i+m_i^\star
\right)
+\sum_{i=1}^H(m_i^\star-m_i)
\log\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|_1 \\
&=D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}^\star)
+\bigl(1-\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\bigr)
\log\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|_1
\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last inequality uses $D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}^\star)\ge0$,
$\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\le1$, and $\log\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|_1>0$.
Equality requires $\mathbf{m}=\mathbf{m}^\star$. Thus,
$\mathbf{m}^\star$ is the unique minimizer.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Closed-form solution of the OMD update]
\label{lem:omd-closed-form}
Fix $\mathbf{m}_t\in\mathcal{M}_H\cap\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$, $\eta>0$, and
$\mathbf{g}_t\in\mathbb{R}^H$. Define
$\widetilde{m}_{t+1,i}\triangleq m_{t,i}\exp\!\bigl(-\eta g_{t,i}\bigr)$ for
$i\in[H]$. Then the
OMD update \eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin} has the unique solution
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{m}_{t+1}
&=\operatorname{Proj}_{\mathcal{M}_H}^{\Psi}(\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}) 
=\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}}
{\max\{1,\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}\|_1\}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Moreover, $\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\in\mathcal{M}_H\cap\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$. Hence, the
initialization \eqref{eq:alg-init} keeps every OMD iterate strictly positive.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$. For each $i$ such that $m_i>0$,
\[
\log\frac{m_i}{\widetilde{m}_{t+1,i}}
=
\log\frac{m_i}{m_{t,i}}+\eta g_{t,i}.
\]
After multiplication by $m_i$, this identity also holds when $m_i=0$ under
the convention $0\log0=0$. Therefore, \eqref{eq:def-bregman} gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\eta\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}\rangle+D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_t) \\
&=\sum_{i=1}^H\left(
\eta g_{t,i}m_i
+m_i\log\frac{m_i}{m_{t,i}}-m_i+m_{t,i}
\right) \\
&=\sum_{i=1}^H\left(
m_i\log\frac{m_i}{\widetilde{m}_{t+1,i}}-m_i+m_{t,i}
\right) \\
&=D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1})
+\sum_{i=1}^H\left(m_{t,i}-\widetilde{m}_{t+1,i}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
The final sum is independent of $\mathbf{m}$. Thus, minimizing the OMD objective
is equivalent to minimizing
$D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1})$ over $\mathcal{M}_H$.
Since $\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}>0$ coordinatewise,
Lemma~\ref{lem:bregman-projection} gives the stated unique minimizer and formula.
The formula also shows that $\mathbf{m}_{t+1}>0$ coordinatewise. The last claim
follows by induction from \eqref{eq:alg-init}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[One-step OMD movement bound]
\label{lem:omd-move}
The OMD update \eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin} with step size $\eta>0$ satisfies
\[
\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1
\le \eta\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty
\le \eta G_\alpha.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For every $\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{M}_H\cap\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$ and
$\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^H$, Cauchy--Schwarz and $\sum_i z_i\le1$ give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-strong-convexity}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{v}^\top\nabla^2\Psi(\mathbf{z})\mathbf{v}
&=
\sum_{i=1}^H\frac{v_i^2}{z_i} \\
&\ge
\frac{\|\mathbf{v}\|_1^2}{\sum_{i=1}^H z_i} \\
&\ge
\|\mathbf{v}\|_1^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:omd-closed-form}, both iterates are positive. Integrating
\eqref{eq:omd-strong-convexity} along the segment joining them gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\|\mathbf{m}_t)
&\ge
\frac12\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1^2, \\
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1})
&\ge
\frac12\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Adding these bounds and using the symmetric Bregman identity,
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1^2 \\
&\quad\le
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\|\mathbf{m}_t)+D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}) \\
&\quad=
\bigl\langle\nabla\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1})-\nabla\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t),
\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\bigr\rangle \\
&\quad\le
\eta\langle\mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\rangle \\
&\quad\le
\eta\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last two inequalities use first-order optimality of
\eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin} at the feasible point $\mathbf{m}_t$ and
H\"older's inequality, respectively. If the iterates differ, divide by
$\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1$; otherwise the bound is trivial.
Finally, $\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\le G_\alpha$ gives the stated uniform bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[OMD regret for the surrogate costs]
\label{lem:omd-surrogate-regret}
For every $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, the update \eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin} satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-surrogate-regret}
\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)
-\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
\le
\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_1)}{\eta}
+
\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{t=1}^T\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty^2.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Convexity of $\ell_t$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-convexity-step}
\begin{aligned}
\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
&\le
\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}\rangle \\
&=
\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\rangle \\
&\quad+
\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
First-order optimality of \eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin}, evaluated at
$\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-three-point-step}
\begin{aligned}
\eta\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}\rangle
&\le
\langle \nabla\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1})-\nabla\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t),\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\rangle \\
&=
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_t)
-D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}) \\
&\qquad
-D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\|\mathbf{m}_t).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The equality follows from the three-point Bregman identity. Substituting
\eqref{eq:omd-three-point-step} into \eqref{eq:omd-convexity-step},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-one-step-regret}
\begin{aligned}
\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
&\le
\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_t)}{\eta}
-\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1})}{\eta} \\
&\quad+
\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\rangle
\\
&\quad
-\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\|\mathbf{m}_t)}{\eta}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The strong-convexity calculation
\eqref{eq:omd-strong-convexity} and H\"older's inequality give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-young-step}
\begin{aligned}
&\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\rangle
-\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\|\mathbf{m}_t)}{\eta} \\
&\qquad\le
\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1
-\frac{\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1^2}{2\eta} \\
&\qquad\le
\frac{\eta}{2}\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The last inequality follows from Young's inequality. Combining
\eqref{eq:omd-one-step-regret} and \eqref{eq:omd-young-step} and summing over $t$
gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)
-\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
&\le
\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_1)}{\eta} \\
&\quad
-\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_{T+1})}{\eta} \\
&\quad+
\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{t=1}^T\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_{T+1})\ge0$, this proves
\eqref{eq:omd-surrogate-regret}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Positive-part representation of the dynamics]
\label{lem:positive-part-dynamics}
For $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-contractive-transition}
F_t(x;\mathbf{m})
\triangleq
\left[\alpha x-\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}\rangle\right]_+
+w_t.
\end{equation}
Then the online state and the state component of every fixed-policy trajectory
satisfy
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:positive-part-transitions}
x_{t+1}=F_t(x_t;\mathbf{m}_t),
\qquad
x_{t+1}(\mathbf{m})=F_t(x_t(\mathbf{m});\mathbf{m}).
\end{equation}
Moreover, for all $x,x'\ge0$ and
$\mathbf{m},\mathbf{m}'\in\mathcal{M}_H$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:transition-contraction}
\begin{aligned}
&|F_t(x;\mathbf{m})-F_t(x';\mathbf{m}')| \\
&\quad\le
\left|
\alpha(x-x')
-\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\rangle
\right| \\
&\quad\le
\alpha|x-x'|
+\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u\|_\infty
\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For the online trajectory, \eqref{eq:alg-feasible-action} implies
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\alpha x_t-u_t
&=
\alpha x_t-
\min\!\left\{
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\rangle,
\alpha x_t
\right\} \\
&=
\left[
\alpha x_t-
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\rangle
\right]_+.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For a fixed $\mathbf{m}$, Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-feasibility} gives
\[
\alpha x_t(\mathbf{m})
-\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}\rangle
\ge0,
\]
so inserting a positive part does not change its state update. This proves
\eqref{eq:positive-part-transitions}. Finally, the map $z\mapsto[z]_+$ is
nonexpansive:
\[
|[z]_+-[z']_+|\le|z-z'|.
\]
Applying this inequality to the two arguments in
\eqref{eq:def-contractive-transition}, followed by H\"older's inequality, gives
\eqref{eq:transition-contraction}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:contractive-deviation}]
Define
\[
d_t\triangleq|x_t-x_t(\mathbf{m}_t)|.
\]
Since $x_1=x_1(\mathbf{m}_1)=0$, we have $d_1=0$. For
$t\in\{1,\ldots,T-1\}$, Lemma~\ref{lem:positive-part-dynamics} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:contractive-deviation-start}
\begin{aligned}
d_{t+1}
&=
\left|
F_t(x_t;\mathbf{m}_t)
-F_t(x_t(\mathbf{m}_{t+1});\mathbf{m}_{t+1})
\right| \\
&\le
\alpha\left|x_t-x_t(\mathbf{m}_{t+1})\right|
+\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u\|_\infty
\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The affine state representation \eqref{eq:surr-affine-pair} implies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fixed-state-parameter-difference}
\begin{aligned}
\left|x_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-x_t(\mathbf{m}_{t+1})\right|
&=
\left|
\left\langle
\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x,
\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}_{t+1}
\right\rangle
\right| \\
&\le
\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x\|_\infty
\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Combining the triangle inequality with
\eqref{eq:contractive-deviation-start} and
\eqref{eq:fixed-state-parameter-difference} yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:contractive-deviation-recursion-raw}
\begin{aligned}
d_{t+1}
&\le
\alpha d_t \\
&\quad+
\left(
\alpha\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x\|_\infty
+\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u\|_\infty
\right)
\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:surr-coefficient-norms},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:combined-feature-bound}
\begin{aligned}
\alpha\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x\|_\infty
+\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u\|_\infty
&\le
\frac{\alpha^2}{1-\alpha}+\alpha \\
&=
\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Lemma~\ref{lem:omd-move} and
$\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\le G_\alpha$ therefore reduce
\eqref{eq:contractive-deviation-recursion-raw} to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:contractive-deviation-recursion}
d_{t+1}
\le
\alpha d_t
+\frac{\alpha\eta G_\alpha}{1-\alpha}.
\end{equation}
Unrolling from $d_1=0$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:contractive-state-unroll}
\begin{aligned}
d_t
&\le
\frac{\alpha\eta G_\alpha}{1-\alpha}
\sum_{j=0}^{t-2}\alpha^j \\
&\le
\frac{\alpha\eta G_\alpha}{(1-\alpha)^2},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with an empty sum when $t=1$. This proves
\eqref{eq:contractive-state-bound}.

Finally,
$\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\rangle=u_t(\mathbf{m}_t)$, and the
feasibility of fixed SDAC policies implies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:contractive-action-start}
\begin{aligned}
0\le u_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-u_t
&=
\left[u_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-\alpha x_t\right]_+ \\
&\le
\left[\alpha x_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-\alpha x_t\right]_+ \\
&\le
\alpha d_t.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{eq:contractive-action-start} with
\eqref{eq:contractive-state-bound} proves
\eqref{eq:contractive-action-bound}.

Both state--action pairs lie in $\mathcal{D}_\alpha$. Hence
\eqref{eq:model-lipschitz} and the two bounds just proved give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stability-one-step-short}
\begin{aligned}
&c_t(x_t,u_t)
-\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t) \\
&\qquad\le
L_x\bigl|x_t-x_t(\mathbf{m}_t)\bigr|
+
L_u\bigl|u_t-u_t(\mathbf{m}_t)\bigr| \\
&\qquad\le
\frac{\alpha\eta G_\alpha L_x}{(1-\alpha)^2}
+
\frac{\alpha^2\eta G_\alpha L_u}{(1-\alpha)^2} \\
&\qquad=
\frac{\alpha\eta G_\alpha(L_x+\alpha L_u)}{(1-\alpha)^2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Summing \eqref{eq:stability-one-step-short} over $t=1,\ldots,T$ proves
\eqref{eq:stability-sum-bound}.
\end{proof}



\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound}]
Define the stability term
\[
\operatorname{Stab}_T
\triangleq
\sum_{t=1}^T
\left(
c_t(x_t,u_t)
-\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)
\right).
\]
Using
$\ell_t(\mathbf{m})=c_t(x_t(\mathbf{m}),u_t(\mathbf{m}))$, the regret decomposes as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:regret-decomp}
\begin{aligned}
\RegSDAC{T}
&=
\operatorname{Stab}_T
+
\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)
-
\min_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H}\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

We first bound the initial Bregman divergence. Fix $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$. Since
$\mathbf{m}_1=\frac{1}{H+1}\mathbf{1}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:breg-init-expand}
\begin{aligned}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_1)
&=
\sum_{i=1}^H
m_i\log\bigl((H+1)m_i\bigr) \\
&\quad
-\|\mathbf{m}\|_1+\frac{H}{H+1} \\
&=
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\log m_i
+\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\log(H+1) \\
&\quad
-\|\mathbf{m}\|_1+\frac{H}{H+1}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Because $z\log z\le0$ for $z\in[0,1]$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:breg-init-affine}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_1)
\le
\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\log(H+1)-\|\mathbf{m}\|_1+\frac{H}{H+1}.
\end{equation}
The right-hand side is affine in $\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\in[0,1]$, and its values at the
endpoints are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{H}{H+1}
&\le
\log(H+1), \\
\log(H+1)-\frac{1}{H+1}
&\le
\log(H+1).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here the first inequality follows from
$\log z\ge1-z^{-1}$ for $z\ge1$. Therefore,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:breg-init-bound}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_1)
\le
\log(H+1)
\qquad
\text{for every }\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H.
\end{equation}

Lemma~\ref{lem:omd-surrogate-regret},
$\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\le G_\alpha$, and \eqref{eq:breg-init-bound} imply, for every
$\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-ell-raw}
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{t=1}^T\bigl(\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-\ell_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr)
&\le
\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_1)}{\eta}
+
\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{t=1}^T\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty^2 \\
&\le
\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta}
+
\frac{\eta G_\alpha^2T}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Since \eqref{eq:omd-ell-raw} holds uniformly over $\mathbf{m}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-term}
\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)
-
\min_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H}\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
\le
\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta}
+
\frac{\eta G_\alpha^2T}{2}.
\end{equation}
The stability bound \eqref{eq:stability-sum-bound} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stab-term}
\operatorname{Stab}_T
\le
\frac{\alpha\eta T G_\alpha(L_x+\alpha L_u)}{(1-\alpha)^2}.
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:omd-term} and \eqref{eq:stab-term} into
\eqref{eq:regret-decomp},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:regret-eta}
\begin{aligned}
\RegSDAC{T}
&\le
\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta}
+\frac{\eta T G_\alpha^2}{2} \\
&\quad
+\frac{\alpha\eta T G_\alpha(L_x+\alpha L_u)}{(1-\alpha)^2} \\
&=
\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta}
+\eta T\Gamma_\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Differentiating with respect to $\eta$ yields
\[
-\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta^2}
+
T\Gamma_\alpha.
\]
The unique minimizer is therefore
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:eta-star}
\eta^*
=
\sqrt{
\frac{\log(H+1)}
{T\Gamma_\alpha}
}.
\end{equation}
At this value, the two summands in \eqref{eq:regret-eta} are equal, so
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:regret-opt}
\RegSDAC{T}
\le
2\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha T\log(H+1)}.
\end{equation}

It remains to verify the dependence on $\alpha$. As $\alpha\downarrow0$,
\begin{align*}
\frac{G_\alpha}{\alpha}
&=
\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}+L_u
\longrightarrow L_x+L_u, \\
\frac{\Gamma_\alpha}{\alpha^2}
&=
\frac12\left(\frac{G_\alpha}{\alpha}\right)^2
+
\frac{G_\alpha}{\alpha}
\frac{L_x+\alpha L_u}{(1-\alpha)^2} \\
&\longrightarrow
\frac{(L_x+L_u)^2}{2}+L_x(L_x+L_u)>0.
\end{align*}
Therefore $\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha}=\Theta(\alpha)$ as
$\alpha\downarrow0$. As $\alpha\uparrow1$, write
$\varepsilon=1-\alpha$. Then
\begin{align*}
\varepsilon G_\alpha
&=
\alpha(L_x+\varepsilon L_u)
\longrightarrow L_x, \\
\varepsilon^3\Gamma_\alpha
&=
\frac{\varepsilon^3G_\alpha^2}{2}
+\alpha(\varepsilon G_\alpha)(L_x+\alpha L_u) \\
&\longrightarrow
L_x(L_x+L_u)>0.
\end{align*}
Thus
$\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha}=\Theta((1-\alpha)^{-3/2})$ as
$\alpha\uparrow1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:regret-vs-allocation}]
Fix $\mathbf{q}\in\mathcal{Q}_\alpha$ and use the SDAC comparator
$\mathbf{m}_{H_T}(\mathbf{q})$ from
Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-allocation-approx}. Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound} and
\eqref{eq:allocation-cost-gap} give
\[
\begin{aligned}
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}-J_T(\mathbf{q})
&\le
2\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha T\log(H_T+1)}\\
&\quad+
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)
\operatorname{Tail}_{H_T}(\mathbf{q})T\\
&\le
2\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha T\log(H_T+1)}\\
&\quad+
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)
\alpha^{H_T+1}T.
\end{aligned}
\]
The bound is uniform in $\mathbf{q}$, so taking the infimum over
$\mathbf{q}\in\mathcal{Q}_\alpha$ proves \eqref{eq:allocation-regret-bound}.
Finally,
$H_T\ge\log T/\log(1/\alpha)$ implies $\alpha^{H_T}\le1/T$, while
$H_T=\Theta(\log T)$ implies $\log(H_T+1)=O(\log\log T)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:regret-vs-fraction}]
For fixed $k$, Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound} applied to
$\mathbf{m}_{H_T}^{\mathrm{frac}}(k)$, followed by
\eqref{eq:sdac-fraction-approx-cost}, gives
\eqref{eq:fraction-regret-decomposition}.
Corollary~\ref{cor:fraction-as-allocation} shows that the geometric allocation
sequence $\mathbf{q}(k)$ belongs to $\mathcal{Q}_\alpha$, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-allocation-approx} gives
$\operatorname{Tail}_{H_T}(\mathbf{q}(k))\le\alpha^{H_T+1}$. This makes the bound
uniform in $k$; minimizing over $k$ proves the stated finite-time and asymptotic
guarantees.
\end{proof}



\section{Proofs for the Minimax Regret Lower Bound}
\label{sec:appendix-minimax-proofs}


\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:sdac-lower-bound}]
Fix a causal feasible controller $\mathcal{A}$. Let
$\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_T$ be independent Rademacher random variables, also independent
of the internal randomization of $\mathcal{A}$, with
\[
\mathbb{P}(\xi_t=1)
=
\mathbb{P}(\xi_t=-1)
=\frac12.
\]
Set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-hard-arrival}
w_t=1
\qquad
\text{for all }t\in[T],
\end{equation}
and define the costs by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-hard-cost}
c_t(x,u)
=
\xi_t\bigl(L_x x-L_u u\bigr)
\qquad
\text{for all }t\in[T].
\end{equation}
Each $c_t$ is affine and hence convex. For any two points in
$\mathcal{D}_\alpha$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-lipschitz-check}
\begin{aligned}
|c_t(x,u)-c_t(x',u')|
&=
|L_x(x-x')-L_u(u-u')| \\
&\le
L_x|x-x'|+L_u|u-u'|.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\[
\nabla c_t=\xi_t(L_x,-L_u),
\qquad
|\partial_x c_t|=L_x,
\qquad
|\partial_u c_t|=L_u,
\]
so the required subgradient bounds hold.


We now select two fixed SDAC policies for the benchmark. The parameter
$\mathbf{m}^{(0)}$ leaves all mass in the subprobability simplex unused, so its
SDAC action is always zero. The parameter $\mathbf{m}^{(1)}$ places all mass on the
first lag, so its action is $\alpha w_{t-1}$. Let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-two-policy-parameters}
\mathbf{m}^{(0)}\triangleq\mathbf{0},
\qquad
\mathbf{m}^{(1)}\triangleq\mathbf{e}_1
\end{equation}
belong to $\mathcal{M}_H$, where
$\mathbf{e}_1=(1,0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{R}^H$. Thus, under
\eqref{eq:lower-hard-arrival}, $\mathbf{m}^{(0)}$ is the no-release policy, whereas
$\mathbf{m}^{(1)}$ releases the retained portion of the preceding arrival. For the
no-release policy,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-policy-zero-trajectory}
u_t(\mathbf{m}^{(0)})=0,
\qquad
x_t(\mathbf{m}^{(0)})
=
\rho_t
\triangleq
\frac{1-\alpha^{t-1}}{1-\alpha}.
\end{equation}
This follows by unrolling $x_{t+1}=\alpha x_t+1$:
\[
x_t(\mathbf{m}^{(0)})
=\sum_{s=0}^{t-2}\alpha^s
=\frac{1-\alpha^{t-1}}{1-\alpha},
\qquad t\ge2,
\]
and the closed form also gives $x_1(\mathbf{m}^{(0)})=0$. For the one-lag policy,
\eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-policy-one-action}
u_1(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})=0,
\qquad
u_t(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})=\alpha,
\quad t\ge2.
\end{equation}
Its state at $t=2$ is
\[
x_2(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})
=\alpha x_1(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})-u_1(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})+w_1
=1.
\]
If $x_t(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})=1$ for some $t\ge2$, then
\[
\begin{aligned}
x_{t+1}(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})
&=\alpha x_t(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})-u_t(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})+w_t \\
&=\alpha-\alpha+1
=1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Induction therefore gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-policy-one-trajectory}
x_t(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})=1,
\qquad
t\ge2.
\end{equation}
For $t\ge2$, the difference between the two states is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-two-policy-state-difference}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_t
&\triangleq
x_t(\mathbf{m}^{(0)})-x_t(\mathbf{m}^{(1)}) \\
&=\rho_t-1 \\
&=\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha^{t-2})}{1-\alpha}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}



The two policies have the same state and action at $t=1$. For $t\ge2$,
\eqref{eq:lower-policy-one-action} and
\eqref{eq:lower-two-policy-state-difference} give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-two-policy-cost-difference}
\begin{aligned}
J_T(\mathbf{m}^{(0)})-J_T(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})
&=
\sum_{t=2}^T\xi_t(L_x\Delta_t+\alpha L_u) \\
&=
\sum_{t=2}^T\xi_t \beta_t,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $\beta_t$ as in \eqref{eq:sdac-lower-beta}.



Let $\mathcal{F}_t^-$ be the information available immediately before $c_t$ is
revealed, including the randomization used to select $u_t$. By causality,
$(x_t,u_t)$ is $\mathcal{F}_t^-$-measurable, whereas $\xi_t$ is independent of
$\mathcal{F}_t^-$ and has mean zero. Hence, by the tower property,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-online-mean-zero}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi},\mathcal{A}}\!\left[
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}
\right]
&=\sum_{t=1}^T
\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi},\mathcal{A}}\!\left[
\bigl(L_x x_t-L_u u_t\bigr)
\mathbb{E}[\xi_t\mid\mathcal{F}_t^-]
\right]
\\
&=0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The fixed-policy trajectories depend only on the arrival sequence and are
therefore independent of the Rademacher variables. Thus, for $j\in\{0,1\}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-two-policy-means-zero}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\!\left[
J_T(\mathbf{m}^{(j)})
\right]
&=\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbb{E}[\xi_t]
\bigl(
L_x x_t(\mathbf{m}^{(j)})-L_u u_t(\mathbf{m}^{(j)})
\bigr)
\\
&=0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}


Because $\mathbf{m}^{(0)},\mathbf{m}^{(1)}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, for every realization of
the Rademacher variables and every choice made by the controller,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-two-policy-regret}
\begin{aligned}
\RegSDAC{T}
&=
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}
-\min_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H}J_T(\mathbf{m}) \\
&\ge
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}
-\min\!\left\{
J_T(\mathbf{m}^{(0)}),
J_T(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})
\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Taking expectations in \eqref{eq:lower-two-policy-regret} and using
$\min\{a,b\}=(a+b-|a-b|)/2$ together with
\eqref{eq:lower-online-mean-zero} and
\eqref{eq:lower-two-policy-means-zero} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-randomized-sequence-bound}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi},\mathcal{A}}\!\left[\RegSDAC{T}\right]
&\ge \frac12\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\!\left[
\left|J_T(\mathbf{m}^{(0)})-J_T(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})\right|
\right] \\
&= \frac12\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\!\left[
\left|\sum_{t=2}^T\xi_t\beta_t\right|
\right] \\
&\ge \frac{1}{2\sqrt2}
\left(\sum_{t=2}^T\beta_t^2\right)^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Khintchine's inequality for Rademacher sums gives the final inequality in
\eqref{eq:lower-randomized-sequence-bound}. It remains to remove the randomization
from the adversarial construction. The
left-hand side of \eqref{eq:lower-randomized-sequence-bound} is the average over
all $2^T$ realizations of the Rademacher vector $\boldsymbol{\xi}$:
\[
\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi},\mathcal{A}}\!\left[\RegSDAC{T}\right]
=
\frac{1}{2^T}
\sum_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\{-1,1\}^T}
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\!\left[
\RegSDAC{T}\,\middle|\,\boldsymbol{\xi}
\right].
\]
Hence, at least one vector $\boldsymbol{\xi}^\star$ makes the conditional expectation
at least the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:lower-randomized-sequence-bound}. Fixing
$\boldsymbol{\xi}^\star$ in \eqref{eq:lower-hard-cost}, together with the arrivals
in \eqref{eq:lower-hard-arrival}, produces a deterministic admissible sequence
fixed before the interaction. The remaining expectation is only over the internal
randomization of $\mathcal{A}$, and \eqref{eq:sdac-lower-exact} follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:sdac-lower-mixing}]
The definition of $t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-mixing-geometric}
\alpha^{t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)}
\le
\alpha^{\log 2/\log(1/\alpha)}
=\frac12.
\end{equation}
Therefore, for every $t\ge t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)+2$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-beta-post-mixing}
\begin{aligned}
\beta_t
&\ge
\alpha\left(
L_u+\frac{L_x}{2(1-\alpha)}
\right) \\
&\ge
\frac{\alpha}{2}
\left(
L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}
\right)
=\frac{G_\alpha}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
There are $T-t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)-1$ indices in
$\{t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)+2,\ldots,T\}$. If
$T\ge2t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)+2$, then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-post-mixing-count}
T-t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)-1
\ge
\frac{T}{2}.
\end{equation}
Applying Theorem~\ref{thm:sdac-lower-bound} and retaining only these indices,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-mixing-final}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\!\left[\RegSDAC{T}\right]
&\ge
\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}
\left[
\frac{T}{2}\left(\frac{G_\alpha}{2}\right)^2
\right]^{1/2} \\
&=
\frac{G_\alpha}{8}\sqrt{T}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This is \eqref{eq:sdac-lower-mixing}.
\end{proof}
