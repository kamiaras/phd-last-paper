\begin{abstract}
We study online control of a scalar storage system with nonnegative adversarial
resource arrivals and state-dependent action constraints. The cost is adversarial,
time-varying, and depends on both the action and the state. At each time, the
controller selects a feasible action before the current resource arrival and cost
are revealed. To handle the resulting coupling
between feasibility and online learning, we introduce \emph{Simplex Disturbance-Action
Control} (SDAC), an $H$-dimensional finite-memory policy class parameterized by a
subprobability simplex. The key feature of SDAC is that every fixed policy in the
class is feasible by construction. Our adaptive controller updates these parameters
by entropic online mirror descent while preserving feasibility along the
time-varying trajectory. We prove regret
$O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log(H+1)}\right)$ relative to the best fixed SDAC policy. As the
retention coefficient $\alpha$ approaches one, the leading factor scales as
$\Theta((1-\alpha)^{-3/2})$. We also derive a minimax lower bound which, when $T$
is at least on the order of $(1-\alpha)^{-1}$, scales as
$\Omega((1-\alpha)^{-1}\sqrt{T})$ near $\alpha=1$, leaving a factor
$(1-\alpha)^{-1/2}$ between the upper and lower bounds. We also show that SDAC
geometrically approximates a decreasing-allocation policy class that spreads each
arrival over time according to a common decreasing allocation sequence, a structure
that arises in stochastic energy-harvesting control. Geometric allocation sequences
induce all feasible proportional state-feedback policies. With
$H=\Theta(\log T)$, the resulting regret against the decreasing-allocation class,
and hence against feasible proportional state-feedback policies, is
$O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log\log T}\right)$. The framework applies to energy-harvesting
batteries and other storage-constrained resource systems.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{Motivation and Problem Setting}
\IEEEPARstart{M}{any} resource-allocation systems have a storage state that changes
over time and limits the control action at each time. An action that lowers the
current cost may leave less resource for future decisions, while conserving the
resource may increase the current cost. The controller must balance these effects
while satisfying the action constraint at every time.

We study this problem for a scalar storage system that loses a fixed fraction of
its stored resource each period. At each time, the controller observes the current
state and all past arrivals and cost functions, chooses a control action, and then
observes the current arrival and cost function. The arrival and cost sequences may
be chosen adversarially based on past observations; we do not assume a known
probability model. Our goal is to construct a feasible online controller whose
cumulative cost is close to that of the best fixed policy, chosen in hindsight,
from a specified benchmark class.

The main difficulty is that the current action affects both the next state and the
actions that will be feasible later. Therefore, the controller cannot update its
decisions independently at each time. \emph{Disturbance-Action Control} (DAC)
policies address this dependence by combining a stabilizing state-feedback term
with a linear function of past disturbances. In our storage model, however,
unrestricted filter weights can produce a negative action or an action larger than
the available resource.

We address this issue by introducing \emph{Simplex Disturbance-Action Control}
(SDAC). SDAC restricts the filter weights to a subprobability simplex and includes
powers of the retention coefficient in the filter. These choices ensure that
every fixed SDAC policy satisfies the action constraint. The induced state and
action are also affine in the policy parameter, so a convex state--action cost is
convex as a function of that parameter. The parameter can therefore be updated
using online convex optimization.

\subsection{Model and Information Pattern}

The storage state $x_t\ge0$ evolves according to
\[
x_{t+1}=\alpha x_t-u_t+w_t,
\]
where $\alpha\in(0,1)$ is a fixed retention coefficient, $u_t$ is the control
action, and $w_t\in[0,1]$ is a nonnegative resource arrival. The action must satisfy
\[
0\le u_t\le\alpha x_t.
\]
Thus, the controller cannot use more than the amount that remains after the storage
loss.

At time $t$, the controller chooses $u_t$ using the current state and the
information available through time $t-1$. The arrival $w_t$ and the convex cost
function $c_t$ are revealed after the action is selected, and the controller incurs
the cost $c_t(x_t,u_t)$. The pair $(w_t,c_t)$ may depend on the observations through
time $t-1$, but not on the current action. Both the online trajectory and every
benchmark trajectory must satisfy the same dynamics and action constraint. The
formal model and assumptions are given in Section~\ref{sec:model-setup}.

Energy-harvesting battery management is our main application. In this case, $x_t$
is the battery state of charge, $w_t$ is the harvested energy, $u_t$ is the energy
used, and $1-\alpha$ is the fraction of stored energy lost during one period.
Similar dynamics arise in perishable inventory systems, water reservoirs with
storage loss, and queues with abandonment. In many standard queueing models,
$\alpha=1$; that case is outside our main results and is discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:extensions}.


\subsection{Contributions}

Our contributions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Policy class.}
We introduce SDAC, a finite-memory policy class parameterized by a
subprobability simplex. We prove that every fixed SDAC policy is feasible
for every arrival sequence with entries in $[0,1]$. We also show that the state
and action generated by a fixed SDAC policy are affine in its parameter. Under our
convexity and Lipschitz assumptions on the state--action costs, the induced cost is
convex and Lipschitz as a function of the policy parameter.

    We further show that SDAC approximates the decreasing-allocation policy
    class defined in
    Section~\ref{subsec:allocation-sequences}. This class allocates each arrival
    over future periods according to the same decreasing sequence. It contains
    every feasible proportional state-feedback policy
    $u_t=kx_t$, with $k\in[0,\alpha]$, and the approximation error decreases
    geometrically with the memory horizon $H$.

\item \textbf{Online control and regret.}
    We develop an online controller that updates its SDAC parameter using
    entropic online mirror descent (OMD) over the subprobability simplex. Because the
    parameter changes over time, the nominal SDAC action is clipped when needed
    to satisfy the current action constraint. We prove that this controller
    remains feasible and achieves
    \[
    O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log(H+1)}\right)
    \]
    regret against the best fixed SDAC policy; see
    Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound}. For fixed $L_x$ and $L_u$, the leading
    factor scales as
    $\Theta((1-\alpha)^{-3/2})$ as $\alpha\uparrow1$.

    For fixed $\alpha$, take the memory horizon $H$ to grow as $\log T$. The
    resulting regret is
    $O(\sqrt{T\log\log T})$ against the decreasing-allocation class. The same bound
    holds against the best feasible proportional state-feedback policy; see
    Corollaries~\ref{cor:regret-vs-allocation}
    and~\ref{cor:regret-vs-linear}.

\item \textbf{Minimax lower bound.}
    We prove a lower bound of order $\sqrt{T}$ on the expected regret of every
    causal feasible controller, including randomized controllers, even when the
    arrival and cost sequences are deterministic and fixed in advance; see
    Theorem~\ref{thm:sdac-lower-bound}. When $T$ is at least on the order of
    $(1-\alpha)^{-1}$, the lower bound scales as
    $\Omega((1-\alpha)^{-1}\sqrt{T})$ near $\alpha=1$; see
    Corollary~\ref{cor:sdac-lower-mixing} and
    Remark~\ref{rem:large-alpha-regime}. The dependence of the upper and lower
    bounds on $1-\alpha$ therefore differs by a factor
    $(1-\alpha)^{-1/2}$. Closing this gap is left for future work.
\end{itemize}


\subsection{Related Work}

\paragraph{Online nonstochastic control}
Online nonstochastic control studies dynamical systems with adversarial disturbances
and costs. Performance is measured by regret against a policy chosen in hindsight,
under suitable stability conditions
\cite{agarwal2019online,agarwal2019logarithmic}; see
\cite{hazan2026introduction} for an overview. For known linear systems,
Simchowitz et al.\ establish the optimal $\sqrt{T}$ dependence on the horizon while
leaving some dependence on the system's stability unresolved
\cite{simchowitz2020improper}. Related work also considers partial observability
\cite{lale2020logarithmic} and bandit feedback
\cite{hazan2020nonstochasticBandit}.

A central idea in this literature is DAC. A DAC policy combines a stabilizing
state-feedback action with a finite linear filter of past disturbances. The action
is therefore described by finitely many policy parameters that can be updated using
online methods. SDAC uses the same basic idea but restricts the parameters in a way
suited to storage systems with nonnegative arrivals.

Online control with affine or more general constraints on states and actions has
been studied in \cite{li2021onlineConstrained,liu2023constrainedControl}. The
constraint $u_t\le\alpha x_t$ is affine in the state and action, but the range of
allowed actions depends on the current storage state. Our approach uses the
structure of the storage dynamics to ensure that every fixed SDAC policy is
feasible and that the online controller remains feasible while its parameter
changes.

Our analysis also considers how regret depends on the retention coefficient
$\alpha$. Kumar et al.\ study online convex optimization in which past decisions can
affect all later costs, and derive bounds that depend on how quickly those effects
decay \cite{kumar2023unbounded}. In their model with geometrically decaying memory,
the lower bound scales as $\sqrt{T}$ divided by one minus the decay factor,
paralleling our $\Omega(\sqrt{T}/(1-\alpha))$ lower bound for sufficiently long
horizons; see Remark~\ref{rem:large-alpha-regime}. Our lower bound is proved
directly for state--action costs under the state-dependent action constraint.

\paragraph{Energy harvesting}
Several energy-harvesting policies adjust energy use according to the available
battery level. Shaviv and \"Ozg\"ur \cite{shaviv2016universally} study a policy that
uses a fixed fraction of the available energy and prove guarantees under
independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) energy arrivals. Arafa
et al.\ \cite{arafa2018online} consider more general sequences for spending energy
after a recharge. Zibaeenejad and Chen
\cite{zibaeenejad2019lookahead} derive a related decreasing spending rule when
future arrivals are known in advance.

These works motivate the \emph{decreasing-allocation policy class} defined in this
paper. Under such a policy, each arrival is allocated over future periods according
to the same decreasing sequence. In our model, this allocation begins one period
after the arrival because the controller does not observe the current arrival before
choosing the action.

The models in \cite{shaviv2016universally,arafa2018online,
zibaeenejad2019lookahead} use random arrivals from a fixed model, reveal the current
harvested energy before allocation, and optimize a fixed reward that depends on the
allocated power. In our setting, the arrival and cost sequences may be chosen
adversarially, the controller acts before observing the current arrival and cost,
the cost can depend on both the storage state and the action, and performance is
measured by regret against a fixed policy.

Yu and Neely \cite{Yu2019} study expected utility maximization under i.i.d.\ system
states. Asgari and Neely \cite{10.1145/3428337} obtain an
$O(\sqrt{T})$ regret--battery-capacity tradeoff for arbitrary loss sequences while
retaining i.i.d.\ energy arrivals. Both works use objectives that depend only on the
action and compare against a fixed action. Here, the arrivals may be chosen
adversarially, the cost depends on the state and action, and the comparator is a
fixed policy rather than a fixed action.

Lin et al.\ \cite{lin2025optimal} study online age-of-information optimization in
an energy-harvesting system with related battery dynamics. They compare their
controller with an unrestricted offline optimum using a competitive ratio. Their
objective depends on the energy allocation but not directly on the battery state,
and the current arrival is observed before the allocation. Our controller acts
before observing the current arrival, and our benchmark is a fixed policy chosen in
hindsight.

\paragraph{Queueing and network optimization}
Queueing control also involves states that change through arrivals and service
decisions. Classical work studies throughput and queue stability
\cite{tassiulas1990stability,tassiulas1993dynamic,mckeown1999achieving}, while
stochastic network optimization studies tradeoffs between time-average objectives
and queue backlog \cite{neely2010stochastic}. Our setting instead allows adversarial
arrival sequences and convex state--action costs, and measures regret against a
policy chosen in hindsight.

In many queueing models, $\alpha=1$ because stored packets do not disappear over
time. Such models are not direct examples of the $\alpha\in(0,1)$ system studied
here. A queue with abandonment or expiration can have $\alpha<1$ and is more closely
related to our model. Section~\ref{sec:extensions} discusses the case when $\alpha\ge1$.

Khojastepour and Sabharwal \cite{khojastepour2004delay} study a fixed scheduler
that can be written as a linear filter of past arrivals. Their scheduler minimizes
average transmit power subject to a maximum-delay guarantee. In contrast, our
controller updates its filter weights online in response to convex state--action
costs revealed after each decision.

\paragraph{Inventory and reservoir management}
Yang et al.\ \cite{yang2020online} study online procurement under inventory
constraints. Their controller observes the current demand and price and then chooses
how much supply to purchase. Performance is measured by a competitive ratio for the
total procurement cost. Our controller instead chooses how much stored resource to
use before observing the current arrival and cost.

Hihat and Fermanian \cite{hihat2024online} consider inventory systems with more
general state-transition functions and study policies that replenish inventory up
to a target level. Their model is broader in its transition dynamics, but it has a
different decision order and a different policy class. Our scalar linear dynamics
allow explicit feasibility, approximation, and regret guarantees.

Classical reservoir models also use storage dynamics with random inflows and
controlled releases \cite{moran1954dams,prabhu1998stochastic}. That literature
mainly studies long-run performance under stochastic inflows. It provides another
example of control under storage constraints, although physical reservoir models
may also include capacity and overflow effects that are not part of our main model.

\section{Notation}
\label{sec:notation}

For every positive integer $n$, we write
$[n]\triangleq\{1,\ldots,n\}$ and $[z]_+\triangleq\max\{z,0\}$. For
$\mathbf v\in\mathbb R^n$, the $i$-th coordinate is denoted by $v_i$. If the
vector already carries a subscript, the coordinate is appended after a comma: the
$i$-th coordinate of $\mathbf v_t$ is $v_{t,i}$. We write
$\mathbf 1$ for the all-ones vector, with its dimension clear from context, and
$\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ for the Euclidean inner product. We use $\log$ for the
natural logarithm and set $w_\tau\equiv0$ for $\tau\le0$.

A fixed SDAC policy with parameter $\mathbf m$ is denoted by
$\pi_{\mathbf m}$, with induced state and action trajectories
$x_t(\mathbf m)$ and $u_t(\mathbf m)$. A feasible proportional state-feedback
policy with gain $k\in[0,\alpha]$ induces $x_t(k)$ and $u_t(k)$, while a decreasing
allocation sequence $\mathbf q$ induces $x_t(\mathbf q)$ and $u_t(\mathbf q)$.
After the online algorithm is introduced, $x_t$ and $u_t$ without arguments denote
the realized online trajectory.

\section{Model and Problem Setup}
\label{sec:model-setup}

Fix a positive integer $T$ and a known retention coefficient
$\alpha\in(0,1)$. The state $x_t\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}$ is the resource level at the
beginning of time $t$. The system starts empty:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-init}
x_1=0.
\end{equation}
At each time $t\in[T]$, the controller chooses an action
$u_t\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-feasibility}
0\le u_t\le\alpha x_t.
\end{equation}
The system then receives an arrival $w_t\in[0,1]$ and evolves according to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-dynamics}
x_{t+1}=\alpha x_t-u_t+w_t.
\end{equation}
Here, $1-\alpha$ is the fraction of the stored resource lost during one period.
The constraint \eqref{eq:model-feasibility} ensures that the action does not exceed
the amount remaining after this loss.

Although $w_t$ is commonly called a disturbance in the online-control literature,
we call it an \emph{arrival} because it is nonnegative. We retain the standard names
Disturbance-Action Control and Simplex Disturbance-Action Control. The
nonnegativity of $w_t$ is important both for the storage interpretation and for the
feasibility properties of SDAC.

At time $t$, the pair $(w_t,c_t)$ may depend on the observations through time
$t-1$, but not on the current action or any random choice made by the controller at
time $t$. The controller chooses $u_t$ before observing $w_t$ or $c_t$. After the
action is selected, the arrival and the entire cost function are revealed, the
controller incurs $c_t(x_t,u_t)$, and the state is updated according to
\eqref{eq:model-dynamics}.

For every feasible trajectory,
\[
0\le x_{t+1}\le\alpha x_t+1.
\]
Since $x_1=0$, induction gives
\[
0\le x_t
\le\frac{1-\alpha^{t-1}}{1-\alpha}
\le\frac{1}{1-\alpha},
\qquad t\in[T+1].
\]
Thus, the storage state is uniformly bounded. In particular, a storage device with
capacity $1/(1-\alpha)$ is sufficient to realize every feasible trajectory without
overflow. The state and action constraints imply that every feasible state--action
pair belongs to the compact set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-domain}
\mathcal D_\alpha
\triangleq
\left\{
(x,u):
0\le x\le\frac{1}{1-\alpha},
0\le u\le\alpha x
\right\}.
\end{equation}

Each revealed cost function has the form
\[
c_t:\mathbb R_{\ge0}^2\to\mathbb R
\]
and is convex in $(x,u)$. We assume that it satisfies the following Lipschitz
condition on $\mathcal D_\alpha$: there are constants $L_x,L_u>0$, independent of
$t$, such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-lipschitz}
|c_t(x,u)-c_t(x',u')|
\le
L_x|x-x'|+L_u|u-u'|
\end{equation}
for all $(x,u),(x',u')\in\mathcal D_\alpha$.
We also assume access, at every $(x,u)\in\mathcal D_\alpha$, to a cost subgradient
\[
\boldsymbol{\zeta}_t(x,u)
=
\bigl(\zeta_t^x(x,u),\zeta_t^u(x,u)\bigr)
\in\partial c_t(x,u)
\]
satisfying
\[
|\zeta_t^x(x,u)|\le L_x,
\qquad
|\zeta_t^u(x,u)|\le L_u.
\]
These subgradient bounds hold, for example, when
\eqref{eq:model-lipschitz} extends to an open neighborhood of
$\mathcal D_\alpha$; see
\cite[Thm.~9.13]{rockafellar1998variational}.

Let $\{(x_t,u_t)\}_{t=1}^T$ denote the trajectory generated by the online
controller. Its cumulative cost is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-cumulative-cost}
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}
\triangleq
\sum_{t=1}^T c_t(x_t,u_t).
\end{equation}

Fix a positive integer $H$, and let $\mathbf m$ range over the SDAC parameter set
$\mathcal M_H$, defined in Section~\ref{sec:sdac}. For each
$\mathbf m\in\mathcal M_H$, let $x_t(\mathbf m)$ and $u_t(\mathbf m)$ denote the
trajectory generated by the fixed policy $\pi_{\mathbf m}$ under the same arrival
sequence. Define its cumulative cost by
\[
J_T(\mathbf m)
\triangleq
\sum_{t=1}^T
c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf m),u_t(\mathbf m)\bigr).
\]
Both the online trajectory and every benchmark trajectory are required to satisfy
\eqref{eq:model-feasibility} under \eqref{eq:model-dynamics}.
The regret against the best fixed SDAC policy is defined by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-regret}
\RegSDAC{T}
\triangleq
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}
-
\min_{\mathbf m\in\mathcal M_H}J_T(\mathbf m).
\end{equation}
The online controller may update its SDAC parameter over time, while the benchmark
in \eqref{eq:def-regret} is the best single fixed SDAC policy chosen in hindsight.
Here, fixed means that $\mathbf m$ is held constant; the policy's actions still
respond to past arrivals.
Our objective is to satisfy \eqref{eq:model-feasibility} at every time and achieve
sublinear regret.

\section{Simplex Disturbance-Action Control (SDAC)}
\label{sec:sdac}

\subsection{From Disturbance-Action Control to SDAC}
DAC combines a stabilizing state-feedback term with a
finite linear filter of past disturbances. Under standard conditions, this
parameterization yields convex surrogate losses and efficient online updates. In
our scalar storage model, specializing the baseline feedback term to zero gives the
representative form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dac-param}
u_t = \sum_{i=1}^{H} \theta_i w_{t-i},
\end{equation}
where $H$ is a positive integer (the memory horizon) and $\boldsymbol{\theta}=(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_H)^\top\in\mathbb{R}^H$ is a
linear filter.

For our constrained model, an unrestricted filter is insufficient: its action can
violate \eqref{eq:model-feasibility} by exceeding $\alpha x_t$ or becoming negative.
We therefore introduce \emph{Simplex Disturbance-Action Control} (SDAC), which uses a
simplex-constrained filter tailored to the storage dynamics. This restriction ensures
that every fixed policy is feasible while preserving convex surrogate losses and
tractable online updates.


\subsection{SDAC Definition}
Fix a memory horizon $H\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}$. Let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-simplex}
\mathcal{M}_H \triangleq \left\{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^H:\;
\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\le1\right\}
\end{equation}
be the subprobability simplex. For any $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, define the policy $\pi_{\mathbf{m}}$ by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-sdac-policy}
u_t(\mathbf{m})
\;\triangleq\;
\sum_{i=1}^{H} m_i\alpha^i w_{t-i}
=
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}\rangle,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-phi-u}
\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u
\triangleq
\big(\alpha w_{t-1},\alpha^2 w_{t-2},\ldots,\alpha^H w_{t-H}\big)^\top
\in \mathbb{R}^H,
\end{equation}

The induced state trajectory satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-state-update}
x_{t+1}(\mathbf{m}) = \alpha x_t(\mathbf{m}) - u_t(\mathbf{m}) + w_t,
\qquad x_1(\mathbf{m})=0.
\end{equation}
The SDAC policy class is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-sdac-class}
\Pi_H^{\mathrm{SDAC}}
\triangleq
\{\pi_{\mathbf{m}}:\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H\}.
\end{equation}
The weights $\alpha^i$ align the arrival filter with the retention coefficient
$\alpha$ in \eqref{eq:model-dynamics}.


We now establish the two structural properties that make SDAC useful: the induced
control action always satisfies the feasibility constraint
\eqref{eq:model-feasibility}, and the induced cost is convex and Lipschitz in $\mathbf{m}$, so
that regret minimization against the best fixed SDAC policy is an instance of online
convex optimization over the simplex. Both properties follow from an explicit
decomposition of the state trajectory that isolates its dependence on $\mathbf{m}$.

\begin{lemma}[State decomposition under SDAC]
\label{lem:sdac-state-decomp}
Fix a positive integer $H$ and $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$.
For $i\in\{1,\ldots,H\}$, define
\[
r_t(i)
\triangleq
\sum_{j=1}^{i}\alpha^{j-1}w_{t-j},
\]
where the sum is empty when $i=0$, and write $r_t\triangleq r_t(t-1)$. Then, for every
$t\in[T]$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-state-decomp}
x_t(\mathbf{m})
=
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i r_t(i)
+\bigl(1-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\bigr)r_t.
\end{equation}
Define the quantity
\[
\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x
\triangleq
\bigl(r_t(1)-r_t,\ldots,r_t(H)-r_t\bigr)^\top.
\]
Then
\[
x_t(\mathbf{m})
=
r_t+\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x,\mathbf{m}\rangle.
\]
Moreover, for all $i\in\{1,\ldots,H\}$,
\[
0\le r_t(i)\le r_t\le\frac{1}{1-\alpha},
\]
and $\mathbf{m}\mapsto x_t(\mathbf{m})$ is affine.
\end{lemma}

The proof is given in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-sdac-proofs}.

The first consequence of this decomposition is that every fixed policy is feasible.

\begin{lemma}[Feasibility of SDAC policies]
\label{lem:sdac-feasibility}
For every $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, the trajectory induced by
\eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy} and \eqref{eq:sdac-state-update} satisfies the action
constraint \eqref{eq:model-feasibility}; explicitly,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-feasibility}
0\le u_t(\mathbf{m})\le \alpha x_t(\mathbf{m})
\qquad
\text{for all }t\in[T].
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Because $m_i$, $\alpha$, and $w_{t-i}$ are nonnegative,
\[
u_t(\mathbf{m})
=
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\alpha^i w_{t-i}
\ge0.
\]
For every $i\in\{1,\ldots,H\}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:feas-r-term}
\alpha r_t(i)
=
\sum_{j=1}^{i}\alpha^j w_{t-j}
\ge
\alpha^i w_{t-i}.
\end{equation}
Because $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$ and $r_t\ge0$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-state-decomp},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:feas-upper-direct}
\begin{aligned}
\alpha x_t(\mathbf{m})
&=
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\alpha r_t(i)
+\alpha\bigl(1-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\bigr)r_t \\
&\ge
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\alpha r_t(i) \\
&\ge
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\alpha^i w_{t-i}
=
u_t(\mathbf{m}),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where the second inequality uses \eqref{eq:feas-r-term}.
\end{proof}

For fixed arrivals, both coordinates of the SDAC state--action pair are affine
in $\mathbf{m}$. The following lemma records the resulting convexity and the constants
used by the online update.

\begin{lemma}[Convexity and Lipschitz continuity of surrogate cost]
\label{lem:sdac-surrogate}
Define
\[
\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
\triangleq
c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{m}),u_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr),
\qquad
\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H,
\]
and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-surrogate-G}
G_\alpha
\triangleq
\alpha\left(\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}+L_u\right).
\end{equation}
Then $\ell_t$ is convex on $\mathcal{M}_H$, and for all $\mathbf{m},\mathbf{m}'\in\mathcal{M}_H$,
\[
|\ell_t(\mathbf{m})-\ell_t(\mathbf{m}')|
\le
G_\alpha\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\|_1.
\]
Moreover, at every $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, one can select
$\mathbf{g}_t\in\partial\ell_t(\mathbf{m})$ such that
$\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\le G_\alpha$.
\end{lemma}

For the online update, let $(\zeta_t^x,\zeta_t^u)$ be the bounded cost
subgradient at the induced state--action pair and use the explicit choice
\[
\mathbf{g}_t
=
\zeta_t^x\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x
+
\zeta_t^u\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u
\]
This choice belongs to $\partial\ell_t(\mathbf{m})$ and satisfies
$\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\le G_\alpha$, as stated in
Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-surrogate}. The proof is given in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-sdac-proofs}.

Regret minimization against the best fixed SDAC policy thus becomes online convex
optimization over the simplex.

\subsection{Expressiveness: Decreasing-Allocation Policies}
\label{subsec:allocation-sequences}

Energy-harvesting policies for Bernoulli recharges can be described by the
sequence of expenditures following each recharge
\cite{arafa2018online,zibaeenejad2019lookahead}. In the corresponding
decreasing-allocation policy class, each unit arriving at time $\tau$ is assigned the
same decreasing allocation sequence, beginning at time $\tau+1$ to respect the
information pattern. The sequence may have an arbitrary decreasing shape;
geometric sequences induce proportional state-feedback policies.

Define the set of feasible decreasing allocation sequences
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-allocation-sequences}
\mathcal Q_\alpha^\downarrow
\triangleq
\left\{
\mathbf q=(q_i)_{i\ge1}:
q_1\ge q_2\ge\cdots\ge0,
\quad
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{q_i}{\alpha^i}\le1
\right\}.
\end{equation}
For $\mathbf q\in\mathcal Q_\alpha^\downarrow$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-allocation-policy}
\begin{aligned}
u_t(\mathbf q)
&\triangleq
\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}q_iw_{t-i},
\\
x_{t+1}(\mathbf q)
&=
\alpha x_t(\mathbf q)-u_t(\mathbf q)+w_t,
\qquad x_1(\mathbf q)=0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $q_iw_s$ is the amount from arrival $w_s$ allocated $i$ periods later.
The weighted budget in \eqref{eq:def-allocation-sequences} accounts for leakage:
reserving $q_i$ units for expenditure $i$ periods after an arrival requires
$q_i/\alpha^i$ units at the arrival time. Let
\[
\Pi_\alpha^{\mathrm{alloc}}
\triangleq
\{\pi_{\mathbf q}:\mathbf q\in\mathcal Q_\alpha^\downarrow\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Finite-memory approximation of decreasing-allocation policies]
\label{lem:sdac-allocation-approx}
Every policy in $\Pi_\alpha^{\mathrm{alloc}}$ is feasible. Fix
$\mathbf q\in\mathcal Q_\alpha^\downarrow$ and $H\ge1$, and define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:allocation-truncation}
m_{H,i}(\mathbf q)
\triangleq
\frac{q_i}{\alpha^i},
\qquad i\in[H].
\end{equation}
Then $\mathbf m_H(\mathbf q)\in\mathcal M_H$. With
\[
\operatorname{Tail}_H(\mathbf q)
\triangleq
\sum_{i=H+1}^{\infty}q_i,
\]
the corresponding trajectories satisfy, for every arrival sequence in $[0,1]$,
\begin{align}
0
&\le
u_t(\mathbf q)-u_t\bigl(\mathbf m_H(\mathbf q)\bigr)
\le \operatorname{Tail}_H(\mathbf q),
\label{eq:allocation-action-gap}\\
0
&\le
x_t\bigl(\mathbf m_H(\mathbf q)\bigr)-x_t(\mathbf q)
\le \frac{\operatorname{Tail}_H(\mathbf q)}{1-\alpha}.
\label{eq:allocation-state-gap}
\end{align}
If each $c_t$ satisfies \eqref{eq:model-lipschitz}, then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:allocation-cost-gap}
\begin{aligned}
&\left|
\sum_{t=1}^T c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf m_H(\mathbf q)),
u_t(\mathbf m_H(\mathbf q))\bigr)
-
\sum_{t=1}^T c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf q),u_t(\mathbf q)\bigr)
\right|\\
&\qquad\le
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)
\operatorname{Tail}_H(\mathbf q)T \\
&\qquad\le
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)\alpha^{H+1}T.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{corollary}[Proportional state feedback as a geometric allocation]
\label{cor:proportional-as-allocation}
Fix $k\in[0,\alpha]$, set $\lambda_k\triangleq\alpha-k$, and define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:proportional-as-allocation}
q_i(k)=k\lambda_k^{i-1},
\qquad i\ge1,
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_k^0\triangleq1$. Then $\mathbf q(k)\in\mathcal Q_\alpha^\downarrow$
and $\pi_{\mathbf q(k)}$ is exactly the feasible proportional state-feedback policy
$u_t(k)=k x_t(k)$. Its SDAC truncation is
\[
\mathbf m_H^{\mathrm{prop}}(k)
\triangleq
\mathbf m_H\bigl(\mathbf q(k)\bigr),
\]
with coordinates
\[
m_{H,i}\bigl(\mathbf q(k)\bigr)
=
\frac{k\lambda_k^{i-1}}{\alpha^i},
\qquad i\in[H],
\]
and its tail is
\[
\operatorname{Tail}_H\bigl(\mathbf q(k)\bigr)
=
\frac{k\lambda_k^H}{1-\lambda_k}.
\]
Therefore, \eqref{eq:allocation-cost-gap} specializes exactly to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-lin-approx-cost}
\begin{aligned}
&\left|
\sum_{t=1}^T c_t\!\bigl(x_t(\mathbf m_H^{\mathrm{prop}}(k)),u_t(\mathbf m_H^{\mathrm{prop}}(k))\bigr)
-
\sum_{t=1}^T c_t\bigl(x_t(k),u_t(k)\bigr)
\right| \\
&\qquad\le
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)
\frac{k\lambda_k^H}{1-\lambda_k}\,T.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

The proofs of Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-allocation-approx} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:proportional-as-allocation} are given in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-sdac-proofs}.

Thus, $\Pi_\alpha^{\mathrm{alloc}}$ contains every feasible proportional
state-feedback policy and also permits nongeometric allocation sequences. Together with
Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound}, the tail bound in
\eqref{eq:allocation-cost-gap} yields no regret against
$\Pi_\alpha^{\mathrm{alloc}}$ and, consequently, against feasible proportional
state-feedback policies.





\section{Online Algorithm}
\label{sec:online-algorithm}

We now present our online algorithm, which updates SDAC parameters by
OMD with unnormalized negentropy (equivalently, the
exponentiated gradient method) and a fixed step size $\eta>0$ over the
subprobability simplex $\mathcal{M}_H\subset\mathbb{R}^H$
\cite{hazan2016introduction}. Let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-negentropy}
\Psi(\mathbf{m}) \triangleq \sum_{i=1}^H\bigl(m_i\log m_i-m_i\bigr), \qquad
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^H,
\end{equation}
denote the unnormalized negentropy (with the convention $0\log0\triangleq0$).
Its Bregman divergence is the generalized Kullback--Leibler divergence
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-bregman}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{v})
\triangleq
\sum_{i=1}^H\Bigl(m_i\log\tfrac{m_i}{v_i}-m_i+v_i\Bigr),
\end{equation}
defined for
\[
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^H,
\qquad
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H.
\]


For each time $t\in[T]$, let $\mathbf{m}_t\in\mathcal{M}_H$ be the online parameter.
We initialize at
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-init}
\mathbf{m}_1\triangleq\frac{1}{H+1}\mathbf{1}
\end{equation}
so that
$\sup_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H}D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_1)\le\log(H+1)$
(see the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound}).

\paragraph{Control-action selection}
The controller first forms the nominal SDAC action
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-unconstrained-action}
\widehat{u}_t \triangleq \langle \boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\rangle,
\end{equation}
and clips it to the admissible interval in \eqref{eq:model-feasibility}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-feasible-action}
u_t \triangleq \min\{\widehat{u}_t,\,\alpha x_t\}.
\end{equation}
The state then evolves according to \eqref{eq:model-dynamics}.
Because $\widehat{u}_t\ge 0$, the clipping rule and induction on
\eqref{eq:model-dynamics}, starting from \eqref{eq:model-init} with $w_t\in[0,1]$, yield
$x_t\ge0$ for all $t\in[T+1]$ and show that the online trajectory satisfies
\eqref{eq:model-feasibility}.

The scalar correction in \eqref{eq:alg-feasible-action} also has an exact
Bregman-projection interpretation. Lemma~\ref{lem:availability-projection} shows
that the negentropy projection of $\mathbf m_t$ onto the parameters compatible
with the available state induces exactly the clipped action $u_t$.

This interpretation does not change the implementation. The full
$H$-dimensional execution vector $\mathbf m_t^{\mathrm{exec}}$ is never calculated
because only its scalar induced action is executed. In particular, the OMD update
below uses the internal iterate $\mathbf m_t$, not
$\mathbf m_t^{\mathrm{exec}}$. Therefore the internal iterates remain on the fixed
domain $\mathcal M_H$, and the analysis does not acquire a time-varying comparator
set. Forming the feature vectors and their inner products, followed by the OMD
update, takes $O(H)$ time and $O(H)$ memory per round.

\paragraph{Surrogate cost and OMD update}
After $(w_t,c_t)$ is revealed, form the surrogate subgradient displayed after
Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-surrogate},
$\mathbf{g}_t\in\partial\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)$, which satisfies
$\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\le G_\alpha$ with $G_\alpha$ defined in
\eqref{eq:def-surrogate-G}. This step uses the revealed cost function and the
full-information subgradient oracle from Section~\ref{sec:model-setup}.
We define the OMD update
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-omd-argmin}
\mathbf{m}_{t+1} \triangleq \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H}
\Bigl\{\eta\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}\rangle + D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_t)\Bigr\}.
\end{equation}
\paragraph{Closed-form solution}
Although \eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin} is a constrained optimization problem, it is easy
to solve analytically because $\Psi$ is separable and the objective is linear in
$\mathbf{g}_t$. As shown in Lemma~\ref{lem:omd-closed-form}, the minimizer in
\eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin} is exactly the result of two elementary steps. First a
multiplicative step,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-omd-mult}
\widetilde{m}_{t+1,i} \triangleq m_{t,i}\exp\!\bigl(-\eta g_{t,i}\bigr), \qquad i=1,\ldots,H,
\end{equation}
where $g_{t,i}$ is the $i$-th coordinate of $\mathbf{g}_t$. This is followed by the Bregman projection onto $\mathcal{M}_H$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-omd-proj}
\mathbf{m}_{t+1}
\triangleq
\operatorname{Proj}_{\mathcal{M}_H}^{\Psi}(\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1})
=
\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}}{\max\{1,\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}\|_1\}},
\end{equation}
where the projection operator is defined and derived in
Lemma~\ref{lem:bregman-projection} of
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-algorithm-proofs}; the scaling is nontrivial only when
$\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}\|_1>1$.


\section{Regret Analysis}
\label{sec:regret-analysis}
The following theorem states that the online controller achieves sublinear regret
against every fixed SDAC policy $\pi_{\mathbf{m}}$, where $\mathbf{m}$ is any parameter in $\mathcal{M}_H$.
The proof combines a standard online convex optimization bound for the surrogate
costs $\ell_t$ with the trajectory-stability result stated below. The complete
proofs are deferred to Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-regret-proofs}.

\begin{theorem}[Regret bound]
\label{thm:regret-bound}
Suppose the cost functions satisfy the regularity and bounded-subgradient
assumptions of Section~\ref{sec:model-setup}. Then the OMD controller with
action clipping from Section~\ref{sec:online-algorithm}, initialized as in
\eqref{eq:alg-init} and using step size $\eta>0$, satisfies, for the regret defined in \eqref{eq:def-regret},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:regret-theorem-bound}
\RegSDAC{T}
\le
\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta}
+
\eta T\Gamma_\alpha,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-Gamma-alpha}
\Gamma_\alpha
\triangleq
\frac{G_\alpha^2}{2}
+
\frac{\alpha G_\alpha(L_x+\alpha L_u)}{(1-\alpha)^2},
\end{equation}
and $G_\alpha=\alpha\bigl(\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}+L_u\bigr)$ as in
\eqref{eq:def-surrogate-G}.
In particular, the choice
\[
\eta^*
=
\sqrt{\frac{\log(H+1)}{T\Gamma_\alpha}}
\]
gives
\[
\RegSDAC{T}
\le
2\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha T\log(H+1)}.
\]
For fixed $L_x,L_u>0$, the leading factor obeys
\[
\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha}=\Theta(\alpha)
\quad\text{as }\alpha\downarrow0,
\]
and
\[
\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha}
=\Theta\!\left((1-\alpha)^{-3/2}\right)
\quad\text{as }\alpha\uparrow1.
\]
\end{theorem}

The nonstandard step is to control the difference between the realized online
trajectory and the fixed-policy trajectory associated with the current internal
iterate.

\begin{lemma}[Trajectory stability]
\label{lem:contractive-deviation}
For the online trajectory and the counterfactual fixed-policy trajectory associated
with the current internal iterate, one has, for every $t\in[T]$,
\begin{align}
\left|x_t-x_t(\mathbf{m}_t)\right|
&\le
\frac{\alpha\eta G_\alpha}{(1-\alpha)^2},
\label{eq:contractive-state-bound}\\
0\le u_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-u_t
&\le
\frac{\alpha^2\eta G_\alpha}{(1-\alpha)^2}.
\label{eq:contractive-action-bound}
\end{align}
Here $x_t(\mathbf{m}_t)$ is generated by using the single fixed parameter
$\mathbf{m}_t$ from time $1$ through time $t$. Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stability-sum-bound}
\sum_{t=1}^T \Bigl(c_t(x_t,u_t)-\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)\Bigr)
\le
\frac{\alpha\eta T G_\alpha(L_x+\alpha L_u)}{(1-\alpha)^2}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

The proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:contractive-deviation} is given in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-regret-proofs}. The proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound} then has three steps. First, the OMD bound for the
surrogate costs gives
\[
\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)
-\min_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H}\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
\le
\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta}+\frac{\eta G_\alpha^2T}{2}.
\]
Second, \eqref{eq:stability-sum-bound} controls the difference between the
realized and surrogate costs. Third, adding the two bounds and using
\eqref{eq:def-Gamma-alpha} gives \eqref{eq:regret-theorem-bound}; optimizing over
$\eta$ gives the stated choice $\eta^*$.

Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-allocation-approx} give the following comparator guarantee.

\begin{corollary}[No-regret against decreasing-allocation policies]
\label{cor:regret-vs-allocation}
Set
\[
H_T\triangleq
\max\!\left\{1,\left\lceil\frac{\log T}{\log(1/\alpha)}\right\rceil\right\},
\]
and run the OMD controller with action clipping, memory horizon $H=H_T$, and the
step size $\eta^*$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound}. For
$\mathbf q\in\mathcal Q_\alpha^\downarrow$, let
\[
J_T(\mathbf q)
\triangleq
\sum_{t=1}^T c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf q),u_t(\mathbf q)\bigr)
\]
and
\[
\RegAlloc{T}
\triangleq
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}
-
\inf_{\mathbf q\in\mathcal Q_\alpha^\downarrow}J_T(\mathbf q).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:allocation-regret-bound}
\begin{aligned}
\RegAlloc{T}
&\le
2\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha T\log(H_T+1)}\\
&\quad+
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)\alpha^{H_T+1}T.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $\Gamma_\alpha$ is defined in \eqref{eq:def-Gamma-alpha}. Since
$\alpha^{H_T}\le1/T$ and
$\log(H_T+1)=O(\log\log T)$ as $T\to\infty$,
the approximation term is $O(1)$. Consequently,
\[
\RegAlloc{T}
=
O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log\log T}\right)
=o(T).
\]
\end{corollary}

Because geometric allocation sequences induce proportional state feedback, the
same controller also satisfies a gain-dependent bound for that policy family.

\begin{corollary}[No-regret against proportional state-feedback policies]
\label{cor:regret-vs-linear}
Run the controller of Corollary~\ref{cor:regret-vs-allocation}. For
$k\in[0,\alpha]$, let
$J_T(k)\triangleq\sum_{t=1}^T c_t\bigl(x_t(k),u_t(k)\bigr)$, where
$u_t(k)=k x_t(k)$, and define
\[
\RegProp{T}
\triangleq
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}-\min_{k\in[0,\alpha]}J_T(k).
\]
For every $k\in[0,\alpha]$, with $\lambda_k=\alpha-k$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:linear-regret-decomposition}
\begin{aligned}
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}-J_T(k)
&\le
2\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha T\log(H_T+1)} \\
&\quad+
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)
\frac{k\lambda_k^{H_T}}{1-\lambda_k}\,T.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\RegProp{T}
&\le
2\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha T\log(H_T+1)}
\\
&\quad+
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)\alpha^{H_T+1}T
\\
&=
O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log\log T}\right)
=o(T).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{corollary}

The proofs of Corollaries~\ref{cor:regret-vs-allocation}
and~\ref{cor:regret-vs-linear} are given in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-regret-proofs}.

\section{Minimax Lower Bound}
\label{sec:minimax-lower-bound}

The next result shows that the $\sqrt{T}$ dependence is unavoidable and
quantifies the dependence on $1-\alpha$ that any causal feasible controller must
incur. Its proof is deferred to Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-minimax-proofs}.

\begin{theorem}[Minimax SDAC regret lower bound]
\label{thm:sdac-lower-bound}
Fix $H\ge1$ and $T\ge2$. For every causal feasible online controller
$\mathcal A$, possibly randomized, there exists a deterministic oblivious sequence
of arrivals and costs satisfying the assumptions of
Section~\ref{sec:model-setup} such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-lower-exact}
\mathbb E_{\mathcal A}\!\left[\RegSDAC{T}\right]
\ge
\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}
\sqrt{\sum_{t=2}^T \beta_t^2},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-lower-beta}
\beta_t
\triangleq
\alpha\left(
L_u
+
\frac{L_x(1-\alpha^{t-2})}{1-\alpha}
\right).
\end{equation}
The expectation is only over the possible internal randomization of $\mathcal A$; the
adversarial sequence whose existence is asserted is deterministic and fixed before
the interaction.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Lower bound for sufficiently long horizons]
\label{cor:sdac-lower-mixing}
Define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-mixing-time}
t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)
\triangleq
\left\lceil
\frac{\log 2}{\log(1/\alpha)}
\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
If $T\ge2t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)+2$, then every causal feasible controller $\mathcal A$
admits a deterministic oblivious sequence for which
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-lower-mixing}
\mathbb E_{\mathcal A}\!\left[\RegSDAC{T}\right]
\ge
\frac{G_\alpha}{8}\sqrt{T},
\end{equation}
where $G_\alpha=\alpha(L_x/(1-\alpha)+L_u)$ as in
\eqref{eq:def-surrogate-G}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Large-$\alpha$ regime]
\label{rem:large-alpha-regime}
As $\alpha\uparrow1$, stored arrivals persist longer and
$t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)=\Theta((1-\alpha)^{-1})$. Hence,
Corollary~\ref{cor:sdac-lower-mixing} yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-lower-large-alpha}
\mathbb E_{\mathcal A}\!\left[\RegSDAC{T}\right]
=
\Omega\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{T}}{1-\alpha}\right)
\end{equation}
whenever $T\ge2t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)+2$. The upper bound in
Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound} is
$O((1-\alpha)^{-3/2}\sqrt{T\log(H+1)})$, so a factor
$(1-\alpha)^{-1/2}$ between the two bounds remains open.

The condition $T\ge2t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)+2$ is essential for interpreting the large-$\alpha$
asymptotic. If $T$ is fixed while $\alpha\uparrow1$, then
\[
\frac{1-\alpha^{t-2}}{1-\alpha}\longrightarrow t-2,
\]
and the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:sdac-lower-exact} converges to the finite
quantity
\[
\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}
\sqrt{\sum_{t=2}^T\bigl(L_u+L_x(t-2)\bigr)^2}.
\]
Thus, for fixed $T$, the exact finite-horizon bound in
\eqref{eq:sdac-lower-exact} applies instead of the long-horizon scaling in
\eqref{eq:sdac-lower-large-alpha}.
\end{remark}

\section{Discussion and Extensions}
\label{sec:extensions}

We briefly discuss three extensions. In each case, the feasibility, convexity, and
stability arguments used above continue to apply after the corresponding changes
to the policy domain and the Lipschitz constants. We state these changes but omit
the resulting regret bounds, whose form is unchanged up to the modified constants
and, in the vector settings, the chosen product norm.

\paragraph{Retention $\alpha\ge 1$.}
For $\alpha\ge1$, fix $k$ such that
$\bar\alpha\triangleq\alpha-k\in(0,1)$ and write $u_t=kx_t+v_t$. Then
\[
x_{t+1}=\bar\alpha x_t-v_t+w_t.
\]
An SDAC filter for $v_t$ uses the powers $\bar\alpha^i$. If
$0\le v_t\le\bar\alpha x_t$, then
\[
kx_t\le u_t=kx_t+v_t
\le(k+\bar\alpha)x_t
=\alpha x_t,
\]
so the original feasibility constraint \eqref{eq:model-feasibility} is satisfied. Since
\[
x_{t+1}\le\bar\alpha x_t+1,
\qquad x_1=0,
\]
induction gives $x_t\le1/(1-\bar\alpha)$. The transformed cost
$c_t(x,kx+v)$ remains convex in $(x,v)$. Thus the analysis applies when the costs
satisfy the corresponding Lipschitz and bounded-subgradient assumptions on the
bounded domain
\[
0\le x\le\frac{1}{1-\bar\alpha},
\qquad
kx\le u\le\alpha x.
\]
A remark is that for a fixed $k$, the comparator class is correspondingly restricted to policies
whose actions satisfy $u_t\ge kx_t$. Selecting $k$ (fixed or even in an online fashion)
remains an interesting direction for future work.

\paragraph{Vector action / multi-task allocation.}
Consider a single storage state $x_t$ with a vector action
$\mathbf{u}_t\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^n$ that allocates resources across $n$ tasks, subject to
$\sum_{a=1}^n u_{t,a}\le\alpha x_t$, so that the total drain from storage is the sum of
the task allocations. The dynamics remain
$x_{t+1}=\alpha x_t-\sum_{a=1}^n u_{t,a}+w_t$, and the cost $c_t(x_t,\mathbf{u}_t)$ is convex in
$(x,\mathbf{u})$. Replace $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$ with a matrix
$\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{n\times H}$ satisfying
\[
\sum_{a=1}^n\sum_{i=1}^H M_{a,i}\le1,
\qquad
u_{t,a}(\mathbf M)
=\sum_{i=1}^H M_{a,i}\alpha^i w_{t-i}.
\]
Set $m_i=\sum_{a=1}^n M_{a,i}$. Then
\[
m_i\ge0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i
=\sum_{a=1}^n\sum_{i=1}^H M_{a,i}
\le1,
\]
so $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$. Moreover,
\[
\sum_{a=1}^n u_{t,a}(\mathbf{M})
=\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\alpha^i w_{t-i}
\le\alpha x_t(\mathbf{M}),
\]
where the inequality is the scalar SDAC feasibility bound.
The state and vector action remain affine in $\mathbf M$, so
$c_t(x_t(\mathbf{M}),\mathbf{u}_t(\mathbf{M}))$ is convex in $\mathbf{M}$. For the
online controller, a nominal vector that exceeds the available total resource can
be scaled proportionally so that its entries remain nonnegative and sum to
$\alpha x_t$. Under an $\ell_1$ Lipschitz bound in the action, the size of this
correction equals the excess total action, and the stability proof proceeds with
the corresponding matrix norm. OMD then runs over the matrix subprobability
simplex above.

\paragraph{Multiple storages with diagonal dynamics.}
Suppose the state is $\mathbf{x}_t\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^d$ and evolves as
$\mathbf{x}_{t+1}=\mathbf{A} \mathbf{x}_t-\mathbf{u}_t+\mathbf{w}_t$ with $\mathbf{A}=\mathrm{diag}(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_d)$,
$\alpha_s\in(0,1)$ for $s\in[d]$, arrivals $\mathbf{w}_t\in[0,1]^d$, and per-storage constraints
$0\le u_{t,s}\le\alpha_s x_{t,s}$. Assign one SDAC parameter in $\mathcal{M}_H$
to each storage and use the powers $\alpha_s^i$ in coordinate $s$. The dynamics
and feasibility constraints then decouple by storage, and the fixed-policy domain
is a product of subprobability simplices, with
$x_{t,s}\le1/(1-\alpha_s)$ for every $s\in[d]$. The online controller clips each nominal
action to $\alpha_s x_{t,s}$. If the cost is jointly convex and satisfies compatible
Lipschitz and bounded-subgradient assumptions on the resulting bounded product
domain, the surrogate cost is jointly convex in all SDAC parameters and OMD applies
on the product set. The update separates by storage only when the cost is
coordinate-separable; otherwise it is a joint update.
Cross-coupled storage systems (non-diagonal \(\mathbf{A}\)) require additional
analysis and remain an interesting direction for future work.



\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied adversarial online control of a storage system under a state-dependent
action constraint. We constructed the SDAC so that every policy trajectory is feasible,
and an online algorithm with
$O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log(H+1)}\right)$ regret against the best fixed SDAC policy.
Once $T$ is at least on the order of $(1-\alpha)^{-1}$, the
minimax lower bound gives
$\Omega((1-\alpha)^{-1}\sqrt{T})$ regret as $\alpha\uparrow1$; hence a factor
$(1-\alpha)^{-1/2}$ between the upper and lower bounds remains open. The
SDAC approximation results further give
$O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log\log T}\right)$ regret against the best decreasing-allocation
policy when $H=\Theta(\log T)$. The
model applies directly to
energy-harvesting battery management and related settings in which storage limits
must be respected in real time.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:sdac-lower-bound}]
Fix a causal feasible controller $\mathcal A$. Let
$\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_T$ be independent Rademacher random variables, also independent
of the internal randomization of $\mathcal A$, with
\[
\mathbb P(\xi_t=1)
=
\mathbb P(\xi_t=-1)
=\frac12.
\]
Set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-hard-arrival}
w_t=1
\qquad
\text{for all }t\in[T],
\end{equation}
and define the costs by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-hard-cost}
c_t(x,u)
=
\xi_t(L_xx-L_uu)
\qquad
\text{for all }t\in[T].
\end{equation}
Each $c_t$ is affine and hence convex, and, for any two points in
$\mathcal D_\alpha$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-lipschitz-check}
\begin{aligned}
|c_t(x,u)-c_t(x',u')|
&=
|L_x(x-x')-L_u(u-u')| \\
&\le
L_x|x-x'|+L_u|u-u'|.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\[
\nabla c_t=\xi_t(L_x,-L_u),
\qquad
|\partial_xc_t|=L_x,
\qquad
|\partial_uc_t|=L_u,
\]
so the required subgradient bounds hold.


We now select two benchmark policies. The parameter
$\mathbf m^{(0)}$ leaves all mass in the subprobability simplex unused, so its
SDAC action is always zero. The parameter $\mathbf m^{(1)}$ places all mass on the
first lag, so its action is $\alpha w_{t-1}$. Let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-two-policy-parameters}
\mathbf m^{(0)}\triangleq\mathbf 0,
\qquad
\mathbf m^{(1)}\triangleq\mathbf e_1
\end{equation}
belong to $\mathcal M_H$, where
$\mathbf e_1=(1,0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb R^H$. Thus, under
\eqref{eq:lower-hard-arrival}, $\mathbf m^{(0)}$ is the no-release policy, whereas
$\mathbf m^{(1)}$ releases the retained portion of the preceding arrival. For the
no-release policy,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-policy-zero-trajectory}
u_t(\mathbf m^{(0)})=0,
\qquad
x_t(\mathbf m^{(0)})
=
\rho_t
\triangleq
\frac{1-\alpha^{t-1}}{1-\alpha}.
\end{equation}
This follows by unrolling $x_{t+1}=\alpha x_t+1$:
\[
x_t(\mathbf m^{(0)})
=\sum_{s=0}^{t-2}\alpha^s
=\frac{1-\alpha^{t-1}}{1-\alpha},
\qquad t\ge2,
\]
and the closed form also gives $x_1(\mathbf m^{(0)})=0$. For the one-lag policy,
\eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-policy-one-action}
u_1(\mathbf m^{(1)})=0,
\qquad
u_t(\mathbf m^{(1)})=\alpha,
\quad t\ge2.
\end{equation}
Its state at $t=2$ is
\[
x_2(\mathbf m^{(1)})
=\alpha x_1(\mathbf m^{(1)})-u_1(\mathbf m^{(1)})+w_1
=1.
\]
If $x_t(\mathbf m^{(1)})=1$ for some $t\ge2$, then
\[
\begin{aligned}
x_{t+1}(\mathbf m^{(1)})
&=\alpha x_t(\mathbf m^{(1)})-u_t(\mathbf m^{(1)})+w_t \\
&=\alpha-\alpha+1
=1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Induction therefore gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-policy-one-trajectory}
x_t(\mathbf m^{(1)})=1,
\qquad
t\ge2.
\end{equation}
For $t\ge2$, the difference between the two states is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-two-policy-state-difference}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_t
&\triangleq
x_t(\mathbf m^{(0)})-x_t(\mathbf m^{(1)}) \\
&=\rho_t-1 \\
&=\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha^{t-2})}{1-\alpha}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}



The two policies have the same state and action at $t=1$. For $t\ge2$,
\eqref{eq:lower-policy-one-action} and
\eqref{eq:lower-two-policy-state-difference} give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-two-policy-cost-difference}
\begin{aligned}
J_T(\mathbf m^{(0)})-J_T(\mathbf m^{(1)})
=
\sum_{t=2}^T\xi_t(L_x\Delta_t+\alpha L_u)
=
\sum_{t=2}^T\xi_t \beta_t,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $\beta_t$ as in \eqref{eq:sdac-lower-beta}.



Let $\mathcal F_t^-$ be the information available immediately before $c_t$ is
revealed, including the randomization used to select $u_t$. By causality,
$(x_t,u_t)$ is $\mathcal F_t^-$-measurable, whereas $\xi_t$ is independent of
$\mathcal F_t^-$ and has mean zero. Hence, by the tower property,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-online-mean-zero}
\mathbb E_{\boldsymbol\xi,\mathcal A}\!\left[
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}
\right]
=\sum_{t=1}^T
\mathbb E_{\boldsymbol\xi,\mathcal A}\!\left[
(L_xx_t-L_uu_t)\mathbb E[\xi_t\mid\mathcal F_t^-]
\right]
=0.
\end{equation}
The fixed-policy trajectories depend only on the arrival sequence and are
therefore independent of the Rademacher variables. Thus, for $j\in\{0,1\}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-two-policy-means-zero}
\mathbb E_{\boldsymbol\xi}\!\left[
J_T(\mathbf m^{(j)})
\right]
=\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbb E[\xi_t]
\bigl(L_xx_t(\mathbf m^{(j)})-L_uu_t(\mathbf m^{(j)})\bigr)
=0.
\end{equation}


Because $\mathbf m^{(0)},\mathbf m^{(1)}\in\mathcal M_H$, for every realization of
the Rademacher variables and every choice made by the controller,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-two-policy-regret}
\begin{aligned}
\RegSDAC{T}
&=
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}
-\min_{\mathbf m\in\mathcal M_H}J_T(\mathbf m) \\
&\ge
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}
-\min\!\left\{
J_T(\mathbf m^{(0)}),
J_T(\mathbf m^{(1)})
\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Using $\min\{a,b\}=(a+b-|a-b|)/2$ together with
\eqref{eq:lower-online-mean-zero} and
\eqref{eq:lower-two-policy-means-zero}, and then taking expectations in
\eqref{eq:lower-two-policy-regret}, gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-randomized-sequence-bound}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\boldsymbol\xi,\mathcal A}\!\left[\RegSDAC{T}\right]
&\ge \frac12\mathbb E_{\boldsymbol\xi}\!\left[
\left|J_T(\mathbf m^{(0)})-J_T(\mathbf m^{(1)})\right|
\right] \\
&= \frac12\mathbb E_{\boldsymbol\xi}\!\left[
\left|\sum_{t=2}^T\xi_t\beta_t\right|
\right] \\
&\ge \frac{1}{2\sqrt2}
\left(\sum_{t=2}^T\beta_t^2\right)^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The last step follows from Khintchine's inequality for Rademacher sums.

It remains to remove the randomization from the adversarial construction. The
left-hand side of \eqref{eq:lower-randomized-sequence-bound} is the average over
all $2^T$ realizations of the Rademacher vector $\boldsymbol\xi$:
\[
\mathbb E_{\boldsymbol\xi,\mathcal A}\!\left[\RegSDAC{T}\right]
=
\frac{1}{2^T}
\sum_{\boldsymbol\xi\in\{-1,1\}^T}
\mathbb E_{\mathcal A}\!\left[
\RegSDAC{T}\,\middle|\,\boldsymbol\xi
\right].
\]
Hence at least one vector $\boldsymbol\xi^\star$ makes the conditional expectation
at least the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:lower-randomized-sequence-bound}. Fixing
$\boldsymbol\xi^\star$ in \eqref{eq:lower-hard-cost}, together with the arrivals
in \eqref{eq:lower-hard-arrival}, produces a deterministic admissible sequence
fixed before the interaction. The remaining expectation is only over the internal
randomization of $\mathcal A$, and \eqref{eq:sdac-lower-exact} follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:sdac-lower-mixing}]
The definition of $t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-mixing-geometric}
\alpha^{t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)}
\le
\alpha^{\log 2/\log(1/\alpha)}
=\frac12.
\end{equation}
Therefore, for every $t\ge t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)+2$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-beta-post-mixing}
\begin{aligned}
\beta_t
&\ge
\alpha\left(
L_u+\frac{L_x}{2(1-\alpha)}
\right) \\
&\ge
\frac{\alpha}{2}
\left(
L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}
\right)
=\frac{G_\alpha}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
There are $T-t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)-1$ indices in
$\{t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)+2,\ldots,T\}$. If
$T\ge2t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)+2$, then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-post-mixing-count}
T-t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)-1
\ge
\frac{T}{2}.
\end{equation}
Applying Theorem~\ref{thm:sdac-lower-bound} and retaining only these indices,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-mixing-final}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\mathcal A}\!\left[\RegSDAC{T}\right]
&\ge
\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}
\left[
\frac{T}{2}\left(\frac{G_\alpha}{2}\right)^2
\right]^{1/2} \\
&=
\frac{G_\alpha}{8}\sqrt{T}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This is \eqref{eq:sdac-lower-mixing}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-state-decomp}]
The auxiliary quantities satisfy
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:r-recursion}
r_{t+1}=\alpha r_t+w_t,
\qquad
r_1=0,
\end{equation}
and, for every $i\in\{1,\ldots,H\}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:r-trunc-recursion}
\begin{aligned}
\alpha r_t(i)-\alpha^i w_{t-i}
&=\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha^j w_{t-j} \\
&=r_{t+1}(i)-w_t.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For $i=1$, the sum in the first line is empty.

At $t=1$, since $w_{\tau}\equiv 0$ for $\tau\le 0$,
\begin{align*}
r_1(i)
&=
\sum_{j=1}^{i}\alpha^{j-1}w_{1-j}
=0,\\
r_1
&=0,\\
x_1(\mathbf{m})
&=0,
\end{align*}
so \eqref{eq:sdac-state-decomp} holds. Suppose it holds at time $t$. Using
\eqref{eq:sdac-state-update} and \eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:decomp-induction-1}
\begin{aligned}
x_{t+1}(\mathbf{m})
&=
\alpha\left(
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i r_t(i)
+\bigl(1-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\bigr)r_t
\right) \\
&\quad
-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\alpha^i w_{t-i}
+w_t \\
&=
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i
\bigl(\alpha r_t(i)-\alpha^i w_{t-i}\bigr) \\
&\quad
+\alpha\bigl(1-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\bigr)r_t+w_t.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:r-recursion} and \eqref{eq:r-trunc-recursion} into
\eqref{eq:decomp-induction-1},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:decomp-induction-2}
\begin{aligned}
x_{t+1}(\mathbf{m})
&=
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i
\bigl(r_{t+1}(i)-w_t\bigr) \\
&\quad
+\bigl(1-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\bigr)(r_{t+1}-w_t)+w_t \\
&=
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i r_{t+1}(i)
+\bigl(1-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\bigr)r_{t+1},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
because the total coefficient of $w_t$ in the first line is
$-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i-\bigl(1-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\bigr)+1=0$. This proves
\eqref{eq:sdac-state-decomp} by induction.

All summands defining $r_t(i)$ and $r_t$ are nonnegative. Since
$w_\tau=0$ for $\tau\le0$, $r_t(i)$ is a truncation of $r_t$; hence
\[
0\le r_t(i)\le r_t
\le\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\alpha^{j-1}
=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}.
\]
Finally,
\[
x_t(\mathbf{m})
=
r_t+\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\bigl(r_t(i)-r_t\bigr)
=
r_t+\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x,\mathbf{m}\rangle,
\]
which is affine in $\mathbf{m}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-surrogate}]
Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-state-decomp} and \eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy} give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-affine-pair}
x_t(\mathbf{m})=r_t+\langle \boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x,\mathbf{m}\rangle,
\qquad
u_t(\mathbf{m})=\langle \boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}\rangle.
\end{equation}
For every $i\in\{1,\ldots,H\}$, the definitions of $r_t(i)$ and $r_t$ give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:phi-x-exact-tail}
\begin{aligned}
\phi_{t,i}^x
&=
r_t(i)-r_t \\
&=
-\sum_{j=i+1}^{t-1}\alpha^{j-1}w_{t-j},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where the sum is empty when $i\ge t-1$. Hence
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-coordinate-decay}
\begin{aligned}
\bigl|\phi_{t,i}^x\bigr|
&\le
\sum_{j=i+1}^{\infty}\alpha^{j-1}
=
\frac{\alpha^i}{1-\alpha}, \\
0\le\phi_{t,i}^u
&=
\alpha^i w_{t-i}
\le
\alpha^i.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Taking the largest coordinate, attained in these upper bounds at $i=1$, yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-coefficient-norms}
\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x\|_\infty\le\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha},
\qquad
\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u\|_\infty\le\alpha.
\end{equation}
Since \eqref{eq:surr-affine-pair} is affine in $\mathbf{m}$ and $c_t$ is convex,
$\ell_t$ is convex.

By \eqref{eq:model-lipschitz} and
\eqref{eq:surr-affine-pair},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-lipschitz-short}
\begin{aligned}
|\ell_t(\mathbf{m})-\ell_t(\mathbf{m}')|
&\le
L_x\bigl|\langle \boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x,\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\rangle\bigr|
+
L_u\bigl|\langle \boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\rangle\bigr| \\
&\le
\bigl(L_x\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x\|_\infty+L_u\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u\|_\infty\bigr)
\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\|_1 \\
&\le
\alpha\left(\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}+L_u\right)
\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\|_1 \\
&=
G_\alpha\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Let $(\zeta_t^x,\zeta_t^u)$ denote the components of the bounded subgradient
$\boldsymbol{\zeta}_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{m}),u_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr)$ from
Section~\ref{sec:model-setup}, and set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-subgradient-short}
\mathbf{g}_t\triangleq
\zeta_t^x\,\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x+\zeta_t^u\,\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u.
\end{equation}
For every $\mathbf{m}'\in\mathcal{M}_H$, the subgradient inequality and
\eqref{eq:surr-affine-pair} yield
\begin{align*}
\ell_t(\mathbf{m}')
&\ge
\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
+\zeta_t^x\langle \boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x,\mathbf{m}'-\mathbf{m}\rangle
+\zeta_t^u\langle \boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}'-\mathbf{m}\rangle \\
&=
\ell_t(\mathbf{m})+\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}'-\mathbf{m}\rangle.
\end{align*}
Thus $\mathbf{g}_t\in\partial\ell_t(\mathbf{m})$. For each
$i\in\{1,\ldots,H\}$, the exact coordinate decay in
\eqref{eq:surr-coordinate-decay} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-subgradient-coordinate-bound}
\begin{aligned}
|g_{t,i}|
&\le
|\zeta_t^x|\bigl|\phi_{t,i}^x\bigr|
+|\zeta_t^u|\phi_{t,i}^u \\
&\le
\alpha^i\left(\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}+L_u\right) \\
&\le
\alpha\left(\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}+L_u\right)
=G_\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Taking the maximum over $i$ proves
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-subgradient-bound-short}
\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\le G_\alpha.
\end{equation}
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-allocation-approx}]
Fix $\mathbf q\in\mathcal Q_\alpha^\downarrow$ and set
\[
a_i\triangleq\frac{q_i}{\alpha^i},
\qquad
b_j\triangleq1-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}a_i,
\qquad j\ge1.
\]
The weighted-budget constraint gives $a_i\ge0$ and $b_j\ge0$. Moreover,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:allocation-state-decomposition}
x_t(\mathbf q)
=
\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}\alpha^{j-1}b_jw_{t-j}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the identity is immediate at $t=1$. If it holds at time $t$, then
\begin{align*}
x_{t+1}(\mathbf q)
&=
w_t+
\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}
\bigl(\alpha^jb_j-q_j\bigr)w_{t-j}\\
&=
w_t+
\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}\alpha^jb_{j+1}w_{t-j},
\end{align*}
because $q_j=\alpha^ja_j$ and $b_{j+1}=b_j-a_j$. Reindexing gives
\eqref{eq:allocation-state-decomposition} at time $t+1$.

For each $j\ge1$,
\[
a_j
\le
1-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}a_i
=b_j.
\]
Consequently,
\begin{align*}
0\le u_t(\mathbf q)
&=
\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}\alpha^ja_jw_{t-j}\\
&\le
\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}\alpha^jb_jw_{t-j}
=\alpha x_t(\mathbf q),
\end{align*}
which proves \eqref{eq:model-feasibility} for the decreasing-allocation policy.

For the truncated vector in \eqref{eq:allocation-truncation},
\[
m_{H,i}(\mathbf q)=a_i\ge0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^H m_{H,i}(\mathbf q)
\le
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_i
\le1.
\]
Thus $\mathbf m_H(\mathbf q)\in\mathcal M_H$. Its SDAC action is
\[
u_t\bigl(\mathbf m_H(\mathbf q)\bigr)
=
\sum_{i=1}^{\min\{H,t-1\}}q_iw_{t-i}.
\]
It follows that
\[
0\le
u_t(\mathbf q)-u_t\bigl(\mathbf m_H(\mathbf q)\bigr)
=
\sum_{i=H+1}^{t-1}q_iw_{t-i}
\le \operatorname{Tail}_H(\mathbf q),
\]
where the middle sum is empty when $t\le H+1$. This proves
\eqref{eq:allocation-action-gap}.

Define
\[
e_t^x
\triangleq
x_t\bigl(\mathbf m_H(\mathbf q)\bigr)-x_t(\mathbf q),
\qquad
e_t^u
\triangleq
u_t(\mathbf q)-u_t\bigl(\mathbf m_H(\mathbf q)\bigr).
\]
The two state recursions give
\[
e_{t+1}^x=\alpha e_t^x+e_t^u,
\qquad e_1^x=0.
\]
Since $0\le e_t^u\le \operatorname{Tail}_H(\mathbf q)$,
\[
0\le e_t^x
=
\sum_{\tau=1}^{t-1}\alpha^{t-1-\tau}e_\tau^u
\le
\frac{\operatorname{Tail}_H(\mathbf q)}{1-\alpha},
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:allocation-state-gap}. The Lipschitz property now yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\bigl|
c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf m_H(\mathbf q)),u_t(\mathbf m_H(\mathbf q))\bigr)
\\
&\qquad
-c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf q),u_t(\mathbf q)\bigr)
\bigr| \\
&\qquad\le
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)\operatorname{Tail}_H(\mathbf q).
\end{aligned}
\]
Finally,
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{Tail}_H(\mathbf q)
&=
\sum_{i=H+1}^{\infty}\alpha^ia_i\\
&\le
\alpha^{H+1}\sum_{i=H+1}^{\infty}a_i
\le\alpha^{H+1}.
\end{align*}
Summing the per-period cost bound proves \eqref{eq:allocation-cost-gap}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:proportional-as-allocation}]
Fix $k\in[0,\alpha]$, let $\lambda_k=\alpha-k$, and let $\mathbf q(k)$ be defined by
\eqref{eq:proportional-as-allocation}. The sequence is nonnegative and decreasing.
If $k=0$, then $\mathbf q(k)=\mathbf 0\in\mathcal Q_\alpha^\downarrow$. If $k>0$,
then $\lambda_k/\alpha<1$ and
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{q_i(k)}{\alpha^i}
=
\frac{k}{\alpha}
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}
\left(\frac{\lambda_k}{\alpha}\right)^j
=
\frac{k}{\alpha-\lambda_k}
=1,
\]
so again $\mathbf q(k)\in\mathcal Q_\alpha^\downarrow$. Substituting
$u_t(k)=k x_t(k)$ into the dynamics and unrolling gives
\[
u_t(k)
=
k\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\lambda_k^{i-1}w_{t-i}
=
\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}q_i(k)w_{t-i}
=u_t\bigl(\mathbf q(k)\bigr).
\]
The two policies therefore have identical action and state trajectories. Their
SDAC truncations also coincide coordinatewise, and the omitted geometric tail is
\[
\operatorname{Tail}_H\bigl(\mathbf q(k)\bigr)
=
k\sum_{i=H+1}^{\infty}\lambda_k^{i-1}
=
\frac{k\lambda_k^H}{1-\lambda_k}.
\]
Substitution in \eqref{eq:allocation-cost-gap} proves
\eqref{eq:sdac-lin-approx-cost}.
\end{proof}

\section{Proofs for the Online Algorithm}
\label{sec:appendix-algorithm-proofs}

The following lemmas establish the Bregman-projection interpretation of scalar
action clipping, the Bregman-projection formula, and the equivalent closed-form
OMD update used in Section~\ref{sec:online-algorithm}.

\begin{lemma}[Action clipping as a Bregman projection]
\label{lem:availability-projection}
At time $t$, define the parameter set compatible with the available state by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-availability-set}
\mathcal F_t(x_t)
\triangleq
\left\{
\mathbf m\in\mathcal M_H:
\langle\boldsymbol\phi_t^u,\mathbf m\rangle
\le \alpha x_t
\right\}.
\end{equation}
For the negentropy $\Psi$ in \eqref{eq:def-negentropy}, let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-exec-projection}
\mathbf m_t^{\mathrm{exec}}
\in
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mathbf m\in\mathcal F_t(x_t)}
D_\Psi(\mathbf m\|\mathbf m_t).
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:availability-projection-action}
\left\langle
\boldsymbol\phi_t^u,\mathbf m_t^{\mathrm{exec}}
\right\rangle
=
\min\!\left\{
\left\langle\boldsymbol\phi_t^u,\mathbf m_t\right\rangle,
\alpha x_t
\right\}
=u_t.
\end{equation}
Thus the projection formulation and the scalar clipping rule induce exactly the
same executed action.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The set $\mathcal F_t(x_t)$ is nonempty because it contains $\mathbf 0$, and it is
compact and convex. By Lemma~\ref{lem:omd-closed-form}, $\mathbf m_t$ is strictly
positive coordinatewise. Hence
$D_\Psi(\cdot\|\mathbf m_t)$ is strictly convex on $\mathcal M_H$ and is minimized
uniquely at $\mathbf m_t$ over any feasible set containing $\mathbf m_t$.
Consequently, if
\[
\langle\boldsymbol\phi_t^u,\mathbf m_t\rangle
\le \alpha x_t,
\]
then $\mathbf m_t^{\mathrm{exec}}=\mathbf m_t$, which proves
\eqref{eq:availability-projection-action} in the feasible case.

Suppose instead that $\mathbf m_t$ is infeasible. We claim that the action
constraint is active at $\mathbf m_t^{\mathrm{exec}}$. If it were slack, then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:availability-projection-slack}
\langle\boldsymbol\phi_t^u,\mathbf m_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\rangle
<\alpha x_t
<\langle\boldsymbol\phi_t^u,\mathbf m_t\rangle.
\end{equation}
For sufficiently small $\lambda\in(0,1)$, the point
\[
\mathbf m_\lambda
\triangleq
(1-\lambda)\mathbf m_t^{\mathrm{exec}}+\lambda\mathbf m_t
\]
would still belong to $\mathcal F_t(x_t)$. Strict convexity and
$D_\Psi(\mathbf m_t\|\mathbf m_t)=0$ would then give
\begin{align*}
D_\Psi(\mathbf m_\lambda\|\mathbf m_t)
&<
(1-\lambda)D_\Psi(\mathbf m_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\|\mathbf m_t) \\
&<
D_\Psi(\mathbf m_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\|\mathbf m_t),
\end{align*}
contradicting \eqref{eq:def-exec-projection}. Hence
\[
\langle\boldsymbol\phi_t^u,\mathbf m_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\rangle
=\alpha x_t
\]
in the infeasible case, which completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Bregman projection onto $\mathcal{M}_H$]
\label{lem:bregman-projection}
Let $D_\Psi$ be the Bregman divergence \eqref{eq:def-bregman} of the unnormalized
negentropy \eqref{eq:def-negentropy}. Fix $\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$. Then the Bregman projection of
$\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}$ onto $\mathcal{M}_H$,
\[
\operatorname{Proj}_{\mathcal{M}_H}^{\Psi}(\widetilde{\mathbf{m}})
\triangleq
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}),
\]
is given by
\[
\operatorname{Proj}_{\mathcal{M}_H}^{\Psi}(\widetilde{\mathbf{m}})
=
\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}}{\max\{1,\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|_1\}}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The projection objective $\mathbf{m}\mapsto D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}})$ is strictly convex, and, for $m_i>0$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:breg-grad}
\frac{\partial}{\partial m_i}D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}})
=
\log\frac{m_i}{\widetilde{m}_i}.
\end{equation}
Its unconstrained minimizer is therefore $\mathbf{m}=\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}$.

If $\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|_1\le1$, this minimizer belongs to $\mathcal{M}_H$, and hence
\[
\operatorname{Proj}_{\mathcal{M}_H}^{\Psi}(\widetilde{\mathbf{m}})
=\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}.
\]
Suppose $\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|_1>1$. The constraint
$\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\le1$ is then active. Moreover, the minimizer has no zero
coordinate: if $m_i=0$, transferring an arbitrarily small amount of mass
from a positive coordinate to coordinate $i$ has directional derivative
$-\infty$, because
\[
\lim_{z\downarrow0}\log\frac{z}{\widetilde{m}_i}=-\infty.
\]
Thus the nonnegativity constraints are inactive. With $\nu\ge0$ denoting
the multiplier of $\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\le1$, stationarity and complementary
slackness give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:breg-kkt}
\log\frac{m_i}{\widetilde{m}_i}+\nu=0,
\qquad
\|\mathbf{m}\|_1=1.
\end{equation}
The first relation in \eqref{eq:breg-kkt} implies
\[
m_i
=
\widetilde{m}_i \exp(-\nu).
\]
Summing over $i$ and using the second relation in \eqref{eq:breg-kkt},
\[
1
=
\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|_1 \exp(-\nu),
\qquad
\exp(-\nu)
=
\frac{1}{\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|_1}.
\]
Hence $\nu=\log\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|_1>0$ and
\[
\operatorname{Proj}_{\mathcal{M}_H}^{\Psi}(\widetilde{\mathbf{m}})
=
\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}}{\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|_1}.
\]
Combining the cases $\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|_1\le1$ and $\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|_1>1$ proves the stated formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Closed-form solution of the OMD update]
\label{lem:omd-closed-form}
Fix $\mathbf{m}_t\in\mathcal{M}_H\cap\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$, $\eta>0$, and
$\mathbf{g}_t\in\mathbb{R}^H$, and let
$\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}$ be defined coordinatewise by \eqref{eq:alg-omd-mult},
i.e.\ $\widetilde{m}_{t+1,i}=m_{t,i}\exp(-\eta g_{t,i})$. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H}\Bigl\{\eta\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}\rangle+D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_t)\Bigr\} \\
&\quad=
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H} D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1})
=
\operatorname{Proj}_{\mathcal{M}_H}^{\Psi}(\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}),
\end{aligned}
\]
where $\operatorname{Proj}_{\mathcal{M}_H}^{\Psi}$ is the Bregman projection of Lemma~\ref{lem:bregman-projection}.
Consequently, the OMD update \eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin} coincides exactly with
the closed-form OMD update \eqref{eq:alg-omd-mult}--\eqref{eq:alg-omd-proj},
and in particular $\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\in\mathcal{M}_H\cap\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$. Thus the positive
initialization \eqref{eq:alg-init} keeps every algorithm iterate strictly positive.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^H$ and a coordinate $i$. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-cf-log}
\begin{aligned}
\eta g_{t,i}\,m_i
+
m_i\log\frac{m_i}{m_{t,i}}
&=
m_i
\left(
\eta g_{t,i}
+
\log\frac{m_i}{m_{t,i}}
\right) \\
&=
m_i\log\frac{m_i}{\widetilde{m}_{t+1,i}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Substitution into the OMD objective gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-cf-breg}
\begin{aligned}
&\eta\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}\rangle+D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_t) \\
&\qquad=
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1})
+
\sum_{i=1}^H
\left(
m_{t,i}-\widetilde{m}_{t+1,i}
\right).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The final sum is independent of $\mathbf{m}$. Hence the two optimization problems have
the same minimizer, and Lemma~\ref{lem:bregman-projection} gives
\eqref{eq:alg-omd-proj}. Since $\mathbf{m}_t>0$ coordinatewise, both the
multiplicative step and the subsequent positive rescaling preserve strict
positivity.
\end{proof}

\section{Proofs for the Regret Analysis}
\label{sec:appendix-regret-proofs}

The following lemmas establish the OMD and stability estimates used in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound}. They rely on the state-decomposition, feasibility,
and surrogate-cost lemmas established in Section~\ref{sec:sdac}
(Lemmas~\ref{lem:sdac-state-decomp}, \ref{lem:sdac-feasibility}, and
\ref{lem:sdac-surrogate}).

\begin{lemma}[One-step movement of OMD]
\label{lem:omd-move}
For the OMD update with step size $\eta>0$ and subgradient
$\mathbf{g}_t\in\partial \ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)$,
\[
\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1 \le \eta\,\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty.
\]
In particular, since $\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\le G_\alpha$,
$\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1\le \eta G_\alpha$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First-order optimality of
\eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin}, evaluated at the feasible point $\mathbf{m}_t$, gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-movement-optimality}
\begin{aligned}
&\left\langle
\eta \mathbf{g}_t+\nabla\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1})-\nabla\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t),
\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}_{t+1}
\right\rangle
\ge0, \\
&D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\|\mathbf{m}_t)+D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}) \\
&\qquad\le
\eta\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\rangle \\
&\qquad\le
\eta\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The second line uses the symmetric Bregman identity.

For every $\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{M}_H\cap\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$ and $\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^H$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-strong-convexity}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{v}^\top\nabla^2\Psi(\mathbf{z})\mathbf{v}
&=
\sum_{i=1}^H\frac{v_i^2}{z_i} \\
&\ge
\frac{\|\mathbf{v}\|_1^2}{\sum_{i=1}^H z_i} \\
&\ge
\|\mathbf{v}\|_1^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The first inequality is Cauchy--Schwarz. The segment joining $\mathbf{m}_t$ and
$\mathbf{m}_{t+1}$ lies in $\mathcal{M}_H\cap\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:omd-closed-form}; integrating
\eqref{eq:omd-strong-convexity} along this segment yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\|\mathbf{m}_t)
&\ge
\frac12\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1^2, \\
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1})
&\ge
\frac12\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Combining these inequalities with \eqref{eq:omd-movement-optimality},
\[
\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1^2
\le
\eta\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1.
\]
The claim is immediate if $\mathbf{m}_{t+1}=\mathbf{m}_t$; otherwise divide by
$\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[OMD regret for the surrogate losses]
\label{lem:omd-surrogate-regret}
For every $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, the update \eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin} satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-surrogate-regret}
\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)
-\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
\le
\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_1)}{\eta}
+
\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{t=1}^T\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty^2.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Convexity of $\ell_t$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-convexity-step}
\begin{aligned}
\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
&\le
\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}\rangle \\
&=
\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\rangle \\
&\quad+
\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
First-order optimality of \eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin}, evaluated at
$\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-three-point-step}
\begin{aligned}
\eta\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}\rangle
&\le
\langle \nabla\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1})-\nabla\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t),\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\rangle \\
&=
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_t)
-D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}) \\
&\qquad
-D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\|\mathbf{m}_t).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The equality is the three-point Bregman identity. Substituting
\eqref{eq:omd-three-point-step} into \eqref{eq:omd-convexity-step},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-one-step-regret}
\begin{aligned}
\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
&\le
\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_t)}{\eta}
-\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1})}{\eta} \\
&\quad+
\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\rangle
\\
&\quad
-\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\|\mathbf{m}_t)}{\eta}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The strong-convexity calculation
\eqref{eq:omd-strong-convexity} and H\"older's inequality give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-young-step}
\begin{aligned}
&\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\rangle
-\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\|\mathbf{m}_t)}{\eta} \\
&\qquad\le
\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1
-\frac{\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1^2}{2\eta} \\
&\qquad\le
\frac{\eta}{2}\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The last inequality is Young's inequality. Combining
\eqref{eq:omd-one-step-regret} and \eqref{eq:omd-young-step}, and then summing
over $t$, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)
-\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
&\le
\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_1)}{\eta} \\
&\quad
-\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_{T+1})}{\eta} \\
&\quad+
\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{t=1}^T\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_{T+1})\ge0$, this proves
\eqref{eq:omd-surrogate-regret}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Positive-part representation of the dynamics]
\label{lem:positive-part-dynamics}
For $\mathbf m\in\mathcal M_H$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-contractive-transition}
F_t(x;\mathbf m)
\triangleq
\left[\alpha x-\langle\boldsymbol\phi_t^u,\mathbf m\rangle\right]_+
+w_t.
\end{equation}
Then the online state and every fixed-policy state satisfy
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:positive-part-transitions}
x_{t+1}=F_t(x_t;\mathbf m_t),
\qquad
x_{t+1}(\mathbf m)=F_t(x_t(\mathbf m);\mathbf m).
\end{equation}
Moreover, for all $x,x'\ge0$ and $\mathbf m,\mathbf m'\in\mathcal M_H$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:transition-contraction}
\begin{aligned}
&|F_t(x;\mathbf m)-F_t(x';\mathbf m')| \\
&\quad\le
\left|
\alpha(x-x')
-\langle\boldsymbol\phi_t^u,\mathbf m-\mathbf m'\rangle
\right| \\
&\quad\le
\alpha|x-x'|
+\|\boldsymbol\phi_t^u\|_\infty
\|\mathbf m-\mathbf m'\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For the online trajectory, \eqref{eq:alg-feasible-action} implies
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\alpha x_t-u_t
&=
\alpha x_t-
\min\!\left\{
\langle\boldsymbol\phi_t^u,\mathbf m_t\rangle,
\alpha x_t
\right\} \\
&=
\left[
\alpha x_t-
\langle\boldsymbol\phi_t^u,\mathbf m_t\rangle
\right]_+.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For a fixed $\mathbf m$, Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-feasibility} gives
\[
\alpha x_t(\mathbf m)
-\langle\boldsymbol\phi_t^u,\mathbf m\rangle
\ge0,
\]
so inserting a positive part does not change its state update. This proves
\eqref{eq:positive-part-transitions}. Finally, the map $z\mapsto[z]_+$ is
nonexpansive:
\[
|[z]_+-[z']_+|\le|z-z'|.
\]
Apply this inequality to the two arguments in
\eqref{eq:def-contractive-transition}, and then apply H\"older's inequality, to
obtain \eqref{eq:transition-contraction}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:contractive-deviation}]
Define
\[
d_t\triangleq|x_t-x_t(\mathbf m_t)|.
\]
Since $x_1=x_1(\mathbf m_1)=0$, one has $d_1=0$. For
$t\in\{1,\ldots,T-1\}$, Lemma~\ref{lem:positive-part-dynamics} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:contractive-deviation-start}
\begin{aligned}
d_{t+1}
&=
\left|
F_t(x_t;\mathbf m_t)
-F_t(x_t(\mathbf m_{t+1});\mathbf m_{t+1})
\right| \\
&\le
\alpha\left|x_t-x_t(\mathbf m_{t+1})\right|
+\|\boldsymbol\phi_t^u\|_\infty
\|\mathbf m_{t+1}-\mathbf m_t\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The affine state representation \eqref{eq:surr-affine-pair} implies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fixed-state-parameter-difference}
\begin{aligned}
\left|x_t(\mathbf m_t)-x_t(\mathbf m_{t+1})\right|
&=
\left|
\left\langle
\boldsymbol\phi_t^x,
\mathbf m_t-\mathbf m_{t+1}
\right\rangle
\right| \\
&\le
\|\boldsymbol\phi_t^x\|_\infty
\|\mathbf m_{t+1}-\mathbf m_t\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Combining the triangle inequality with
\eqref{eq:contractive-deviation-start} and
\eqref{eq:fixed-state-parameter-difference} yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:contractive-deviation-recursion-raw}
\begin{aligned}
d_{t+1}
&\le
\alpha d_t \\
&\quad+
\left(
\alpha\|\boldsymbol\phi_t^x\|_\infty
+\|\boldsymbol\phi_t^u\|_\infty
\right)
\|\mathbf m_{t+1}-\mathbf m_t\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:surr-coefficient-norms},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:combined-feature-bound}
\begin{aligned}
\alpha\|\boldsymbol\phi_t^x\|_\infty
+\|\boldsymbol\phi_t^u\|_\infty
&\le
\frac{\alpha^2}{1-\alpha}+\alpha \\
&=
\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Lemma~\ref{lem:omd-move} and
$\|\mathbf g_t\|_\infty\le G_\alpha$ therefore reduce
\eqref{eq:contractive-deviation-recursion-raw} to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:contractive-deviation-recursion}
d_{t+1}
\le
\alpha d_t
+\frac{\alpha\eta G_\alpha}{1-\alpha}.
\end{equation}
Unrolling from $d_1=0$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:contractive-state-unroll}
\begin{aligned}
d_t
&\le
\frac{\alpha\eta G_\alpha}{1-\alpha}
\sum_{j=0}^{t-2}\alpha^j \\
&\le
\frac{\alpha\eta G_\alpha}{(1-\alpha)^2},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with an empty sum when $t=1$. This proves
\eqref{eq:contractive-state-bound}.

Finally, $\widehat u_t=u_t(\mathbf m_t)$ and the feasibility of fixed SDAC policies imply
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:contractive-action-start}
\begin{aligned}
0\le u_t(\mathbf m_t)-u_t
&=
\left[u_t(\mathbf m_t)-\alpha x_t\right]_+ \\
&\le
\left[\alpha x_t(\mathbf m_t)-\alpha x_t\right]_+ \\
&\le
\alpha d_t.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{eq:contractive-action-start} with
\eqref{eq:contractive-state-bound} proves
\eqref{eq:contractive-action-bound}.

Both state--action pairs lie in $\mathcal{D}_\alpha$. Hence
\eqref{eq:model-lipschitz} and the two bounds just proved give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stability-one-step-short}
\begin{aligned}
&c_t(x_t,u_t)
-\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t) \\
&\qquad\le
L_x\bigl|x_t-x_t(\mathbf m_t)\bigr|
+
L_u\bigl|u_t-u_t(\mathbf{m}_t)\bigr| \\
&\qquad\le
\frac{\alpha\eta G_\alpha L_x}{(1-\alpha)^2}
+
\frac{\alpha^2\eta G_\alpha L_u}{(1-\alpha)^2} \\
&\qquad=
\frac{\alpha\eta G_\alpha(L_x+\alpha L_u)}{(1-\alpha)^2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Summing \eqref{eq:stability-one-step-short} over $t=1,\ldots,T$ proves
\eqref{eq:stability-sum-bound}.
\end{proof}



\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound}]
Define the stability term
\[
\operatorname{Stab}_T
\triangleq
\sum_{t=1}^T
\left(
c_t(x_t,u_t)
-\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)
\right).
\]
Using
$\ell_t(\mathbf{m})=c_t(x_t(\mathbf{m}),u_t(\mathbf{m}))$, the regret decomposes as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:regret-decomp}
\begin{aligned}
\RegSDAC{T}
&=
\operatorname{Stab}_T
+
\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)
-
\min_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H}\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

We first bound the initial Bregman divergence. Fix $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$. Since
$\mathbf{m}_1=\frac{1}{H+1}\mathbf{1}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:breg-init-expand}
\begin{aligned}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_1)
&=
\sum_{i=1}^H
m_i\log\bigl((H+1)m_i\bigr) \\
&\quad
-\|\mathbf{m}\|_1+\frac{H}{H+1} \\
&=
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\log m_i
+\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\log(H+1) \\
&\quad
-\|\mathbf{m}\|_1+\frac{H}{H+1}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Because $z\log z\le0$ for $z\in[0,1]$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:breg-init-affine}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_1)
\le
\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\log(H+1)-\|\mathbf{m}\|_1+\frac{H}{H+1}.
\end{equation}
The right-hand side is affine in $\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\in[0,1]$, and its values at the
endpoints are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{H}{H+1}
&\le
\log(H+1), \\
\log(H+1)-\frac{1}{H+1}
&\le
\log(H+1).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here the first inequality follows from
$\log z\ge1-z^{-1}$ for $z\ge1$. Therefore,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:breg-init-bound}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_1)
\le
\log(H+1)
\qquad
\text{for every }\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H.
\end{equation}

Lemma~\ref{lem:omd-surrogate-regret},
$\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\le G_\alpha$, and \eqref{eq:breg-init-bound} imply, for every
$\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-ell-raw}
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{t=1}^T\bigl(\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-\ell_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr)
&\le
\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_1)}{\eta}
+
\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{t=1}^T\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty^2 \\
&\le
\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta}
+
\frac{\eta G_\alpha^2T}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Since \eqref{eq:omd-ell-raw} holds uniformly over $\mathbf{m}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-term}
\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)
-
\min_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H}\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
\le
\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta}
+
\frac{\eta G_\alpha^2T}{2}.
\end{equation}
The stability bound \eqref{eq:stability-sum-bound} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stab-term}
\operatorname{Stab}_T
\le
\frac{\alpha\eta T G_\alpha(L_x+\alpha L_u)}{(1-\alpha)^2}.
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:omd-term} and \eqref{eq:stab-term} into
\eqref{eq:regret-decomp},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:regret-eta}
\begin{aligned}
\RegSDAC{T}
&\le
\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta}
+\frac{\eta T G_\alpha^2}{2} \\
&\quad
+\frac{\alpha\eta T G_\alpha(L_x+\alpha L_u)}{(1-\alpha)^2} \\
&=
\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta}
+\eta T\Gamma_\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Differentiating with respect to $\eta$ yields
\[
-\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta^2}
+
T\Gamma_\alpha.
\]
The unique minimizer is therefore
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:eta-star}
\eta^*
=
\sqrt{
\frac{\log(H+1)}
{T\Gamma_\alpha}
}.
\end{equation}
At this value the two summands in \eqref{eq:regret-eta} are equal, so
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:regret-opt}
\RegSDAC{T}
\le
2\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha T\log(H+1)}.
\end{equation}

It remains to verify the dependence on $\alpha$. As $\alpha\downarrow0$,
\begin{align*}
\frac{G_\alpha}{\alpha}
&=
\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}+L_u
\longrightarrow L_x+L_u, \\
\frac{\Gamma_\alpha}{\alpha^2}
&=
\frac12\left(\frac{G_\alpha}{\alpha}\right)^2
+
\frac{G_\alpha}{\alpha}
\frac{L_x+\alpha L_u}{(1-\alpha)^2} \\
&\longrightarrow
\frac{(L_x+L_u)^2}{2}+L_x(L_x+L_u)>0.
\end{align*}
Therefore $\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha}=\Theta(\alpha)$ as
$\alpha\downarrow0$. As $\alpha\uparrow1$, write
$\varepsilon=1-\alpha$. Then
\begin{align*}
\varepsilon G_\alpha
&=
\alpha(L_x+\varepsilon L_u)
\longrightarrow L_x, \\
\varepsilon^3\Gamma_\alpha
&=
\frac{\varepsilon^3G_\alpha^2}{2}
+\alpha(\varepsilon G_\alpha)(L_x+\alpha L_u) \\
&\longrightarrow
L_x(L_x+L_u)>0.
\end{align*}
Thus
$\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha}=\Theta((1-\alpha)^{-3/2})$ as
$\alpha\uparrow1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:regret-vs-allocation}]
Fix $\mathbf q\in\mathcal Q_\alpha^\downarrow$ and use the SDAC comparator
$\mathbf m_{H_T}(\mathbf q)$ from
Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-allocation-approx}. Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound} and
\eqref{eq:allocation-cost-gap} give
\[
\begin{aligned}
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}-J_T(\mathbf q)
&\le
2\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha T\log(H_T+1)}\\
&\quad+
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)
\operatorname{Tail}_{H_T}(\mathbf q)T\\
&\le
2\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha T\log(H_T+1)}\\
&\quad+
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)
\alpha^{H_T+1}T.
\end{aligned}
\]
The bound is uniform in $\mathbf q$, so taking the infimum comparator cost proves
\eqref{eq:allocation-regret-bound}. Finally,
$H_T\ge\log T/\log(1/\alpha)$ implies $\alpha^{H_T}\le1/T$, while
$H_T=\Theta(\log T)$ implies $\log(H_T+1)=O(\log\log T)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:regret-vs-linear}]
For fixed $k$, Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound} applied to
$\mathbf m_{H_T}^{\mathrm{prop}}(k)$, followed by \eqref{eq:sdac-lin-approx-cost}, gives
\eqref{eq:linear-regret-decomposition}.
Corollary~\ref{cor:proportional-as-allocation} shows that the geometric allocation
sequence $\mathbf q(k)$ belongs to $\mathcal Q_\alpha^\downarrow$, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-allocation-approx} gives
$\operatorname{Tail}_{H_T}(\mathbf q(k))\le\alpha^{H_T+1}$. This makes the bound
uniform in $k$; minimizing over $k$ proves the stated finite-time and asymptotic
guarantees.
\end{proof}
