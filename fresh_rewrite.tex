\begin{abstract}
We study online control of a scalar storage system with nonnegative adversarial
resource arrivals and state-dependent action constraints. The adversarial,
time-varying cost depends on both state and action. At each time, the
controller selects a feasible action before the current resource arrival and cost
are revealed. To handle the coupling between feasibility and online learning,
we introduce \emph{Simplex Disturbance-Action Control} (SDAC), whose policies
have $H$ nonnegative parameters summing to at most one. Every fixed SDAC policy
is feasible by construction. Our online SDAC controller updates these
parameters using entropic online mirror descent while preserving feasibility along
the time-varying trajectory. We prove a regret bound of
$O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log(H+1)}\right)$ relative to the best fixed SDAC policy, and prove a
lower bound showing that the dependence on $T$ is tight for fixed $H$. We also introduce
infinite-memory SDAC policies.
SDAC policies approximate this class with an error that decreases geometrically
with $H$. For a suitable choice $H=\Theta(\log T)$,
the resulting regret against infinite-memory SDAC policies is
$O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log\log T}\right)$. The framework applies to energy-harvesting
batteries and other storage-constrained resource systems.
\end{abstract}

Online control, disturbance-action policies, storage systems, energy harvesting,

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{Motivation and Problem Setting}
\IEEEPARstart{M}{any} resource-allocation systems have a storage state that changes
over time and limits the control action at each time. An action that yields an immediate
benefit or reward may leave less resource for future decisions. On the other hand, an
overly conservative resource allocation policy can miss many opportunities for reward. The
controller must balance these effects
while satisfying the action constraint at every time.

We study this problem for a scalar storage system that loses a fixed fraction of
its stored resource each period.
For example, an imperfect battery can lose some of its charge even if it is not used.
At each time $t$, the controller observes the current resource state $x_t$ and chooses an amount $u_t$ to use.
This incurs a cost that depends on both $x_t$ and $u_t$. Rewards can be modeled as negative costs.
The cost $c_t(x_t, u_t)$ is revealed \emph{after} the decision $u_t$ is made.
The cost sequence and the sequence of new resource arrivals may
be chosen adversarially, so we do not assume a known
probability model. Our goal is to construct an online controller whose
cumulative cost is close to that of the best SDAC policy, chosen in hindsight,
from a specified benchmark class.

One difficulty is that the current action affects the next state. In online control,
\emph{Disturbance-Action Control} (DAC) parameterizes actions using a linear filter
of past disturbances, enabling efficient algorithms with bounded regret.
In our storage model, however, some DAC policies can produce negative actions or
actions larger than the available resource.

We address this issue by introducing \emph{Simplex Disturbance-Action Control}
(SDAC). SDAC restricts the filter parameters to a subprobability simplex and includes
powers of the retention coefficient in the filter. These choices ensure that
every fixed SDAC policy satisfies the action constraint. The state and action
along each SDAC-policy trajectory are also affine in the policy parameter.
Thus, a convex state--action cost is convex in that parameter, allowing
updates through online convex optimization.

\subsection{Model and Information Pattern}

The storage state $x_t\ge0$ evolves according to
\[
x_{t+1}=\alpha x_t-u_t+w_t,
\]
where $\alpha\in(0,1)$ is a fixed retention coefficient, $u_t$ is the control
action, and $w_t\in[0,1]$ is a nonnegative resource arrival. The action must satisfy
\[
0\le u_t\le\alpha x_t.
\]
Thus, the controller cannot use more resource than remains after storage loss.

At time $t$, the controller chooses $u_t$ using the current state and the
information available through time $t-1$. The arrival $w_t$ and the convex cost
function $c_t$ are revealed after the action is selected, and the controller incurs
the cost $c_t(x_t,u_t)$. Both the online trajectory and every SDAC-policy
trajectory used as a benchmark must satisfy the same dynamics and action
constraint. The formal model and assumptions are given in
Section~\ref{sec:model-setup}.

Our main application is energy-harvesting battery management, where $x_t$ is the
battery state of charge, $w_t$ is the harvested energy, $u_t$ is the energy
used, and $1-\alpha$ is the fraction of stored energy lost per period.
Similar dynamics arise in perishable inventory systems, water reservoirs with
storage loss, and queues with abandonment. In many standard queueing models,
$\alpha=1$; that case is outside our main results and is discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:extensions}.


\subsection{Contributions}

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Policy class.}
    We introduce SDAC policies parameterized by a
    subprobability simplex. We prove that every such SDAC policy is feasible
    for every arrival sequence with entries in $[0,1]$.

    We also introduce infinite-memory SDAC policies in
    Section~\ref{subsec:infinite-memory-sdac}. Their parameter domain extends the
    subprobability simplex to nonnegative sequences whose sum is at most one.
    This class contains every SDAC policy and every feasible
    fixed-fraction policy $u_t=kx_t$ with $k\in[0,\alpha]$.
    Truncating the parameter sequence gives an SDAC policy whose
    approximation error decreases geometrically with the memory horizon $H$.

\item \textbf{Online control and regret.}
    We develop an online SDAC controller that updates its parameter using
    entropic online mirror descent (OMD) over the subprobability simplex.
    We prove that the controller remains feasible and achieves
    $O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log(H+1)}\right)$
    regret against the best fixed SDAC policy.
    For a suitable choice $H=\Theta(\log T)$, the regret against
    infinite-memory SDAC policies is
    $O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log\log T}\right)$. The same bound
    holds against the best fixed-fraction policy.

\item \textbf{Lower bound.}
    We prove a lower bound of order $\sqrt{T}$ for the expected regret of every
    causal feasible controller, including randomized controllers; see
    Theorem~\ref{thm:sdac-lower-bound}. When $T$ is at least on the order of
    $(1-\alpha)^{-1}$, the lower bound scales as
    $\Omega((1-\alpha)^{-1}\sqrt{T})$ near $\alpha=1$.
    The upper bound contains a factor of $\sqrt{\log(H+1)}$ that does not
    appear in the lower bound. The bounds also differ by a factor of
    $(1-\alpha)^{-1/2}$ in their dependence on $1-\alpha$.
    Subsequent work closes these gaps for the same policy class
    \cite{asgari2026cumulative}.
\end{itemize}


\subsection{Related Work}

\paragraph{Online control}
Goulart et al.\ use a parameterization similar to DAC for a related constrained
control problem under bounded disturbances \cite{goulart2006optimization}.
Agarwal et al.\ use DAC policies to obtain $\widetilde O(\sqrt{T})$ regret
against strongly stable linear controllers \cite{agarwal2019online}.
Golowich et al.\ use nonnegative, normalized DAC parameters to control
population dynamics \cite{golowich2024population}. They use entropic mirror
descent and bound the additional cost caused by changing the parameters
over time. Their state lies in a probability simplex, and their feasible
action set does not depend on the current state. In SDAC, the parameters
distribute arrivals across future allocations, with powers of the retention
coefficient accounting for storage loss. This construction ensures that each
fixed policy satisfies $0\le u_t\le\alpha x_t$.

Li et al.\ study separate fixed affine constraints on states and actions
\cite{li2021onlineConstrained}. They assume that a linear controller satisfies
the constraints with a positive margin, and project DAC updates onto a
restricted parameter set to preserve feasibility. Such a margin need not
exist here: when $x_t=0$, feasibility requires $u_t=0$.
Liu et al.\ study fixed
and adversarial time-varying convex constraints on the state and action
\cite{liu2023constrainedControl}. They bound constraint violations and also
give a projection method that satisfies fixed affine constraints at every
time. Our constraint $0\le u_t\le\alpha x_t$ is also fixed and affine.
The clipping rule \eqref{eq:alg-feasible-action} preserves feasibility when
the SDAC parameters change online.

Online convex optimization with memory studies costs that depend on past
decisions \cite{anava2015memory,kumar2023unbounded}. A standard approach
first analyzes the cost of repeatedly using the current decision, then
bounds the additional cost caused by changing decisions over time.
Our surrogate costs use the state and action obtained by keeping the current
SDAC parameter fixed from the start. Lemma~\ref{lem:contractive-deviation}
bounds their difference from the actual costs using storage decay and
accounting for action clipping. Kumar et al.\ also derive lower bounds when
the influence of past decisions decreases geometrically
\cite{kumar2023unbounded}. Section~\ref{sec:regret-lower-bound} gives a related
lower bound for causal feasible controllers, with fixed SDAC policies
evaluated on their own trajectories.


\paragraph{Energy harvesting}
Our infinite-memory SDAC policies are motivated by fixed-fraction policies
for stochastic energy arrivals. Shaviv and \"Ozg\"ur
\cite{shaviv2016universally} prove constant-gap throughput guarantees for
fixed-fraction policies under i.i.d.\ arrivals. Arafa et al.\
\cite{arafa2018online} extend the analysis to increasing concave rewards and
characterize optimal allocations under Bernoulli arrivals, where each
nonzero arrival fully recharges the battery. For the same arrival model,
Zibaeenejad and Chen \cite{zibaeenejad2019lookahead} study optimal allocations
when arrivals within a fixed future window are known.
For the storage dynamics studied here, Corollary~\ref{cor:fraction-as-sdac}
proves that infinite-memory SDAC contains every feasible fixed-fraction
policy.

Yu and Neely \cite{Yu2019} study online energy allocation with delayed
information about the wireless state. Their results allow wireless states
that need not be i.i.d.\ and independent energy arrivals with a constant
mean. Asgari and Neely
\cite{10.1145/3428337} obtain $O(\sqrt{T})$ expected regret for fixed action
dimension using $O(\sqrt{T})$ battery capacity, with i.i.d.\ energy arrivals
and time-varying convex costs satisfying their independence assumptions. In that
work, the current arrival is known before allocation and the cost is revealed
afterward. Both works compare expected performance to fixed actions whose
energy use does not exceed the mean energy arrival. These fixed actions need
not satisfy the battery constraint on every realized arrival sequence.
Their objectives do not depend directly on the battery state. Our fixed
SDAC policies satisfy the action constraint on their own trajectories,
and their actions vary with the arrival history.

Lin et al.\ \cite{lin2025optimal} study online energy allocation with
arbitrary arrivals and a class of time-varying increasing concave rewards. They give
competitive-ratio guarantees against an offline optimum with the same
battery-capacity and allocation-rate constraints. The current arrival and
reward function are known before allocation.
Our setting combines adversarial arrivals and costs,
decisions before the current arrival and cost are revealed, and convex costs
depending on both storage state and action. The benchmark is a fixed SDAC
policy evaluated on its own induced trajectory.

\paragraph{Storage management}
Qin et al.\ study storage control with energy loss, capacity constraints,
and limits on charging and discharging \cite{qin2016online}. They observe
the current price and net demand before acting and bound the difference
between their long-run expected average cost and the optimal value under
stochastic assumptions. Kim et al.\ study energy allocation with storage
loss and capacity constraints \cite{kim2016storage}. They give competitive
guarantees for the value of meeting demand. Their result that includes
production costs assumes shared storage and demands known in advance.
Both works account for a prescribed storage capacity. Our analysis assumes
sufficient capacity to avoid overflow and studies finite-horizon regret
under adversarial arrivals and costs revealed after the action.

\paragraph{Queueing and network optimization}
Classical queueing control studies throughput and queue stability
\cite{tassiulas1992stability,tassiulas1993dynamic,mckeown1999achieving}, while
stochastic network optimization studies tradeoffs between time-average performance
and average queue backlog \cite{neely2010stochastic}.

Khojastepour and Sabharwal \cite{khojastepour2004delay} study scheduling
with a delay constraint using finite-memory filters of current and past
arrivals. Their fixed filters have nonnegative coefficients summing to one.
Under i.i.d.\ arrivals, equal coefficients minimize long-run average
transmit power over a Gaussian channel within this class. They also develop
a time-varying policy that does not require the arrival distribution.
SDAC uses nonnegative parameters summing to at most one, together with
powers of the retention coefficient, and acts before the current arrival
is observed. We study regret against a fixed SDAC policy under adversarial
convex costs depending on both state and action.

\paragraph{Inventory and reservoir management}
Yang et al.\ \cite{yang2020online} study online inventory control with
linear costs and capacity and rate constraints. They obtain optimal
competitive ratios relative to an offline optimum.
They observe the current demand and price before acting, whereas our controller
acts before the current arrival and cost are revealed.
Hihat et al.\ study inventory control with demands that need not be i.i.d.\
\cite{hihat2023inventory}. They prove feasibility and $O(\sqrt{T})$ expected
regret against the best fixed target for the inventory level after ordering.
Their guarantee requires a uniformly positive conditional probability,
given the past, that all product demands are at least a fixed positive amount.
Our arrival sequences have no such probability assumption, and our benchmark
is a fixed SDAC policy. Hihat and Fermanian
\cite{hihat2024online} learn policies for inventory systems with multiple
products, capacity constraints, and delays between ordering and delivery.
They evaluate this more general method empirically and leave its regret
guarantees open. Classical reservoir theory studies storage
under random arrivals and specified rules for resource use \cite{moran1954dams}.

\paragraph{Subsequent work}
Asgari and Neely \cite{asgari2026cumulative} use a different parameterization
and update rule for the same SDAC policy class. They remove the dependence
on $H$ from the regret bound and obtain $O(\sqrt{T})$ regret against
infinite-memory SDAC policies for fixed retention and cost constants.
They also give upper and lower bounds with matching dependence on the
horizon and retention coefficient for $\alpha\in[1/2,1)$ and $T\ge4$,
with fixed positive cost constants.

\section{Model and Regret Benchmark}
\label{sec:model-setup}

For every positive integer $n$, we write
$[n]\triangleq\{1,\ldots,n\}$. For every $z\in\mathbb{R}$, we define
$[z]_+\triangleq\max\{z,0\}$. For
$\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^n$, the $i$-th coordinate is denoted by $v_i$. If the
vector already carries a subscript, the coordinate is appended after a comma: the
$i$-th coordinate of $\mathbf{v}_t$ is $v_{t,i}$. We write
$\mathbf{1}$ for the all-ones vector, with dimension clear from context, and
$\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ for the Euclidean inner product. We use $\log$ for the
natural logarithm.

Fix a positive integer $T$ and a known retention coefficient
$\alpha\in(0,1)$. The state $x_t\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}$ is the resource level at the
beginning of time $t$. The system starts empty:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-init}
x_1=0.
\end{equation}
At each time $t\in[T]$, the controller chooses an action
$u_t\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-feasibility}
0\le u_t\le\alpha x_t.
\end{equation}
The system then receives an arrival $w_t\in[0,1]$ and evolves according to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-dynamics}
x_{t+1}=\alpha x_t-u_t+w_t.
\end{equation}
Here, $1-\alpha$ is the fraction of stored resource lost per period.
The constraint \eqref{eq:model-feasibility} ensures that the action does not exceed
the amount remaining after this loss.

In the online-control literature, $w_t$ is commonly called a disturbance. Here, we
call it an \emph{arrival} because it is nonnegative, while retaining the standard
name Disturbance-Action Control. Nonnegativity is important for both the storage
interpretation and the
feasibility properties of SDAC.

At time $t$, the controller chooses $u_t$ before observing $w_t$ or $c_t$. After the
action is selected, the arrival and the entire cost function are revealed, the
controller incurs $c_t(x_t,u_t)$, and the state is updated according to
\eqref{eq:model-dynamics}.

For every feasible trajectory, \eqref{eq:model-feasibility},
\eqref{eq:model-dynamics}, and $w_t\in[0,1]$ give
\[
0\le x_{t+1}\le\alpha x_t+1.
\]
Since $x_1=0$, induction gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-state-bound}
\begin{aligned}
0\le x_t
&\le\sum_{s=0}^{t-2}\alpha^s
=\frac{1-\alpha^{t-1}}{1-\alpha}\\
&\le\frac{1}{1-\alpha},
\qquad t\in[T+1].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Together with \eqref{eq:model-feasibility}, this bound places every feasible
state--action pair in the compact set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-domain}
\mathcal{D}_\alpha
\triangleq
\left\{
(x,u):
0\le x\le\frac{1}{1-\alpha},
0\le u\le\alpha x
\right\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{remark}[Storage capacity]
A storage capacity of $1/(1-\alpha)$ suffices to avoid overflow.
\end{remark}

Each revealed cost function $c_t:\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^2\to\mathbb{R}$ is convex in
$(x,u)$ and satisfies the following Lipschitz condition on $\mathcal{D}_\alpha$:
there are constants $L_x,L_u>0$ such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-lipschitz}
|c_t(x,u)-c_t(x',u')|
\le
L_x|x-x'|+L_u|u-u'|
\end{equation}
for all $(x,u),(x',u')\in\mathcal{D}_\alpha$.
At every $(x,u)\in\mathcal{D}_\alpha$, we also assume access to a cost subgradient
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-cost-subgradient}
\boldsymbol{\zeta}_t(x,u)
=
\bigl(\zeta_t^x(x,u),\zeta_t^u(x,u)\bigr)
\in\partial c_t(x,u)
\end{equation}
satisfying
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-subgradient-bounds}
|\zeta_t^x(x,u)|\le L_x,
\qquad
|\zeta_t^u(x,u)|\le L_u.
\end{equation}
These subgradient bounds hold, for example, when $c_t$ has a convex
extension to an open neighborhood of $\mathcal{D}_\alpha$ satisfying
\eqref{eq:model-lipschitz}; see
\cite[Thm.~9.13]{rockafellar1998variational}.

We use $x_t$ and $u_t$ without parameter arguments for the trajectory generated by
the online controller. Its cumulative cost is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-cumulative-cost}
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}
\triangleq
\sum_{t=1}^T c_t(x_t,u_t).
\end{equation}
The superscript $\mathrm{on}$ stands for online.


Section~\ref{sec:sdac} defines a class of SDAC policies characterized by a
parameter $\mathbf{m}$ in a parameter set $\mathcal{M}_H$.
For a fixed $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, the corresponding \emph{SDAC-policy
trajectory} is the state--action
sequence $\{(x_t(\mathbf{m}),u_t(\mathbf{m}))\}_{t=1}^T$ obtained by applying
the SDAC policy with parameter $\mathbf{m}$ at every time, starting from
$x_1(\mathbf{m})=0$.
Its cumulative cost is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-sdac-cumulative-cost}
J_T(\mathbf{m})
\triangleq
\sum_{t=1}^T c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{m}),u_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr).
\end{equation}
The online policy in \eqref{eq:def-cumulative-cost} and the fixed SDAC policy in
\eqref{eq:def-sdac-cumulative-cost} are evaluated on the same arrival sequence
$\{w_t\}_{t=1}^T$ and cost function sequence $\{c_t\}_{t=1}^T$.
For a fixed positive integer $H$, the regret against the best fixed
SDAC policy is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-regret}
\RegSDAC{T}(H)
\triangleq
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}
-\min_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H}J_T(\mathbf{m}).
\end{equation}
Our objective is to satisfy \eqref{eq:model-feasibility} at every time and achieve
sublinear regret.

\section{Simplex Disturbance-Action Control (SDAC)}
\label{sec:sdac}

\subsection{From Disturbance-Action Control to SDAC}
DAC uses a finite linear filter of past disturbances. Under standard conditions, this
parameterization yields convex surrogate costs and efficient online updates. In
our scalar storage model, DAC has the following form:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dac-param}
u_t = \sum_{i=1}^{H} \theta_i w_{t-i},
\end{equation}
where $H$ is a positive integer (the memory horizon) and
$\boldsymbol{\theta}=(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_H)^\top\in\mathbb{R}^H$ is the vector
of filter coefficients.

For our constrained model, an unrestricted filter does not ensure feasibility: its
action can exceed $\alpha x_t$ or become negative.
We therefore introduce \emph{Simplex Disturbance-Action Control} (SDAC), which uses a
simplex-constrained filter tailored to the storage dynamics. This restriction makes
every fixed SDAC policy feasible while keeping surrogate costs convex and online
updates efficient.


\subsection{Policies and Structural Properties}
Fix a memory horizon $H\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}$ and define the subprobability simplex
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-simplex}
\mathcal{M}_H
\triangleq
\left\{
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{R}^H:
m_i\ge0,\quad
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\le1
\right\}.
\end{equation}
The inequalities $m_i\ge0$ hold for every $i\in[H]$.
We write $\|\mathbf{m}\|_1=\sum_{i=1}^H|m_i|$ for the $\ell_1$ norm.
With the convention $w_\tau\equiv0$ for $\tau\le0$, each
$\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$ defines an SDAC policy through
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-sdac-policy}
u_t(\mathbf{m})
\;\triangleq\;
\sum_{i=1}^{H} m_i\alpha^i w_{t-i}.
\end{equation}
Here, $m_i\alpha^i w_s$ is the amount from arrival $w_s$ used $i$ periods later.

The following state decomposition yields two structural properties: every
fixed SDAC policy satisfies the feasibility constraint
\eqref{eq:model-feasibility}, and its induced cost is convex and Lipschitz in
$\mathbf{m}$. Omitted proofs appear in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-sdac-proofs}.

\begin{lemma}[State decomposition under SDAC]
\label{lem:sdac-state-decomp}
Fix a positive integer $H$.
For $t\in[T]$ and $i\in[H]$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-arrival-sums}
\begin{aligned}
r_t(i)
&\triangleq
\sum_{j=1}^{i}\alpha^{j-1}w_{t-j},
\\
r_t
&\triangleq
\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}\alpha^{j-1}w_{t-j},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where the empty sum defining $r_1$ is zero. Then, for every
$\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$ and $t\in[T]$, the SDAC-policy state associated
with $\mathbf{m}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-state-decomp}
x_t(\mathbf{m})
=
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i r_t(i)
+\bigl(1-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\bigr)r_t.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

By \eqref{eq:sdac-state-decomp} and \eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy}, the state and
action of an SDAC policy can be written together as
\[
\begin{aligned}
x_t(\mathbf{m})&=r_t+\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x,\mathbf{m}\rangle,\\
u_t(\mathbf{m})&=\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}\rangle,
\end{aligned}
\]
where the coefficient vectors in $\mathbb{R}^H$ are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-phi-u}
\begin{aligned}
\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x
&\triangleq\bigl(r_t(1)-r_t,\ldots,r_t(H)-r_t\bigr)^\top,\\
\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u
&\triangleq\bigl(\alpha w_{t-1},\alpha^2 w_{t-2},\ldots,\alpha^H w_{t-H}\bigr)^\top.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The superscripts $x$ and $u$ identify the coefficients for the state and action,
respectively. Both vectors and $r_t$ depend only on past arrivals and $\alpha$,
so they are independent of $\mathbf{m}$. Thus, both the state and action are
affine in $\mathbf{m}$.

\begin{lemma}[Feasibility of SDAC policies]
\label{lem:sdac-feasibility}
For every $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, the fixed SDAC policy
indexed by $\mathbf{m}$ is feasible:
\[
0\le u_t(\mathbf{m})\le \alpha x_t(\mathbf{m})
\qquad \text{for every }t\in[T].
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $t\in[T]$. By \eqref{eq:def-arrival-sums}, for every $i\in[H]$,
\[
\alpha r_t(i)-\alpha^i w_{t-i}
=\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha^j w_{t-j}
\ge0.
\]
Since $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, we have $m_i\ge0$ for every $i\in[H]$ and
$1-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\ge0$. Also, $w_{t-i}\ge0$ for every $i\in[H]$ and
$r_t\ge0$. Therefore, using \eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy},
\begin{align*}
0\le u_t(\mathbf{m})
&=\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\alpha^i w_{t-i} \\
&\overset{\mathrm{(A)}}{\le}\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\alpha r_t(i) \\
&\overset{\mathrm{(B)}}{\le}\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\alpha r_t(i)
+\alpha\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\right)r_t \\
&=\alpha x_t(\mathbf{m}).
\end{align*}
Step~(A) multiplies
$\alpha^i w_{t-i}\le\alpha r_t(i)$ by $m_i\ge0$ and sums over $i$.
Step~(B) adds the nonnegative term
$\alpha(1-\sum_i m_i)r_t$. The final equality uses
\eqref{eq:sdac-state-decomp}.
\end{proof}


\begin{lemma}[Convexity and Lipschitz continuity of the surrogate cost]
\label{lem:sdac-surrogate}
For $t\in[T]$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-surrogate-cost}
\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
\triangleq
c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{m}),u_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr),
\qquad
\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-surrogate-G}
G_\alpha
\triangleq
\alpha\left(\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}+L_u\right).
\end{equation}
Then $\ell_t$ is convex on $\mathcal{M}_H$ and, for all
$\mathbf{m},\mathbf{m}'\in\mathcal{M}_H$,
\[
|\ell_t(\mathbf{m})-\ell_t(\mathbf{m}')|
\le
G_\alpha\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\|_1.
\]
For $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, use \eqref{eq:model-cost-subgradient} to define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-subgradient}
\begin{aligned}
(\zeta_t^x,\zeta_t^u)
&=
\boldsymbol{\zeta}_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{m}),u_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr), \\
\mathbf{g}_t
&\triangleq
\zeta_t^x\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x
+\zeta_t^u\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Then
\[
\mathbf{g}_t\in\partial\ell_t(\mathbf{m}),
\qquad
\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\le G_\alpha.
\]
\end{lemma}

\subsection{Approximation of Infinite-Memory SDAC Policies}
\label{subsec:infinite-memory-sdac}

Define the infinite-memory parameter domain
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-infinite-simplex}
\mathcal{M}_\infty
\triangleq
\left\{
\mathbf{m}=(m_i)_{i\ge1}:m_i\ge0,\quad
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}m_i\le1
\right\}.
\end{equation}
Each $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_\infty$ defines an infinite-memory SDAC policy
through
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-infinite-sdac-policy}
u_t(\mathbf{m})
\triangleq
\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}m_i\alpha^i w_{t-i}.
\end{equation}
The state $x_t(\mathbf{m})$ follows \eqref{eq:model-dynamics} with initial
condition \eqref{eq:model-init}.
We use \eqref{eq:def-sdac-cumulative-cost} to define $J_T(\mathbf{m})$ for
$\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_\infty$ as well.
After extending vectors in $\mathcal{M}_H$ by zeros, SDAC policies with horizon $H$
are exactly the infinite-memory SDAC policies with $m_i=0$ for every $i>H$.

\begin{lemma}[Approximation of infinite-memory SDAC policies]
\label{lem:sdac-truncation-approx}
Every infinite-memory SDAC policy is feasible for every arrival sequence with
entries in $[0,1]$. Fix $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_\infty$ and an integer $H\ge1$,
and define its truncation by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-sdac-truncation}
\mathbf{m}_H(\mathbf{m})\triangleq(m_1,\ldots,m_H)^\top.
\end{equation}
Then $\mathbf{m}_H(\mathbf{m})\in\mathcal{M}_H$. If each $c_t$ satisfies
\eqref{eq:model-lipschitz}, then, for every such arrival sequence,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-truncation-cost-gap}
\begin{aligned}
&\left|J_T\bigl(\mathbf{m}_H(\mathbf{m})\bigr)-J_T(\mathbf{m})\right| \\
&\qquad\le
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)\alpha^{H+1}T.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

A \emph{fixed-fraction policy}, parameterized by $k\in[0,\alpha]$, chooses
$u_t=kx_t$ at every time. We denote its trajectory by
$\{(x_t(k),u_t(k))\}_{t=1}^T$, where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-fraction-trajectory}
\begin{aligned}
u_t(k)&=kx_t(k),\qquad x_1(k)=0,\\
x_{t+1}(k)&=(\alpha-k)x_t(k)+w_t.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Since $\alpha-k\ge0$ and $w_t\ge0$, induction gives $x_t(k)\ge0$.
Thus, $0\le u_t(k)=kx_t(k)\le\alpha x_t(k)$, so every such policy is feasible.

\begin{corollary}[Fixed-fraction policies as infinite-memory SDAC policies]
\label{cor:fraction-as-sdac}
For $k\in[0,\alpha]$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-fraction-parameter}
m_i(k)=\frac{k}{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{k}{\alpha}\right)^{i-1},
\qquad i\ge1,
\end{equation}
where $0^0=1$.
Then $\mathbf{m}(k)\in\mathcal{M}_\infty$ and, for every arrival sequence with
entries in $[0,1]$, the infinite-memory SDAC policy indexed by $\mathbf{m}(k)$
coincides with the feasible fixed-fraction policy $u_t(k)=kx_t(k)$:
\[
\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{m}(k)),u_t(\mathbf{m}(k))\bigr)
=\bigl(x_t(k),u_t(k)\bigr),
\qquad t\in[T].
\]
\end{corollary}

\section{Online SDAC Controller}
\label{sec:online-algorithm}

The controller maintains a parameter $\mathbf{m}_t\in\mathcal{M}_H$ and updates
it by OMD \cite{hazan2016introduction} with step size $\eta>0$ and unnormalized
negentropy:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-negentropy}
\Psi(\mathbf{m}) \triangleq \sum_{i=1}^H\bigl(m_i\log m_i-m_i\bigr), \qquad
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^H,
\end{equation}
where $0\log0\triangleq0$. For $\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^H$ and
$\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$, the associated Bregman divergence is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-bregman}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{v})
\triangleq
\sum_{i=1}^H\Bigl(m_i\log\tfrac{m_i}{v_i}-m_i+v_i\Bigr).
\end{equation}
Initialize the online parameter as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-init}
\mathbf{m}_1\triangleq\frac{1}{H+1}\mathbf{1}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Action selection}
At time $t$, the controller uses $\mathbf{m}_t$, initialized by
\eqref{eq:alg-init} for $t=1$ and produced by the OMD update at time $t-1$
for $t\ge2$. The feasibility guarantee for an SDAC policy uses the same parameter at every
time. When $\mathbf{m}_t$ varies, the
nominal action $\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\rangle$ can exceed
$\alpha x_t$. The controller therefore selects
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-feasible-action}
u_t
\triangleq
\min\!\left\{
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\rangle,
\alpha x_t
\right\}.
\end{equation}
The state then evolves according to \eqref{eq:model-dynamics}.
Since $\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u$ and $\mathbf{m}_t$ are componentwise nonnegative,
$\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\rangle\ge0$. If $x_t\ge0$, then
\eqref{eq:alg-feasible-action} gives $0\le u_t\le\alpha x_t$, and
\eqref{eq:model-dynamics} gives $x_{t+1}\ge w_t\ge0$. Since $x_1=0$, induction
shows that the online trajectory is feasible.

Lemma~\ref{lem:availability-projection} shows that the $D_\Psi$-projection of
$\mathbf{m}_t$ onto the parameters compatible with $x_t$ induces exactly the
clipped action $u_t$.
\paragraph{Surrogate cost and OMD update}
After $w_t$ and $c_t$ are revealed, the controller evaluates the subgradient
$\mathbf{g}_t$ in \eqref{eq:surr-subgradient} at $\mathbf{m}_t$ and sets
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-omd-argmin}
\mathbf{m}_{t+1}
\triangleq
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H}
\left\{
\eta\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}\rangle
+D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_t)
\right\}.
\end{equation}
Lemma~\ref{lem:omd-closed-form} gives a two-step implementation. First, take the
coordinatewise multiplicative step
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-omd-mult}
\widetilde{m}_{t+1,i}
\triangleq
m_{t,i}\exp\!\bigl(-\eta g_{t,i}\bigr),
\qquad
i\in[H].
\end{equation}
Next, project onto $\mathcal{M}_H$ by rescaling if needed:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-omd-proj}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{m}_{t+1}
&=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}) \\
&=\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}}
{\max\{1,\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}\|_1\}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}


\section{Regret Upper Bounds}
\label{sec:regret-analysis}
The next theorem bounds the regret of the online SDAC controller against the
best fixed SDAC policy. Proofs appear in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-regret-proofs}.

\begin{theorem}[Regret against fixed SDAC policies]
\label{thm:regret-bound}
Suppose the costs satisfy the assumptions of Section~\ref{sec:model-setup}.
For any step size $\eta>0$, the controller in
Section~\ref{sec:online-algorithm}, initialized by \eqref{eq:alg-init}, satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:regret-theorem-bound}
\RegSDAC{T}(H)
\le
\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta}+\eta T\Gamma_\alpha,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-Gamma-alpha}
\Gamma_\alpha
\triangleq
\frac{G_\alpha^2}{2}
+
\frac{\alpha G_\alpha(L_x+\alpha L_u)}{(1-\alpha)^2},
\end{equation}
and $G_\alpha=\alpha\bigl(\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}+L_u\bigr)$ as in
\eqref{eq:def-surrogate-G}.

Choosing
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-optimal-step-size}
\eta^*=\sqrt{\frac{\log(H+1)}{T\Gamma_\alpha}}
\end{equation}
gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:regret-optimized-bound}
\RegSDAC{T}(H)\le 2\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha T\log(H+1)}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The proof combines the OMD bound for the surrogate costs
(Lemma~\ref{lem:omd-surrogate-regret}) with the following bound on the
difference between realized and surrogate costs.
\begin{lemma}[Stability under online parameter updates]
\label{lem:contractive-deviation}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound}, the online SDAC
controller satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stability-sum-bound}
\sum_{t=1}^T \Bigl(c_t(x_t,u_t)-\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)\Bigr)
\le
\frac{\alpha\eta T G_\alpha(L_x+\alpha L_u)}{(1-\alpha)^2}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}


\begin{corollary}[No-regret against infinite-memory SDAC policies]
\label{cor:regret-vs-infinite-sdac}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound}, run the online SDAC
controller with step size $\eta^*$ and memory horizon
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-memory-horizon}
H=H_T
\triangleq
\max\!\left\{1,\left\lceil\frac{\log T}{\log(1/\alpha)}\right\rceil\right\},
\end{equation}
where $\eta^*$ is given by \eqref{eq:def-optimal-step-size} with $H=H_T$.
Define the regret against the best fixed
infinite-memory SDAC policy by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-infinite-regret}
\RegSDAC{T}(\infty)
\triangleq
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}-\inf_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_\infty}J_T(\mathbf{m}).
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:infinite-sdac-regret-bound}
\begin{aligned}
\RegSDAC{T}(\infty)
&\le
2\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha T\log(H_T+1)}\\
&\quad+
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)\alpha^{H_T+1}T.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For fixed $\alpha,L_x,L_u$, the right-hand side is
$O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log\log T}\right)=o(T)$.
The same bound holds against the best fixed-fraction policy, since
Corollary~\ref{cor:fraction-as-sdac} gives
\[
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}
-\min_{k\in[0,\alpha]}\sum_{t=1}^T c_t\bigl(x_t(k),u_t(k)\bigr)
\le\RegSDAC{T}(\infty).
\]
\end{corollary}

\section{Regret Lower Bound}
\label{sec:regret-lower-bound}

The next result shows that the $\sqrt{T}$ dependence is unavoidable and
quantifies the necessary dependence on $1-\alpha$ for any
causal feasible controller. Omitted proofs appear in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-lower-proofs}.

The construction follows the argument in Hazan, Agarwal, and Kale
\cite[Sec.~2.3, footnote~3]{hazan2007logarithmic};
see also \cite[Sec.~3.2, Thm.~3.2]{hazan2016introduction}.
In that argument, linear costs with independent random signs give every
causal decision rule zero expected cost, while the best decision in hindsight
has an advantage determined by the absolute sum of the signs.
Here, we use constant arrivals and compare two feasible SDAC policies.
Their difference in cumulative costs is a weighted Rademacher sum, with
coefficients $\beta_t$ in \eqref{eq:sdac-lower-beta} determined by the storage
dynamics. Applying Khintchine's inequality \cite{szarek1976best} to this sum
gives the bound below. Kumar, Dean, and Kleinberg
\cite[App.~D, Thm.~D.2]{kumar2023unbounded} use a related construction with
Rademacher signs assigned to blocks for discounted memory. Our construction
uses an independent sign at each time and evaluates SDAC policies on their
own feasible storage trajectories.

\begin{theorem}[SDAC regret lower bound]
\label{thm:sdac-lower-bound}
Fix $H\ge1$ and $T\ge2$. For every causal feasible online controller
$\mathcal{A}$, possibly randomized, there is a deterministic oblivious sequence
of arrivals and costs satisfying the assumptions of
Section~\ref{sec:model-setup} such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-lower-exact}
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\!\left[\RegSDAC{T}(H)\right]
\ge
\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}
\sqrt{\sum_{t=2}^T \beta_t^2},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-lower-beta}
\beta_t
\triangleq
\alpha\left(
L_u+\frac{L_x(1-\alpha^{t-2})}{1-\alpha}
\right),
\qquad t=2,\ldots,T.
\end{equation}
The expectation is over the internal randomization of $\mathcal{A}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Lower bound for sufficiently large horizons]
\label{cor:sdac-lower-large-horizon}
Fix $H\ge1$. If
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-lower-horizon}
T\ge2\left\lceil\frac{\log 2}{\log(1/\alpha)}\right\rceil+2,
\end{equation}
then for every causal feasible controller
$\mathcal{A}$, possibly randomized, there is a deterministic oblivious sequence
satisfying the model assumptions such that
\[
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\!\left[\RegSDAC{T}(H)\right]
\ge
\frac{G_\alpha}{8}\sqrt{T},
\]
where $G_\alpha=\alpha(L_x/(1-\alpha)+L_u)$ as in
\eqref{eq:def-surrogate-G}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Large-$\alpha$ regime]
\label{rem:large-alpha-regime}
For fixed $L_x,L_u>0$, both the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:sdac-lower-horizon} and $G_\alpha$ are of order $(1-\alpha)^{-1}$
as $\alpha\uparrow1$: here
$\log(1/\alpha)/(1-\alpha)\to1$, and taking a ceiling changes a number
by less than one. If \eqref{eq:sdac-lower-horizon} holds, then
Corollary~\ref{cor:sdac-lower-large-horizon} yields
\[
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\!\left[\RegSDAC{T}(H)\right]
=
\Omega\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{T}}{1-\alpha}\right).
\]
For the upper bound, as $\alpha\uparrow1$,
\[
(1-\alpha)G_\alpha
=\alpha\bigl(L_x+(1-\alpha)L_u\bigr)
\longrightarrow L_x,
\]
and \eqref{eq:def-Gamma-alpha} gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
(1-\alpha)^3\Gamma_\alpha
&=\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\bigl((1-\alpha)G_\alpha\bigr)^2 \\
&\quad+\alpha\bigl((1-\alpha)G_\alpha\bigr)
(L_x+\alpha L_u) \\
&\longrightarrow L_x(L_x+L_u)>0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus,
\[
\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha}
=\Theta\!\left((1-\alpha)^{-3/2}\right)
\quad\text{as }\alpha\uparrow1.
\]
The upper bound is $O((1-\alpha)^{-3/2}\sqrt{T\log(H+1)})$
by Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound}. The factor $\sqrt{\log(H+1)}$ in this upper
bound does not appear in the lower bound. The dependence on $1-\alpha$ also
differs by a factor of $(1-\alpha)^{-1/2}$. Thus, the bounds match in their
$\sqrt{T}$ dependence for fixed $H$ and $\alpha$, but do not establish matching
dependence on $H$ or $1-\alpha$.

The horizon condition \eqref{eq:sdac-lower-horizon} requires $T$ to grow at
least on the order of $(1-\alpha)^{-1}$ as $\alpha\uparrow1$.
For fixed $T$,
\[
\frac{1-\alpha^{t-2}}{1-\alpha}
=\sum_{s=0}^{t-3}\alpha^s\longrightarrow t-2
\qquad\text{as }\alpha\uparrow1,
\]
where the sum is empty for $t=2$, so the lower bound in
\eqref{eq:sdac-lower-exact} remains finite.
\end{remark}

\section{Discussion and Extensions}
\label{sec:extensions}

We discuss three extensions. In each case, the feasibility, convexity, and
stability arguments apply with the stated changes to the policy domain and
Lipschitz constants. We omit the resulting regret bounds, whose form is unchanged
up to these constants and, in the vector settings, the chosen product norm.

\paragraph{Retention $\alpha\ge 1$}
Here we extend the model beyond the assumption $\alpha\in(0,1)$.
The case $\alpha=1$ has no storage loss, while $\alpha>1$ represents growth
between decisions. Fix $k$ with $\alpha-1<k<\alpha$ and set
$\bar\alpha\triangleq\alpha-k\in(0,1)$. In particular, $k>0$.
Define $v_t\triangleq u_t-kx_t$ as the action beyond $kx_t$, and restrict
$v_t$ to $0\le v_t\le\bar\alpha x_t$. The original action is
$u_t=kx_t+v_t$, so
\[
\begin{aligned}
x_{t+1}&=\alpha x_t-(kx_t+v_t)+w_t\\
&=(\alpha-k)x_t-v_t+w_t
=\bar\alpha x_t-v_t+w_t.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus, $v_t$ is the control action for a storage system with retention
coefficient $\bar\alpha\in(0,1)$. For $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, define
\[
\begin{aligned}
v_t(\mathbf{m})&=\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\bar\alpha^i w_{t-i},\\
u_t(\mathbf{m})&=kx_t(\mathbf{m})+v_t(\mathbf{m}),
\end{aligned}
\]
where $x_1(\mathbf{m})=0$ and
$x_{t+1}(\mathbf{m})=\bar\alpha x_t(\mathbf{m})-v_t(\mathbf{m})+w_t$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-feasibility}, with $\alpha$ replaced by $\bar\alpha$,
gives $0\le v_t(\mathbf{m})\le\bar\alpha x_t(\mathbf{m})$.
For $\mathbf{m}_t\in\mathcal{M}_H$, the online controller instead selects
\[
\begin{aligned}
v_t&=\min\!\left\{\sum_{i=1}^H m_{t,i}\bar\alpha^i w_{t-i},\,
\bar\alpha x_t\right\},\\
u_t&=kx_t+v_t.
\end{aligned}
\]
In both cases,
\[
kx_t\le u_t=kx_t+v_t
\le(k+\bar\alpha)x_t
=\alpha x_t,
\]
so the original feasibility constraint \eqref{eq:model-feasibility} is satisfied.
Equation~\eqref{eq:model-state-bound}, with $\alpha$ replaced by
$\bar\alpha\in(0,1)$, gives $0\le x_t\le1/(1-\bar\alpha)$.
Assume that the original costs $c_t(x,u)$ are convex and satisfy the
Lipschitz and subgradient bounds of Section~\ref{sec:model-setup} on
\[
0\le x\le\frac{1}{1-\bar\alpha},
\qquad
kx\le u\le\alpha x.
\]
The cost in the variables $(x,v)$ is $c_t(x,kx+v)$, which is convex because
$(x,v)\mapsto(x,kx+v)$ is affine. For two feasible pairs $(x,v)$ and $(x',v')$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
&|c_t(x,kx+v)-c_t(x',kx'+v')|\\
&\quad\overset{\mathrm{(A)}}{\le} L_x|x-x'|+L_u|k(x-x')+v-v'|\\
&\quad\overset{\mathrm{(B)}}{\le}(L_x+kL_u)|x-x'|+L_u|v-v'|.
\end{aligned}
\]
Step~(A) uses the assumed form of \eqref{eq:model-lipschitz} on the
stated domain, with $u=kx+v$ and $u'=kx'+v'$.
Step~(B) uses the triangle inequality and $k>0$.
If $(\zeta_t^x,\zeta_t^u)=\boldsymbol{\zeta}_t(x,kx+v)$, then
\[
\begin{aligned}
&c_t(x',kx'+v')-c_t(x,kx+v)\\
&\quad\ge\zeta_t^x(x'-x)+\zeta_t^u\bigl(k(x'-x)+v'-v\bigr)\\
&\quad=(\zeta_t^x+k\zeta_t^u)(x'-x)+\zeta_t^u(v'-v).
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence, $(\zeta_t^x+k\zeta_t^u,\zeta_t^u)$ is a subgradient of the transformed
cost, with
\[
|\zeta_t^x+k\zeta_t^u|\le L_x+kL_u,\qquad |\zeta_t^u|\le L_u.
\]
The online controller uses the same OMD update with surrogate cost
\[
\mathbf{m}\mapsto
c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{m}),kx_t(\mathbf{m})+v_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr).
\]
The feasibility and regret upper-bound analysis therefore applies with $\alpha$
replaced by $\bar\alpha$ and $L_x$ replaced by $L_x+kL_u$, while $L_u$ is
unchanged.
Both the online controller and the comparators use the same fixed $k$.
Each comparator is a policy $u_t(\mathbf{m})=kx_t(\mathbf{m})+v_t(\mathbf{m})$
with $v_t(\mathbf{m})$ given by the SDAC filter above.
The substitution preserves the cost at every time, so it also preserves
cumulative costs and regret against this class. Selecting $k$, either in
advance or online, is left for future work.

\paragraph{Vector actions and multi-task allocation}
Consider a single storage state $x_t$ with a vector action
$\mathbf{u}_t\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^n$ that allocates resources across $n$ tasks,
subject to $\sum_{a=1}^n u_{t,a}\le\alpha x_t$. The dynamics are
$x_{t+1}=\alpha x_t-\sum_{a=1}^n u_{t,a}+w_t$, and the cost
$c_t(x,\mathbf{u})$ is convex in $(x,\mathbf{u})$. Replace
$\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$ with a matrix
$\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^{n\times H}$ satisfying
\[
\sum_{a=1}^n\sum_{i=1}^H M_{a,i}\le1,
\qquad
u_{t,a}(\mathbf{M})
=\sum_{i=1}^H M_{a,i}\alpha^i w_{t-i}.
\]
Set $m_i=\sum_{a=1}^n M_{a,i}$. Then
\[
m_i\ge0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i
=\sum_{a=1}^n\sum_{i=1}^H M_{a,i}
\le1,
\]
so $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$. Moreover,
\[
\sum_{a=1}^n u_{t,a}(\mathbf{M})
=\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\alpha^i w_{t-i}
\le\alpha x_t(\mathbf{M}),
\]
where the inequality is Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-feasibility}, applied to
$m_i=\sum_a M_{a,i}$. The scalar SDAC trajectory and $x_t(\mathbf{M})$
have the same initial state and total action, so their states coincide.
The state and vector action are affine in $\mathbf{M}$, so
$c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{M}),\mathbf{u}_t(\mathbf{M})\bigr)$ is convex in $\mathbf{M}$. For the
online SDAC controller, a nominal vector whose entries sum to more than
$\alpha x_t$ can be scaled proportionally so that its entries remain nonnegative
and sum to $\alpha x_t$. Under an $\ell_1$ Lipschitz bound in the action, the size of this
correction equals the excess total action, and the stability proof proceeds with
the corresponding matrix norm. OMD then runs over the matrix subprobability
simplex above.

\paragraph{Multiple storage states with diagonal dynamics}
Suppose the state is $\mathbf{x}_t\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^d$ and evolves according to
$\mathbf{x}_{t+1}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_t-\mathbf{u}_t+\mathbf{w}_t$, where
$\mathbf{A}=\operatorname{diag}(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_d)$,
$\alpha_s\in(0,1)$ for $s\in[d]$, the arrivals satisfy
$\mathbf{w}_t\in[0,1]^d$, and the per-storage constraints are
$0\le u_{t,s}\le\alpha_s x_{t,s}$. Since $\mathbf{A}$ is diagonal,
\[
x_{t+1,s}=\alpha_s x_{t,s}-u_{t,s}+w_{t,s},\qquad s\in[d].
\]
Thus, the next state of each storage system depends only on its own current
state, action, and arrival. Assign one SDAC parameter in $\mathcal{M}_H$
to each storage state and use the powers $\alpha_s^i$ in coordinate $s$. The dynamics
and feasibility constraints then decouple by storage state, and the SDAC parameter domain
is a product of subprobability simplices, with
$x_{t,s}\le1/(1-\alpha_s)$ for every $s\in[d]$. The online SDAC controller clips
each nominal action to $\alpha_s x_{t,s}$. If the cost is jointly convex and
satisfies compatible Lipschitz and bounded-subgradient assumptions on the resulting bounded product
domain, the surrogate cost is jointly convex in all SDAC parameters and OMD applies
on the product set. With a sum of negentropy regularizers, the OMD minimization
separates by storage state, although each subgradient may depend on all storage states
and actions. Cross-coupled storage systems (nondiagonal $\mathbf{A}$) require
further analysis.


\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied adversarial online control of a storage system under a state-dependent
action constraint. We introduced SDAC policies, which are feasible by
construction, and developed an online SDAC controller with
$O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log(H+1)}\right)$ regret against the best fixed SDAC policy.
The SDAC approximation results further give
$O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log\log T}\right)$ regret against the best fixed
infinite-memory SDAC policy for a suitable choice $H=\Theta(\log T)$.
Under the horizon condition \eqref{eq:sdac-lower-horizon}, the
lower bound gives
$\Omega((1-\alpha)^{-1}\sqrt{T})$ regret as $\alpha\uparrow1$.
For the bounds proved here, a factor of $\sqrt{\log(H+1)}$ remains between
the upper and lower bounds
in their dependence on $H$, together with a factor of $(1-\alpha)^{-1/2}$
in their dependence on $1-\alpha$. Subsequent work closes these gaps for
the same policy class \cite{asgari2026cumulative}. The model applies directly to
energy-harvesting battery management and related settings in which storage limits
must be respected in real time.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-state-decomp}]
For $1\le t<T$ and $i\in[H]$, shifting the summation index in
\eqref{eq:def-arrival-sums} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-arrival-recursions}
\begin{aligned}
r_{t+1}
&=\sum_{j=1}^{t}\alpha^{j-1}w_{t+1-j}
 =w_t+\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}\alpha^j w_{t-j} \\
&=w_t+\alpha r_t, \\
r_{t+1}(i)
&=w_t+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha^j w_{t-j} \\
&=w_t+\alpha r_t(i)-\alpha^i w_{t-i}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The sums with upper limits $t-1$ and $i-1$ are empty when $t=1$ and $i=1$,
respectively.

At $t=1$, \eqref{eq:model-init} gives $x_1(\mathbf{m})=0$, while
$r_1(i)=r_1=0$ by the arrival and empty-sum conventions. Thus,
\eqref{eq:sdac-state-decomp} holds at $t=1$. Suppose it holds for some
$1\le t<T$. Substitute the induction hypothesis and
\eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy} into \eqref{eq:model-dynamics}, then use
\eqref{eq:sdac-arrival-recursions}:
\begin{align*}
x_{t+1}(\mathbf{m})
&=\sum_{i=1}^H m_i
\bigl(\alpha r_t(i)-\alpha^i w_{t-i}\bigr) \\
&\quad
+\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\right)\alpha r_t+w_t \\
&=\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\bigl(r_{t+1}(i)-w_t\bigr) \\
&\quad
+\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\right)(r_{t+1}-w_t)+w_t \\
&=\sum_{i=1}^H m_i r_{t+1}(i)
+\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\right)r_{t+1}.
\end{align*}
The coefficients of $w_t$ in the penultimate expression sum to
$-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i-(1-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i)+1=0$.
Thus, \eqref{eq:sdac-state-decomp} follows by induction.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-surrogate}]
Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-state-decomp} and \eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy} give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-affine-pair}
\begin{aligned}
x_t(\mathbf{m})&=r_t+\langle \boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x,\mathbf{m}\rangle,\\
u_t(\mathbf{m})&=\langle \boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For $i\in[H]$, \eqref{eq:def-phi-u} and \eqref{eq:def-arrival-sums} give
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\phi_{t,i}^x|&=r_t-r_t(i)
=\sum_{j=i+1}^{t-1}\alpha^{j-1}w_{t-j} \\
&\overset{\mathrm{(A)}}{\le}\sum_{j=i+1}^{\infty}\alpha^{j-1}
=\frac{\alpha^i}{1-\alpha}
\overset{\mathrm{(B)}}{\le}\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}, \\
0\le\phi_{t,i}^u&=\alpha^i w_{t-i}\le\alpha^i\le\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\]
The finite sum is empty when $i\ge t-1$, since $w_\tau=0$ for $\tau\le0$.
Step~(A) uses $0\le w_{t-j}\le1$ and extends a nonnegative sum.
Step~(B) uses $i\ge1$ and $0<\alpha<1$; the same bounds give the estimate
for $\phi_{t,i}^u$. Taking the maximum over $i\in[H]$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-coefficient-norms}
\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x\|_\infty\le\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha},
\qquad
\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u\|_\infty\le\alpha.
\end{equation}
Since the state--action pair in \eqref{eq:surr-affine-pair} is affine in
$\mathbf{m}$ and $c_t$ is convex, $\ell_t$ is convex.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-feasibility} and \eqref{eq:model-state-bound}, the
SDAC-policy state--action pairs associated with $\mathbf{m}$ and
$\mathbf{m}'$ belong to $\mathcal{D}_\alpha$.
Thus,
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\ell_t(\mathbf{m})-\ell_t(\mathbf{m}')|
&\overset{\mathrm{(A)}}{\le}
L_x|x_t(\mathbf{m})-x_t(\mathbf{m}')| \\
&\quad{}
+L_u|u_t(\mathbf{m})-u_t(\mathbf{m}')| \\
&=
L_x\bigl|\langle \boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x,\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\rangle\bigr| \\
&\quad{}
+
L_u\bigl|\langle \boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\rangle\bigr| \\
&\overset{\mathrm{(B)}}{\le}
\left(\frac{\alpha L_x}{1-\alpha}+\alpha L_u\right)
\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\|_1 \\
&=
G_\alpha\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Step~(A) is \eqref{eq:model-lipschitz}. The next equality uses
\eqref{eq:surr-affine-pair}, and (B) applies
$|\langle\mathbf{v},\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\rangle|
\le\|\mathbf{v}\|_\infty\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\|_1$
to each coefficient vector, followed by \eqref{eq:surr-coefficient-norms}.
The last equality uses \eqref{eq:def-surrogate-G}.

Fix $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, and let $(\zeta_t^x,\zeta_t^u)$ and
$\mathbf{g}_t$ be defined by \eqref{eq:surr-subgradient}. For every
$\mathbf{m}'\in\mathcal{M}_H$, step~(A) applies the cost subgradient
inequality from \eqref{eq:model-cost-subgradient} at
$\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{m}),u_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr)$ and substitutes
the affine differences from \eqref{eq:surr-affine-pair}:
\begin{align*}
\ell_t(\mathbf{m}')
&\overset{\mathrm{(A)}}{\ge}\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
+\zeta_t^x\langle \boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x,\mathbf{m}'-\mathbf{m}\rangle \\
&\quad
+\zeta_t^u\langle \boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}'-\mathbf{m}\rangle \\
&=
\ell_t(\mathbf{m})+\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}'-\mathbf{m}\rangle.
\end{align*}
Thus, $\mathbf{g}_t\in\partial\ell_t(\mathbf{m})$. The triangle inequality gives
the following bound, where (A) uses \eqref{eq:model-subgradient-bounds} and
\eqref{eq:surr-coefficient-norms}:
\begin{align*}
\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty
&\le
|\zeta_t^x|\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x\|_\infty
+|\zeta_t^u|\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u\|_\infty \\
&\overset{\mathrm{(A)}}{\le}
\frac{\alpha L_x}{1-\alpha}+\alpha L_u \\
&=G_\alpha.\qedhere
\end{align*}
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-truncation-approx}]
For the truncation \eqref{eq:def-sdac-truncation},
\eqref{eq:def-infinite-simplex} gives $m_i\ge0$ for every $i\in[H]$ and
\[
\|\mathbf{m}_H(\mathbf{m})\|_1
=\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\le\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}m_i\le1.
\]
Thus, $\mathbf{m}_H(\mathbf{m})\in\mathcal{M}_H$.

To prove feasibility of the infinite-memory SDAC policy, truncate at length $T$.
Since $w_\tau=0$ for $\tau\le0$, \eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy} and
\eqref{eq:def-infinite-sdac-policy} give, for every $t\in[T]$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
u_t\bigl(\mathbf{m}_T(\mathbf{m})\bigr)
&=\sum_{i=1}^T m_i\alpha^i w_{t-i}\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}m_i\alpha^i w_{t-i}=u_t(\mathbf{m}).
\end{aligned}
\]
The infinite-memory SDAC policy and its truncation have the same initial state
and actions, so their state trajectories coincide.
Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-feasibility} therefore gives
\[
0\le u_t(\mathbf{m})\le\alpha x_t(\mathbf{m}),
\qquad t\in[T].
\]

For the approximation error, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-truncation-errors}
\begin{aligned}
e_t^u&\triangleq
u_t(\mathbf{m})-u_t\bigl(\mathbf{m}_H(\mathbf{m})\bigr),\\
e_t^x&\triangleq
x_t\bigl(\mathbf{m}_H(\mathbf{m})\bigr)-x_t(\mathbf{m}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy} and \eqref{eq:def-infinite-sdac-policy},
for every $t\in[T]$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-truncation-action-error}
\begin{aligned}
0\le e_t^u
&=\sum_{i=H+1}^{t-1}\alpha^i m_i w_{t-i}\\
&\overset{\mathrm{(A)}}{\le}\sum_{i=H+1}^{t-1}\alpha^i m_i\\
&\overset{\mathrm{(B)}}{\le}\alpha^{H+1}\sum_{i=H+1}^{t-1}m_i
\overset{\mathrm{(C)}}{\le}\alpha^{H+1}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The sums are empty when $t\le H+1$. Step~(A) uses $w_{t-i}\le1$ and
$m_i\ge0$. Step~(B) uses $i\ge H+1$ and $0<\alpha<1$.
Step~(C) uses $\sum_{i\ge1}m_i\le1$ from \eqref{eq:def-infinite-simplex}.

Subtracting the two instances of \eqref{eq:model-dynamics}, with the
error signs in \eqref{eq:sdac-truncation-errors}, gives
\[
e_1^x=0,\qquad e_{t+1}^x=\alpha e_t^x+e_t^u.
\]
Iterating this recurrence yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-truncation-state-error}
\begin{aligned}
0\le e_t^x
&=\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\alpha^{t-1-s}e_s^u\\
&\overset{\mathrm{(A)}}{\le}
\alpha^{H+1}\sum_{j=0}^{t-2}\alpha^j
\overset{\mathrm{(B)}}{\le}\frac{\alpha^{H+1}}{1-\alpha}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Step~(A) uses \eqref{eq:sdac-truncation-action-error} and the change of index
$j=t-1-s$. Step~(B) extends the nonnegative sum to the geometric series
$\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\alpha^j=(1-\alpha)^{-1}$, valid because $0<\alpha<1$.
For $t=1$, both finite sums are empty.

Both policies are feasible, so \eqref{eq:model-state-bound} places their
state--action pairs in $\mathcal{D}_\alpha$. Therefore,
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\left|J_T\bigl(\mathbf{m}_H(\mathbf{m})\bigr)-J_T(\mathbf{m})\right|\\
&\quad\overset{\mathrm{(A)}}{\le}
\sum_{t=1}^T\left(L_xe_t^x+L_ue_t^u\right)\\
&\quad\overset{\mathrm{(B)}}{\le}
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)\alpha^{H+1}T.
\end{aligned}
\]
Step~(A) uses \eqref{eq:def-sdac-cumulative-cost}, the triangle inequality,
and \eqref{eq:model-lipschitz}; the errors in
\eqref{eq:sdac-truncation-errors} are nonnegative.
Step~(B) sums \eqref{eq:sdac-truncation-action-error} and
\eqref{eq:sdac-truncation-state-error} over $t\in[T]$.
This proves \eqref{eq:sdac-truncation-cost-gap}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:fraction-as-sdac}]
Since $0\le k/\alpha\le1$,
\[
m_i(k)=\frac{k}{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{k}{\alpha}\right)^{i-1}\ge0,
\qquad i\ge1.
\]
The definition \eqref{eq:def-fraction-parameter} also gives
\[
m_i(k)
=\left(1-\frac{k}{\alpha}\right)^{i-1}
-\left(1-\frac{k}{\alpha}\right)^i.
\]
Summing this difference cancels the terms with exponents $1,\ldots,H-1$.
Thus, for every positive integer $H$,
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{H}m_i(k)
=1-\left(1-\frac{k}{\alpha}\right)^H
\le1.
\]
The nonnegative partial sums increase to $\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}m_i(k)\le1$.
Hence, $\mathbf{m}(k)\in\mathcal{M}_\infty$ for every $k\in[0,\alpha]$,
including both endpoints under the convention $0^0=1$.

Iterating the fixed-fraction recursion \eqref{eq:def-fraction-trajectory}
from $x_1(k)=0$ gives
\[
x_t(k)=\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}(\alpha-k)^{i-1}w_{t-i}.
\]
For every $i\ge1$, \eqref{eq:def-fraction-parameter} and $\alpha>0$ give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha^i m_i(k)
&=\alpha^i\frac{k}{\alpha}
\left(\frac{\alpha-k}{\alpha}\right)^{i-1}\\
&=k(\alpha-k)^{i-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Substituting these two identities into \eqref{eq:def-infinite-sdac-policy},
\[
\begin{aligned}
u_t(k)&=kx_t(k)
=\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}k(\alpha-k)^{i-1}w_{t-i}\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\alpha^i m_i(k)w_{t-i}
=u_t\bigl(\mathbf{m}(k)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Both state trajectories start at zero and follow \eqref{eq:model-dynamics} with
the same actions. Hence, $x_t\bigl(\mathbf{m}(k)\bigr)=x_t(k)$ for every $t\in[T]$.
\end{proof}

\section{Proofs for the Online SDAC Controller}
\label{sec:appendix-algorithm-proofs}

The OMD results for unnormalized negentropy and its Bregman divergence on the
probability simplex are standard \cite{hazan2016introduction}. We prove the
corresponding forms for the subprobability simplex $\mathcal{M}_H$ used below.

\begin{lemma}[Strong convexity of negentropy on the subprobability simplex]
\label{lem:subprob-negentropy-strong-convexity}
For every $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$ and
$\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{M}_H\cap\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:subprob-negentropy-strong-convexity}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{v})
\ge
\frac12\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{v}\|_1^2.
\end{equation}
Thus, $\Psi$ is $1$-strongly convex with respect to $\|\cdot\|_1$ on
$\mathcal{M}_H$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First suppose $\mathbf{m}>0$ coordinatewise. Set
$\mathbf{d}\triangleq\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{v}$ and
$\mathbf{z}_s\triangleq\mathbf{v}+s\mathbf{d}$ for $s\in[0,1]$. Then
$\mathbf{z}_s\in\mathcal{M}_H\cap\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$. By
\eqref{eq:def-negentropy},
$\nabla^2\Psi(\mathbf{z}_s)=\operatorname{diag}(1/z_{s,1},\ldots,1/z_{s,H})$.
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:negentropy-hessian-bound}
\begin{aligned}
\|\mathbf{d}\|_1^2
&=\left(\sum_{i=1}^H
\frac{|d_i|}{\sqrt{z_{s,i}}}\sqrt{z_{s,i}}\right)^2 \\
&\overset{\mathrm{(A)}}{\le}
\left(\sum_{i=1}^H\frac{d_i^2}{z_{s,i}}\right)
\left(\sum_{i=1}^H z_{s,i}\right) \\
&\overset{\mathrm{(B)}}{\le}
\mathbf{d}^\top\nabla^2\Psi(\mathbf{z}_s)\mathbf{d}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here (A) is Cauchy--Schwarz, with $z_{s,i}>0$, and (B) uses
$\sum_i z_{s,i}\le1$ from \eqref{eq:def-simplex} and the Hessian above.
Taylor's formula with integral remainder and
\eqref{eq:negentropy-hessian-bound} give
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{v})
&=\int_0^1(1-s)
\mathbf{d}^\top\nabla^2\Psi(\mathbf{z}_s)\mathbf{d}\,ds \\
&\overset{\mathrm{(A)}}{\ge}
\|\mathbf{d}\|_1^2\int_0^1(1-s)\,ds
=\frac12\|\mathbf{d}\|_1^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
In (A), the weight $1-s$ is nonnegative on $[0,1]$.
For a general $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$ and $0<\varepsilon<1$, apply the bound to
$\mathbf{m}^{\varepsilon}\triangleq(1-\varepsilon)\mathbf{m}
+\varepsilon\mathbf{v}$ and let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. The result follows
from the convention $0\log0=0$ and continuity in the first argument of
$D_\Psi$.
\end{proof}


\begin{lemma}[Action clipping as a Bregman projection]
\label{lem:availability-projection}
For $t\in[T]$, define the set of parameters compatible with the current state by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:availability-set}
\mathcal{B}_t
\triangleq
\left\{
\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H:
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}\rangle
\le \alpha x_t
\right\}.
\end{equation}
Let $\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}$ be the Bregman projection of
$\mathbf{m}_t$ onto $\mathcal{B}_t$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:availability-projection}
\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}
\in
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{B}_t}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_t).
\end{equation}
Then
\[
\left\langle
\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}
\right\rangle
=
\min\!\left\{
\left\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\right\rangle,
\alpha x_t
\right\}
=u_t.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The feasibility argument following \eqref{eq:alg-feasible-action} gives
$x_t\ge0$, so $\mathbf{0}\in\mathcal{B}_t$ by
\eqref{eq:availability-set}. This set is compact and convex because it is
the intersection of $\mathcal{M}_H$ and a closed halfspace. The initialization
\eqref{eq:alg-init} and update
\eqref{eq:alg-omd-mult}--\eqref{eq:alg-omd-proj} ensure that every coordinate of
$\mathbf{m}_t$ is positive. By \eqref{eq:def-bregman},
$D_\Psi(\cdot\|\mathbf{m}_t)$ is continuous on $\mathcal{M}_H$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:subprob-negentropy-strong-convexity}, it is strictly convex and,
for every $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$,
\[
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_t)
\ge \frac12\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1^2
\ge0.
\]
Thus, the projection exists and is unique, and equality holds only at
$\mathbf{m}=\mathbf{m}_t$.

If $\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\rangle\le\alpha x_t$, then
$\mathbf{m}_t\in\mathcal{B}_t$ and
$\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}=\mathbf{m}_t$.

Now suppose $\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\rangle>\alpha x_t$.
Assume, for contradiction, that the action constraint is slack:
\[
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\rangle
<\alpha x_t
<\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\rangle.
\]
Set
\[
\delta\triangleq
\frac{\alpha x_t-
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\rangle}
{\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\rangle-
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\rangle}.
\]
The strict inequalities above give $0<\delta<1$. Define
\[
\mathbf{m}_\delta
\triangleq
(1-\delta)\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}+\delta\mathbf{m}_t.
\]
By convexity, $\mathbf{m}_\delta\in\mathcal{M}_H$, and
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_\delta\rangle \\
&=
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\rangle
\\
&\quad+\delta\left(
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\rangle-
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\rangle
\right)\\
&=\alpha x_t.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last equality substitutes the definition of $\delta$. Thus,
$\mathbf{m}_\delta\in\mathcal{B}_t$ by \eqref{eq:availability-set}. Since
$\mathbf{m}_t\notin\mathcal{B}_t$,
$\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\ne\mathbf{m}_t$, and hence
$D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\|\mathbf{m}_t)>0$. Using convexity
and $D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t\|\mathbf{m}_t)=0$, we obtain
\begin{align*}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_\delta\|\mathbf{m}_t)
&\overset{\mathrm{(A)}}{\le}
(1-\delta)D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\|\mathbf{m}_t)
\\
&\quad+\delta D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t\|\mathbf{m}_t) \\
&=
(1-\delta)D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\|\mathbf{m}_t)
\\
&\overset{\mathrm{(B)}}{<}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\|\mathbf{m}_t),
\end{align*}
where (A) uses convexity in the first argument, and (B) uses
$\delta>0$ and the strict positivity of the divergence established above.
This contradicts \eqref{eq:availability-projection}. Therefore, the
constraint is active:
\[
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\rangle
=\alpha x_t.
\]
Combining the two cases with \eqref{eq:alg-feasible-action} proves the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Closed-form solution of the OMD update]
\label{lem:omd-closed-form}
Fix $\mathbf{m}_t\in\mathcal{M}_H\cap\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$, $\eta>0$, and
$\mathbf{g}_t\in\mathbb{R}^H$. Define
$\widetilde{m}_{t+1,i}\triangleq m_{t,i}\exp(-\eta g_{t,i})$ for $i\in[H]$.
Then the OMD update \eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin} has the unique solution
\[
\mathbf{m}_{t+1}
=\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}}
{\max\{1,\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}\|_1\}}.
\]
Moreover, $\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\in\mathcal{M}_H\cap\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$. Thus, the
OMD iterates initialized by \eqref{eq:alg-init} remain strictly positive.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $m_{t,i}>0$, we have $\widetilde{m}_{t+1,i}>0$ for every $i\in[H]$.
For any $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$,
\[
m_i\log\frac{m_i}{\widetilde{m}_{t+1,i}}
=m_i\log\frac{m_i}{m_{t,i}}+\eta g_{t,i}m_i,
\]
where the identity at $m_i=0$ uses the convention $0\log0=0$. Substituting into
\eqref{eq:def-bregman} gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\eta\langle\mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}\rangle+D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_t) \\
&=\sum_{i=1}^H\left(
m_i\log\frac{m_i}{\widetilde{m}_{t+1,i}}-m_i+m_{t,i}
\right) \\
&=D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1})
+\sum_{i=1}^H(m_{t,i}-\widetilde{m}_{t+1,i}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus, it suffices to minimize
$D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1})$ over $\mathcal{M}_H$.

Define
\[
\mathbf{m}^\star
\triangleq
\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}}
{\max\{1,\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}\|_1\}}.
\]
Then $\mathbf{m}^\star>0$ coordinatewise and
\[
\|\mathbf{m}^\star\|_1
=\frac{\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}\|_1}
{\max\{1,\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}\|_1\}}
\le1.
\]
If $\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}\|_1\le1$, the logarithm below is zero;
otherwise, $\|\mathbf{m}^\star\|_1=1$. Hence,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-complementarity}
\bigl(1-\|\mathbf{m}^\star\|_1\bigr)
\log\max\{1,\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}\|_1\}=0.
\end{equation}
For any $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$ and $i\in[H]$, the definition of
$\mathbf{m}^\star$ gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
m_i\log\frac{m_i}{\widetilde{m}_{t+1,i}}
&=m_i\log\frac{m_i}{m_i^\star}\\
&\quad-m_i\log\max\{1,\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}\|_1\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This identity also holds at $m_i=0$ under the convention $0\log0=0$.
Substituting it into \eqref{eq:def-bregman} gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1})
-D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}^\star\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}) \\
&=D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}^\star)
\\
&\quad+\bigl(\|\mathbf{m}^\star\|_1-\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\bigr)
\log\max\{1,\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}\|_1\}\\
&=D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}^\star)
\\
&\quad+\bigl(1-\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\bigr)
\log\max\{1,\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}\|_1\}\\
&\overset{\mathrm{(A)}}{\ge}D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}^\star)
\overset{\mathrm{(B)}}{\ge}\frac12\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}^\star\|_1^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
The second equality uses \eqref{eq:omd-complementarity}. In (A),
$1-\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\ge0$ by \eqref{eq:def-simplex}, and the logarithm is
nonnegative. Step (B) applies
Lemma~\ref{lem:subprob-negentropy-strong-convexity} with
$\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{m}^\star\in\mathcal{M}_H\cap\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$.
Thus, the objective is minimized uniquely at $\mathbf{m}=\mathbf{m}^\star$.
The minimizer has the stated form and is strictly positive.
Induction from \eqref{eq:alg-init} proves that every iterate is strictly positive.
\end{proof}

\section{Proofs for the Regret Upper Bounds}
\label{sec:appendix-regret-proofs}

\begin{lemma}[One-step OMD movement bound]
\label{lem:omd-move}
The OMD update \eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin} with step size $\eta>0$ satisfies
\[
\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1
\le \eta\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty
\le \eta G_\alpha.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:omd-closed-form}, both iterates are positive. Applying
\eqref{eq:subprob-negentropy-strong-convexity} in both directions gives
step (A) below; the equality follows by adding the two instances of
\eqref{eq:def-bregman}:
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1^2 \\
&\quad\overset{\mathrm{(A)}}{\le}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\|\mathbf{m}_t)+D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}) \\
&\quad=
\bigl\langle\nabla\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1})-\nabla\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t),\\
&\qquad\qquad\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\bigr\rangle \\
&\quad\overset{\mathrm{(B)}}{\le}
\eta\langle\mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\rangle \\
&\quad\overset{\mathrm{(C)}}{\le}
\eta\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Step (B) uses the first-order optimality condition for
\eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin} with comparison point $\mathbf{m}_t$ and
step (C) uses H\"older's inequality. Dividing by
$\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1$ gives the first bound when the iterates
differ; when they coincide, its left-hand side is zero.
Finally, Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-surrogate} gives
$\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\le G_\alpha$ and hence the uniform bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[OMD regret for the surrogate costs]
\label{lem:omd-surrogate-regret}
For every $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, the update \eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin} satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-surrogate-regret}
\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)
-\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
\le
\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_1)}{\eta}
+
\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{t=1}^T\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty^2.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-surrogate} gives convexity of $\ell_t$ and
$\mathbf{g}_t\in\partial\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)$, so
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-convexity-step}
\begin{aligned}
\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
&\le
\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}\rangle \\
&=
\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\rangle \\
&\quad+
\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The iterates are positive by Lemma~\ref{lem:omd-closed-form}. Thus, the
first-order optimality condition for \eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin}, with
comparison point $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, gives (A) below. Expanding
\eqref{eq:def-bregman} gives the subsequent equality, even when the
comparison point has zero coordinates:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-three-point-step}
\begin{aligned}
&\eta\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}\rangle \\
&\quad\overset{\mathrm{(A)}}{\le}
\bigl\langle \nabla\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1})-\nabla\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t),\\
&\qquad\qquad\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\bigr\rangle \\
&\quad=
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_t)
-D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}) \\
&\qquad\quad
-D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\|\mathbf{m}_t).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:omd-three-point-step} into
\eqref{eq:omd-convexity-step} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-one-step-regret}
\begin{aligned}
\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
&\le
\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_t)}{\eta}
-\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1})}{\eta} \\
&\quad+
\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\rangle
\\
&\quad
-\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\|\mathbf{m}_t)}{\eta}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The last two terms in \eqref{eq:omd-one-step-regret} satisfy
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-young-step}
\begin{aligned}
&\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\rangle
-\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\|\mathbf{m}_t)}{\eta} \\
&\qquad\overset{\mathrm{(A)}}{\le}
\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1\\
&\qquad\quad-\frac{\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1^2}{2\eta} \\
&\qquad=\frac{\eta}{2}\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty^2\\
&\qquad\quad-\frac{\bigl(\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1
-\eta\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\bigr)^2}{2\eta}\\
&\qquad\overset{\mathrm{(B)}}{\le}
\frac{\eta}{2}\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Step (A) uses H\"older's inequality and
\eqref{eq:subprob-negentropy-strong-convexity} with
$(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{v})=(\mathbf{m}_{t+1},\mathbf{m}_t)$.
After completing the square, (B) discards a nonnegative term since $\eta>0$.
Substituting \eqref{eq:omd-young-step} into
\eqref{eq:omd-one-step-regret} and summing over $t$ gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)
-\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
&\le
\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_1)}{\eta}
-\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_{T+1})}{\eta} \\
&\quad+
\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{t=1}^T\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
The Bregman terms telescope. By
\eqref{eq:subprob-negentropy-strong-convexity},
$D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_{T+1})\ge0$, so dropping its negative
proves \eqref{eq:omd-surrogate-regret}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:contractive-deviation}]
Let $d_t\triangleq|x_t-x_t(\mathbf{m}_t)|$. Both trajectories start at zero,
so $d_1=0$. By \eqref{eq:model-dynamics}, the clipping rule
\eqref{eq:alg-feasible-action}, and feasibility of the fixed policy
(Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-feasibility}), for $1\le t<T$ we have
\[
\begin{aligned}
x_{t+1}
&=[\alpha x_t-u_t(\mathbf{m}_t)]_++w_t,\\
x_{t+1}(\mathbf{m}_t)
&=[\alpha x_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-u_t(\mathbf{m}_t)]_++w_t.
\end{aligned}
\]
The triangle inequality and the affine formula
\eqref{eq:surr-affine-pair} therefore yield
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:deviation-recursion}
\begin{aligned}
d_{t+1}
&\le|x_{t+1}-x_{t+1}(\mathbf{m}_t)|\\
&\quad+|x_{t+1}(\mathbf{m}_t)-x_{t+1}(\mathbf{m}_{t+1})|\\
&\overset{\mathrm{(A)}}{\le}\alpha d_t+
|\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_{t+1}^x,
\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\rangle|\\
&\overset{\mathrm{(B)}}{\le}\alpha d_t+
\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
In (A), $|[z]_+-[z']_+|\le|z-z'|$ is applied with
$z=\alpha x_t-u_t(\mathbf{m}_t)$ and
$z'=\alpha x_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-u_t(\mathbf{m}_t)$; the common action and
arrival cancel. Step (B) uses H\"older's inequality and
\eqref{eq:surr-coefficient-norms} at time $t+1$.
Summing \eqref{eq:deviation-recursion} and using $d_1=0$ gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{t=1}^T d_t
&=\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}d_{t+1}\\
&\le\alpha\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}d_t+
\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}
\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1\\
&\overset{\mathrm{(A)}}{\le}\alpha\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_t+
\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}
\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Step (A) uses $\alpha d_T\ge0$. We subtract
$\alpha\sum_{t=1}^T d_t$ from both sides and divide by $1-\alpha>0$ to obtain
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:deviation-sum}
\sum_{t=1}^T d_t
\le\frac{\alpha}{(1-\alpha)^2}
\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1.
\end{equation}
When $T=1$, the movement sum is empty and both sides are zero.

For every $t\in[T]$, the clipping rule \eqref{eq:alg-feasible-action} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:online-clipping-gap}
\begin{aligned}
0\le u_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-u_t
&=[u_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-\alpha x_t]_+\\
&\overset{\mathrm{(A)}}{\le}[\alpha x_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-\alpha x_t]_+\\
&\le\alpha d_t.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Step (A) uses $u_t(\mathbf{m}_t)\le\alpha x_t(\mathbf{m}_t)$ from
Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-feasibility} and monotonicity of $[\cdot]_+$.
Both state--action pairs are feasible, so \eqref{eq:model-state-bound}
places them in $\mathcal{D}_\alpha$. Hence,
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{t=1}^T\bigl(c_t(x_t,u_t)-\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)\bigr)\\
&\quad\overset{\mathrm{(A)}}{\le}
\sum_{t=1}^T\bigl(L_xd_t+L_u|u_t-u_t(\mathbf{m}_t)|\bigr)\\
&\quad\overset{\mathrm{(B)}}{\le}
\frac{\alpha(L_x+\alpha L_u)}{(1-\alpha)^2}
\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1\\
&\quad\overset{\mathrm{(C)}}{\le}
\frac{\alpha\eta(T-1)G_\alpha(L_x+\alpha L_u)}{(1-\alpha)^2}\\
&\quad\le\frac{\alpha\eta T G_\alpha(L_x+\alpha L_u)}{(1-\alpha)^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Step (A) uses \eqref{eq:def-surrogate-cost} and
\eqref{eq:model-lipschitz}; (B) uses \eqref{eq:online-clipping-gap} and
\eqref{eq:deviation-sum}; (C) applies Lemma~\ref{lem:omd-move} to the
$T-1$ increments. The last step uses $T-1\le T$ and nonnegativity of
the coefficient, proving \eqref{eq:stability-sum-bound}.
\end{proof}


\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound}]
Fix $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$. We first bound the initial Bregman
divergence. Substituting \eqref{eq:alg-init} into
\eqref{eq:def-bregman} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-initial-divergence}
\begin{aligned}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_1)
&=\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\log m_i \\
&\quad+\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\bigl(\log(H+1)-1\bigr)
+\frac{H}{H+1} \\
&\overset{\mathrm{(A)}}{\le}
(1-\|\mathbf{m}\|_1)\frac{H}{H+1} \\
&\quad+\|\mathbf{m}\|_1
\left(\log(H+1)-\frac{1}{H+1}\right) \\
&\overset{\mathrm{(B)}}{\le}\log(H+1).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Step (A) discards $\sum_i m_i\log m_i\le0$ and regroups the remaining
terms, using $0\log0=0$. In (B), $\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\in[0,1]$ by
\eqref{eq:def-simplex}, and both terms in this convex combination are
at most $\log(H+1)$: indeed,
$\log(H+1)=\int_1^{H+1}z^{-1}\,dz\ge H/(H+1)$.

Using the surrogate definition \eqref{eq:def-surrogate-cost}, we obtain
\begingroup
\allowdisplaybreaks[4]
\begin{align*}
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}-J_T(\mathbf{m})
&=\sum_{t=1}^T\bigl(c_t(x_t,u_t)-\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)\bigr) \\
&\quad+\sum_{t=1}^T
\bigl(\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-\ell_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr) \\
&\overset{\mathrm{(A)}}{\le}
\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_1)}{\eta}
+\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{t=1}^T\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty^2 \\
&\quad+\frac{\alpha\eta T G_\alpha(L_x+\alpha L_u)}
{(1-\alpha)^2} \\
&\overset{\mathrm{(B)}}{\le}\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta}
+\frac{\eta T G_\alpha^2}{2} \\
&\quad+\frac{\alpha\eta T G_\alpha(L_x+\alpha L_u)}
{(1-\alpha)^2} \\
&=\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta}+\eta T\Gamma_\alpha.
\end{align*}
\endgroup
Step (A) adds \eqref{eq:omd-surrogate-regret} and
\eqref{eq:stability-sum-bound}. Step (B) uses
\eqref{eq:omd-initial-divergence} and the gradient bound in
Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-surrogate}; the last equality is
\eqref{eq:def-Gamma-alpha}. Taking the maximum over
$\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$ and using \eqref{eq:def-regret} proves
\eqref{eq:regret-theorem-bound}. Since $H,T\ge1$ and
$\Gamma_\alpha>0$, the step size \eqref{eq:def-optimal-step-size} is positive
and satisfies
\[
\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta^*}
=\eta^*T\Gamma_\alpha
=\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha T\log(H+1)},
\]
which gives \eqref{eq:regret-optimized-bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:regret-vs-infinite-sdac}]
Fix an infinite-memory SDAC policy $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_\infty$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-truncation-approx}, its truncation satisfies
$\mathbf{m}_{H_T}(\mathbf{m})\in\mathcal{M}_{H_T}$.
Comparing first with this truncation gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}-J_T(\mathbf{m})
&=J_T^{\mathrm{on}}-J_T\bigl(\mathbf{m}_{H_T}(\mathbf{m})\bigr)\\
&\quad+J_T\bigl(\mathbf{m}_{H_T}(\mathbf{m})\bigr)-J_T(\mathbf{m})\\
&\overset{\mathrm{(A)}}{\le}\RegSDAC{T}(H_T)\\
&\quad+J_T\bigl(\mathbf{m}_{H_T}(\mathbf{m})\bigr)-J_T(\mathbf{m})\\
&\overset{\mathrm{(B)}}{\le}\RegSDAC{T}(H_T)\\
&\quad+\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)\alpha^{H_T+1}T.
\end{aligned}
\]
Step (A) uses \eqref{eq:def-regret} with $H=H_T$ and the feasible
comparison point $\mathbf{m}_{H_T}(\mathbf{m})$; (B) uses
\eqref{eq:sdac-truncation-cost-gap} with the same memory horizon.
The final bound is independent of $\mathbf{m}$. Taking the supremum over
$\mathcal{M}_\infty$, using \eqref{eq:def-infinite-regret}, and applying
\eqref{eq:regret-optimized-bound} with $H=H_T$ and the corresponding step
size \eqref{eq:def-optimal-step-size} proves
\eqref{eq:infinite-sdac-regret-bound}.

For the asymptotic rate, $T\ge1$ and $0<\alpha<1$ imply
$\log T/\log(1/\alpha)\ge0$. The ceiling in
\eqref{eq:def-memory-horizon} raises this quantity by at most one;
the outer maximum with $1$ preserves the same upper bound. Hence,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:memory-horizon-rounding}
\frac{\log T}{\log(1/\alpha)}
\le H_T
\le 1+\frac{\log T}{\log(1/\alpha)}.
\end{equation}
In particular, $H_T=1$ when $T=1$. Since $0<\alpha<1$, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha^{H_T+1}T
&\overset{\mathrm{(A)}}{\le}
\alpha^{1+\log T/\log(1/\alpha)}T\\
&=\alpha\exp\!\left(
\frac{\log\alpha}{\log(1/\alpha)}\log T\right)T \\
&=\alpha e^{-\log T}T=\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\]
Step (A) uses the lower bound in \eqref{eq:memory-horizon-rounding} and
the fact that powers of $\alpha$ decrease with the exponent. The final
line uses $\log\alpha=-\log(1/\alpha)$. The upper bound in
\eqref{eq:memory-horizon-rounding} and monotonicity of $\log$ also give,
for fixed $\alpha$ as $T\to\infty$,
\[
\log(H_T+1)
\le\log\!\left(2+\frac{\log T}{\log(1/\alpha)}\right)
=O(\log\log T).
\]
Thus, for fixed $\alpha,L_x,L_u$, the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:infinite-sdac-regret-bound} is
$O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log\log T}\right)=o(T)$.
For fixed-fraction policies, Corollary~\ref{cor:fraction-as-sdac} gives
$\mathbf{m}(k)\in\mathcal{M}_\infty$ for every $k\in[0,\alpha]$. Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\inf_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_\infty}J_T(\mathbf{m})
&\le\min_{k\in[0,\alpha]}J_T(\mathbf{m}(k))\\
&=\min_{k\in[0,\alpha]}\sum_{t=1}^T c_t\bigl(x_t(k),u_t(k)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Subtracting from $J_T^{\mathrm{on}}$ yields the stated fixed-fraction regret bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:sdac-lower-bound}]
Fix a causal feasible controller $\mathcal{A}$. Let
$\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_T$ be independent Rademacher random variables, independent
of the controller's randomization. Set
\[
w_t=1,\qquad c_t(x,u)=\xi_t(L_xx-L_uu),\qquad t\in[T].
\]
Each cost is affine with gradient $\xi_t(L_x,-L_u)$, so it is convex and
satisfies \eqref{eq:model-subgradient-bounds} because $|\xi_t|=1$. Also,
\[
\begin{aligned}
|c_t(x,u)-c_t(x',u')|
&=|L_x(x-x')-L_u(u-u')|\\
&\le L_x|x-x'|+L_u|u-u'|.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus, every realization satisfies \eqref{eq:model-lipschitz} and gives an
admissible sequence.

Consider the two fixed SDAC parameters
\[
\mathbf{m}^{(0)}=\mathbf{0},\qquad
\mathbf{m}^{(1)}=\mathbf{e}_1=(1,0,\ldots,0)\in\mathcal{M}_H.
\]
Both trajectories start at $(x_1,u_1)=(0,0)$. For $t\ge2$,
\eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy} and \eqref{eq:model-dynamics}, with $w_t=1$, give
\[
\begin{aligned}
u_t(\mathbf{m}^{(0)})&=0,
&
x_t(\mathbf{m}^{(0)})
&=\sum_{s=0}^{t-2}\alpha^s
=\frac{1-\alpha^{t-1}}{1-\alpha},\\
u_t(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})&=\alpha,
&
x_t(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})&=1.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last identity follows by induction from $x_2(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})=1$
and $\alpha\cdot1-\alpha+1=1$. Hence, for $t\ge2$,
\[
x_t(\mathbf{m}^{(0)})-x_t(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})
=\frac{1-\alpha^{t-1}}{1-\alpha}-1
=\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha^{t-2})}{1-\alpha}.
\]
Since both policies incur zero cost at $t=1$,
\eqref{eq:def-sdac-cumulative-cost} and \eqref{eq:sdac-lower-beta} give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-cost-difference}
\begin{aligned}
&J_T(\mathbf{m}^{(0)})-J_T(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})\\
&\quad=\sum_{t=2}^T\xi_t
\left(\frac{\alpha L_x(1-\alpha^{t-2})}{1-\alpha}+\alpha L_u\right)\\
&\quad=\sum_{t=2}^T\xi_t\beta_t.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

By causality, $(x_t,u_t)$ depends only on past signs and the controller's
randomization, so it is independent of $\xi_t$. Since $\mathbb{E}[\xi_t]=0$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-online-zero-mean}
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi},\mathcal{A}}[J_T^{\mathrm{on}}]\\
&\quad=\sum_{t=1}^T
\mathbb{E}[\xi_t]\,
\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi},\mathcal{A}}[L_xx_t-L_uu_t]
=0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The two SDAC-policy trajectories are deterministic because $w_t=1$ at every
time and their actions do not depend on the costs. Thus, for $j\in\{0,1\}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-sdac-zero-mean}
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}[J_T(\mathbf{m}^{(j)})]\\
&\quad=\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbb{E}[\xi_t]
\bigl(L_xx_t(\mathbf{m}^{(j)})-L_uu_t(\mathbf{m}^{(j)})\bigr)
=0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Using \eqref{eq:def-regret} and
$\min\{a,b\}=(a+b-|a-b|)/2$, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\RegSDAC{T}(H)
&\overset{\mathrm{(A)}}{\ge}J_T^{\mathrm{on}}
-\min\{J_T(\mathbf{m}^{(0)}),J_T(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})\}\\
&=J_T^{\mathrm{on}}
-\frac{J_T(\mathbf{m}^{(0)})+J_T(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})}{2}\\
&\quad+\frac12
\left|J_T(\mathbf{m}^{(0)})-J_T(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})\right|.
\end{aligned}
\]
Step~(A) restricts the minimum to two parameters in $\mathcal{M}_H$;
this increases the minimum and decreases its negative.
Taking expectations gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-sign-average}
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi},\mathcal{A}}[\RegSDAC{T}(H)]\\
&\quad\overset{\mathrm{(A)}}{\ge}\frac12\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}
\left|\sum_{t=2}^T\xi_t\beta_t\right|\\
&\quad\overset{\mathrm{(B)}}{\ge}\frac{1}{2\sqrt2}
\sqrt{\sum_{t=2}^T\beta_t^2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Step~(A) uses the preceding display, the zero expectations in
\eqref{eq:lower-online-zero-mean}--\eqref{eq:lower-sdac-zero-mean}, and
\eqref{eq:lower-cost-difference}. Step~(B) applies the $p=1$ Khintchine
inequality \cite{szarek1976best} to the deterministic real coefficients
$\beta_2,\ldots,\beta_T$ and independent Rademacher signs. Its constant
$1/\sqrt2$, multiplied by $1/2$, gives $1/(2\sqrt2)$.
The left side of \eqref{eq:lower-sign-average} is the average of
$\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}[\RegSDAC{T}(H)]$ over the $2^T$ sign sequences.
At least one sequence $\boldsymbol{\xi}^\star$ attains or exceeds this
average, with expectation only over the controller's randomization.
Fixing $\boldsymbol{\xi}^\star$ before the interaction gives the required
deterministic oblivious sequence.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:sdac-lower-large-horizon}]
For $t=\left\lceil\log 2/\log(1/\alpha)\right\rceil+2,\ldots,T$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha^{t-2}
&\overset{\mathrm{(A)}}{\le}
\alpha^{\lceil\log 2/\log(1/\alpha)\rceil}\\
&\overset{\mathrm{(B)}}{\le}
\alpha^{\log 2/\log(1/\alpha)}
=\frac12.
\end{aligned}
\]
Steps~(A) and~(B) use that $s\mapsto\alpha^s$ is decreasing for
$0<\alpha<1$: first $t-2\ge\lceil\log 2/\log(1/\alpha)\rceil$,
then $\lceil z\rceil\ge z$ with $z=\log 2/\log(1/\alpha)$.
The equality uses $\log\alpha=-\log(1/\alpha)$.
Thus,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-beta-half}
\begin{aligned}
\beta_t
&\overset{\mathrm{(A)}}{\ge}
\alpha\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{2(1-\alpha)}\right)\\
&\overset{\mathrm{(B)}}{\ge}
\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)
=\frac{G_\alpha}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Step~(A) substitutes $1-\alpha^{t-2}\ge1/2$ in
\eqref{eq:sdac-lower-beta}, using $L_x/(1-\alpha)>0$.
Step~(B) uses $L_u\ge L_u/2$; the equality is
\eqref{eq:def-surrogate-G}.
By \eqref{eq:sdac-lower-horizon}, the number of these indices satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-index-count}
T-\left\lceil\frac{\log 2}{\log(1/\alpha)}\right\rceil-1\ge\frac{T}{2}.
\end{equation}
Theorem~\ref{thm:sdac-lower-bound} therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\!\left[\RegSDAC{T}(H)\right]
&\overset{\mathrm{(A)}}{\ge}
\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{\sum_{t=2}^T\beta_t^2}\\
&\overset{\mathrm{(B)}}{\ge}
\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}
\left[\frac{T}{2}\left(\frac{G_\alpha}{2}\right)^2\right]^{1/2}\\
&=\frac{G_\alpha}{8}\sqrt{T}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Step~(A) is \eqref{eq:sdac-lower-exact}. Step~(B) retains the indices
counted in \eqref{eq:lower-index-count} and applies
\eqref{eq:lower-beta-half}; all discarded squares are nonnegative.
The last equality uses
$(2\sqrt2)^{-1}(T/2)^{1/2}(G_\alpha/2)=G_\alpha\sqrt{T}/8$ and
$G_\alpha>0$.
\end{proof}
