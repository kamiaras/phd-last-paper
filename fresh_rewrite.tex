\begin{abstract}
We study online control of a scalar storage system with nonnegative adversarial
resource arrivals, convex state--action costs, and state-dependent action
constraints. At each time, the controller selects a feasible action before the
current resource arrival and cost are revealed. To handle the resulting coupling
between feasibility and online learning, we introduce \emph{Simplex Disturbance-Action
Control} (SDAC), an $H$-dimensional finite-memory policy class parameterized by a
subprobability simplex. The key feature of SDAC is that every fixed policy in the
class is feasible by construction. Our adaptive controller updates these parameters
by entropic online mirror descent while preserving feasibility along the
time-varying trajectory. We prove regret
$O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log(H+1)}\right)$ relative to the best fixed SDAC policy. As the
retention coefficient $\alpha$ approaches one, the leading factor scales as
$\Theta((1-\alpha)^{-3/2})$. We also derive a minimax lower bound which, when $T$
is at least on the order of $(1-\alpha)^{-1}$, scales as
$\Omega((1-\alpha)^{-1}\sqrt{T})$ near $\alpha=1$, leaving a factor
$(1-\alpha)^{-1/2}$ between the upper and lower bounds. We also show that SDAC
geometrically approximates a decreasing-allocation policy class that spreads each
arrival over time according to a common decreasing allocation sequence, a structure
that arises in stochastic energy-harvesting control. Geometric allocation sequences
induce all feasible proportional state-feedback policies. With
$H=\Theta(\log T)$, the resulting regret against the decreasing-allocation class,
and hence against feasible proportional state-feedback policies, is
$O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log\log T}\right)$. The framework applies to energy-harvesting
batteries and other storage-constrained resource systems.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{Motivation and Problem Setting}
\IEEEPARstart{M}{any} dynamic resource systems can be modeled by a storage state that
evolves over time and constrains the control action at every time.

This creates a constrained discrete-time control problem with state--action
coupling: aggressive early actions can harm later performance, while overly
conservative actions can increase the stage cost. Our objective is to remain
feasible at every time while competing with the best fixed policy in a tractable
class that approximates a broad family of state-responsive allocation rules.

\subsection{Model and Information Structure}
We study a discrete-time storage system with state $x_t \geq 0$, where $x_t$ models
the storage level at the beginning of time $t$. At each time $t$, the controller
chooses a control action $u_t$ under the state-dependent action constraint
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-feasibility}
0 \leq u_t \leq \alpha x_t,
\end{equation}
where $\alpha\in(0,1)$ is a fixed known retention coefficient (so a fraction
$1-\alpha$ of the state is lost each period). After the action is chosen, a
nonnegative resource arrival $w_t$ is revealed, and the state
evolves according to
\[
x_{t+1} = \alpha x_t - u_t + w_t.
\]
Although this quantity is commonly called a disturbance in the online-control
literature, we call $w_t\in[0,1]$ an \emph{arrival} to emphasize its nonnegativity;
we retain the standard names Disturbance-Action Control (DAC) and Simplex
Disturbance-Action Control (SDAC). Nonnegativity is essential to the storage
interpretation and to the feasibility properties of SDAC.

Unlike stochastic models, our formulation is adversarial and non-anticipatory. At
time $t$, the environment may select $w_t$ and $c_t$ using the history through time
$t-1$, but not the current action; the controller then chooses $u_t$, after which
$w_t$ and the entire function $c_t$ are revealed. Both the online and benchmark
trajectories must remain feasible.

\subsection{Application Domains}
This abstraction captures a broad set of domains; in each case, the action is
limited by \eqref{eq:model-feasibility}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Energy harvesting and battery management:} $x_t$ is battery state
        of charge (retention coefficient $\alpha$ after self-discharge), $w_t$ is
        harvested energy, and $u_t$ is discharge or consumption.
    \item \textbf{Queueing networks and buffer management:} $x_t$ is queue/buffer
        occupancy, $w_t$ is
        packet arrivals, and $u_t$ is service.
    \item \textbf{Inventory control:} $x_t$ is on-hand inventory (retention coefficient
        $\alpha$ after spoilage/deterioration), $w_t$ is supply or replenishment, and
        $u_t$ is sales.
    \item \textbf{Water-reservoir management:} $x_t$ is stored water
        (retention coefficient $\alpha$ after evaporation/seepage), $w_t$ is inflow, and
        $u_t$ is controlled release.
\end{itemize}
We take energy harvesting as the running interpretation.

\subsection{Contributions}
Our contributions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Policy class.} We introduce SDAC, a DAC-inspired policy class.
        We prove that every fixed SDAC policy is feasible,
        its induced loss is convex and Lipschitz in the policy parameter, and the
        class geometrically approximates a \emph{decreasing-allocation policy
        class}, defined in Section~\ref{subsec:allocation-sequences}. Its geometric
        sequences induce all feasible proportional state-feedback policies.
    \item \textbf{Online control and regret.} We develop an entropic online mirror
        descent (OMD) controller
        that updates SDAC parameters over the subprobability simplex while maintaining
        feasibility of the online trajectory. Our analysis yields
        $O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log(H+1)}\right)$ regret against the best fixed SDAC policy,
        as stated in Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound}. The challenging regime is
        $\alpha\uparrow1$, where stored resources persist longer and the leading
        factor scales as $\Theta((1-\alpha)^{-3/2})$. The SDAC approximation
        guarantees extend the regret result: choosing $H=\Theta(\log T)$ gives
        $O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log\log T}\right)$ regret against the best
        decreasing-allocation policy and, as a special case, the best proportional
        state-feedback policy; see Corollaries~\ref{cor:regret-vs-allocation}
        and~\ref{cor:regret-vs-linear}.
    \item \textbf{Minimax lower bound.} We prove that $\sqrt{T}$-order regret is
        unavoidable for every causal feasible controller, even when the adversarial
        sequence is deterministic and oblivious; see
        Theorem~\ref{thm:sdac-lower-bound}. For sufficiently long horizons and
        $\alpha\uparrow1$, Corollary~\ref{cor:sdac-lower-mixing} gives the lower
        dependence $\Omega((1-\alpha)^{-1})$, whereas
        Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound} gives the upper dependence
        $O((1-\alpha)^{-3/2})$. Closing the remaining factor
        $(1-\alpha)^{-1/2}$ between these dependences is left for future work.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Related Work}

\paragraph{Online nonstochastic control}
A recent line of work studies online control of linear dynamical systems under
adversarial disturbances and costs. Its performance criterion is cumulative regret
relative to a benchmark policy class chosen in hindsight, under suitable stability
conditions \cite{agarwal2019online,agarwal2019logarithmic}; see
\cite{hazan2026introduction} for an overview. For
known systems in the fully adversarial setting, Simchowitz et al. establish the
optimal $\sqrt{T}$ horizon dependence without resolving all stability-parameter
dependence \cite{simchowitz2020improper}.

A central idea in this literature is DAC,
a policy parameterization that combines a stabilizing state-feedback term with a
finite linear filter of past disturbances. Under standard assumptions, this
representation yields tractable surrogate losses in the policy parameters and
supports efficient no-regret updates. Extensions include partial observability
\cite{lale2020logarithmic} and bandit feedback
\cite{hazan2020nonstochasticBandit}.

Constrained online-control methods have also been developed for affine and general
static state--input constraints \cite{li2021onlineConstrained,
liu2023constrainedControl}. Our focus is the storage-specific constraint
\eqref{eq:model-feasibility}, whose feasible set depends on the current state; SDAC
handles this dependence with a simplex parameterization.

Beyond algorithm design, our lower-bound analysis studies the joint dependence of
regret on the horizon $T$ and the coefficient $\alpha$. Kumar et al.
establish matching memory-dependent upper and lower bounds for online convex
optimization with unbounded memory \cite{kumar2023unbounded}. For discounted
memory, their lower bound is of order $\sqrt{T}/(1-\rho)$, paralleling the
$\Omega(\sqrt{T}/(1-\alpha))$ rate when the horizon is sufficiently long; see
Corollary~\ref{cor:sdac-lower-mixing}. This agreement is at the level of scaling,
while our lower bound is established directly for state--action costs under the
state-dependent storage constraint.

\paragraph{Energy harvesting}
Prior work on online energy harvesting often studies simple rules that adjust
power use to the available battery level. Shaviv and \"Ozg\"ur
\cite{shaviv2016universally} proposed using a fixed fraction of the available
energy at each time and proved performance guarantees that hold for any
independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) energy-arrival model. Arafa et al.
\cite{arafa2018online} allowed more general rewards and characterized how energy
should be spent after an occasional full recharge; the optimal amounts need not
decrease by the same proportion each period. Zibaeenejad and Chen
\cite{zibaeenejad2019lookahead} derive a related decreasing spending rule when
upcoming arrivals are known in advance. Unlike our setting,
these models reveal the current harvested energy before transmission, assume
random arrivals from a fixed model, and optimize a fixed reward that depends only
on the allocated power, rather than adversarially varying costs that depend jointly
on storage state and action.

To demonstrate the expressive power of SDAC, we introduce a decreasing-allocation
policy class as a benchmark inspired by this literature. Each policy allocates
every arrival over future periods according to the same decreasing sequence,
beginning one period later to respect our information pattern.
Section~\ref{subsec:allocation-sequences} shows that finite-memory SDAC policies
approximate every policy in this class with error that decreases geometrically in
the memory horizon.

Lin et al. \cite{lin2025optimal} study online age-of-information optimization in an
energy-harvesting system with related battery dynamics. They compare with an
unrestricted offline optimum through competitive ratio; their objective depends on
the energy allocation but not directly on the battery state, and the current input
is revealed before allocation. In our model, the controller acts before observing
the current arrival and cost, the cost may depend jointly on state and action, and
performance is measured by regret against a fixed policy class.

Yu and Neely \cite{Yu2019} study expected utility maximization under i.i.d. system
states. Asgari and Neely \cite{10.1145/3428337} express the guarantee as an
$O(\sqrt{T})$ regret--battery-capacity tradeoff and improve its dependence on the
decision dimension, allowing arbitrary loss sequences while retaining i.i.d.
energy arrivals. Both works use action-only objectives and fixed-decision
comparators. Here, arrivals are adversarial, costs depend on both state and action,
and the comparator is a fixed state-responsive policy.

\paragraph{Queueing and network optimization}
Queueing control provides a mathematically related perspective based on stochastic
arrivals and real-time service decisions. Foundational work emphasizes throughput
and queue stability \cite{tassiulas1990stability,tassiulas1993dynamic,
mckeown1999achieving}, while stochastic network optimization also studies tradeoffs
between time-average objectives and backlog \cite{neely2010stochastic}. Our setting
differs in its adversarial arrivals, convex state--action costs, and regret benchmark
against a policy chosen in hindsight.

In many queueing applications, the natural retention coefficient is $\alpha=1$
(no abandonment or leakage), so those systems are not direct instances of the
$\alpha\in(0,1)$ model analyzed here. Section~\ref{sec:extensions} discusses the
additional issues that arise when $\alpha\ge1$.

Among other results, Khojastepour and Sabharwal \cite{khojastepour2004delay} study a
time-invariant robust scheduler representable as a linear filter of past arrivals.
It is designed to minimize average transmit power subject to a maximum packet-delay
guarantee, rather than updated online to minimize revealed general costs.

\paragraph{Inventory management}
Yang et al. \cite{yang2020online} study online procurement under inventory
constraints. The controller observes the current demand and price, then procures
supply while ensuring that demand is met; performance is measured by competitive
ratio for aggregate procurement cost. Our controller instead chooses an outflow
before observing the current resource arrival and cost, and permits convex
state--action costs.

Hihat and Fermanian \cite{hihat2024online} consider an
inventory-control formulation with potentially nonlinear and nondifferentiable
state-transition functions and study base-stock policies. While that model is more
general in scope, its decision structure differs from ours: as in
\cite{yang2020online}, the controller chooses supply and then observes demand through
the transition, whereas our controller chooses an outflow and then observes the
resource arrival. Our scalar linear recursion and simplex policy class yield
explicit feasibility and regret guarantees; the two frameworks target different
dynamics, decisions, and policy classes.

\paragraph{Water-reservoir management}
Classical dam theory studies storage dynamics with random inflows and controlled
releases (see, for example, \cite{moran1954dams,prabhu1998stochastic}). Relative to
our setting, this literature assumes stochastic inputs and emphasizes stationary or
long-run performance quantities. It provides historical context for storage control
under state constraints.

\subsection{Paper Organization}
Section~\ref{sec:model-setup} formalizes the storage dynamics, information pattern,
cost assumptions, feasible domain, and regret benchmark. Section~\ref{sec:sdac}
then introduces the SDAC policy class. It derives an affine representation of the
induced state--action trajectory, uses this representation to establish feasibility
and convexity of the surrogate losses, and studies the finite-memory approximation
of decreasing-allocation policies, including proportional state feedback as a
special case. Section~\ref{sec:online-algorithm} presents the feasible online
controller: a clipped SDAC action coupled with entropic OMD over the
subprobability simplex. Section~\ref{sec:regret-analysis} proves the regret upper
bound, establishes a minimax lower bound, compares their dependence on the
retention coefficient, and extends the guarantee to the larger benchmark classes.
Section~\ref{sec:extensions} outlines extensions to noncontractive retention,
vector allocations, and multiple storage states, and
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} summarizes the main conclusions. The Appendix contains
the deferred approximation, lower-bound, OMD, and stability proofs.

\section{Model and Problem Setup}
\label{sec:model-setup}

Let $T$ be a positive integer. The system state $x_t\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$
denotes the resource level at the beginning of time $t$. The initial state
is given as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-init}
x_1 = 0.
\end{equation}
Fix a retention coefficient $\alpha\in(0,1)$. At each time $t\in[T]$,
the controller chooses a control action $u_t\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ subject to the
state-dependent action constraint \eqref{eq:model-feasibility}.
The system then receives an arrival $w_t\in[0,1]$ and evolves according to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-dynamics}
x_{t+1} = \alpha x_t - u_t + w_t.
\end{equation}
For every feasible trajectory,
\[
0\le x_{t+1}\le \alpha x_t+1.
\]
Since $x_1=0$, induction gives
\[
0\le x_t
\le \frac{1-\alpha^{t-1}}{1-\alpha}
\le \frac{1}{1-\alpha},
\qquad t\in[T+1].
\]
Thus, the storage level is uniformly bounded over time. In particular, a
storage device with capacity \(1/(1-\alpha)\) suffices to realize every feasible
trajectory, including the trajectory generated by our online controller, without
overflow.
Together with the state bound above, \eqref{eq:model-feasibility} implies that every
feasible state--action pair belongs to the compact set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-domain}
\mathcal{D}_\alpha
\triangleq
\left\{(x,u):
0\le x\le\frac{1}{1-\alpha},\
0\le u\le\alpha x
\right\}.
\end{equation}

After the control action is selected, a convex cost function
\[
c_t: \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^2 \to \mathbb{R}
\]
is revealed, and the incurred cost is $c_t(x_t,u_t)$. We assume each $c_t$ is convex
in $(x,u)$ and satisfies the following Lipschitz condition on
$\mathcal{D}_\alpha$: there exist constants $L_x,L_u>0$, independent of $t$, such
that for all $(x,u),(x',u')\in\mathcal{D}_\alpha$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-lipschitz}
|c_t(x,u)-c_t(x',u')| \le L_x|x-x'| + L_u|u-u'|.
\end{equation}
We also assume access, at every $(x,u)\in\mathcal{D}_\alpha$, to a cost subgradient
\[
\boldsymbol{\zeta}_t(x,u)
=
\bigl(\zeta_t^x(x,u),\zeta_t^u(x,u)\bigr)
\in\partial c_t(x,u)
\]
satisfying
\[
|\zeta_t^x(x,u)|\le L_x,
\qquad
|\zeta_t^u(x,u)|\le L_u.
\]
The stated subgradient bounds hold, for example, when
\eqref{eq:model-lipschitz} extends to an open neighborhood of
$\mathcal{D}_\alpha$; see \cite[Thm.~9.13]{rockafellar1998variational}.


\paragraph{Assumptions}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Retention coefficient:} $\alpha\in(0,1)$ is fixed and known (a fraction
        $1-\alpha$ of the state is lost each period).\footnote{We focus on
        $\alpha\in(0,1)$; the case $\alpha\ge 1$ is discussed in
        Section~\ref{sec:extensions}.}
    \item \textbf{Arrival bounds:} $w_t\in[0,1]$ for all $t\in[T]$.
        In particular, resource arrivals are nonnegative, in contrast to
        signed process-noise disturbances common in classical control.
    \item \textbf{Information pattern:} $(w_t,c_t)$ may depend on the history
        through time $t-1$, but not on the current action or randomization, and is
        revealed only after $u_t$ is chosen.
    \item \textbf{Cost regularity:} each $c_t$ is convex in $(x,u)$, obeys
        \eqref{eq:model-lipschitz} on $\mathcal{D}_\alpha$, and admits the bounded
        subgradient oracle described above.
    \item \textbf{Feasible policy class:} both the online policy and the benchmark
        policies are required to satisfy \eqref{eq:model-feasibility} under
        \eqref{eq:model-dynamics}.
\end{enumerate}

Let $\{(x_t,u_t)\}_{t=1}^T$ denote the realized trajectory of the online
controller. Define its cumulative cost by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-cumulative-cost}
J_T^{\mathrm{on}} \triangleq \sum_{t=1}^T c_t(x_t,u_t).
\end{equation}
For a fixed positive integer $H$, let $\mathbf{m}$ range over the SDAC
parameter set $\mathcal{M}_H$ (defined in Section~\ref{sec:sdac}). For each
$\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, write
\[
J_T(\mathbf{m})
\triangleq
\sum_{t=1}^T c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{m}),u_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr)
\]
for the cumulative cost of the induced feasible trajectory under the same
arrival sequence. The regret against the best fixed SDAC policy is defined by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-regret}
\RegSDAC{T}
\triangleq
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}
-
\min_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H}
J_T(\mathbf{m}).
\end{equation}
While the online controller may update its SDAC parameters over time, the benchmark
in \eqref{eq:def-regret} is the best single fixed SDAC policy chosen in hindsight.
The objective is to design an online policy that satisfies
\eqref{eq:model-feasibility} for all $t\in[T]$ and achieves sublinear regret as defined in
\eqref{eq:def-regret}.

\section{Simplex Disturbance-Action Control (SDAC)}
\label{sec:sdac}

\subsection{From Disturbance-Action Control to SDAC}
DAC combines a stabilizing state-feedback term with a
finite linear filter of past disturbances. Under standard conditions, this
parameterization yields convex surrogate losses and efficient online updates. In
our scalar storage model, specializing the baseline feedback term to zero gives the
representative form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dac-param}
u_t = \sum_{i=1}^{H} \theta_i w_{t-i},
\end{equation}
where $H$ is a positive integer (the memory horizon) and $\boldsymbol{\theta}=(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_H)^\top\in\mathbb{R}^H$ is a
linear filter.

For our constrained model, an unrestricted filter is insufficient: its action can
violate \eqref{eq:model-feasibility} by exceeding $\alpha x_t$ or becoming negative.
We therefore introduce \emph{Simplex Disturbance-Action Control} (SDAC), which uses a
simplex-constrained filter tailored to the storage dynamics. This restriction ensures
that every fixed policy is feasible while preserving convex surrogate losses and
tractable online updates.


\subsection{SDAC Definition}
Fix a memory horizon $H\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}$. Let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-simplex}
\mathcal{M}_H \triangleq \left\{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^H:\;
\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\le1\right\}
\end{equation}
be the subprobability simplex. For any $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, define the policy $\pi_{\mathbf{m}}$ by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-sdac-policy}
u_t(\mathbf{m})
\;\triangleq\;
\sum_{i=1}^{H} m_i\alpha^i w_{t-i}
=
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}\rangle,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-phi-u}
\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u
\triangleq
\big(\alpha w_{t-1},\alpha^2 w_{t-2},\ldots,\alpha^H w_{t-H}\big)^\top
\in \mathbb{R}^H,
\end{equation}

The induced state trajectory satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-state-update}
x_{t+1}(\mathbf{m}) = \alpha x_t(\mathbf{m}) - u_t(\mathbf{m}) + w_t,
\qquad x_1(\mathbf{m})=0.
\end{equation}
The SDAC policy class is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-sdac-class}
\Pi_H^{\mathrm{SDAC}}
\triangleq
\{\pi_{\mathbf{m}}:\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H\}.
\end{equation}
The weights $\alpha^i$ align the arrival filter with the retention coefficient
$\alpha$ in \eqref{eq:model-dynamics}.


We now establish the two structural properties that make SDAC useful: the induced
control action always satisfies the feasibility constraint
\eqref{eq:model-feasibility}, and the induced cost is convex and Lipschitz in $\mathbf{m}$, so
that regret minimization against the best fixed SDAC policy is an instance of online
convex optimization over the simplex. Both properties follow from an explicit
decomposition of the state trajectory that isolates its dependence on $\mathbf{m}$.

\begin{lemma}[State decomposition under SDAC]
\label{lem:sdac-state-decomp}
Fix a positive integer $H$ and $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$.
For $i\in\{1,\ldots,H\}$, define
\[
r_{t,i}
\triangleq
\sum_{j=1}^{i}\alpha^{j-1}w_{t-j},
\qquad
r_t
\triangleq
\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}\alpha^{j-1}w_{t-j},
\]
where the second sum is empty when $t=1$. Then, for every
$t\in[T]$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-state-decomp}
x_t(\mathbf{m})
=
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i r_{t,i}
+\bigl(1-\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{m}\bigr)r_t.
\end{equation}
Define the quantity
\[
\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x
\triangleq
\bigl(r_{t,1}-r_t,\ldots,r_{t,H}-r_t\bigr)^\top.
\]
Then
\[
x_t(\mathbf{m})
=
r_t+\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x,\mathbf{m}\rangle.
\]
Moreover,
\[
0\le r_{t,i}\le r_t\le\frac{1}{1-\alpha},
\]
and $\mathbf{m}\mapsto x_t(\mathbf{m})$ is affine.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The auxiliary quantities satisfy
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:r-recursion}
r_{t+1}=\alpha r_t+w_t,
\qquad
r_1=0,
\end{equation}
and, for every $i\in\{1,\ldots,H\}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:r-coord-recursion}
\begin{aligned}
\alpha r_{t,i}-\alpha^i w_{t-i}
&=\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha^j w_{t-j} \\
&=r_{t+1,i}-w_t.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For $i=1$, the sum in the first line is empty.

At $t=1$, since $w_{\tau}\equiv 0$ for $\tau\le 0$,
\begin{align*}
r_{1,i}
&=
\sum_{j=1}^{i}\alpha^{j-1}w_{1-j}
=0,\\
r_1
&=0,\\
x_1(\mathbf{m})
&=0,
\end{align*}
so \eqref{eq:sdac-state-decomp} holds. Suppose it holds at time $t$. Using
\eqref{eq:sdac-state-update} and \eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:decomp-induction-1}
\begin{aligned}
x_{t+1}(\mathbf{m})
&=
\alpha\left(
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i r_{t,i}
+\bigl(1-\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{m}\bigr)r_t
\right) \\
&\quad
-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\alpha^i w_{t-i}
+w_t \\
&=
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i
\bigl(\alpha r_{t,i}-\alpha^i w_{t-i}\bigr) \\
&\quad
+\alpha\bigl(1-\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{m}\bigr)r_t+w_t.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:r-recursion} and \eqref{eq:r-coord-recursion} into
\eqref{eq:decomp-induction-1},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:decomp-induction-2}
\begin{aligned}
x_{t+1}(\mathbf{m})
&=
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i
\bigl(r_{t+1,i}-w_t\bigr) \\
&\quad
+\bigl(1-\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{m}\bigr)(r_{t+1}-w_t)+w_t \\
&=
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i r_{t+1,i}
+\bigl(1-\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{m}\bigr)r_{t+1},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
because the total coefficient of $w_t$ in the first line is
$-\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{m}-\bigl(1-\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{m}\bigr)+1=0$. This proves \eqref{eq:sdac-state-decomp} by induction.

All summands defining $r_{t,i}$ and $r_t$ are nonnegative. Since
$w_\tau=0$ for $\tau\le0$, $r_{t,i}$ is a truncation of $r_t$; hence
\[
0\le r_{t,i}\le r_t
\le\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\alpha^{j-1}
=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}.
\]
Finally,
\[
x_t(\mathbf{m})
=
r_t+\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\bigl(r_{t,i}-r_t\bigr)
=
r_t+\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x,\mathbf{m}\rangle,
\]
which is affine in $\mathbf{m}$.
\end{proof}

The first consequence of this decomposition is that every fixed policy is feasible.

\begin{lemma}[Feasibility of SDAC policies]
\label{lem:sdac-feasibility}
For every $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, the trajectory induced by
\eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy} and \eqref{eq:sdac-state-update} satisfies the action
constraint \eqref{eq:model-feasibility}; explicitly,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-feasibility}
0\le u_t(\mathbf{m})\le \alpha x_t(\mathbf{m})
\qquad
\text{for all }t\in[T].
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Because $m_i$, $\alpha$, and $w_{t-i}$ are nonnegative,
\[
u_t(\mathbf{m})
=
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\alpha^i w_{t-i}
\ge0.
\]
For every $i\in\{1,\ldots,H\}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:feas-r-term}
\alpha r_{t,i}
=
\sum_{j=1}^{i}\alpha^j w_{t-j}
\ge
\alpha^i w_{t-i}.
\end{equation}
Because $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$ and $r_t\ge0$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-state-decomp},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:feas-upper-direct}
\begin{aligned}
\alpha x_t(\mathbf{m})
&=
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\alpha r_{t,i}
+\alpha\bigl(1-\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{m}\bigr)r_t \\
&\ge
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\alpha r_{t,i} \\
&\ge
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\alpha^i w_{t-i}
=
u_t(\mathbf{m}),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where the second inequality uses \eqref{eq:feas-r-term}.
\end{proof}

For fixed arrivals, both coordinates of the SDAC state--action pair are affine
in $\mathbf{m}$. The following lemma records the resulting convexity and the constants
used by the online update.

\begin{lemma}[Convexity and Lipschitz continuity of surrogate cost]
\label{lem:sdac-surrogate}
Define
\[
\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
\triangleq
c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{m}),u_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr),
\qquad
\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H,
\]
and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-surrogate-G}
G_\alpha
\triangleq
\alpha\left(\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}+L_u\right).
\end{equation}
Then $\ell_t$ is convex on $\mathcal{M}_H$, and for all $\mathbf{m},\mathbf{m}'\in\mathcal{M}_H$,
\[
|\ell_t(\mathbf{m})-\ell_t(\mathbf{m}')|
\le
G_\alpha\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\|_1.
\]
Moreover, at every $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, one can select
$\mathbf{g}_t\in\partial\ell_t(\mathbf{m})$ such that
$\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\le G_\alpha$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-state-decomp} and \eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy} give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-affine-pair}
x_t(\mathbf{m})=r_t+\langle \boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x,\mathbf{m}\rangle,
\qquad
u_t(\mathbf{m})=\langle \boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}\rangle.
\end{equation}
For every $i\in\{1,\ldots,H\}$, the definitions of $r_{t,i}$ and $r_t$ give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:phi-x-exact-tail}
\begin{aligned}
(\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x)_i
&=
r_{t,i}-r_t \\
&=
-\sum_{j=i+1}^{t-1}\alpha^{j-1}w_{t-j},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where the sum is empty when $i\ge t-1$. Hence
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-coordinate-decay}
\begin{aligned}
\bigl|(\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x)_i\bigr|
&\le
\sum_{j=i+1}^{\infty}\alpha^{j-1}
=
\frac{\alpha^i}{1-\alpha}, \\
0\le(\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u)_i
&=
\alpha^i w_{t-i}
\le
\alpha^i.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Taking the largest coordinate, attained in these upper bounds at $i=1$, yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-coefficient-norms}
\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x\|_\infty\le\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha},
\qquad
\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u\|_\infty\le\alpha.
\end{equation}
Since \eqref{eq:surr-affine-pair} is affine in $\mathbf{m}$ and $c_t$ is convex,
$\ell_t$ is convex.

By \eqref{eq:model-lipschitz} and
\eqref{eq:surr-affine-pair},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-lipschitz-short}
\begin{aligned}
|\ell_t(\mathbf{m})-\ell_t(\mathbf{m}')|
&\le
L_x\bigl|\langle \boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x,\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\rangle\bigr|
+
L_u\bigl|\langle \boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\rangle\bigr| \\
&\le
\bigl(L_x\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x\|_\infty+L_u\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u\|_\infty\bigr)
\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\|_1 \\
&\le
\alpha\left(\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}+L_u\right)
\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\|_1 \\
&=
G_\alpha\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Let $(\zeta_t^x,\zeta_t^u)$ denote the components of the bounded subgradient
$\boldsymbol{\zeta}_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{m}),u_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr)$ from Section~\ref{sec:model-setup},
and set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-subgradient-short}
\mathbf{g}_t\triangleq \zeta_t^x\,\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x+\zeta_t^u\,\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u.
\end{equation}
For every $\mathbf{m}'\in\mathcal{M}_H$, the subgradient inequality and
\eqref{eq:surr-affine-pair} yield
\begin{align*}
\ell_t(\mathbf{m}')
&\ge
\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
+\zeta_t^x\langle \boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x,\mathbf{m}'-\mathbf{m}\rangle
+\zeta_t^u\langle \boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}'-\mathbf{m}\rangle \\
&=
\ell_t(\mathbf{m})+\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}'-\mathbf{m}\rangle.
\end{align*}
Thus $\mathbf{g}_t\in\partial\ell_t(\mathbf{m})$. For each
$i\in\{1,\ldots,H\}$, the exact coordinate decay in
\eqref{eq:surr-coordinate-decay} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-subgradient-coordinate-bound}
\begin{aligned}
|g_{t,i}|
&\le
|\zeta_t^x|\bigl|(\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x)_i\bigr|
+|\zeta_t^u|(\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u)_i \\
&\le
\alpha^i\left(\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}+L_u\right) \\
&\le
\alpha\left(\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}+L_u\right)
=G_\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Taking the maximum over $i$ proves
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-subgradient-bound-short}
\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\le G_\alpha.
\end{equation}
\end{proof}

Regret minimization against the best fixed SDAC policy thus becomes online convex
optimization over the simplex.

\subsection{Expressiveness: Decreasing-Allocation Policies}
\label{subsec:allocation-sequences}

Energy-harvesting policies for Bernoulli recharges can be described by the
sequence of expenditures following each recharge
\cite{arafa2018online,zibaeenejad2019lookahead}. In the corresponding
decreasing-allocation policy class, each unit arriving at time $s$ is assigned the
same decreasing allocation sequence, beginning at time $s+1$ to respect the
information pattern. The sequence may have an arbitrary decreasing shape;
geometric sequences induce proportional state-feedback policies.

Define the set of feasible decreasing allocation sequences
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-allocation-sequences}
\mathcal Q_\alpha^\downarrow
\triangleq
\left\{
\mathbf q=(q_i)_{i\ge1}:
q_1\ge q_2\ge\cdots\ge0,
\quad
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{q_i}{\alpha^i}\le1
\right\}.
\end{equation}
For $\mathbf q\in\mathcal Q_\alpha^\downarrow$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-allocation-policy}
\begin{aligned}
u_t(\mathbf q)
&\triangleq
\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}q_iw_{t-i},
\\
x_{t+1}(\mathbf q)
&=
\alpha x_t(\mathbf q)-u_t(\mathbf q)+w_t,
\qquad x_1(\mathbf q)=0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $q_iw_s$ is the amount from arrival $w_s$ allocated $i$ periods later.
The weighted budget in \eqref{eq:def-allocation-sequences} accounts for leakage:
reserving $q_i$ units for expenditure $i$ periods after an arrival requires
$q_i/\alpha^i$ units at the arrival time. Let
\[
\Pi_\alpha^{\mathrm{alloc}}
\triangleq
\{\pi_{\mathbf q}:\mathbf q\in\mathcal Q_\alpha^\downarrow\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Finite-memory approximation of decreasing-allocation policies]
\label{lem:sdac-allocation-approx}
Every policy in $\Pi_\alpha^{\mathrm{alloc}}$ is feasible. Fix
$\mathbf q\in\mathcal Q_\alpha^\downarrow$ and $H\ge1$, and define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:allocation-truncation}
m_{H,i}(\mathbf q)
\triangleq
\frac{q_i}{\alpha^i},
\qquad i\in[H].
\end{equation}
Then $\mathbf m_H(\mathbf q)\in\mathcal M_H$. With
\[
\operatorname{Tail}_H(\mathbf q)
\triangleq
\sum_{i=H+1}^{\infty}q_i,
\]
the corresponding trajectories satisfy, for every arrival sequence in $[0,1]$,
\begin{align}
0
&\le
u_t(\mathbf q)-u_t\bigl(\mathbf m_H(\mathbf q)\bigr)
\le \operatorname{Tail}_H(\mathbf q),
\label{eq:allocation-action-gap}\\
0
&\le
x_t\bigl(\mathbf m_H(\mathbf q)\bigr)-x_t(\mathbf q)
\le \frac{\operatorname{Tail}_H(\mathbf q)}{1-\alpha}.
\label{eq:allocation-state-gap}
\end{align}
If each $c_t$ satisfies \eqref{eq:model-lipschitz}, then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:allocation-cost-gap}
\begin{aligned}
&\left|
\sum_{t=1}^T c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf m_H(\mathbf q)),
u_t(\mathbf m_H(\mathbf q))\bigr)
-
\sum_{t=1}^T c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf q),u_t(\mathbf q)\bigr)
\right|\\
&\qquad\le
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)
\operatorname{Tail}_H(\mathbf q)T \\
&\qquad\le
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)\alpha^{H+1}T.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

In particular, fix $k\in[0,\alpha]$, set $\lambda_k\triangleq\alpha-k$, and define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:proportional-as-allocation}
q_i(k)=k\lambda_k^{i-1},
\qquad i\ge1,
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_k^0\triangleq1$. Then $\mathbf q(k)\in\mathcal Q_\alpha^\downarrow$
and $\pi_{\mathbf q(k)}$ is exactly the feasible proportional state-feedback policy
$u_t(k)=k x_t(k)$. Its SDAC truncation is
\[
\bigl[\mathbf m_H^{\mathrm{prop}}(k)\bigr]_i
\triangleq
m_{H,i}\bigl(\mathbf q(k)\bigr)
=
\frac{k\lambda_k^{i-1}}{\alpha^i},
\qquad i\in[H],
\]
and its tail is
\[
\operatorname{Tail}_H\bigl(\mathbf q(k)\bigr)
=
\frac{k\lambda_k^H}{1-\lambda_k}.
\]
Therefore, \eqref{eq:allocation-cost-gap} specializes exactly to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-lin-approx-cost}
\begin{aligned}
&\left|
\sum_{t=1}^T c_t\!\bigl(x_t(\mathbf m_H^{\mathrm{prop}}(k)),u_t(\mathbf m_H^{\mathrm{prop}}(k))\bigr)
-
\sum_{t=1}^T c_t\bigl(x_t(k),u_t(k)\bigr)
\right| \\
&\qquad\le
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)
\frac{k\lambda_k^H}{1-\lambda_k}\,T.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The proof is deferred to Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-proofs}.
\end{lemma}

$\Pi_\alpha^{\mathrm{alloc}}$ contains every feasible proportional state-feedback
policy and also permits nongeometric allocation sequences. Together with
Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound}, the tail bound in
\eqref{eq:allocation-cost-gap} yields no regret against
$\Pi_\alpha^{\mathrm{alloc}}$ and, consequently, against feasible proportional
state-feedback policies.





\section{Online Algorithm}
\label{sec:online-algorithm}

We now present our online algorithm, which updates SDAC parameters by
OMD with unnormalized negentropy (equivalently, the
exponentiated gradient method) and a fixed step size $\eta>0$ over the
subprobability simplex $\mathcal{M}_H\subset\mathbb{R}^H$
\cite{hazan2016introduction}. Let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-negentropy}
\Psi(\mathbf{m}) \triangleq \sum_{i=1}^H\bigl(m_i\log m_i-m_i\bigr), \qquad
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^H,
\end{equation}
denote the unnormalized negentropy (with the convention $0\log0\triangleq0$).
Its Bregman divergence is the generalized Kullback--Leibler divergence
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-bregman}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{v})
\triangleq
\sum_{i=1}^H\Bigl(m_i\log\tfrac{m_i}{v_i}-m_i+v_i\Bigr),
\end{equation}
defined for
\[
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^H,
\qquad
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H.
\]


For each time $t\in[T]$, let $\mathbf{m}_t\in\mathcal{M}_H$ be the online parameter.
We initialize at
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-init}
\mathbf{m}_1\triangleq\frac{1}{H+1}\mathbf{1}
\end{equation}
so that
$\sup_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H}D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_1)\le\log(H+1)$
(see the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound}).

\paragraph{Control-action selection}
The controller first forms the nominal SDAC action
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-unconstrained-action}
\widehat{u}_t \triangleq \langle \boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\rangle,
\end{equation}
and clips it to the admissible interval in \eqref{eq:model-feasibility}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-feasible-action}
u_t \triangleq \min\{\widehat{u}_t,\,\alpha x_t\}.
\end{equation}
The state then evolves according to \eqref{eq:model-dynamics}.
Because $\widehat{u}_t\ge 0$, the clipping rule and induction on
\eqref{eq:model-dynamics}, starting from \eqref{eq:model-init} with $w_t\in[0,1]$, yield
$x_t\ge0$ for all $t\in[T+1]$ and show that the online trajectory satisfies
\eqref{eq:model-feasibility}.

The scalar correction in \eqref{eq:alg-feasible-action} also has an exact
Bregman-projection equivalent.

\begin{lemma}[Action clipping as a Bregman projection]
\label{lem:availability-projection}
At time $t$, define the parameter set compatible with the available state by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-availability-set}
\mathcal F_t(x_t)
\triangleq
\left\{
\mathbf m\in\mathcal M_H:
\langle\boldsymbol\phi_t^u,\mathbf m\rangle
\le \alpha x_t
\right\}.
\end{equation}
For the negentropy $\Psi$ in \eqref{eq:def-negentropy}, let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-exec-projection}
\mathbf m_t^{\mathrm{exec}}
\in
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mathbf m\in\mathcal F_t(x_t)}
D_\Psi(\mathbf m\|\mathbf m_t).
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:availability-projection-action}
\left\langle
\boldsymbol\phi_t^u,\mathbf m_t^{\mathrm{exec}}
\right\rangle
=
\min\!\left\{
\left\langle\boldsymbol\phi_t^u,\mathbf m_t\right\rangle,
\alpha x_t
\right\}
=u_t.
\end{equation}
Thus the projection formulation and the scalar clipping rule induce exactly the
same executed action.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The set $\mathcal F_t(x_t)$ is nonempty because it contains $\mathbf 0$, and it is
compact and convex. Since $\mathbf m_t$ is strictly positive coordinatewise,
$D_\Psi(\cdot\|\mathbf m_t)$ is strictly convex on $\mathcal M_H$ and is minimized
uniquely at $\mathbf m_t$ over any feasible set containing $\mathbf m_t$.
Consequently, if
\[
\langle\boldsymbol\phi_t^u,\mathbf m_t\rangle
\le \alpha x_t,
\]
then $\mathbf m_t^{\mathrm{exec}}=\mathbf m_t$, which proves
\eqref{eq:availability-projection-action} in the feasible case.

Suppose instead that $\mathbf m_t$ is infeasible. We claim that the action
constraint is active at $\mathbf m_t^{\mathrm{exec}}$. If it were slack, then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:availability-projection-slack}
\langle\boldsymbol\phi_t^u,\mathbf m_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\rangle
<\alpha x_t
<\langle\boldsymbol\phi_t^u,\mathbf m_t\rangle.
\end{equation}
For sufficiently small $\lambda\in(0,1)$, the point
\[
\mathbf m_\lambda
\triangleq
(1-\lambda)\mathbf m_t^{\mathrm{exec}}+\lambda\mathbf m_t
\]
would still belong to $\mathcal F_t(x_t)$. Strict convexity and
$D_\Psi(\mathbf m_t\|\mathbf m_t)=0$ would then give
\begin{align*}
D_\Psi(\mathbf m_\lambda\|\mathbf m_t)
&<
(1-\lambda)D_\Psi(\mathbf m_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\|\mathbf m_t) \\
&<
D_\Psi(\mathbf m_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\|\mathbf m_t),
\end{align*}
contradicting \eqref{eq:def-exec-projection}. Hence
\[
\langle\boldsymbol\phi_t^u,\mathbf m_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\rangle
=\alpha x_t
\]
in the infeasible case, which completes the proof.
\end{proof}

The implemented rule remains the scalar minimum
\eqref{eq:alg-feasible-action}; the full $H$-dimensional vector
$\mathbf m_t^{\mathrm{exec}}$ is never calculated because only its scalar induced
action is executed. Forming the feature vectors and their inner products, followed
by the OMD update below, takes $O(H)$ time and $O(H)$ memory per round. In
particular, the next unconstrained OMD update uses the internal iterate
$\mathbf m_t$, not $\mathbf m_t^{\mathrm{exec}}$. Therefore the internal iterates
remain on the fixed domain $\mathcal M_H$, and the analysis does not acquire a
time-varying comparator set. The direct implementation and its projection
interpretation produce the same actions, states, and internal OMD iterates.

\paragraph{Surrogate cost and OMD update}
After $(w_t,c_t)$ is revealed, select a subgradient of the convex surrogate loss
defined in Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-surrogate},
$\mathbf{g}_t\in\partial\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)$ satisfying
$\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\le G_\alpha$ as in
\eqref{eq:def-surrogate-G}, whose existence and
construction are given in that lemma. This step uses the revealed cost function and
the full-information subgradient oracle from Section~\ref{sec:model-setup}.
We define the OMD update
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-omd-argmin}
\mathbf{m}_{t+1} \triangleq \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H}
\Bigl\{\eta\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}\rangle + D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_t)\Bigr\}.
\end{equation}
\paragraph{Closed-form solution}
Although \eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin} is a constrained optimization problem, it is easy
to solve analytically because $\Psi$ is separable and the objective is linear in
$\mathbf{g}_t$. As shown in Lemma~\ref{lem:omd-closed-form}, the minimizer in
\eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin} is exactly the result of two elementary steps. First a
multiplicative step,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-omd-mult}
\widetilde{m}_{t+1,i} \triangleq m_{t,i}\exp\!\bigl(-\eta g_{t,i}\bigr), \qquad i=1,\ldots,H,
\end{equation}
where $g_{t,i}$ is the $i$-th coordinate of $\mathbf{g}_t$. This is followed by the Bregman projection onto $\mathcal{M}_H$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-omd-proj}
\mathbf{m}_{t+1}
\triangleq
\operatorname{Proj}_{\mathcal{M}_H}^{\Psi}(\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1})
=
\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}}{\max\{1,\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}\|_1\}},
\end{equation}
where the projection operator is defined and derived in
Lemma~\ref{lem:bregman-projection}; the scaling is nontrivial only when
$\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}\|_1>1$.


\section{Regret Analysis}
\label{sec:regret-analysis}
The following theorem states that the online controller achieves sublinear regret
against every fixed SDAC policy $\pi_{\mathbf{m}}$, where $\mathbf{m}$ is any parameter in $\mathcal{M}_H$.
The regret proof has two parts: a standard online convex optimization bound for the
surrogate costs $\ell_t$, and a stability bound showing that the
online trajectory stays close to the SDAC policy trajectory
driven by the same parameters. Some technical lemmas required for the proof are deferred to
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-proofs}.

\begin{theorem}[Regret bound]
\label{thm:regret-bound}
Suppose the cost functions satisfy the regularity and bounded-subgradient
assumptions of Section~\ref{sec:model-setup}. Then the OMD controller with
action clipping from Section~\ref{sec:online-algorithm}, initialized as in
\eqref{eq:alg-init} and using step size $\eta>0$, satisfies, for the regret defined in \eqref{eq:def-regret},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:regret-theorem-bound}
\RegSDAC{T}
\le
\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta}
+
\eta T\Gamma_\alpha,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-Gamma-alpha}
\Gamma_\alpha
\triangleq
\frac{G_\alpha^2}{2}
+
\frac{\alpha G_\alpha(L_x+\alpha L_u)}{(1-\alpha)^2},
\end{equation}
and $G_\alpha=\alpha\bigl(\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}+L_u\bigr)$ as in
\eqref{eq:def-surrogate-G}.
In particular, the choice
\[
\eta^*
=
\sqrt{\frac{\log(H+1)}{T\Gamma_\alpha}}
\]
gives
\[
\RegSDAC{T}
\le
2\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha T\log(H+1)}.
\]
For fixed $L_x,L_u>0$, the leading factor obeys
\[
\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha}=\Theta(\alpha)
\quad\text{as }\alpha\downarrow0,
\]
and
\[
\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha}
=\Theta\!\left((1-\alpha)^{-3/2}\right)
\quad\text{as }\alpha\uparrow1.
\]
\end{theorem}

The next result shows that the $\sqrt{T}$ dependence is unavoidable and
quantifies the dependence on $1-\alpha$ that any causal feasible controller must
incur. Its proof is deferred to Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-proofs}.

\begin{theorem}[Minimax SDAC regret lower bound]
\label{thm:sdac-lower-bound}
Fix $H\ge1$ and $T\ge2$. For every causal feasible online controller
$\mathcal A$, possibly randomized, there exists a deterministic oblivious sequence
of arrivals and costs satisfying the assumptions of
Section~\ref{sec:model-setup} such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-lower-exact}
\mathbb E_{\mathcal A}\!\left[\RegSDAC{T}\right]
\ge
\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}
\sqrt{\sum_{t=2}^T \beta_t^2},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-lower-beta}
\beta_t
\triangleq
\alpha\left(
L_u
+
\frac{L_x(1-\alpha^{t-2})}{1-\alpha}
\right).
\end{equation}
The expectation is only over the possible internal randomization of $\mathcal A$; the
adversarial sequence whose existence is asserted is deterministic and fixed before
the interaction.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Lower bound for sufficiently long horizons]
\label{cor:sdac-lower-mixing}
Define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-mixing-time}
t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)
\triangleq
\left\lceil
\frac{\log 2}{\log(1/\alpha)}
\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
If $T\ge2t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)+2$, then every causal feasible controller $\mathcal A$
admits a deterministic oblivious sequence for which
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-lower-mixing}
\mathbb E_{\mathcal A}\!\left[\RegSDAC{T}\right]
\ge
\frac{G_\alpha}{8}\sqrt{T},
\end{equation}
where $G_\alpha=\alpha(L_x/(1-\alpha)+L_u)$ as in
\eqref{eq:def-surrogate-G}.
\end{corollary}

The limit $\alpha\uparrow1$ is more challenging because stored arrivals persist
longer. In this limit,
$t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)=\Theta((1-\alpha)^{-1})$, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:sdac-lower-mixing} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-lower-large-alpha}
\mathbb E_{\mathcal A}\!\left[\RegSDAC{T}\right]
=
\Omega\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{T}}{1-\alpha}\right)
\end{equation}
whenever $T\ge2t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)+2$. The upper bound in
Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound} is
$O((1-\alpha)^{-3/2}\sqrt{T\log(H+1)})$, so a factor
$(1-\alpha)^{-1/2}$ between the two bounds remains open, apart from the
memory-dependent logarithm.

The condition $T\ge2t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)+2$ is essential for interpreting the large-$\alpha$
asymptotic. If $T$ is fixed while $\alpha\uparrow1$, then
\[
\frac{1-\alpha^{t-2}}{1-\alpha}\longrightarrow t-2,
\]
and the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:sdac-lower-exact} converges to the finite
quantity
\[
\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}
\sqrt{\sum_{t=2}^T\bigl(L_u+L_x(t-2)\bigr)^2}.
\]
Thus the exact finite-horizon formula, rather than the long-horizon scaling in
\eqref{eq:sdac-lower-large-alpha}, applies in this regime.

The large-$\alpha$ dependence improves the coarser analysis that ignores the exact
coordinate decay in \eqref{eq:surr-coordinate-decay} and the nonexpansiveness of
$z\mapsto[z]_+$ used in \eqref{eq:transition-contraction}. That analysis gives a leading
factor of order $(1-\alpha)^{-2}$ as $\alpha\uparrow1$. The sharper rate follows
from the gradient and stability estimates.


Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-allocation-approx} give the following comparator guarantee.

\begin{corollary}[No-regret against decreasing-allocation policies]
\label{cor:regret-vs-allocation}
Set
\[
H_T\triangleq
\max\!\left\{1,\left\lceil\frac{\log T}{\log(1/\alpha)}\right\rceil\right\},
\]
and run the OMD controller with action clipping, memory horizon $H=H_T$, and the
step size $\eta^*$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound}. For
$\mathbf q\in\mathcal Q_\alpha^\downarrow$, let
\[
J_T(\mathbf q)
\triangleq
\sum_{t=1}^T c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf q),u_t(\mathbf q)\bigr)
\]
and
\[
\RegAlloc{T}
\triangleq
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}
-
\inf_{\mathbf q\in\mathcal Q_\alpha^\downarrow}J_T(\mathbf q).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:allocation-regret-bound}
\begin{aligned}
\RegAlloc{T}
&\le
2\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha T\log(H_T+1)}\\
&\quad+
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)\alpha^{H_T+1}T.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $\Gamma_\alpha$ is defined in \eqref{eq:def-Gamma-alpha}. Since
$\alpha^{H_T}\le1/T$ and
$\log(H_T+1)=O(\log\log T)$ as $T\to\infty$,
the approximation term is $O(1)$. Consequently,
\[
\RegAlloc{T}
=
O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log\log T}\right)
=o(T).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Fix $\mathbf q\in\mathcal Q_\alpha^\downarrow$ and use the SDAC comparator
$\mathbf m_{H_T}(\mathbf q)$ from
Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-allocation-approx}. Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound} and
\eqref{eq:allocation-cost-gap} give
\[
\begin{aligned}
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}-J_T(\mathbf q)
&\le
2\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha T\log(H_T+1)}\\
&\quad+
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)
\operatorname{Tail}_{H_T}(\mathbf q)T\\
&\le
2\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha T\log(H_T+1)}\\
&\quad+
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)
\alpha^{H_T+1}T.
\end{aligned}
\]
The bound is uniform in $\mathbf q$, so taking the infimum comparator cost proves
\eqref{eq:allocation-regret-bound}. Finally,
$H_T\ge\log T/\log(1/\alpha)$ implies $\alpha^{H_T}\le1/T$, while
$H_T=\Theta(\log T)$ implies $\log(H_T+1)=O(\log\log T)$.
\end{proof}

Because geometric allocation sequences induce proportional state feedback, the
same controller also satisfies a gain-dependent bound for that policy family.

\begin{corollary}[No-regret against proportional state-feedback policies]
\label{cor:regret-vs-linear}
Run the controller of Corollary~\ref{cor:regret-vs-allocation}. For
$k\in[0,\alpha]$, let
$J_T(k)\triangleq\sum_{t=1}^T c_t\bigl(x_t(k),u_t(k)\bigr)$, where
$u_t(k)=k x_t(k)$, and define
\[
\RegProp{T}
\triangleq
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}-\min_{k\in[0,\alpha]}J_T(k).
\]
For every $k\in[0,\alpha]$, with $\lambda_k=\alpha-k$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:linear-regret-decomposition}
\begin{aligned}
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}-J_T(k)
&\le
2\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha T\log(H_T+1)} \\
&\quad+
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)
\frac{k\lambda_k^{H_T}}{1-\lambda_k}\,T.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\RegProp{T}
&\le
2\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha T\log(H_T+1)}
\\
&\quad+
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)\alpha^{H_T+1}T
\\
&=
O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log\log T}\right)
=o(T).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For fixed $k$, Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound} applied to
$\mathbf m_{H_T}^{\mathrm{prop}}(k)$, followed by \eqref{eq:sdac-lin-approx-cost}, gives
\eqref{eq:linear-regret-decomposition}. The geometric allocation sequence
$\mathbf q(k)$ belongs to $\mathcal Q_\alpha^\downarrow$, so
$\operatorname{Tail}_{H_T}(\mathbf q(k))\le\alpha^{H_T+1}$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-allocation-approx}. This makes the bound uniform in $k$;
minimizing over $k$ proves the stated finite-time and asymptotic guarantees.
\end{proof}

\section{Extensions}
\label{sec:extensions}

We briefly discuss three possible extensions. Establishing their full regret bounds
is left for future work.

\paragraph{Retention $\alpha\ge 1$.}
For $\alpha\ge1$, choose a fixed baseline drain $Kx_t$ such that
$\bar\alpha\triangleq\alpha-K\in(0,1)$, and write $u_t=Kx_t+v_t$. The residual
dynamics become $x_{t+1}=\bar\alpha x_t-v_t+w_t$. An SDAC filter for $v_t$ should
therefore use the powers $\bar\alpha^i$, not $\alpha^i$. If
$0\le v_t\le\bar\alpha x_t$, then
$0\le u_t\le(K+\bar\alpha)x_t=\alpha x_t$, so \eqref{eq:model-feasibility} holds.
This reduction suggests an extension of
the present analysis with transformed cost and Lipschitz constants; we do not pursue
the details here.

\paragraph{Vector action / multi-task allocation.}
Consider a single storage state $x_t$ with a vector action
$\mathbf{u}_t\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^n$ that allocates resources across $n$ tasks, subject to
$\mathbf{1}^\top \mathbf{u}_t\le\alpha x_t$, so that the total drain from storage is the sum of
the task allocations. The dynamics remain
$x_{t+1}=\alpha x_t-\mathbf{1}^\top \mathbf{u}_t+w_t$, and the cost $c_t(x_t,\mathbf{u}_t)$ is convex in
$(x,\mathbf{u})$. The fixed-policy SDAC construction lifts by replacing the filter vector
$\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$ with a matrix
$\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{n\times H}$ whose entries $M_{a,i}$ sum to at most
one, with each row $a$ corresponding to a task and each column $i$ to a memory lag.
OMD can then run over this set. If the nominal total allocation
exceeds $\alpha x_t$, the action vector is scaled by a nonnegative factor so that
$\mathbf{1}^\top \mathbf{u}_t=\alpha x_t$.

\paragraph{Multiple storages with diagonal dynamics.}
Suppose the state is $\mathbf{x}_t\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^d$ and evolves as
$\mathbf{x}_{t+1}=\mathbf{A} \mathbf{x}_t-\mathbf{u}_t+\mathbf{w}_t$ with $\mathbf{A}=\mathrm{diag}(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_d)$,
$\alpha_i\in(0,1)$, arrivals $\mathbf{w}_t\in[0,1]^d$, and per-coordinate constraints
$0\le u_{t,i}\le\alpha_i x_{t,i}$. The dynamics and feasible set then decouple
by coordinate, and the natural SDAC class is a product of simplices. For a jointly
convex cost, OMD operates jointly on this product set; the optimization separates by
coordinate only when the cost is coordinate-separable. Cross-coupled storage systems
(non-diagonal $\mathbf{A}$) require additional analysis.

These constructions retain the basic simplex/OMD structure, but their complete
feasibility and regret analyses require separate treatment.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied adversarial online control of a storage system under a state-dependent
action constraint. Every fixed SDAC policy is feasible by construction. For the
time-varying online controller, the OMD update maintains the SDAC parameter
constraint, while the clipping rule maintains feasibility of the actual trajectory.
Exact feature decay and the contraction of the online state dynamics yield
$O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log(H+1)}\right)$ regret against the best fixed SDAC policy,
with upper-bound dependence $\Theta((1-\alpha)^{-3/2})$ as
$\alpha\uparrow1$. Once $T$ is at least on the order of $(1-\alpha)^{-1}$, the
minimax lower bound gives
$\Omega((1-\alpha)^{-1}\sqrt{T})$ regret as $\alpha\uparrow1$; hence a factor
$(1-\alpha)^{-1/2}$ between the upper and lower bounds remains open. For fixed
$T$ and $\alpha\uparrow1$, the exact finite-horizon lower bound must be used instead,
because the long-horizon asymptotic does not apply. The
SDAC approximation results further give
$O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log\log T}\right)$ regret against the best decreasing-allocation
policy when $H=\Theta(\log T)$. This class contains all feasible proportional
state-feedback policies and also permits nongeometric allocation sequences. The
model applies directly to
energy-harvesting battery management and related settings in which storage limits
must be respected in real time. Section~\ref{sec:extensions} discusses possible
extensions to $\alpha\ge1$, vector allocation, and diagonal multi-storage systems.

\section{Deferred Proofs}
\label{sec:appendix-proofs}

This appendix proves the minimax lower bound from
Section~\ref{sec:regret-analysis} and the expressiveness lemma from
Section~\ref{sec:sdac},
together with the OMD and stability lemmas leading up to the upper regret bound.

We first prove the lower bound. Following the standard random-sign mechanism for
online linear-optimization lower bounds \cite{hazan2016introduction}, the
construction embeds a two-comparator online linear problem into the two endpoint
SDAC policies $\mathbf 0$ and $\mathbf e_1$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:sdac-lower-bound}]
Fix a causal feasible controller $\mathcal A$. Let
$\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_T$ be independent Rademacher random variables, sampled before
the interaction and independently of the internal randomization of $\mathcal A$.
Consider the oblivious random construction
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-hard-instance}
w_t=1,
\qquad
c_t(x,u)
=
C_\alpha+\xi_t(L_xx-L_uu),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-cost-offset}
C_\alpha
\triangleq
\frac{L_x+\alpha L_u}{1-\alpha}.
\end{equation}
Each $c_t$ is affine and hence convex. For any two points in
$\mathcal D_\alpha$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-lipschitz-check}
\begin{aligned}
|c_t(x,u)-c_t(x',u')|
&=
|L_x(x-x')-L_u(u-u')| \\
&\le
L_x|x-x'|+L_u|u-u'|.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The constant gradient
$\xi_t(L_x,-L_u)$ also obeys the bounded-subgradient assumption. Moreover, for
$(x,u)\in\mathcal D_\alpha$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-cost-nonnegative}
\begin{aligned}
|L_xx-L_uu|
&\le L_xx+L_uu \\
&\le \frac{L_x+\alpha L_u}{1-\alpha}
=C_\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Thus the offset even makes the costs nonnegative on the feasible domain, although
it will cancel from regret.

Because $H\ge1$, both
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-endpoint-parameters}
\mathbf m^{(0)}\triangleq\mathbf 0,
\qquad
\mathbf m^{(1)}\triangleq\mathbf e_1
\end{equation}
belong to $\mathcal M_H$, where $\mathbf e_1$ is the first standard basis vector
in $\mathbb R^H$. Under the arrivals in
\eqref{eq:lower-hard-instance}, the first endpoint has
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-endpoint-zero-trajectory}
u_t(\mathbf m^{(0)})=0,
\qquad
x_t(\mathbf m^{(0)})
=
\rho_t
\triangleq
\frac{1-\alpha^{t-1}}{1-\alpha}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, this follows by unrolling
$x_{t+1}=\alpha x_t+1$ from $x_1=0$. At the second endpoint,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-endpoint-one-action}
u_1(\mathbf m^{(1)})=0,
\qquad
u_t(\mathbf m^{(1)})=\alpha,
\quad t\ge2.
\end{equation}
The state satisfies $x_2(\mathbf m^{(1)})=1$. If it equals one at a time
$t\ge2$, then
\[
x_{t+1}(\mathbf m^{(1)})
=\alpha-\alpha+1
=1.
\]
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-endpoint-one-trajectory}
x_t(\mathbf m^{(1)})=1,
\qquad
t\ge2.
\end{equation}
For $t\ge2$, the state difference between the endpoints is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-endpoint-state-gap}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_t^{\mathrm{end}}
&\triangleq
x_t(\mathbf m^{(0)})-x_t(\mathbf m^{(1)}) \\
&=\rho_t-1 \\
&=\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha^{t-2})}{1-\alpha}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Remove the common offset by defining the centered online and endpoint costs
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-centered-costs}
\begin{aligned}
J_{T,\mathrm{ctr}}^{\mathrm{on}}
&\triangleq
\sum_{t=1}^T\xi_t(L_xx_t-L_uu_t), \\
J_{T,\mathrm{ctr}}^{(j)}
&\triangleq
\sum_{t=1}^T\xi_t
\left(
L_xx_t(\mathbf m^{(j)})
-L_uu_t(\mathbf m^{(j)})
\right),
\quad j\in\{0,1\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
At time $t$, the pair $(x_t,u_t)$ is determined by the past observations and the
controller randomization used before $c_t$ is revealed. It is therefore
independent of the fresh sign $\xi_t$. Conditional mean zero gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-online-mean-zero}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\boldsymbol\xi,\mathcal A}\!\left[
\xi_t(L_xx_t-L_uu_t)
\,\middle|\,
x_t,u_t
\right]
&=0, \\
\mathbb E_{\boldsymbol\xi,\mathcal A}\!\left[J_{T,\mathrm{ctr}}^{\mathrm{on}}\right]
&=0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Likewise, the two fixed endpoint trajectories do not depend on the signs, so
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-endpoint-means-zero}
\mathbb E_{\boldsymbol\xi}\!\left[J_{T,\mathrm{ctr}}^{(0)}\right]
=
\mathbb E_{\boldsymbol\xi}\!\left[J_{T,\mathrm{ctr}}^{(1)}\right]
=0.
\end{equation}

Since the two endpoints agree at $t=1$, equations
\eqref{eq:lower-endpoint-zero-trajectory},
\eqref{eq:lower-endpoint-one-action}, and
\eqref{eq:lower-endpoint-state-gap} give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-endpoint-cost-gap}
\begin{aligned}
J_{T,\mathrm{ctr}}^{(0)}-J_{T,\mathrm{ctr}}^{(1)}
&=
\sum_{t=2}^T\xi_t(L_x\Delta_t^{\mathrm{end}}+\alpha L_u) \\
&=
\sum_{t=2}^T\xi_t \beta_t,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $\beta_t$ as in \eqref{eq:sdac-lower-beta}.

Minimizing over all of $\mathcal M_H$ can only decrease the comparator cost.
After canceling the common term $TC_\alpha$, the regret therefore satisfies
pointwise
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-two-comparator-reduction}
\RegSDAC{T}
\ge
J_{T,\mathrm{ctr}}^{\mathrm{on}}
-
\min\!\left\{
J_{T,\mathrm{ctr}}^{(0)},
J_{T,\mathrm{ctr}}^{(1)}
\right\}.
\end{equation}
Using
$\min\{a,b\}=(a+b-|a-b|)/2$ together with
\eqref{eq:lower-online-mean-zero} and
\eqref{eq:lower-endpoint-means-zero}, we obtain
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-absolute-rademacher}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\boldsymbol\xi,\mathcal A}\!\left[\RegSDAC{T}\right]
&\ge
\frac12
\mathbb E_{\boldsymbol\xi}\!\left[
\left|
J_{T,\mathrm{ctr}}^{(0)}-J_{T,\mathrm{ctr}}^{(1)}
\right|
\right] \\
&=
\frac12
\mathbb E_{\boldsymbol\xi}\!\left[
\left|
\sum_{t=2}^T\xi_t\beta_t
\right|
\right].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Khintchine's inequality for Rademacher sums implies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-khintchine}
\mathbb E_{\boldsymbol\xi}\!\left[
\left|
\sum_{t=2}^T\xi_t\beta_t
\right|
\right]
\ge
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
\left(
\sum_{t=2}^T\beta_t^2
\right)^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
Substitution of \eqref{eq:sdac-lower-beta} into
\eqref{eq:lower-absolute-rademacher}--\eqref{eq:lower-khintchine} proves
\eqref{eq:sdac-lower-exact} under the joint randomization of the signs and the
controller.

It remains to make the adversarial sequence deterministic. For a fixed sign vector
$\boldsymbol\xi\in\{-1,1\}^T$, let
\[
R_{\mathcal A}(\boldsymbol\xi)
\triangleq
\mathbb E_{\mathcal A}\!\left[
\RegSDAC{T}\,\middle|\,\boldsymbol\xi
\right].
\]
The bound just proved states that the average of
$R_{\mathcal A}(\boldsymbol\xi)$ over the $2^T$ sign vectors is at least the
right-hand side of \eqref{eq:sdac-lower-exact}. Hence at least one deterministic
vector $\boldsymbol\xi^\star$ attains that value. Fixing
$\boldsymbol\xi^\star$ in \eqref{eq:lower-hard-instance} produces a deterministic
oblivious admissible sequence, and the only remaining expectation is over the
internal randomization of $\mathcal A$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:sdac-lower-mixing}]
The definition of $t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-mixing-geometric}
\alpha^{t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)}
\le
\alpha^{\log 2/\log(1/\alpha)}
=\frac12.
\end{equation}
Therefore, for every $t\ge t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)+2$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-beta-post-mixing}
\begin{aligned}
\beta_t
&\ge
\alpha\left(
L_u+\frac{L_x}{2(1-\alpha)}
\right) \\
&\ge
\frac{\alpha}{2}
\left(
L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}
\right)
=\frac{G_\alpha}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
There are $T-t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)-1$ indices in
$\{t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)+2,\ldots,T\}$. If
$T\ge2t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)+2$, then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-post-mixing-count}
T-t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha)-1
\ge
\frac{T}{2}.
\end{equation}
Applying Theorem~\ref{thm:sdac-lower-bound} and retaining only these indices,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-mixing-final}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\mathcal A}\!\left[\RegSDAC{T}\right]
&\ge
\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}
\left[
\frac{T}{2}\left(\frac{G_\alpha}{2}\right)^2
\right]^{1/2} \\
&=
\frac{G_\alpha}{8}\sqrt{T}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This is \eqref{eq:sdac-lower-mixing}.
\end{proof}

We next prove the expressiveness result stated in
Section~\ref{subsec:allocation-sequences}.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-allocation-approx}]
Fix $\mathbf q\in\mathcal Q_\alpha^\downarrow$ and set
\[
a_i\triangleq\frac{q_i}{\alpha^i},
\qquad
b_j\triangleq1-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}a_i,
\qquad j\ge1.
\]
The weighted-budget constraint gives $a_i\ge0$ and $b_j\ge0$. Moreover,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:allocation-state-decomposition}
x_t(\mathbf q)
=
\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}\alpha^{j-1}b_jw_{t-j}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the identity is immediate at $t=1$. If it holds at time $t$, then
\begin{align*}
x_{t+1}(\mathbf q)
&=
w_t+
\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}
\bigl(\alpha^jb_j-q_j\bigr)w_{t-j}\\
&=
w_t+
\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}\alpha^jb_{j+1}w_{t-j},
\end{align*}
because $q_j=\alpha^ja_j$ and $b_{j+1}=b_j-a_j$. Reindexing gives
\eqref{eq:allocation-state-decomposition} at time $t+1$.

For each $j\ge1$,
\[
a_j
\le
1-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}a_i
=b_j.
\]
Consequently,
\begin{align*}
0\le u_t(\mathbf q)
&=
\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}\alpha^ja_jw_{t-j}\\
&\le
\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}\alpha^jb_jw_{t-j}
=\alpha x_t(\mathbf q),
\end{align*}
which proves \eqref{eq:model-feasibility} for the decreasing-allocation policy.

For the truncated vector in \eqref{eq:allocation-truncation},
\[
m_{H,i}(\mathbf q)=a_i\ge0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^H m_{H,i}(\mathbf q)
\le
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_i
\le1.
\]
Thus $\mathbf m_H(\mathbf q)\in\mathcal M_H$. Its SDAC action is
\[
u_t\bigl(\mathbf m_H(\mathbf q)\bigr)
=
\sum_{i=1}^{\min\{H,t-1\}}q_iw_{t-i}.
\]
It follows that
\[
0\le
u_t(\mathbf q)-u_t\bigl(\mathbf m_H(\mathbf q)\bigr)
=
\sum_{i=H+1}^{t-1}q_iw_{t-i}
\le \operatorname{Tail}_H(\mathbf q),
\]
where the middle sum is empty when $t\le H+1$. This proves
\eqref{eq:allocation-action-gap}.

Define
\[
e_t^x
\triangleq
x_t\bigl(\mathbf m_H(\mathbf q)\bigr)-x_t(\mathbf q),
\qquad
e_t^u
\triangleq
u_t(\mathbf q)-u_t\bigl(\mathbf m_H(\mathbf q)\bigr).
\]
The two state recursions give
\[
e_{t+1}^x=\alpha e_t^x+e_t^u,
\qquad e_1^x=0.
\]
Since $0\le e_t^u\le \operatorname{Tail}_H(\mathbf q)$,
\[
0\le e_t^x
=
\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\alpha^{t-1-s}e_s^u
\le
\frac{\operatorname{Tail}_H(\mathbf q)}{1-\alpha},
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:allocation-state-gap}. The Lipschitz property now yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\bigl|
c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf m_H(\mathbf q)),u_t(\mathbf m_H(\mathbf q))\bigr)
\\
&\qquad
-c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf q),u_t(\mathbf q)\bigr)
\bigr| \\
&\qquad\le
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)\operatorname{Tail}_H(\mathbf q).
\end{aligned}
\]
Finally,
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{Tail}_H(\mathbf q)
&=
\sum_{i=H+1}^{\infty}\alpha^ia_i\\
&\le
\alpha^{H+1}\sum_{i=H+1}^{\infty}a_i
\le\alpha^{H+1}.
\end{align*}
Summing the per-period cost bound proves \eqref{eq:allocation-cost-gap}.

It remains to identify proportional state-feedback policies within the
decreasing-allocation policy class. For
$k\in[0,\alpha]$, let $\lambda_k=\alpha-k$ and let $\mathbf q(k)$ be defined by
\eqref{eq:proportional-as-allocation}. The sequence is nonnegative and decreasing.
If $k=0$, then $\mathbf q(k)=\mathbf 0\in\mathcal Q_\alpha^\downarrow$. If $k>0$,
then $\lambda_k/\alpha<1$ and
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{q_i(k)}{\alpha^i}
=
\frac{k}{\alpha}
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}
\left(\frac{\lambda_k}{\alpha}\right)^j
=
\frac{k}{\alpha-\lambda_k}
=1,
\]
so again $\mathbf q(k)\in\mathcal Q_\alpha^\downarrow$. Substituting
$u_t(k)=k x_t(k)$ into the dynamics and unrolling gives
\[
u_t(k)
=
k\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\lambda_k^{i-1}w_{t-i}
=
\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}q_i(k)w_{t-i}
=u_t\bigl(\mathbf q(k)\bigr).
\]
The two policies therefore have identical action and state trajectories. Their
SDAC truncations also coincide coordinatewise, and the omitted geometric tail is
\[
\operatorname{Tail}_H\bigl(\mathbf q(k)\bigr)
=
k\sum_{i=H+1}^{\infty}\lambda_k^{i-1}
=
\frac{k\lambda_k^H}{1-\lambda_k}.
\]
Substitution in \eqref{eq:allocation-cost-gap} proves
\eqref{eq:sdac-lin-approx-cost}.
\end{proof}

The remaining lemmas establish the OMD and stability estimates used in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound}; they rely on the state-decomposition, feasibility,
and surrogate-cost lemmas already established in Section~\ref{sec:sdac}
(Lemmas~\ref{lem:sdac-state-decomp}, \ref{lem:sdac-feasibility}, and
\ref{lem:sdac-surrogate}).

\begin{lemma}[Bregman projection onto $\mathcal{M}_H$]
\label{lem:bregman-projection}
Let $D_\Psi$ be the Bregman divergence \eqref{eq:def-bregman} of the unnormalized
negentropy \eqref{eq:def-negentropy}. Fix $\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$. Then the Bregman projection of
$\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}$ onto $\mathcal{M}_H$,
\[
\operatorname{Proj}_{\mathcal{M}_H}^{\Psi}(\widetilde{\mathbf{m}})
\triangleq
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}),
\]
is given by
\[
\operatorname{Proj}_{\mathcal{M}_H}^{\Psi}(\widetilde{\mathbf{m}})
=
\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}}{\max\{1,\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|_1\}}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The projection objective $\mathbf{m}\mapsto D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}})$ is strictly convex, and, for $m_i>0$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:breg-grad}
\frac{\partial}{\partial m_i}D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}})
=
\log\frac{m_i}{\widetilde{m}_i}.
\end{equation}
Its unconstrained minimizer is therefore $\mathbf{m}=\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}$.

If $\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|_1\le1$, this minimizer belongs to $\mathcal{M}_H$, and hence
\[
\operatorname{Proj}_{\mathcal{M}_H}^{\Psi}(\widetilde{\mathbf{m}})
=\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}.
\]
Suppose $\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|_1>1$. The constraint
$\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\le1$ is then active. Moreover, the minimizer has no zero
coordinate: if $m_{i_0}=0$, transferring an arbitrarily small amount of mass
from a positive coordinate to coordinate $i_0$ has directional derivative
$-\infty$, because
\[
\lim_{z\downarrow0}\log\frac{z}{\widetilde{m}_{i_0}}=-\infty.
\]
Thus the nonnegativity constraints are inactive. With $\nu\ge0$ denoting
the multiplier of $\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\le1$, stationarity and complementary
slackness give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:breg-kkt}
\log\frac{m_i}{\widetilde{m}_i}+\nu=0,
\qquad
\|\mathbf{m}\|_1=1.
\end{equation}
The first relation in \eqref{eq:breg-kkt} implies
\[
m_i
=
\widetilde{m}_i \exp(-\nu).
\]
Summing over $i$ and using the second relation in \eqref{eq:breg-kkt},
\[
1
=
\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|_1 \exp(-\nu),
\qquad
\exp(-\nu)
=
\frac{1}{\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|_1}.
\]
Hence $\nu=\log\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|_1>0$ and
\[
\operatorname{Proj}_{\mathcal{M}_H}^{\Psi}(\widetilde{\mathbf{m}})
=
\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}}{\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|_1}.
\]
Combining the cases $\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|_1\le1$ and $\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|_1>1$ proves the stated formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Closed-form solution of the OMD update]
\label{lem:omd-closed-form}
Fix $\mathbf{m}_t\in\mathcal{M}_H\cap\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$, $\eta>0$, and
$\mathbf{g}_t\in\mathbb{R}^H$, and let
$\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}$ be defined coordinatewise by \eqref{eq:alg-omd-mult},
i.e.\ $\widetilde{m}_{t+1,i}=m_{t,i}\exp(-\eta g_{t,i})$. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H}\Bigl\{\eta\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}\rangle+D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_t)\Bigr\} \\
&\quad=
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H} D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1})
=
\operatorname{Proj}_{\mathcal{M}_H}^{\Psi}(\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}),
\end{aligned}
\]
where $\operatorname{Proj}_{\mathcal{M}_H}^{\Psi}$ is the Bregman projection of Lemma~\ref{lem:bregman-projection}.
Consequently, the OMD update \eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin} coincides exactly with
the closed-form OMD update \eqref{eq:alg-omd-mult}--\eqref{eq:alg-omd-proj},
and in particular $\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\in\mathcal{M}_H\cap\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$. Thus the positive
initialization \eqref{eq:alg-init} keeps every algorithm iterate strictly positive.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^H$ and a coordinate $i$. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-cf-log}
\begin{aligned}
\eta g_{t,i}\,m_i
+
m_i\log\frac{m_i}{m_{t,i}}
&=
m_i
\left(
\eta g_{t,i}
+
\log\frac{m_i}{m_{t,i}}
\right) \\
&=
m_i\log\frac{m_i}{\widetilde{m}_{t+1,i}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Substitution into the OMD objective gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-cf-breg}
\begin{aligned}
&\eta\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}\rangle+D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_t) \\
&\qquad=
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1})
+
\sum_{i=1}^H
\left(
m_{t,i}-\widetilde{m}_{t+1,i}
\right).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The final sum is independent of $\mathbf{m}$. Hence the two optimization problems have
the same minimizer, and Lemma~\ref{lem:bregman-projection} gives
\eqref{eq:alg-omd-proj}. Since $\mathbf{m}_t>0$ coordinatewise, both the
multiplicative step and the subsequent positive rescaling preserve strict
positivity.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[One-step movement of OMD]
\label{lem:omd-move}
For the OMD update with step size $\eta>0$ and subgradient
$\mathbf{g}_t\in\partial \ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)$,
\[
\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1 \le \eta\,\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty.
\]
In particular, since $\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\le G_\alpha$,
$\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1\le \eta G_\alpha$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First-order optimality of
\eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin}, evaluated at the feasible point $\mathbf{m}_t$, gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-movement-optimality}
\begin{aligned}
&\left\langle
\eta \mathbf{g}_t+\nabla\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1})-\nabla\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t),
\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}_{t+1}
\right\rangle
\ge0, \\
&D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\|\mathbf{m}_t)+D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}) \\
&\qquad\le
\eta\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\rangle \\
&\qquad\le
\eta\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The second line uses the symmetric Bregman identity.

For every $\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{M}_H\cap\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$ and $\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^H$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-strong-convexity}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{v}^\top\nabla^2\Psi(\mathbf{z})\mathbf{v}
&=
\sum_{i=1}^H\frac{v_i^2}{z_i} \\
&\ge
\frac{\|\mathbf{v}\|_1^2}{\sum_{i=1}^H z_i} \\
&\ge
\|\mathbf{v}\|_1^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The first inequality is Cauchy--Schwarz. The segment joining $\mathbf{m}_t$ and
$\mathbf{m}_{t+1}$ lies in $\mathcal{M}_H\cap\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:omd-closed-form}; integrating
\eqref{eq:omd-strong-convexity} along this segment yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\|\mathbf{m}_t)
&\ge
\frac12\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1^2, \\
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1})
&\ge
\frac12\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Combining these inequalities with \eqref{eq:omd-movement-optimality},
\[
\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1^2
\le
\eta\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1.
\]
The claim is immediate if $\mathbf{m}_{t+1}=\mathbf{m}_t$; otherwise divide by
$\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[OMD regret for the surrogate losses]
\label{lem:omd-surrogate-regret}
For every $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, the update \eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin} satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-surrogate-regret}
\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)
-\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
\le
\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_1)}{\eta}
+
\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{t=1}^T\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty^2.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Convexity of $\ell_t$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-convexity-step}
\begin{aligned}
\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
&\le
\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}\rangle \\
&=
\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\rangle \\
&\quad+
\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
First-order optimality of \eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin}, evaluated at
$\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-three-point-step}
\begin{aligned}
\eta\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}\rangle
&\le
\langle \nabla\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1})-\nabla\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t),\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\rangle \\
&=
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_t)
-D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}) \\
&\qquad
-D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\|\mathbf{m}_t).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The equality is the three-point Bregman identity. Substituting
\eqref{eq:omd-three-point-step} into \eqref{eq:omd-convexity-step},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-one-step-regret}
\begin{aligned}
\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
&\le
\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_t)}{\eta}
-\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1})}{\eta} \\
&\quad+
\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\rangle
\\
&\quad
-\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\|\mathbf{m}_t)}{\eta}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The strong-convexity calculation
\eqref{eq:omd-strong-convexity} and H\"older's inequality give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-young-step}
\begin{aligned}
&\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\rangle
-\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\|\mathbf{m}_t)}{\eta} \\
&\qquad\le
\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1
-\frac{\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1^2}{2\eta} \\
&\qquad\le
\frac{\eta}{2}\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The last inequality is Young's inequality. Combining
\eqref{eq:omd-one-step-regret} and \eqref{eq:omd-young-step}, and then summing
over $t$, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)
-\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
&\le
\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_1)}{\eta} \\
&\quad
-\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_{T+1})}{\eta} \\
&\quad+
\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{t=1}^T\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_{T+1})\ge0$, this proves
\eqref{eq:omd-surrogate-regret}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Positive-part representation of the dynamics]
\label{lem:positive-part-dynamics}
For $\mathbf m\in\mathcal M_H$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-contractive-transition}
F_t(x;\mathbf m)
\triangleq
\left[\alpha x-\langle\boldsymbol\phi_t^u,\mathbf m\rangle\right]_+
+w_t.
\end{equation}
Then the online state and every fixed-policy state satisfy
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:positive-part-transitions}
x_{t+1}=F_t(x_t;\mathbf m_t),
\qquad
x_{t+1}(\mathbf m)=F_t(x_t(\mathbf m);\mathbf m).
\end{equation}
Moreover, for all $x,x'\ge0$ and $\mathbf m,\mathbf m'\in\mathcal M_H$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:transition-contraction}
\begin{aligned}
&|F_t(x;\mathbf m)-F_t(x';\mathbf m')| \\
&\quad\le
\left|
\alpha(x-x')
-\langle\boldsymbol\phi_t^u,\mathbf m-\mathbf m'\rangle
\right| \\
&\quad\le
\alpha|x-x'|
+\|\boldsymbol\phi_t^u\|_\infty
\|\mathbf m-\mathbf m'\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For the online trajectory, \eqref{eq:alg-feasible-action} implies
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\alpha x_t-u_t
&=
\alpha x_t-
\min\!\left\{
\langle\boldsymbol\phi_t^u,\mathbf m_t\rangle,
\alpha x_t
\right\} \\
&=
\left[
\alpha x_t-
\langle\boldsymbol\phi_t^u,\mathbf m_t\rangle
\right]_+.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For a fixed $\mathbf m$, Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-feasibility} gives
\[
\alpha x_t(\mathbf m)
-\langle\boldsymbol\phi_t^u,\mathbf m\rangle
\ge0,
\]
so inserting a positive part does not change its state update. This proves
\eqref{eq:positive-part-transitions}. Finally, the map $z\mapsto[z]_+$ is
nonexpansive:
\[
|[z]_+-[z']_+|\le|z-z'|.
\]
Apply this inequality to the two arguments in
\eqref{eq:def-contractive-transition}, and then apply H\"older's inequality, to
obtain \eqref{eq:transition-contraction}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Contractive state and action deviation]
\label{lem:contractive-deviation}
For the online trajectory and the counterfactual fixed-policy trajectory
associated with the current internal iterate, one has, for every $t\in[T]$,
\begin{align}
\left|x_t-x_t(\mathbf m_t)\right|
&\le
\frac{\alpha\eta G_\alpha}{(1-\alpha)^2},
\label{eq:contractive-state-bound}\\
0\le u_t(\mathbf m_t)-u_t
&\le
\frac{\alpha^2\eta G_\alpha}{(1-\alpha)^2}.
\label{eq:contractive-action-bound}
\end{align}
Here $x_t(\mathbf m_t)$ is generated by using the single fixed parameter
$\mathbf m_t$ from time $1$ through time $t$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Define
\[
d_t\triangleq|x_t-x_t(\mathbf m_t)|.
\]
Since $x_1=x_1(\mathbf m_1)=0$, one has $d_1=0$. For
$t\in\{1,\ldots,T-1\}$, Lemma~\ref{lem:positive-part-dynamics} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:contractive-deviation-start}
\begin{aligned}
d_{t+1}
&=
\left|
F_t(x_t;\mathbf m_t)
-F_t(x_t(\mathbf m_{t+1});\mathbf m_{t+1})
\right| \\
&\le
\alpha\left|x_t-x_t(\mathbf m_{t+1})\right|
+\|\boldsymbol\phi_t^u\|_\infty
\|\mathbf m_{t+1}-\mathbf m_t\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The affine state representation \eqref{eq:surr-affine-pair} implies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fixed-state-parameter-difference}
\begin{aligned}
\left|x_t(\mathbf m_t)-x_t(\mathbf m_{t+1})\right|
&=
\left|
\left\langle
\boldsymbol\phi_t^x,
\mathbf m_t-\mathbf m_{t+1}
\right\rangle
\right| \\
&\le
\|\boldsymbol\phi_t^x\|_\infty
\|\mathbf m_{t+1}-\mathbf m_t\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Combining the triangle inequality with
\eqref{eq:contractive-deviation-start} and
\eqref{eq:fixed-state-parameter-difference} yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:contractive-deviation-recursion-raw}
\begin{aligned}
d_{t+1}
&\le
\alpha d_t \\
&\quad+
\left(
\alpha\|\boldsymbol\phi_t^x\|_\infty
+\|\boldsymbol\phi_t^u\|_\infty
\right)
\|\mathbf m_{t+1}-\mathbf m_t\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:surr-coefficient-norms},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:combined-feature-bound}
\begin{aligned}
\alpha\|\boldsymbol\phi_t^x\|_\infty
+\|\boldsymbol\phi_t^u\|_\infty
&\le
\frac{\alpha^2}{1-\alpha}+\alpha \\
&=
\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Lemma~\ref{lem:omd-move} and
$\|\mathbf g_t\|_\infty\le G_\alpha$ therefore reduce
\eqref{eq:contractive-deviation-recursion-raw} to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:contractive-deviation-recursion}
d_{t+1}
\le
\alpha d_t
+\frac{\alpha\eta G_\alpha}{1-\alpha}.
\end{equation}
Unrolling from $d_1=0$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:contractive-state-unroll}
\begin{aligned}
d_t
&\le
\frac{\alpha\eta G_\alpha}{1-\alpha}
\sum_{j=0}^{t-2}\alpha^j \\
&\le
\frac{\alpha\eta G_\alpha}{(1-\alpha)^2},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with an empty sum when $t=1$. This proves
\eqref{eq:contractive-state-bound}.

Finally, $\widehat u_t=u_t(\mathbf m_t)$ and the feasibility of fixed SDAC policies imply
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:contractive-action-start}
\begin{aligned}
0\le u_t(\mathbf m_t)-u_t
&=
\left[u_t(\mathbf m_t)-\alpha x_t\right]_+ \\
&\le
\left[\alpha x_t(\mathbf m_t)-\alpha x_t\right]_+ \\
&\le
\alpha d_t.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{eq:contractive-action-start} with
\eqref{eq:contractive-state-bound} proves
\eqref{eq:contractive-action-bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Stability term]
\label{lem:stab-sum}
For all horizons $T$,
\[
\sum_{t=1}^T \Bigl(c_t(x_t,u_t)
-\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)\Bigr)
\le
\frac{\alpha\eta T G_\alpha(L_x+\alpha L_u)}{(1-\alpha)^2}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Both state--action pairs lie in $\mathcal{D}_\alpha$. Hence
\eqref{eq:model-lipschitz} and Lemma~\ref{lem:contractive-deviation} give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stability-one-step-short}
\begin{aligned}
&c_t(x_t,u_t)
-\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t) \\
&\qquad\le
L_x\bigl|x_t-x_t(\mathbf m_t)\bigr|
+
L_u\bigl|u_t-u_t(\mathbf{m}_t)\bigr| \\
&\qquad\le
\frac{\alpha\eta G_\alpha L_x}{(1-\alpha)^2}
+
\frac{\alpha^2\eta G_\alpha L_u}{(1-\alpha)^2} \\
&\qquad=
\frac{\alpha\eta G_\alpha(L_x+\alpha L_u)}{(1-\alpha)^2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Summing \eqref{eq:stability-one-step-short} over $t=1,\ldots,T$ proves the
claim.
\end{proof}



\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound}]
Define the stability term
\[
\operatorname{Stab}_T
\triangleq
\sum_{t=1}^T
\left(
c_t(x_t,u_t)
-\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)
\right).
\]
Using
$\ell_t(\mathbf{m})=c_t(x_t(\mathbf{m}),u_t(\mathbf{m}))$, the regret decomposes as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:regret-decomp}
\begin{aligned}
\RegSDAC{T}
&=
\operatorname{Stab}_T
+
\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)
-
\min_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H}\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

We first bound the initial Bregman divergence. Fix $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$. Since
$\mathbf{m}_1=\frac{1}{H+1}\mathbf{1}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:breg-init-expand}
\begin{aligned}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_1)
&=
\sum_{i=1}^H
m_i\log\bigl((H+1)m_i\bigr) \\
&\quad
-\|\mathbf{m}\|_1+\frac{H}{H+1} \\
&=
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\log m_i
+\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\log(H+1) \\
&\quad
-\|\mathbf{m}\|_1+\frac{H}{H+1}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Because $z\log z\le0$ for $z\in[0,1]$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:breg-init-affine}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_1)
\le
\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\log(H+1)-\|\mathbf{m}\|_1+\frac{H}{H+1}.
\end{equation}
The right-hand side is affine in $\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\in[0,1]$, and its values at the
endpoints are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{H}{H+1}
&\le
\log(H+1), \\
\log(H+1)-\frac{1}{H+1}
&\le
\log(H+1).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here the first inequality follows from
$\log z\ge1-z^{-1}$ for $z\ge1$. Therefore,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:breg-init-bound}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_1)
\le
\log(H+1)
\qquad
\text{for every }\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H.
\end{equation}

Lemma~\ref{lem:omd-surrogate-regret},
$\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\le G_\alpha$, and \eqref{eq:breg-init-bound} imply, for every
$\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-ell-raw}
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{t=1}^T\bigl(\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-\ell_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr)
&\le
\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_1)}{\eta}
+
\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{t=1}^T\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty^2 \\
&\le
\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta}
+
\frac{\eta G_\alpha^2T}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Since \eqref{eq:omd-ell-raw} holds uniformly over $\mathbf{m}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-term}
\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)
-
\min_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H}\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
\le
\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta}
+
\frac{\eta G_\alpha^2T}{2}.
\end{equation}
Lemma~\ref{lem:stab-sum} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stab-term}
\operatorname{Stab}_T
\le
\frac{\alpha\eta T G_\alpha(L_x+\alpha L_u)}{(1-\alpha)^2}.
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:omd-term} and \eqref{eq:stab-term} into
\eqref{eq:regret-decomp},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:regret-eta}
\begin{aligned}
\RegSDAC{T}
&\le
\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta}
+\frac{\eta T G_\alpha^2}{2} \\
&\quad
+\frac{\alpha\eta T G_\alpha(L_x+\alpha L_u)}{(1-\alpha)^2} \\
&=
\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta}
+\eta T\Gamma_\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Differentiating with respect to $\eta$ yields
\[
-\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta^2}
+
T\Gamma_\alpha.
\]
The unique minimizer is therefore
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:eta-star}
\eta^*
=
\sqrt{
\frac{\log(H+1)}
{T\Gamma_\alpha}
}.
\end{equation}
At this value the two summands in \eqref{eq:regret-eta} are equal, so
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:regret-opt}
\RegSDAC{T}
\le
2\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha T\log(H+1)}.
\end{equation}

It remains to verify the dependence on $\alpha$. As $\alpha\downarrow0$,
\begin{align*}
\frac{G_\alpha}{\alpha}
&=
\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}+L_u
\longrightarrow L_x+L_u, \\
\frac{\Gamma_\alpha}{\alpha^2}
&=
\frac12\left(\frac{G_\alpha}{\alpha}\right)^2
+
\frac{G_\alpha}{\alpha}
\frac{L_x+\alpha L_u}{(1-\alpha)^2} \\
&\longrightarrow
\frac{(L_x+L_u)^2}{2}+L_x(L_x+L_u)>0.
\end{align*}
Therefore $\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha}=\Theta(\alpha)$ as
$\alpha\downarrow0$. As $\alpha\uparrow1$, write
$\varepsilon=1-\alpha$. Then
\begin{align*}
\varepsilon G_\alpha
&=
\alpha(L_x+\varepsilon L_u)
\longrightarrow L_x, \\
\varepsilon^3\Gamma_\alpha
&=
\frac{\varepsilon^3G_\alpha^2}{2}
+\alpha(\varepsilon G_\alpha)(L_x+\alpha L_u) \\
&\longrightarrow
L_x(L_x+L_u)>0.
\end{align*}
Thus
$\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha}=\Theta((1-\alpha)^{-3/2})$ as
$\alpha\uparrow1$.
\end{proof}
