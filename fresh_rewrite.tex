\begin{abstract}
We study online control of a scalar storage system under nonnegative adversarial
arrivals, convex state--action costs, and state-dependent action constraints. At each
time, the controller chooses a feasible action before the current arrival and cost
are revealed. We introduce \emph{Simplex Disturbance-Action Control} (SDAC), a
simplex-constrained finite-memory policy class whose fixed policies satisfy the
per-step action constraint by construction. For online adaptation, an entropic
online mirror descent (OMD) update maintains the SDAC parameters on the
subprobability simplex, while scalar action clipping ensures feasibility of the
actual time-varying trajectory. We prove
$O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log(H+1)}\right)$ regret against the best fixed SDAC policy, where
$H$ is the memory horizon. We further show that SDAC geometrically approximates
every feasible proportional state-feedback policy; choosing $H=\Theta(\log T)$
yields $O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log\log T}\right)$ regret against the best policy in this
class. The setting is motivated by energy-harvesting battery management and related
storage systems.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Online control, disturbance-action policies, storage systems, energy harvesting,
regret minimization, state-dependent action constraints.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{Motivation and Problem Setting}
\IEEEPARstart{M}{any} dynamic resource systems can be modeled by a storage state that
evolves over time and constrains the control action at every time. The controller
must choose an action subject to a state-dependent action constraint before observing the
corresponding arrival and cost.

This creates a constrained discrete-time control problem with state--action
coupling: aggressive early actions can harm later performance, while overly
conservative actions can increase the stage cost. Our objective is to remain
feasible at every time while competing with the best fixed policy in a tractable
class that approximates feasible proportional state-feedback policies.

\subsection{Model and Information Structure}
We study a discrete-time storage system with state $x_t \geq 0$, where $x_t$ models
the storage level at the beginning of time $t$. At each time $t$, the controller
chooses a control action $u_t$ under the state-dependent action constraint
\[
0 \leq u_t \leq \alpha x_t,
\]
where $\alpha\in(0,1)$ is a fixed known retention coefficient (so a fraction
$1-\alpha$ of the state is lost each period). After the action is chosen, a
nonnegative adversarial arrival $w_t$ (a resource inflow) is revealed, and the state
evolves according to
\[
x_{t+1} = \alpha x_t - u_t + w_t.
\]
Although this input is commonly called a disturbance in the online-control
literature, we call $w_t\in[0,1]$ an \emph{arrival} to emphasize its nonnegativity;
we retain the standard names Disturbance-Action Control (DAC) and Simplex
Disturbance-Action Control (SDAC). Nonnegativity is essential to the storage
interpretation and to the feasibility properties of SDAC.

Unlike stochastic models, our formulation is adversarial and non-anticipatory. At
time $t$, the environment may select $w_t$ and $c_t$ using the history through time
$t-1$, but not the current action; the controller then chooses $u_t$, after which
$w_t$ and the entire function $c_t$ are revealed. The goal is low cumulative cost
relative to the best fixed policy in a class defined below, with both the online and
benchmark trajectories remaining feasible.

\subsection{Application Domains}
This abstraction captures a broad set of domains:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Energy harvesting and battery management:} $x_t$ is battery state
        of charge (retention coefficient $\alpha$ after self-discharge), $w_t$ is
        harvested energy, and $u_t$ is discharge or consumption bounded by available
        energy $\alpha x_t$.
    \item \textbf{Queueing networks and buffer management:} $x_t$ is queue/buffer
        occupancy, $w_t$ is
        packet arrivals, and $u_t$ is service bounded by $\alpha x_t$.
    \item \textbf{Inventory control:} $x_t$ is on-hand inventory (retention coefficient
        $\alpha$ after spoilage/deterioration), $w_t$ is supply or replenishment, and
        $u_t$ is sales bounded by $\alpha x_t$.
    \item \textbf{Water-reservoir management:} $x_t$ is stored water
        (retention coefficient $\alpha$ after evaporation/seepage), $w_t$ is inflow, and
        $u_t$ is controlled release bounded by $\alpha x_t$.
\end{itemize}
We take energy harvesting as the running interpretation; the other domains share the
same storage dynamics and state-dependent action constraint.

\subsection{Contributions}
Our contributions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Policy class.} We introduce SDAC, a DAC-inspired policy class for
        storage dynamics with the state-dependent action constraint
        $0\le u_t\le\alpha x_t$. We prove that every fixed SDAC policy is feasible,
        its induced loss is convex and Lipschitz in the policy parameter, and the
        class geometrically approximates feasible proportional state-feedback
        policies.
    \item \textbf{Online control and regret.} We develop an entropic OMD controller
        over SDAC parameters. A Bregman projection maintains the simplex constraint
        on the parameters, and scalar action clipping maintains feasibility of the actual
        trajectory. The controller achieves
        $O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log(H+1)}\right)$ regret against the best fixed SDAC policy
        and, with $H=\Theta(\log T)$, achieves
        $O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log\log T}\right)$ regret against the best feasible
        proportional state-feedback policy.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Related Work}

\paragraph{Online nonstochastic control}
A recent line of work studies online control of linear dynamical systems under
adversarial disturbances and costs. Its performance criterion is cumulative regret
relative to a benchmark policy class chosen in hindsight, under suitable stability
conditions \cite{agarwal2019online,agarwal2019logarithmic,
simchowitz2020improper}; see \cite{hazan2026introduction} for an overview.

A central idea in this literature is \emph{Disturbance-Action Control}
(DAC),\footnote{``Disturbance-Action Control'' is the standard name in the
online-control literature. In this paper the underlying signal is the nonnegative
arrival $w_t$; we retain the DAC/SDAC names for continuity with that literature.}
a policy parameterization that combines a stabilizing state-feedback term with a
finite linear filter of past disturbances. Under standard assumptions, this
representation yields tractable surrogate losses in the policy parameters and
supports efficient no-regret updates. Extensions include partial observability
\cite{lale2020logarithmic} and bandit feedback
\cite{hazan2020nonstochasticBandit}.

Constrained online-control methods have also been developed for affine and general
static state--input constraints \cite{li2021onlineConstrained,
liu2023constrainedControl}. Our focus is the storage-specific constraint
$0\le u_t\le\alpha x_t$, whose feasible action set depends on the current state.
Exploiting nonnegative arrivals, SDAC uses a simplex parameterization to make every
fixed policy feasible; scalar action clipping preserves feasibility when the
parameter changes online.

\paragraph{Energy harvesting}
Lin et al. \cite{lin2025optimal} study online age-of-information optimization in an
energy-harvesting system with related battery dynamics. They compare with an
unrestricted offline optimum through competitive ratio; their objective depends on
the energy allocation but not directly on the battery state, and the current input
is revealed before allocation. In our model, the controller acts before observing
the current arrival and cost, the cost may depend jointly on state and action, and
performance is measured by regret against a fixed policy class.

Yu and Neely \cite{Yu2019} study expected utility maximization under i.i.d. system
states. Asgari and Neely \cite{10.1145/3428337} express the guarantee as an
$O(\sqrt{T})$ regret--battery-capacity tradeoff and improve its dependence on the
decision dimension, allowing arbitrary loss sequences while retaining i.i.d.
energy arrivals. Both works use action-only objectives and fixed-decision
comparators. Here, arrivals are adversarial, costs depend on both state and action,
and the comparator is a fixed state-responsive policy.

\paragraph{Queueing and network optimization}
Queueing control provides a mathematically related perspective based on stochastic
arrivals and real-time service decisions. Foundational work emphasizes throughput
and queue stability \cite{tassiulas1990stability,tassiulas1993dynamic,
mckeown1999achieving}, while stochastic network optimization also studies tradeoffs
between time-average objectives and backlog \cite{neely2010stochastic}. Our setting
differs in its adversarial arrivals, convex state--action costs, and regret benchmark
against a policy chosen in hindsight.

In many queueing applications, the natural retention coefficient is $\alpha=1$
(no abandonment or leakage), so those systems are not direct instances of the
$\alpha\in(0,1)$ model analyzed here. Section~\ref{sec:extensions} discusses the
additional issues that arise when $\alpha\ge1$.

Among other results, Khojastepour and Sabharwal \cite{khojastepour2004delay} study a
time-invariant robust scheduler representable as a linear filter of past arrivals.
It is designed to minimize average transmit power subject to a maximum packet-delay
guarantee, rather than updated online to minimize revealed general costs.

\paragraph{Inventory management}
Yang et al. \cite{yang2020online} study online procurement under inventory
constraints. The controller observes the current demand and price, then procures
supply while ensuring that demand is met; performance is measured by competitive
ratio for aggregate procurement cost. Our controller instead chooses an outflow
before observing the current inflow and cost, and permits convex state--action
costs.

Hihat and Fermanian \cite{hihat2024online} consider an
inventory-control formulation with potentially nonlinear and nondifferentiable
state-transition functions and study base-stock policies. While that model is more
general in scope, its decision structure differs from ours: as in
\cite{yang2020online}, the controller chooses supply and then observes demand through
the transition, whereas our controller chooses an outflow and then observes incoming
supply. Our scalar linear recursion and simplex policy class yield explicit
feasibility and regret guarantees; the two frameworks target different dynamics,
decisions, and policy classes.

\paragraph{Water-reservoir management}
Classical dam theory studies storage dynamics with random inflows and controlled
releases (see, for example, \cite{moran1954dams,prabhu1998stochastic}). Relative to
our setting, this literature assumes stochastic inputs and emphasizes stationary or
long-run performance quantities. It provides historical context for storage control
under state constraints.

\section{Model and Problem Setup}
\label{sec:model-setup}

We consider a discrete-time process indexed by $t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$. The system
state $x_t\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ denotes the resource level at the beginning of
time $t$. The initial state is given as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-init}
x_1 = 0.
\end{equation}
Fix a retention coefficient $\alpha\in(0,1)$. At each time $t$, the controller
chooses a control action $u_t\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ subject to the state-dependent
action constraint
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-feasibility}
0 \le u_t \le \alpha x_t.
\end{equation}
The system then receives an arrival $w_t\in[0,1]$ and evolves according to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-dynamics}
x_{t+1} = \alpha x_t - u_t + w_t.
\end{equation}
For every feasible trajectory,
\[
0\le x_{t+1}\le \alpha x_t+1.
\]
Since $x_1=0$, induction gives
\[
0\le x_t\le \frac{1-\alpha^{t-1}}{1-\alpha}
\le \frac{1}{1-\alpha},
\qquad
0\le u_t\le \frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}.
\]
Define the reachable state--action domain
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-domain}
\mathcal{D}_\alpha
\triangleq
\left\{(x,u):0\le x\le\frac{1}{1-\alpha},\ 0\le u\le\alpha x\right\}.
\end{equation}

After the control action is selected, a convex cost function
\[
c_t: \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^2 \to \mathbb{R}
\]
is revealed, and the incurred cost is $c_t(x_t,u_t)$. We assume each $c_t$ is convex
in $(x,u)$ and satisfies the following Lipschitz condition on
$\mathcal{D}_\alpha$: there exist constants $L_x,L_u>0$, independent of $t$, such
that for all $(x,u),(x',u')\in\mathcal{D}_\alpha$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-lipschitz}
|c_t(x,u)-c_t(x',u')| \le L_x|x-x'| + L_u|u-u'|.
\end{equation}
We also assume access, at every $(x,u)\in\mathcal{D}_\alpha$, to a cost subgradient
$s_t(x,u)=(s_{x,t},s_{u,t})\in\partial c_t(x,u)$ satisfying
$|s_{x,t}|\le L_x$ and $|s_{u,t}|\le L_u$. This oracle condition holds, for
example, when the Lipschitz property above extends to an open neighborhood of
$\mathcal{D}_\alpha$.

The information pattern is adversarial and non-anticipatory. At time $t$, the
environment may choose $(w_t,c_t)$ as a function of the history through time $t-1$,
but not of the current action (or current randomization). The controller chooses
$u_t$ without observing $(w_t,c_t)$; the pair is then revealed. Subject to this
timing and the stated regularity conditions, the arrival and cost sequences may be
arbitrary.

\paragraph{Assumptions}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Retention coefficient:} $\alpha\in(0,1)$ is fixed and known (a fraction
        $1-\alpha$ of the state is lost each period).
    \item \textbf{Arrival bounds:} $w_t\in[0,1]$ for all $t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$.
        In particular, arrivals are nonnegative resource increments, in contrast to
        signed process-noise disturbances common in classical control.
    \item \textbf{Adversarial timing:} $(w_t,c_t)$ may depend on the history through
        time $t-1$ but not on the current action, and is revealed only after $u_t$ is
        chosen.
    \item \textbf{Cost regularity:} each $c_t$ is convex in $(x,u)$, obeys
        \eqref{eq:model-lipschitz} on $\mathcal{D}_\alpha$, and admits the bounded
        subgradient oracle described above.
    \item \textbf{Feasible policy class:} both the online policy and the benchmark
        policies are required to satisfy \eqref{eq:model-feasibility} under
        \eqref{eq:model-dynamics}.
\end{enumerate}
We focus on $\alpha\in(0,1)$; the case $\alpha\ge 1$ is discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:extensions}.

Let $\{(x_t^{\mathcal{A}},u_t^{\mathcal{A}})\}_{t=1}^T$ denote the trajectory of an
online algorithm $\mathcal{A}$. Define its cumulative cost by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-cumulative-cost}
J_T(\mathcal{A}) \triangleq \sum_{t=1}^T c_t\bigl(x_t^{\mathcal{A}},u_t^{\mathcal{A}}\bigr).
\end{equation}
For a fixed positive integer $H$, let $\pi$ range over the policy class
$\mathrm{SDAC}_H$ (defined in Section~\ref{sec:sdac}). For each
$\pi\in\mathrm{SDAC}_H$, let $\{(x_t^{\pi},u_t^{\pi})\}_{t=1}^T$ be the induced
feasible trajectory under the same arrival sequence. The regret against the best
fixed SDAC policy is defined by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-regret}
\RegSDAC{T}
\triangleq
J_T(\mathcal{A})
-
\min_{\pi\in\mathrm{SDAC}_H}
\sum_{t=1}^T c_t\bigl(x_t^{\pi},u_t^{\pi}\bigr).
\end{equation}
While the online controller may update its SDAC parameters over time, the benchmark
in \eqref{eq:def-regret} is the best single fixed SDAC policy chosen in hindsight.
The objective is to design an online policy that satisfies
\eqref{eq:model-feasibility} for all $t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$ and achieves sublinear regret as defined in
\eqref{eq:def-regret}.

\section{Simplex Disturbance-Action Control (SDAC)}
\label{sec:sdac}

\subsection{From Disturbance-Action Control to SDAC}
Disturbance-Action Control (DAC) combines a stabilizing state-feedback term with a
finite linear filter of past disturbances. Under standard conditions, this
parameterization yields convex surrogate losses and efficient online updates. In
our scalar storage model, specializing the baseline feedback term to zero gives the
representative form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dac-param}
u_t = \sum_{i=1}^{H} M^{(i)} w_{t-i},
\end{equation}
where $H\in\mathbb{N}$ is the memory horizon and $M\in\mathbb{R}^H$ is a linear
filter. We allow nonpositive indices in such sums, with the convention $w_\tau\equiv
0$ for $\tau\le 0$.

For our constrained model, an unrestricted filter is insufficient: its action can
violate \eqref{eq:model-feasibility} by exceeding $\alpha x_t$ or becoming negative.
We therefore introduce \emph{Simplex Disturbance-Action Control} (SDAC), which uses a
simplex-constrained filter tailored to the storage dynamics. This restriction yields
fixed-policy feasibility while preserving convex surrogate losses and tractable
online updates.


\subsection{SDAC Definition}
Fix a memory horizon $H\in\mathbb{N}$. Let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-simplex}
\Delta_H \triangleq \left\{M\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^H:\; \sum_{i=1}^H
M^{(i)}\le1\right\}
\end{equation}
be the subprobability simplex. For any $M\in\Delta_H$, define the policy $\pi(M)$ by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-sdac-policy}
u_t^{\pi(M)} \;\triangleq\; \sum_{i=1}^{H} M^{(i)}\alpha^i w_{t-i},
\qquad w_\tau\equiv 0\;\text{for}\;\tau\le 0.
\end{equation}
For each time $t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$, let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-w-tilde}
\widetilde{w}_t
\triangleq
\big(\alpha w_{t-1},\alpha^2 w_{t-2},\ldots,\alpha^H w_{t-H}\big)^\top
\in \mathbb{R}^H,
\end{equation}
with the same convention $w_\tau\equiv 0$ for $\tau\le 0$, so that
\eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy} can be written as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-sdac-policy-vector}
u_t^{\pi(M)} = \langle \widetilde{w}_t,M\rangle.
\end{equation}

The induced state trajectory satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-state-update}
x_{t+1}^{\pi(M)} = \alpha x_t^{\pi(M)} - u_t^{\pi(M)} + w_t,
\qquad x_1^{\pi(M)}=0.
\end{equation}
The weights $\alpha^i$ align the arrival filter with the retention coefficient
$\alpha$ in \eqref{eq:model-dynamics}.


We now establish the two structural properties that make SDAC useful: the induced
control action always satisfies the feasibility constraint
\eqref{eq:model-feasibility}, and the induced cost is convex and Lipschitz in $M$, so
that regret minimization against the best fixed SDAC policy is an instance of online
convex optimization over the simplex. Both properties follow from an explicit
decomposition of the state trajectory that isolates its dependence on $M$.

Recall that we set $w_\tau\equiv 0$ for $\tau\le 0$, which is used in the base-case
arguments below.

\begin{lemma}[State decomposition under SDAC]
\label{lem:sdac-state-decomp}
Fix $H\in\mathbb{N}$ and $M\in\Delta_H$, and write
\[
s_M \triangleq \sum_{i=1}^H M^{(i)}.
\]
For $i\in\{1,\ldots,H\}$, define
\[
\psi_t^{(i)}
\triangleq
\sum_{j=1}^{i}\alpha^{j-1}w_{t-j},
\qquad
\rho_t
\triangleq
\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}\alpha^{j-1}w_{t-j},
\]
where the second sum is empty when $t=1$. Then, for every
$t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-state-decomp}
x_t^{\pi(M)}
=
\sum_{i=1}^H M^{(i)}\psi_t^{(i)}
+(1-s_M)\rho_t.
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\[
0\le \psi_t^{(i)}\le \rho_t\le\frac{1}{1-\alpha},
\]
and $M\mapsto x_t^{\pi(M)}$ is affine.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The auxiliary quantities satisfy
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rho-recursion}
\rho_{t+1}=\alpha\rho_t+w_t,
\qquad
\rho_1=0,
\end{equation}
and, for every $i\in\{1,\ldots,H\}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:psi-recursion}
\begin{aligned}
\alpha\psi_t^{(i)}-\alpha^i w_{t-i}
&=\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha^j w_{t-j} \\
&=\psi_{t+1}^{(i)}-w_t.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For $i=1$, the sum in the first line is empty.

At $t=1$, since $w_{\tau}\equiv 0$ for $\tau\le 0$,
\begin{align*}
\psi_1^{(i)}
&=
\sum_{j=1}^{i}\alpha^{j-1}w_{1-j}
=0,\\
\rho_1
&=0,\\
x_1^{\pi(M)}
&=0,
\end{align*}
so \eqref{eq:sdac-state-decomp} holds. Suppose it holds at time $t$. Using
\eqref{eq:sdac-state-update} and \eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:decomp-induction-1}
\begin{aligned}
x_{t+1}^{\pi(M)}
&=
\alpha\left(
\sum_{i=1}^H M^{(i)}\psi_t^{(i)}
+(1-s_M)\rho_t
\right) \\
&\quad
-\sum_{i=1}^H M^{(i)}\alpha^i w_{t-i}
+w_t \\
&=
\sum_{i=1}^H M^{(i)}
\bigl(\alpha\psi_t^{(i)}-\alpha^i w_{t-i}\bigr) \\
&\quad
+\alpha(1-s_M)\rho_t+w_t.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:rho-recursion} and \eqref{eq:psi-recursion} into
\eqref{eq:decomp-induction-1},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:decomp-induction-2}
\begin{aligned}
x_{t+1}^{\pi(M)}
&=
\sum_{i=1}^H M^{(i)}
\bigl(\psi_{t+1}^{(i)}-w_t\bigr) \\
&\quad
+(1-s_M)(\rho_{t+1}-w_t)+w_t \\
&=
\sum_{i=1}^H M^{(i)}\psi_{t+1}^{(i)}
+(1-s_M)\rho_{t+1},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
because the total coefficient of $w_t$ in the first line is
$-s_M-(1-s_M)+1=0$. This proves \eqref{eq:sdac-state-decomp} by induction.

All summands defining $\psi_t^{(i)}$ and $\rho_t$ are nonnegative. Since
$w_\tau=0$ for $\tau\le0$, $\psi_t^{(i)}$ is a truncation of $\rho_t$; hence
\[
0\le\psi_t^{(i)}\le\rho_t
\le\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\alpha^{j-1}
=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}.
\]
Finally,
\[
x_t^{\pi(M)}
=
\rho_t+\sum_{i=1}^H M^{(i)}\bigl(\psi_t^{(i)}-\rho_t\bigr),
\]
which is affine in $M$.
\end{proof}

The first consequence of this decomposition is fixed-policy feasibility.

\begin{lemma}[Feasibility of SDAC policies]
\label{lem:sdac-feasibility}
For every $M\in\Delta_H$, the trajectory induced by
\eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy} and \eqref{eq:sdac-state-update} satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-feasibility}
0\le u_t^{\pi(M)}\le \alpha x_t^{\pi(M)}
\qquad
\text{for all }t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Because $M^{(i)},\alpha$, and $w_{t-i}$ are nonnegative,
\[
u_t^{\pi(M)}
=
\sum_{i=1}^H M^{(i)}\alpha^i w_{t-i}
\ge0.
\]
For every $i\in\{1,\ldots,H\}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:feas-psi-term}
\alpha\psi_t^{(i)}
=
\sum_{j=1}^{i}\alpha^j w_{t-j}
\ge
\alpha^i w_{t-i}.
\end{equation}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-state-decomp}, $1-s_M\ge0$ and $\rho_t\ge0$. Therefore,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:feas-upper-direct}
\begin{aligned}
\alpha x_t^{\pi(M)}
&=
\sum_{i=1}^H M^{(i)}\alpha\psi_t^{(i)}
+\alpha(1-s_M)\rho_t \\
&\ge
\sum_{i=1}^H M^{(i)}\alpha\psi_t^{(i)} \\
&\ge
\sum_{i=1}^H M^{(i)}\alpha^i w_{t-i}
=
u_t^{\pi(M)},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where the second inequality uses \eqref{eq:feas-psi-term}.
\end{proof}

For fixed arrivals, both coordinates of the SDAC state--action pair are affine
in $M$. The following lemma records the resulting convexity and the constants
used by the online update.

\begin{lemma}[Convexity and Lipschitz continuity of surrogate cost]
\label{lem:sdac-surrogate}
Define
\[
\ell_t(M)
\triangleq
c_t\bigl(x_t^{\pi(M)},u_t^{\pi(M)}\bigr),
\qquad
M\in\Delta_H,
\]
and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-surrogate-L}
L\triangleq\alpha L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}.
\end{equation}
Then $\ell_t$ is convex on $\Delta_H$, and for all $M,M'\in\Delta_H$,
\[
|\ell_t(M)-\ell_t(M')|
\le
L\|M-M'\|_1.
\]
Moreover, at every $M\in\Delta_H$, one can select
$g\in\partial\ell_t(M)$ such that $\|g\|_\infty\le L$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $i\in\{1,\ldots,H\}$, define
\[
a_t^{(i)}
\triangleq
\psi_t^{(i)}-\rho_t,
\qquad
b_t^{(i)}
\triangleq
\alpha^i w_{t-i}.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-state-decomp} and \eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy} give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-affine-pair}
x_t^{\pi(M)}=\rho_t+\langle a_t,M\rangle,
\qquad
u_t^{\pi(M)}=\langle b_t,M\rangle.
\end{equation}
The coefficient bounds in Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-state-decomp}, together with
$w_{t-i}\in[0,1]$, imply
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-coefficient-norms}
\|a_t\|_\infty\le\frac{1}{1-\alpha},
\qquad
\|b_t\|_\infty\le\alpha.
\end{equation}
Since \eqref{eq:surr-affine-pair} is affine in $M$ and $c_t$ is convex,
$\ell_t$ is convex.

Let $\Delta M\triangleq M-M'$. By \eqref{eq:model-lipschitz} and
\eqref{eq:surr-affine-pair},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-lipschitz-short}
\begin{aligned}
|\ell_t(M)-\ell_t(M')|
&\le
L_x|\langle a_t,\Delta M\rangle|
+
L_u|\langle b_t,\Delta M\rangle| \\
&\le
\bigl(L_x\|a_t\|_\infty+L_u\|b_t\|_\infty\bigr)
\|\Delta M\|_1 \\
&\le
\left(\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}+\alpha L_u\right)
\|\Delta M\|_1 \\
&=
L\|M-M'\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Choose $(s_x,s_u)\in
\partial c_t(x_t^{\pi(M)},u_t^{\pi(M)})$ with
$|s_x|\le L_x$ and $|s_u|\le L_u$, and set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-subgradient-short}
g\triangleq s_x a_t+s_u b_t.
\end{equation}
For every $M'\in\Delta_H$, the subgradient inequality and
\eqref{eq:surr-affine-pair} yield
\begin{align*}
\ell_t(M')
&\ge
\ell_t(M)
+s_x\langle a_t,M'-M\rangle
+s_u\langle b_t,M'-M\rangle \\
&=
\ell_t(M)+\langle g,M'-M\rangle.
\end{align*}
Thus $g\in\partial\ell_t(M)$. Finally,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-subgradient-bound-short}
\begin{aligned}
\|g\|_\infty
&\le
|s_x|\|a_t\|_\infty+|s_u|\|b_t\|_\infty \\
&\le
\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}+\alpha L_u
=
L.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{proof}

\subsection{Expressiveness: Proportional State-Feedback Approximation}
\label{subsec:sdac-expressiveness}

A standard policy class in online control is the family of linear state-feedback
policies $u_t=-Kx_t$, and a foundational property of Disturbance-Action Control is
that the DAC class \emph{approximates} any strongly stable linear policy, with
approximation error decaying geometrically in the memory horizon $H$
\cite{agarwal2019online,hazan2026introduction}.

Under our sign convention, positive $u_t$ drains storage, so proportional feedback
is written $u_t=q x_t$. In our scalar model, the feasible proportional
state-feedback policies are exactly those with gain
$q\in[0,\alpha]$: since $x_t\ge 0$, the feasibility constraint $0\le u_t\le\alpha
x_t$ in \eqref{eq:model-feasibility} holds if and only if $q\in[0,\alpha]$. The next
lemma gives an explicit approximation within the feasible class $\mathrm{SDAC}_H$;
the two endpoint policies are represented exactly.

\begin{lemma}[SDAC approximation of proportional state feedback]
\label{lem:sdac-linear-approx}
Fix $q\in[0,\alpha]$, set $\beta\triangleq\alpha-q$, and let $\pi_q^{\mathrm{lin}}$
denote the feasible linear policy
\[
\begin{aligned}
u_t^{\mathrm{lin}}&=q\,x_t^{\mathrm{lin}},
\qquad
x_{t+1}^{\mathrm{lin}}=\beta\,x_t^{\mathrm{lin}}+w_t, \\
t&\in\{1,\ldots,T\},
\qquad
x_1^{\mathrm{lin}}=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Define $M_q^\star\in\mathbb{R}^H$ by
\[
M_q^{\star(i)}\triangleq\frac{q\,\beta^{i-1}}{\alpha^i},
\qquad i=1,\ldots,H.
\]
Then $M_q^\star\in\Delta_H$, and if each $c_t$ satisfies
\eqref{eq:model-lipschitz}, then for every $\{w_t\}\subset[0,1]$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-lin-approx-cost}
\begin{aligned}
&\Bigl|\sum_{t=1}^T c_t\bigl(x_t^{\pi(M_q^\star)},u_t^{\pi(M_q^\star)}\bigr)
-\sum_{t=1}^T c_t\bigl(x_t^{\mathrm{lin}},u_t^{\mathrm{lin}}\bigr)\Bigr| \\
&\qquad\le
\Bigl(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\Bigr)\frac{q\,\beta^H}{1-\beta}\,T.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The proof is deferred to Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-proofs}.
\end{lemma}

Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound} depends on the memory horizon only through
$\sqrt{\log(H+1)}$. Combining that bound with
Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-linear-approx} therefore yields a no-regret guarantee against the best
feasible proportional state-feedback policy; Corollary~\ref{cor:regret-vs-linear}
makes this statement precise.





\section{Online Algorithm}
\label{sec:online-algorithm}

We now present our online algorithm, which updates SDAC parameters by
online mirror descent (OMD) with unnormalized negentropy (equivalently, the
exponentiated gradient method) and a fixed step size $\eta>0$ over the
subprobability simplex $\Delta_H\subset\mathbb{R}^H$
\cite{hazan2016introduction}.

For each time $t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$, let $M_t\in\Delta_H$ be the online parameter. We initialize
at
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-init}
M_1^{(i)}\triangleq\frac{1}{H+1},\qquad i=1,\ldots,H
\end{equation}
so that the maximum initial divergence
$\sup_{M\in\Delta_H}D_\Psi(M\|M_1)$ is at most $\log(H+1)$ (see the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound}).

\paragraph{Control-action selection}
The controller first forms the nominal SDAC action
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-unconstrained-action}
\widehat{u}_t \triangleq \langle \widetilde{w}_t,M_t\rangle,
\end{equation}
and clips it to the admissible interval $[0,\alpha x_t^{\mathcal{A}}]$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-feasible-action}
u_t^{\mathcal{A}} \triangleq \min\{\widehat{u}_t,\,\alpha x_t^{\mathcal{A}}\}.
\end{equation}
The state update is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-state-update}
x_{t+1}^{\mathcal{A}} = \alpha x_t^{\mathcal{A}} - u_t^{\mathcal{A}} + w_t,
\qquad x_1^{\mathcal{A}}=0.
\end{equation}
By construction, $\widehat{u}_t\ge 0$ and $u_t^{\mathcal{A}}\in[0,\alpha x_t^{\mathcal{A}}]$;
with $x_1^{\mathcal{A}}=0$ and $w_t\in[0,1]$, induction on
\eqref{eq:alg-state-update} yields $x_t^{\mathcal{A}}\ge 0$ for all $t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$,
so the online trajectory satisfies \eqref{eq:model-feasibility}.

\paragraph{Surrogate cost and OMD update}
After $(w_t,c_t)$ is revealed, define the surrogate cost by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-surrogate-cost}
\ell_t(M) \triangleq c_t\bigl(x_t^{\pi(M)},u_t^{\pi(M)}\bigr), \qquad M\in\Delta_H,
\end{equation}
which is convex and Lipschitz by Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-surrogate}. Select a subgradient
$g_t\in\partial\ell_t(M_t)$ satisfying $\|g_t\|_\infty\le L$, whose existence and
construction are given in that lemma. Let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-negentropy}
\Psi(M) \triangleq \sum_{i=1}^H\bigl(M^{(i)}\log M^{(i)}-M^{(i)}\bigr), \qquad
M\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^H,
\end{equation}
denote the unnormalized negentropy (with the convention $0\log0\triangleq0$), and let
\[
D_\Psi(M\|V)\triangleq\sum_{i=1}^H\Bigl(M^{(i)}\log\tfrac{M^{(i)}}{V^{(i)}}-M^{(i)}+V^{(i)}\Bigr)
\]
be its Bregman divergence for $V\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$, the generalized
Kullback--Leibler divergence. Terms with $M^{(i)}=0$ are interpreted by
continuity as zero in the logarithmic part.

We define the OMD update
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-omd-argmin}
M_{t+1} \triangleq \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{M\in\Delta_H}
\Bigl\{\eta\langle g_t,M\rangle + D_\Psi(M\|M_t)\Bigr\}.
\end{equation}
The regret analysis in Section~\ref{sec:regret-analysis} works directly with
this optimization-based definition.

\paragraph{Closed-form solution}
Although \eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin} is a constrained optimization problem, it is easy
to solve analytically because $\Psi$ is separable and the objective is linear in
$g_t$. As shown in Lemma~\ref{lem:omd-closed-form}, the minimizer in
\eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin} is exactly the result of two elementary steps: a
multiplicative step,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-omd-mult}
\widetilde{M}_{t+1}^{(i)} \triangleq M_t^{(i)}\exp\!\bigl(-\eta g_t^{(i)}\bigr), \qquad i=1,\ldots,H,
\end{equation}
followed by the Bregman projection onto $\Delta_H$ given by
\eqref{eq:alg-omd-proj} (the scaling is nontrivial only when $S_{t+1}>1$):
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-omd-proj}
M_{t+1}^{(i)}
\triangleq
\frac{\widetilde{M}_{t+1}^{(i)}}{\max\{1,S_{t+1}\}},
\quad
S_{t+1}\triangleq\sum_{j=1}^H \widetilde{M}_{t+1}^{(j)},
\end{equation}
for $i=1,\ldots,H$. This is the form we implement in practice.


\section{Regret Analysis}
\label{sec:regret-analysis}
The following theorem states that the online controller achieves sublinear regret
against every fixed SDAC policy $\pi(M)$, where $M$ is any parameter in $\Delta_H$.
The regret proof has two parts: a standard online convex optimization bound for the
surrogate costs $\ell_t$, and a stability bound showing that the
action-clipped online trajectory stays close to the SDAC policy trajectory
driven by the same parameters. Some technical lemmas required for the proof are deferred to
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-proofs}.

\begin{theorem}[Regret bound]
\label{thm:regret-bound}
Suppose the cost functions satisfy the regularity and bounded-subgradient
assumptions of Section~\ref{sec:model-setup}. Then the OMD controller with
action clipping from Section~\ref{sec:online-algorithm}, initialized as in
\eqref{eq:alg-init} and using step size $\eta>0$, satisfies
\[
\RegSDAC{T}
\le
\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta}
+
\eta L^2 T\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{\alpha}{(1-\alpha)^2}\Bigr),
\]
where $L\triangleq\alpha L_u+\tfrac{L_x}{1-\alpha}$ as in
\eqref{eq:def-surrogate-L}.
In particular, the choice
\[
\eta^*
=
\sqrt{\frac{\log(H+1)}{L^2 T\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{\alpha}{(1-\alpha)^2}\bigr)}}
\]
gives
\[
\RegSDAC{T}
\le
2L\sqrt{\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{\alpha}{(1-\alpha)^2}\Bigr)T\log(H+1)}.
\]
\end{theorem}


Combining Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound} with
Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-linear-approx} yields a uniform guarantee against the best
feasible proportional state-feedback policy.

\begin{corollary}[No-regret against proportional state feedback]
\label{cor:regret-vs-linear}
Set
\[
H_T\triangleq
\max\!\left\{1,\left\lceil\frac{\log T}{\log(1/\alpha)}\right\rceil\right\}.
\]
Run the OMD controller with action clipping using memory horizon $H=H_T$ and the
step size $\eta^*$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound}. For each
$q\in[0,\alpha]$, let
$\bigl(x_t^{(q)},u_t^{(q)}\bigr)$ denote the trajectory of the proportional policy
$u_t^{(q)}=q x_t^{(q)}$. Define
\[
\RegLin{T}
\triangleq
\sum_{t=1}^T c_t\bigl(x_t^{\mathcal{A}},u_t^{\mathcal{A}}\bigr)
-\min_{q\in[0,\alpha]}
\sum_{t=1}^T c_t\bigl(x_t^{(q)},u_t^{(q)}\bigr).
\]
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\RegLin{T}
&\le
2L\sqrt{\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{\alpha}{(1-\alpha)^2}\Bigr)T\log(H_T+1)} \\
&\quad +\Bigl(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\Bigr)
\frac{\alpha^{H_T+1}}{1-\alpha}\,T,
\end{aligned}
\]
where $L=\alpha L_u+\tfrac{L_x}{1-\alpha}$. Since
$\alpha^{H_T}\le1/T$, the second term is $O(1)$, while
$\log(H_T+1)=O(\log\log T)$ as $T\to\infty$. Consequently,
\[
\RegLin{T}
=
O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log\log T}\right).
\]
Thus $\RegLin{T}=o(T)$ against the best proportional state-feedback policy with
$q\in[0,\alpha]$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For $M\in\Delta_{H_T}$ and $q\in[0,\alpha]$, write
\[
\begin{aligned}
J_T(M)
&\triangleq
\sum_{t=1}^T c_t\bigl(x_t^{\pi(M)},u_t^{\pi(M)}\bigr), \\
J_T(q)
&\triangleq
\sum_{t=1}^T c_t\bigl(x_t^{(q)},u_t^{(q)}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Let
\[
R_T
\triangleq
2L\sqrt{
\left(\tfrac12+\tfrac{\alpha}{(1-\alpha)^2}\right)
T\log(H_T+1)
}.
\]
Fix $q\in[0,\alpha]$, set $\beta=\alpha-q$, and use the comparator
$M_q^\star$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-linear-approx}. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:linear-regret-decomposition}
\begin{aligned}
J_T(\mathcal{A})-J_T(q)
&=
\left(
J_T(\mathcal{A})-\min_{M\in\Delta_{H_T}}J_T(M)
\right) \\
&\quad+
\left(
\min_{M\in\Delta_{H_T}}J_T(M)-J_T(q)
\right) \\
&\le
R_T+J_T(M_q^\star)-J_T(q) \\
&\le
R_T+
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)
\frac{q\beta^{H_T}}{1-\beta}\,T.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The third line uses Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound}; the fourth uses
Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-linear-approx}. Since
$q\le\alpha$, $\beta\le\alpha$, and $1-\beta\ge1-\alpha$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:linear-uniform-tail}
\frac{q\beta^{H_T}}{1-\beta}
\le
\frac{\alpha^{H_T+1}}{1-\alpha}.
\end{equation}
The right-hand side is independent of $q$. Taking the minimum comparator cost
over $q\in[0,\alpha]$ in \eqref{eq:linear-regret-decomposition} and applying
\eqref{eq:linear-uniform-tail} proves the finite-time bound.

Finally,
\[
H_T
\ge
\frac{\log T}{\log(1/\alpha)}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\alpha^{H_T}\le\frac{1}{T}.
\]
Also $H_T=\Theta(\log T)$, so
$\log(H_T+1)=O(\log\log T)$ as $T\to\infty$. These two estimates give the
stated asymptotic rate.
\end{proof}


\section{Extensions}
\label{sec:extensions}

We briefly discuss three possible extensions. Establishing their full regret bounds
is left for future work.

\paragraph{Retention $\alpha\ge 1$.}
For $\alpha\ge1$, choose a fixed baseline drain $Kx_t$ such that
$\bar\alpha\triangleq\alpha-K\in(0,1)$, and write $u_t=Kx_t+v_t$. The residual
dynamics become $x_{t+1}=\bar\alpha x_t-v_t+w_t$. An SDAC filter for $v_t$ should
therefore use the powers $\bar\alpha^i$, not $\alpha^i$. If
$0\le v_t\le\bar\alpha x_t$, then
$0\le u_t\le(K+\bar\alpha)x_t=\alpha x_t$. This reduction suggests an extension of
the present analysis with transformed cost and Lipschitz constants; we do not pursue
the details here.

\paragraph{Vector action / multi-task allocation.}
Consider a single storage state $x_t$ with a vector action
$u_t\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^n$ that allocates resources across $n$ tasks, subject to
$\mathbf{1}^\top u_t\le\alpha x_t$, so that the total drain from storage is the sum of
the task allocations. The dynamics remain
$x_{t+1}=\alpha x_t-\mathbf{1}^\top u_t+w_t$, and the cost $c_t(x_t,u_t)$ is convex in
$(x,u)$. The fixed-policy SDAC construction lifts by replacing the filter vector
$M\in\Delta_H$ with a matrix
$M\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{n\times H}$ whose entries sum to at most one (a matrix
simplex), with each row corresponding to a task and each column to a memory lag.
OMD can then run over this matrix simplex, with radial action clipping when the
nominal total allocation exceeds $\alpha x_t$.

\paragraph{Multiple storages with diagonal dynamics.}
Suppose the state is $x_t\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^d$ and evolves as
$x_{t+1}=A x_t-u_t+w_t$ with $A=\mathrm{diag}(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_d)$,
$\alpha_i\in(0,1)$, arrivals $w_t\in[0,1]^d$, and per-coordinate constraints
$0\le u_t^{(i)}\le\alpha_i x_t^{(i)}$. The dynamics and feasible set then decouple
by coordinate, and the natural SDAC class is a product of simplices. For a jointly
convex cost, OMD operates jointly on this product set; the optimization separates by
coordinate only when the cost is coordinate-separable. Cross-coupled storage systems
(non-diagonal $A$) require additional analysis.

These constructions retain the basic simplex/OMD structure, but their complete
feasibility and regret analyses require separate treatment.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied adversarial online control of a storage system under a state-dependent
action constraint. Every fixed SDAC policy is feasible by construction. For the
time-varying online controller, a Bregman projection maintains the SDAC parameter
constraint, while scalar action clipping maintains feasibility of the actual
trajectory. The resulting controller achieves
$O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log(H+1)}\right)$ regret against the best fixed SDAC policy. The
SDAC approximation result further gives
$O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log\log T}\right)$ regret against the best feasible proportional
state-feedback policy when $H=\Theta(\log T)$. The model applies directly to
energy-harvesting battery management and related settings in which storage limits
must be respected in real time. Section~\ref{sec:extensions} discusses possible
extensions to $\alpha\ge1$, vector allocation, and diagonal multi-storage systems.

\section{Deferred Proofs}
\label{sec:appendix-proofs}

This appendix collects the deferred proof of the proportional-state-feedback
approximation guarantee from Section~\ref{sec:sdac}, together with the OMD and
stability lemmas leading up to the regret bound stated in
Section~\ref{sec:regret-analysis}.

Recall that we set $w_\tau\equiv 0$ for $\tau\le 0$, which is used in several
base-case arguments below.

We first prove the linear-policy approximation guarantee stated in
Section~\ref{subsec:sdac-expressiveness}.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-linear-approx}]
Fix $q\in[0,\alpha]$ and set $\beta\triangleq\alpha-q\in[0,\alpha]$.

\emph{Proportional-policy trajectory.}
Substituting $u_t^{\mathrm{lin}}=q x_t^{\mathrm{lin}}$ into the dynamics gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-closed-loop}
x_{t+1}^{\mathrm{lin}}
=
\beta x_t^{\mathrm{lin}}+w_t,
\qquad
x_1^{\mathrm{lin}}=0.
\end{equation}
Unrolling \eqref{eq:model-closed-loop},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lin-closed-form}
\begin{aligned}
x_t^{\mathrm{lin}}
&=
\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}\beta^{j-1}w_{t-j}, \\
u_t^{\mathrm{lin}}
&=
q\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}\beta^{j-1}w_{t-j}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Thus $x_t^{\mathrm{lin}}\ge0$, and $q\in[0,\alpha]$ implies
\[
0\le u_t^{\mathrm{lin}}
=
q x_t^{\mathrm{lin}}
\le
\alpha x_t^{\mathrm{lin}}.
\]

\emph{Simplex membership.}
Each coordinate of $M_q^\star$ is nonnegative. If $q=0$, then
$M_q^\star=0\in\Delta_H$. Suppose $q>0$ and define
\[
r\triangleq\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\in[0,1).
\]
Since $q=\alpha-\beta$, one has $q/\alpha=1-r$. Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lin-simplex-sum}
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i=1}^H M_q^{\star(i)}
&=
\frac{q}{\alpha}\sum_{i=1}^H r^{i-1} \\
&=
(1-r)\frac{1-r^H}{1-r} \\
&=
1-r^H
\le1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Hence $M_q^\star\in\Delta_H$.

\emph{Action approximation.}
The identity $M_q^{\star(i)}\alpha^i=q\beta^{i-1}$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lin-sdac-u}
u_t^{\pi(M_q^\star)}
=
q\sum_{i=1}^H\beta^{i-1}w_{t-i}.
\end{equation}
Define
\[
e_t
\triangleq
u_t^{\mathrm{lin}}-u_t^{\pi(M_q^\star)}.
\]
Using $w_\tau=0$ for $\tau\le0$ in \eqref{eq:lin-closed-form} and
\eqref{eq:lin-sdac-u},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lin-u-gap}
e_t
=
q\sum_{j=H+1}^{t-1}\beta^{j-1}w_{t-j},
\end{equation}
where the sum is empty when $t\le H+1$. Therefore,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lin-e-range}
\begin{aligned}
0\le e_t
&\le
q\sum_{j=H+1}^{\infty}\beta^{j-1} \\
&=
\frac{q\beta^H}{1-\beta}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\emph{State approximation.}
Let
\[
\delta_t
\triangleq
x_t^{\pi(M_q^\star)}-x_t^{\mathrm{lin}}.
\]
Subtracting the two state recursions yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lin-delta-dyn}
\delta_{t+1}
=
\alpha\delta_t+e_t,
\qquad
\delta_1=0.
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lin-delta-unroll}
\delta_t
=
\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\alpha^{t-1-s}e_s.
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{eq:lin-e-range} and \eqref{eq:lin-delta-unroll},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lin-delta-bound}
\begin{aligned}
0\le\delta_t
&\le
\frac{q\beta^H}{1-\beta}
\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\alpha^{t-1-s} \\
&\le
\frac{q\beta^H}{(1-\beta)(1-\alpha)}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\emph{Cost approximation.}
By \eqref{eq:model-lipschitz}, \eqref{eq:lin-e-range}, and
\eqref{eq:lin-delta-bound},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lin-cost-step}
\begin{aligned}
&\left|
c_t\bigl(x_t^{\pi(M_q^\star)},u_t^{\pi(M_q^\star)}\bigr)
-c_t\bigl(x_t^{\mathrm{lin}},u_t^{\mathrm{lin}}\bigr)
\right| \\
&\qquad\le
L_x\delta_t+L_u e_t \\
&\qquad\le
\frac{L_x q\beta^H}{(1-\beta)(1-\alpha)}
+
\frac{L_u q\beta^H}{1-\beta} \\
&\qquad=
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)
\frac{q\beta^H}{1-\beta}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Summing \eqref{eq:lin-cost-step} over $t=1,\ldots,T$ and applying the triangle
inequality proves \eqref{eq:sdac-lin-approx-cost}.
\end{proof}

The remaining lemmas establish the OMD and stability estimates used in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound}; they rely on the state-decomposition, feasibility,
and surrogate-cost lemmas already established in Section~\ref{sec:sdac}
(Lemmas~\ref{lem:sdac-state-decomp}, \ref{lem:sdac-feasibility}, and
\ref{lem:sdac-surrogate}).

\begin{lemma}[Bregman projection onto $\Delta_H$]
\label{lem:bregman-projection}
Let $D_\Psi$ be the Bregman divergence of the unnormalized negentropy
\eqref{eq:def-negentropy}. Fix $\widetilde{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$ and set
$S\triangleq\sum_{i=1}^H \widetilde{M}^{(i)}$. Then the Bregman projection of
$\widetilde{M}$ onto $\Delta_H=\{M\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^H:\sum_{i} M^{(i)}\le1\}$,
\[
P(\widetilde{M})\triangleq\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{M\in\Delta_H} D_\Psi(M\|\widetilde{M}),
\]
is given coordinatewise by
\[
P(\widetilde{M})^{(i)}=
\frac{\widetilde{M}^{(i)}}{\max\{1,S\}}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Up to the constant $\sum_i\widetilde{M}^{(i)}$, the objective is
\[
F(M)
\triangleq
\sum_{i=1}^H
\left(
M^{(i)}\log\frac{M^{(i)}}{\widetilde{M}^{(i)}}-M^{(i)}
\right).
\]
The function $F$ is strictly convex, and, for $M^{(i)}>0$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:breg-grad}
\frac{\partial F}{\partial M^{(i)}}
=
\log\frac{M^{(i)}}{\widetilde{M}^{(i)}}.
\end{equation}
Its unconstrained minimizer is therefore $M=\widetilde{M}$.

If $S\le1$, this minimizer belongs to $\Delta_H$, so
$P(\widetilde{M})=\widetilde{M}$. Suppose $S>1$. The constraint
$\sum_iM^{(i)}\le1$ is then active. Moreover, the minimizer has no zero
coordinate: if $M^{(k)}=0$, transferring an arbitrarily small amount of mass
from a positive coordinate to coordinate $k$ has directional derivative
$-\infty$, because
\[
\lim_{z\downarrow0}\log\frac{z}{\widetilde{M}^{(k)}}=-\infty.
\]
Thus the nonnegativity constraints are inactive. With $\lambda\ge0$ denoting
the multiplier of $\sum_iM^{(i)}\le1$, stationarity and complementary
slackness give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:breg-kkt}
\log\frac{M^{(i)}}{\widetilde{M}^{(i)}}+\lambda=0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^H M^{(i)}=1.
\end{equation}
The first relation in \eqref{eq:breg-kkt} implies
\[
M^{(i)}
=
\widetilde{M}^{(i)}e^{-\lambda}.
\]
Summing over $i$ and using the second relation in \eqref{eq:breg-kkt},
\[
1
=
Se^{-\lambda},
\qquad
e^{-\lambda}
=
\frac{1}{S}.
\]
Hence $\lambda=\log S>0$ and
\[
P(\widetilde{M})^{(i)}
=
\frac{\widetilde{M}^{(i)}}{S}.
\]
Combining the cases $S\le1$ and $S>1$ proves the stated formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Closed-form solution of the OMD update]
\label{lem:omd-closed-form}
Fix $M_t\in\Delta_H\cap\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$, $\eta>0$, and
$g_t\in\mathbb{R}^H$, and let
$\widetilde{M}_{t+1}$ be defined coordinatewise by \eqref{eq:alg-omd-mult},
i.e.\ $\widetilde{M}_{t+1}^{(i)}=M_t^{(i)}e^{-\eta g_t^{(i)}}$. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{M\in\Delta_H}\Bigl\{\eta\langle g_t,M\rangle+D_\Psi(M\|M_t)\Bigr\} \\
&\quad=
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{M\in\Delta_H} D_\Psi(M\|\widetilde{M}_{t+1})
=
P(\widetilde{M}_{t+1}),
\end{aligned}
\]
where $P(\cdot)$ is the Bregman projection of Lemma~\ref{lem:bregman-projection}.
Consequently, the OMD update \eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin} coincides exactly with
the closed-form OMD update \eqref{eq:alg-omd-mult}--\eqref{eq:alg-omd-proj},
and in particular $M_{t+1}\in\Delta_H\cap\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$. Thus the positive
initialization \eqref{eq:alg-init} keeps every algorithm iterate strictly positive.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $M\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^H$ and a coordinate $i$, and abbreviate
$m=M^{(i)}$, $m_t=M_t^{(i)}$, and
$\widetilde m=\widetilde M_{t+1}^{(i)}$. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-cf-log}
\begin{aligned}
\eta g_t^{(i)}m
+
m\log\frac{m}{m_t}
&=
m
\left(
\eta g_t^{(i)}
+
\log\frac{m}{m_t}
\right) \\
&=
m\log\frac{m}{\widetilde m}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Substitution into the OMD objective gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-cf-breg}
\begin{aligned}
&\eta\langle g_t,M\rangle+D_\Psi(M\|M_t) \\
&\qquad=
D_\Psi(M\|\widetilde{M}_{t+1})
+
\sum_{i=1}^H
\left(
M_t^{(i)}-\widetilde{M}_{t+1}^{(i)}
\right).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The final sum is independent of $M$. Hence the two optimization problems have
the same minimizer, and Lemma~\ref{lem:bregman-projection} gives
\eqref{eq:alg-omd-proj}. Since $M_t>0$ coordinatewise, both the
multiplicative step and the subsequent positive rescaling preserve strict
positivity.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[One-step movement of OMD]
\label{lem:omd-move}
For the OMD update with step size $\eta>0$ and subgradient
$g_t\in\partial \ell_t(M_t)$,
\[
\|M_{t+1}-M_t\|_1 \le \eta\,\|g_t\|_\infty.
\]
In particular, if $\|g_t\|_\infty\le L$, then $\|M_{t+1}-M_t\|_1\le \eta L$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $\Delta_t\triangleq M_{t+1}-M_t$. First-order optimality of
\eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin}, evaluated at the feasible point $M_t$, gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-movement-optimality}
\begin{aligned}
&\left\langle
\eta g_t+\nabla\Psi(M_{t+1})-\nabla\Psi(M_t),
M_t-M_{t+1}
\right\rangle
\ge0, \\
&D_\Psi(M_{t+1}\|M_t)+D_\Psi(M_t\|M_{t+1}) \\
&\qquad\le
\eta\langle g_t,M_t-M_{t+1}\rangle \\
&\qquad\le
\eta\|g_t\|_\infty\|\Delta_t\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The second line uses the symmetric Bregman identity.

For every $Z\in\Delta_H\cap\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$ and $v\in\mathbb{R}^H$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-strong-convexity}
\begin{aligned}
v^\top\nabla^2\Psi(Z)v
&=
\sum_{i=1}^H\frac{(v^{(i)})^2}{Z^{(i)}} \\
&\ge
\frac{\|v\|_1^2}{\sum_{i=1}^H Z^{(i)}} \\
&\ge
\|v\|_1^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The first inequality is Cauchy--Schwarz. The segment joining $M_t$ and
$M_{t+1}$ lies in $\Delta_H\cap\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:omd-closed-form}; integrating
\eqref{eq:omd-strong-convexity} along this segment yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_\Psi(M_{t+1}\|M_t)
&\ge
\frac12\|\Delta_t\|_1^2, \\
D_\Psi(M_t\|M_{t+1})
&\ge
\frac12\|\Delta_t\|_1^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Combining these inequalities with \eqref{eq:omd-movement-optimality},
\[
\|\Delta_t\|_1^2
\le
\eta\|g_t\|_\infty\|\Delta_t\|_1.
\]
The claim is immediate if $\Delta_t=0$; otherwise divide by
$\|\Delta_t\|_1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[OMD regret for the surrogate losses]
\label{lem:omd-surrogate-regret}
For every $M\in\Delta_H$, the update \eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin} satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-surrogate-regret}
\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(M_t)
-\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(M)
\le
\frac{D_\Psi(M\|M_1)}{\eta}
+
\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{t=1}^T\|g_t\|_\infty^2.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Convexity of $\ell_t$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-convexity-step}
\begin{aligned}
\ell_t(M_t)-\ell_t(M)
&\le
\langle g_t,M_t-M\rangle \\
&=
\langle g_t,M_t-M_{t+1}\rangle \\
&\quad+
\langle g_t,M_{t+1}-M\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Write
$h_t\triangleq\nabla\Psi(M_{t+1})-\nabla\Psi(M_t)$.
First-order optimality of \eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin}, evaluated at
$M\in\Delta_H$, yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-three-point-step}
\begin{aligned}
\eta\langle g_t,M_{t+1}-M\rangle
&\le
\langle h_t,M-M_{t+1}\rangle \\
&=
D_\Psi(M\|M_t)
-D_\Psi(M\|M_{t+1}) \\
&\qquad
-D_\Psi(M_{t+1}\|M_t).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The equality is the three-point Bregman identity. Substituting
\eqref{eq:omd-three-point-step} into \eqref{eq:omd-convexity-step},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-one-step-regret}
\begin{aligned}
\ell_t(M_t)-\ell_t(M)
&\le
\frac{D_\Psi(M\|M_t)}{\eta}
-\frac{D_\Psi(M\|M_{t+1})}{\eta} \\
&\quad+
\langle g_t,M_t-M_{t+1}\rangle
\\
&\quad
-\frac{D_\Psi(M_{t+1}\|M_t)}{\eta}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Let $r_t\triangleq\|M_{t+1}-M_t\|_1$. The strong-convexity calculation
\eqref{eq:omd-strong-convexity} and H\"older's inequality give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-young-step}
\begin{aligned}
&\langle g_t,M_t-M_{t+1}\rangle
-\frac{D_\Psi(M_{t+1}\|M_t)}{\eta} \\
&\qquad\le
\|g_t\|_\infty r_t-\frac{r_t^2}{2\eta} \\
&\qquad\le
\frac{\eta}{2}\|g_t\|_\infty^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The last inequality is Young's inequality. Combining
\eqref{eq:omd-one-step-regret} and \eqref{eq:omd-young-step}, and then summing
over $t$, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(M_t)
-\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(M)
&\le
\frac{D_\Psi(M\|M_1)}{\eta} \\
&\quad
-\frac{D_\Psi(M\|M_{T+1})}{\eta} \\
&\quad+
\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{t=1}^T\|g_t\|_\infty^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $D_\Psi(M\|M_{T+1})\ge0$, this proves
\eqref{eq:omd-surrogate-regret}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Cumulative movement]
\label{lem:cum-move}
For every $t\in\{2,\ldots,T\}$ and every integer $i$ with $1\le i\le t-1$,
\[
\|M_{t-i}-M_t\|_1 \le i\,\eta L.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By the triangle inequality,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cum-move-tri}
\|M_{t-i}-M_t\|_1
\le
\sum_{j=1}^i \|M_{t-j}-M_{t-j+1}\|_1.
\end{equation}
Substituting $\|M_{t-j}-M_{t-j+1}\|_1\le\eta L$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:omd-move}
into \eqref{eq:cum-move-tri},
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|M_{t-i}-M_t\|_1
&\le
\sum_{j=1}^i \eta L \\
&=
i\,\eta L.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Clipping error]
\label{lem:bounding-u-eff}
For $M\in\Delta_H$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-state-gap}
\Gamma_t^M
\triangleq
x_t^{\pi(M)}-x_t^{\mathcal{A}}.
\end{equation}
Define the clipping error
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-clipping-error}
d_t
\triangleq
\widehat{u}_t-u_t^{\mathcal{A}}.
\end{equation}
Then, for every $t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:clipping-error-bound}
u_t^{\mathcal{A}}
=
u_t^{\pi(M_t)}-d_t,
\qquad
0\le d_t\le\alpha[\Gamma_t^{M_t}]_+.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The clipping rule and the identity $\min\{a,b\}=a-[a-b]_+$ give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:clipping-error-positive-part}
d_t
=
\bigl[\widehat{u}_t-\alpha x_t^{\mathcal{A}}\bigr]_+
\ge0.
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy-vector},
$\widehat{u}_t=u_t^{\pi(M_t)}$. Fixed-policy feasibility gives
\[
\widehat{u}_t
=
u_t^{\pi(M_t)}
\le
\alpha x_t^{\pi(M_t)}.
\]
Using monotonicity and positive homogeneity of $[\cdot]_+$ in
\eqref{eq:clipping-error-positive-part},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:clipping-error-gamma}
\begin{aligned}
d_t
&\le
\bigl[\alpha x_t^{\pi(M_t)}-\alpha x_t^{\mathcal{A}}\bigr]_+ \\
&=
\alpha[\Gamma_t^{M_t}]_+.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Finally, \eqref{eq:def-clipping-error} and
$\widehat{u}_t=u_t^{\pi(M_t)}$ give the first identity in
\eqref{eq:clipping-error-bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[State-gap recursion]
\label{lem:state-gap-recursion-eff}
For every fixed $M\in\Delta_H$ and every $t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:state-gap-exact}
\Gamma_{t+1}^M
=
\alpha\Gamma_t^M
+
\langle \widetilde{w}_t,M_t-M\rangle
-d_t.
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{align}
\Gamma_{t+1}^M
&\le
\alpha\Gamma_t^M
+
\langle \widetilde{w}_t,M_t-M\rangle,
\label{eq:GAMMA-Upper}\\
\Gamma_{t+1}^M
&\ge
\alpha\Gamma_t^M
+
\langle \widetilde{w}_t,M_t-M\rangle
-\alpha[\Gamma_t^{M_t}]_+.
\label{eq:GAMMA-Lower}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The two trajectories use the same arrival $w_t$. Hence
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:state-gap-short}
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma_{t+1}^M
&=
\alpha\Gamma_t^M
-u_t^{\pi(M)}
+u_t^{\mathcal{A}} \\
&=
\alpha\Gamma_t^M
-u_t^{\pi(M)}
+u_t^{\pi(M_t)}
-d_t \\
&=
\alpha\Gamma_t^M
+
\langle \widetilde{w}_t,M_t-M\rangle
-d_t.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This proves \eqref{eq:state-gap-exact}. The two bounds follow from
$0\le d_t\le\alpha[\Gamma_t^{M_t}]_+$ in
Lemma~\ref{lem:bounding-u-eff}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[State deviation bound]
\label{lem:state-gap-bound}
For every $t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$, with $\Gamma_t^{M}$ as in \eqref{eq:def-state-gap},
\[
-\frac{\alpha\,\eta L}{(1-\alpha)^3}
\le
\Gamma_t^{M_t}
\le
\frac{\alpha\,\eta L}{(1-\alpha)^2}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$. For $s<t$, define
\[
\xi_{s,t}
\triangleq
\langle \widetilde{w}_s,M_s-M_t\rangle.
\]
Since $\widetilde{w}_s^{(i)}=\alpha^i w_{s-i}$,
\[
\|\widetilde{w}_s\|_\infty\le\alpha.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:cum-move} and H\"older's inequality therefore give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:xi-movement-short}
\begin{aligned}
|\xi_{s,t}|
&\le
\|M_s-M_t\|_1\|\widetilde{w}_s\|_\infty \\
&\le
\alpha\eta L(t-s).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

For the fixed comparator $M_t$, unrolling
\eqref{eq:state-gap-exact} from $\Gamma_1^{M_t}=0$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gamma-exact-unroll}
\Gamma_t^{M_t}
=
\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}
\alpha^{t-1-s}\bigl(\xi_{s,t}-d_s\bigr).
\end{equation}
Since $d_s\ge0$, \eqref{eq:xi-movement-short} implies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gamma-upper-final}
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma_t^{M_t}
&\le
\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\alpha^{t-1-s}|\xi_{s,t}| \\
&\le
\alpha\eta L
\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\alpha^{t-1-s}(t-s) \\
&=
\alpha\eta L
\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}j\alpha^{j-1} \\
&\le
\frac{\alpha\eta L}{(1-\alpha)^2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The last line uses
$\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j\alpha^{j-1}=(1-\alpha)^{-2}$.

Applying \eqref{eq:clipping-error-bound} and
\eqref{eq:gamma-upper-final} at time $s$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:clipping-error-uniform}
\begin{aligned}
0\le d_s
&\le
\alpha[\Gamma_s^{M_s}]_+ \\
&\le
\frac{\alpha^2\eta L}{(1-\alpha)^2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This use of \eqref{eq:gamma-upper-final} is noncircular: its derivation used
only $d_s\ge0$. From \eqref{eq:gamma-exact-unroll},
\eqref{eq:xi-movement-short}, and \eqref{eq:clipping-error-uniform},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gamma-lower-final}
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma_t^{M_t}
&\ge
-\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\alpha^{t-1-s}|\xi_{s,t}|
-\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\alpha^{t-1-s}d_s \\
&\ge
-\frac{\alpha\eta L}{(1-\alpha)^2}
-\frac{\alpha^2\eta L}{(1-\alpha)^2}
\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\alpha^{t-1-s} \\
&\ge
-\frac{\alpha\eta L}{(1-\alpha)^2}
-\frac{\alpha^2\eta L}{(1-\alpha)^3} \\
&=
-\frac{\alpha\eta L}{(1-\alpha)^3}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Together, \eqref{eq:gamma-upper-final} and
\eqref{eq:gamma-lower-final} prove the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Action gap induced by clipping]
\label{lem:u-gap-by-b-gap-eff}
For all $t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$,
\[
0
\le
u_t^{\pi(M_t)}-u_t^{\mathcal{A}}
\le
\frac{\alpha^2\,\eta L}{(1-\alpha)^2}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:clipping-error-bound},
\[
u_t^{\pi(M_t)}-u_t^{\mathcal{A}}
=
d_t
\ge0.
\]
The same bound and Lemma~\ref{lem:state-gap-bound} give
\[
\begin{aligned}
d_t
&\le
\alpha[\Gamma_t^{M_t}]_+ \\
&\le
\frac{\alpha^2\eta L}{(1-\alpha)^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Stability term]
\label{lem:stab-sum}
For all horizons $T$,
\[
\sum_{t=1}^T \Bigl(c_t(x_t^{\mathcal{A}},u_t^{\mathcal{A}})
-c_t\bigl(x_t^{\pi(M_t)},u_t^{\pi(M_t)}\bigr)\Bigr)
\le
\frac{\alpha\,T\eta L^2}{(1-\alpha)^2}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Both state--action pairs lie in $\mathcal{D}_\alpha$. Hence
\eqref{eq:model-lipschitz}, Lemma~\ref{lem:state-gap-bound}, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:u-gap-by-b-gap-eff} give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stability-one-step-short}
\begin{aligned}
&c_t(x_t^{\mathcal{A}},u_t^{\mathcal{A}})
-c_t\bigl(x_t^{\pi(M_t)},u_t^{\pi(M_t)}\bigr) \\
&\qquad\le
L_x|\Gamma_t^{M_t}|
+
L_u\bigl|u_t^{\mathcal{A}}-u_t^{\pi(M_t)}\bigr| \\
&\qquad\le
\frac{\alpha\eta L L_x}{(1-\alpha)^3}
+
\frac{\alpha^2\eta L L_u}{(1-\alpha)^2} \\
&\qquad=
\frac{\alpha\eta L}{(1-\alpha)^2}
\left(
\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}+\alpha L_u
\right) \\
&\qquad=
\frac{\alpha\eta L^2}{(1-\alpha)^2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Summing \eqref{eq:stability-one-step-short} over $t=1,\ldots,T$ proves the
claim.
\end{proof}



\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound}]
Define the stability term
\[
S_T
\triangleq
\sum_{t=1}^T
\left(
c_t(x_t^{\mathcal{A}},u_t^{\mathcal{A}})
-\ell_t(M_t)
\right).
\]
Using
$\ell_t(M)=c_t(x_t^{\pi(M)},u_t^{\pi(M)})$, the regret decomposes as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:regret-decomp}
\begin{aligned}
\RegSDAC{T}
&=
S_T
+
\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(M_t)
-
\min_{M\in\Delta_H}\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(M).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

We first bound the initial Bregman divergence. Fix $M\in\Delta_H$ and set
$s_M\triangleq\sum_{i=1}^H M^{(i)}\in[0,1]$. Since
$M_1^{(i)}=1/(H+1)$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:breg-init-expand}
\begin{aligned}
D_\Psi(M\|M_1)
&=
\sum_{i=1}^H
M^{(i)}\log\bigl((H+1)M^{(i)}\bigr) \\
&\quad
-s_M+\frac{H}{H+1} \\
&=
\sum_{i=1}^H M^{(i)}\log M^{(i)}
+s_M\log(H+1) \\
&\quad
-s_M+\frac{H}{H+1}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Define the affine function
\[
f_H(s)\triangleq s\log(H+1)-s+\frac{H}{H+1},
\qquad s\in[0,1].
\]
Because $z\log z\le0$ for $z\in[0,1]$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:breg-init-affine}
D_\Psi(M\|M_1)
\le
f_H(s_M).
\end{equation}
The function $f_H$ is affine on $[0,1]$, and
\[
\begin{aligned}
f_H(0)
&=
\frac{H}{H+1}
\le
\log(H+1), \\
f_H(1)
&=
\log(H+1)-\frac{1}{H+1}
\le
\log(H+1).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here the first inequality follows from
$\log z\ge1-z^{-1}$ for $z\ge1$. Therefore,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:breg-init-bound}
D_\Psi(M\|M_1)
\le
\log(H+1)
\qquad
\text{for every }M\in\Delta_H.
\end{equation}

Lemma~\ref{lem:omd-surrogate-regret},
$\|g_t\|_\infty\le L$, and \eqref{eq:breg-init-bound} imply, for every
$M\in\Delta_H$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-ell-raw}
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{t=1}^T\bigl(\ell_t(M_t)-\ell_t(M)\bigr)
&\le
\frac{D_\Psi(M\|M_1)}{\eta}
+
\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{t=1}^T\|g_t\|_\infty^2 \\
&\le
\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta}
+
\frac{\eta L^2T}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Since \eqref{eq:omd-ell-raw} holds uniformly over $M$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-term}
\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(M_t)
-
\min_{M\in\Delta_H}\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(M)
\le
\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta}
+
\frac{\eta L^2T}{2}.
\end{equation}
Lemma~\ref{lem:stab-sum} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stab-term}
S_T
\le
\frac{\alpha\eta L^2T}{(1-\alpha)^2}.
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:omd-term} and \eqref{eq:stab-term} into
\eqref{eq:regret-decomp},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:regret-eta}
\RegSDAC{T}
\le
\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta}
+
\eta L^2T
\left(
\frac12+\frac{\alpha}{(1-\alpha)^2}
\right).
\end{equation}

Set
\[
A\triangleq\log(H+1),
\qquad
B\triangleq
L^2T
\left(
\frac12+\frac{\alpha}{(1-\alpha)^2}
\right).
\]
Both constants are positive. The right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:regret-eta} is $A/\eta+B\eta$, whose derivative is
\[
-\frac{A}{\eta^2}+B.
\]
Its unique minimizer is therefore
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:eta-star}
\eta^*
=
\sqrt{\frac{A}{B}}
=
\sqrt{
\frac{\log(H+1)}
{L^2T\left(\tfrac12+\tfrac{\alpha}{(1-\alpha)^2}\right)}
}.
\end{equation}
At this value,
\[
\frac{A}{\eta^*}
=
\sqrt{AB},
\qquad
B\eta^*
=
\sqrt{AB}.
\]
Thus \eqref{eq:regret-eta} becomes
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:regret-opt}
\RegSDAC{T}
\le
2L
\sqrt{
\left(
\frac12+\frac{\alpha}{(1-\alpha)^2}
\right)
T\log(H+1)
}.
\end{equation}
\end{proof}
