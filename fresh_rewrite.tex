\begin{abstract}
We study online control of a scalar storage system with nonnegative adversarial
resource arrivals and state-dependent action constraints. The adversarial,
time-varying cost depends on both state and action. At each time, the
controller selects a feasible action before the current resource arrival and cost
are revealed. To handle the coupling between feasibility and online learning,
we introduce \emph{Simplex Disturbance-Action Control} (SDAC), whose policies
have $H$ nonnegative parameters summing to at most one. Every fixed SDAC policy
is feasible by construction. Our online SDAC controller updates these
parameters using entropic online mirror descent while preserving feasibility along
the time-varying trajectory. We prove a regret bound of
$O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log(H+1)}\right)$ relative to the best fixed SDAC policy, and prove a
lower bound showing that the dependence on $T$ is tight for fixed $H$. We also introduce
infinite-memory SDAC policies.
SDAC policies approximate this class with an error that decreases geometrically
with $H$. For a suitable choice $H=\Theta(\log T)$,
the resulting regret against infinite-memory SDAC policies is
$O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log\log T}\right)$. The framework applies to energy-harvesting
batteries and other storage-constrained resource systems.
\end{abstract}

Online control, disturbance-action policies, storage systems, energy harvesting,

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{Motivation and Problem Setting}
\IEEEPARstart{M}{any} resource-allocation systems have a storage state that changes
over time and limits the control action at each time. An action that yields an immediate
benefit or reward may leave less resource for future decisions. On the other hand, an
overly conservative resource allocation policy can miss many opportunities for reward. The
controller must balance these effects
while satisfying the action constraint at every time.

We study this problem for a scalar storage system that loses a fixed fraction of
its stored resource each period.
For example, an imperfect battery can lose some of its charge even if it is not used.
At each time $t$, the controller observes the current resource state $x_t$ and chooses an amount $u_t$ to use.
This incurs a cost that depends on both $x_t$ and $u_t$. Rewards can be modeled as negative costs.
The cost $c_t(x_t, u_t)$ is revealed \emph{after} the decision $u_t$ is made.
The cost sequence and the sequence of new resource arrivals may
be chosen adversarially, so we do not assume a known
probability model. Our goal is to construct an online controller whose
cumulative cost is close to that of the best SDAC policy, chosen in hindsight,
from a specified benchmark class.

One difficulty is that the current action affects the next state. In online control,
\emph{Disturbance-Action Control} (DAC) parameterizes actions using a linear filter
of past disturbances, enabling efficient algorithms with bounded regret.
In our storage model, however, some DAC policies can produce negative actions or
actions larger than the available resource.

We address this issue by introducing \emph{Simplex Disturbance-Action Control}
(SDAC). SDAC restricts the filter parameters to a subprobability simplex and includes
powers of the retention coefficient in the filter. These choices ensure that
every fixed SDAC policy satisfies the action constraint. The state and action
along each SDAC-policy trajectory are also affine in the policy parameter.
Thus, a convex state--action cost is convex in that parameter, allowing
updates through online convex optimization.

\subsection{Model and Information Pattern}

The storage state $x_t\ge0$ evolves according to
\[
x_{t+1}=\alpha x_t-u_t+w_t,
\]
where $\alpha\in(0,1)$ is a fixed retention coefficient, $u_t$ is the control
action, and $w_t\in[0,1]$ is a nonnegative resource arrival. The action must satisfy
\[
0\le u_t\le\alpha x_t.
\]
Thus, the controller cannot use more resource than remains after storage loss.

At time $t$, the controller chooses $u_t$ using the current state and the
information available through time $t-1$. The arrival $w_t$ and the convex cost
function $c_t$ are revealed after the action is selected, and the controller incurs
the cost $c_t(x_t,u_t)$. Both the online trajectory and every SDAC-policy
trajectory used as a benchmark must satisfy the same dynamics and action
constraint. The formal model and assumptions are given in
Section~\ref{sec:model-setup}.

Our main application is energy-harvesting battery management, where $x_t$ is the
battery state of charge, $w_t$ is the harvested energy, $u_t$ is the energy
used, and $1-\alpha$ is the fraction of stored energy lost per period.
Similar dynamics arise in perishable inventory systems, water reservoirs with
storage loss, and queues with abandonment. In many standard queueing models,
$\alpha=1$; that case is outside our main results and is discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:extensions}.


\subsection{Contributions}

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Policy class.}
    We introduce SDAC policies parameterized by a
    subprobability simplex. We prove that every such SDAC policy is feasible
    for every arrival sequence with entries in $[0,1]$.

    We also introduce infinite-memory SDAC policies in
    Section~\ref{subsec:infinite-memory-sdac}. Their parameter domain extends the
    subprobability simplex to nonnegative sequences whose sum is at most one.
    This class contains every SDAC policy and every feasible
    fixed-fraction policy $u_t=kx_t$ with $k\in[0,\alpha]$.
    Truncating the parameter sequence gives an SDAC policy whose
    approximation error decreases geometrically with the memory horizon $H$.

\item \textbf{Online control and regret.}
    We develop an online SDAC controller that updates its parameter using
    entropic online mirror descent (OMD) over the subprobability simplex.
    We prove that the controller remains feasible and achieves
    $O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log(H+1)}\right)$
    regret against the best fixed SDAC policy.
    For a suitable choice $H=\Theta(\log T)$, the regret against
    infinite-memory SDAC policies is
    $O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log\log T}\right)$. The same bound
    holds against the best fixed-fraction policy.

\item \textbf{Lower bound.}
    We prove a lower bound of order $\sqrt{T}$ for the expected regret of every
    causal feasible controller, including randomized controllers; see
    Theorem~\ref{thm:sdac-lower-bound}. When $T$ is at least on the order of
    $(1-\alpha)^{-1}$, the lower bound scales as
    $\Omega((1-\alpha)^{-1}\sqrt{T})$ near $\alpha=1$.
    The upper bound contains a factor of $\sqrt{\log(H+1)}$ that does not
    appear in the lower bound. The bounds also differ by a factor of
    $(1-\alpha)^{-1/2}$ in their dependence on $1-\alpha$.
    Subsequent work closes these gaps for the same policy class
    \cite{asgari2026cumulative}.
\end{itemize}


\subsection{Related Work}

\paragraph{Online control}
Goulart et al.\ use a parameterization similar to DAC for a related constrained
control problem under bounded disturbances \cite{goulart2006optimization}.
Agarwal et al.\ use DAC policies to obtain $\widetilde O(\sqrt{T})$ regret
against strongly stable linear controllers \cite{agarwal2019online}.
Golowich et al.\ use simplex constraints on DAC parameters and entropic
mirror descent to control population dynamics \cite{golowich2024population}.
Their feasible action set is independent of the current state. In our
storage model, the feasible action interval $[0,\alpha x_t]$ changes with
the state. SDAC combines a subprobability simplex with the factors
$\alpha^i$ accounting for storage loss to ensure that every fixed SDAC
policy satisfies this constraint for every admissible arrival sequence.
When the policy parameters change online, our controller clips the action
to preserve feasibility. Our regret analysis bounds the additional cost
caused by parameter updates and clipping.

Li et al.\ study online control with fixed state and action constraints
\cite{li2021onlineConstrained}. Their method requires a linear controller
that stays strictly within the constraints. This condition can fail here:
when $x_t=0$, feasibility requires $u_t=0$.
Liu et al.\ study online nonstochastic control with static and adversarial
constraints \cite{liu2023constrainedControl}. They allow joint state--action
constraints, including our fixed affine constraint $0\le u_t\le\alpha x_t$.
Their $\widetilde O(\sqrt{T})$ regret guarantee requires a fixed DAC policy
that satisfies the constraints with a uniform positive margin. Their
projection method for avoiding constraint violations also relies on strict
feasibility.

This margin is unavailable in our model. If $x_1=0$ and $w_t=0$ for every
$t$, then $x_t=u_t=0$ at every time, so both inequalities in the action
constraint hold with equality.

Without strict feasibility, their result allows constraint violations.
Our controller satisfies the action constraint at every time and achieves
$O(\sqrt{T\log(H+1)})$ regret against the best fixed SDAC policy, without
assuming strict feasibility.

Online convex optimization with memory studies costs that depend on past
decisions \cite{anava2015memory,kumar2023unbounded}. We use a related
approach: analyze costs with the SDAC parameter held fixed, then bound
the additional cost caused by online updates. Our analysis accounts for
storage loss and action clipping (Lemma~\ref{lem:contractive-deviation}).
Kumar et al.\ also give lower bounds when the effect of past decisions
decays geometrically \cite{kumar2023unbounded}. Our lower bound applies to
causal feasible controllers, with fixed SDAC policies as the benchmark
(Section~\ref{sec:regret-lower-bound}).


\paragraph{Energy harvesting}
Fixed-fraction policies for stochastic arrivals motivate our infinite-memory
SDAC class. Shaviv and \"Ozg\"ur \cite{shaviv2016universally} study these
policies under i.i.d.\ energy arrivals. Arafa et al.\ \cite{arafa2018online}
extend the analysis to increasing concave rewards. Zibaeenejad and Chen
\cite{zibaeenejad2019lookahead} study allocation with knowledge of upcoming
arrivals under a Bernoulli model in which each nonzero arrival fully
recharges the battery. For our storage dynamics, infinite-memory SDAC
includes every feasible fixed-fraction policy
(Corollary~\ref{cor:fraction-as-sdac}).

Yu and Neely \cite{Yu2019} and Asgari and Neely \cite{10.1145/3428337}
study online energy allocation under stochastic arrivals. In the latter
work, each arrival is known before allocation and the cost is revealed
afterward. Both works compare expected performance to fixed actions whose
energy use does not exceed the mean arrival. These benchmark actions need
not be feasible on every arrival sequence, and the costs do not depend
directly on the battery state. Our fixed SDAC policies remain feasible on
their own trajectories, with actions that depend on the arrival history.

Lin et al.\ \cite{lin2025optimal} study online energy allocation with
arbitrary arrivals and a class of time-varying concave rewards. They give
competitive-ratio guarantees against an offline optimum and observe the current
arrival and reward before acting. In our setting, the arrival and cost are
revealed after the action, and convex costs may depend on both the storage
state and the action.

\paragraph{Storage management}
Qin et al.\ study storage control with energy loss and capacity constraints
under stochastic assumptions \cite{qin2016online}. They observe the current
price and net demand before acting and give guarantees for long-run average
cost. Kim et al.\ study a related storage problem and give competitive
guarantees for the value of meeting demand \cite{kim2016storage}.
Both works account for limited storage capacity. Our analysis assumes
enough capacity to avoid overflow and studies regret under adversarial
arrivals and costs revealed after the action.

\paragraph{Queueing and network optimization}
Classical queueing control studies throughput and queue stability
\cite{tassiulas1992stability,tassiulas1993dynamic,mckeown1999achieving}.
Stochastic network optimization studies tradeoffs between average performance
and average queue backlog \cite{neely2010stochastic}.

Khojastepour and Sabharwal \cite{khojastepour2004delay} use filters similar
to SDAC for scheduling with a delay constraint. Their fixed filters combine
current and past arrivals using nonnegative coefficients summing to one.
They study these filters under i.i.d.\ arrivals and also develop a
time-varying policy that does not require the arrival distribution.
SDAC uses parameters summing to at most one, accounts for storage loss,
and acts before the current arrival is observed. We study regret under
adversarial convex costs depending on both state and action.

\paragraph{Inventory and reservoir management}
Yang et al.\ \cite{yang2020online} study online inventory control with
linear costs and capacity and rate constraints. They obtain optimal
competitive-ratio guarantees against an offline optimum. They observe the current
demand and price before acting, whereas our controller acts before the
current arrival and cost are revealed.

Hihat et al.\ \cite{hihat2023inventory} allow non-i.i.d.\ demands and prove
feasibility and $O(\sqrt{T})$ expected regret against the best fixed
inventory target. Their analysis requires probabilistic assumptions on
demand. Our arrivals are arbitrary, and our benchmark is a fixed SDAC policy.
Hihat and Fermanian \cite{hihat2024online} study inventory systems with
multiple products, capacity constraints, and delivery delays. They evaluate
their method empirically and leave regret guarantees open. Classical
reservoir theory studies storage under random arrivals \cite{moran1954dams}.


\section{Model and Regret Benchmark}
\label{sec:model-setup}

For every positive integer $n$, we write
$[n]\triangleq\{1,\ldots,n\}$. For every $z\in\mathbb{R}$, we define
$[z]_+\triangleq\max\{z,0\}$. For
$\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^n$, the $i$-th coordinate is denoted by $v_i$. If the
vector already carries a subscript, the coordinate is appended after a comma: the
$i$-th coordinate of $\mathbf{v}_t$ is $v_{t,i}$. We write
$\mathbf{1}$ for the all-ones vector, with dimension clear from context, and
$\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ for the Euclidean inner product. We use $\log$ for the
natural logarithm.

Fix a positive integer $T$ and a known retention coefficient
$\alpha\in(0,1)$. The state $x_t\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}$ is the resource level at the
beginning of time $t$. The system starts empty:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-init}
x_1=0.
\end{equation}
At each time $t\in[T]$, the controller chooses an action
$u_t\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-feasibility}
0\le u_t\le\alpha x_t.
\end{equation}
The system then receives an arrival $w_t\in[0,1]$ and evolves according to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-dynamics}
x_{t+1}=\alpha x_t-u_t+w_t.
\end{equation}
Here, $1-\alpha$ is the fraction of stored resource lost per period.
The constraint \eqref{eq:model-feasibility} ensures that the action does not exceed
the amount remaining after this loss.

In the online-control literature, $w_t$ is commonly called a disturbance. Here, we
call it an \emph{arrival} because it is nonnegative, while retaining the standard
name Disturbance-Action Control. Nonnegativity is important for both the storage
interpretation and the
feasibility properties of SDAC.

At time $t$, the controller chooses $u_t$ before observing $w_t$ or $c_t$. After the
action is selected, the arrival and the entire cost function are revealed, the
controller incurs $c_t(x_t,u_t)$, and the state is updated according to
\eqref{eq:model-dynamics}.

For every feasible trajectory, \eqref{eq:model-feasibility},
\eqref{eq:model-dynamics}, and $w_t\in[0,1]$ give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-state-recursion-bound}
0\le x_{t+1}\le\alpha x_t+1.
\end{equation}
Induction using \eqref{eq:model-init} and
\eqref{eq:model-state-recursion-bound}, with $0<\alpha<1$, gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-state-bound}
\begin{aligned}
0\le x_t
&\le\sum_{s=0}^{t-2}\alpha^s
=\frac{1-\alpha^{t-1}}{1-\alpha}\\
&\le\frac{1}{1-\alpha},
\qquad t\in[T+1].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Equations \eqref{eq:model-feasibility} and \eqref{eq:model-state-bound}
place every feasible
state--action pair in the compact set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-domain}
\mathcal{D}_\alpha
\triangleq
\left\{
(x,u):
0\le x\le\frac{1}{1-\alpha},
0\le u\le\alpha x
\right\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{remark}[Storage capacity]
A storage capacity of $1/(1-\alpha)$ suffices to avoid overflow.
\end{remark}

Each revealed cost function $c_t:\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^2\to\mathbb{R}$ is convex in
$(x,u)$ and satisfies the following Lipschitz condition on $\mathcal{D}_\alpha$:
there are constants $L_x,L_u>0$ such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-lipschitz}
|c_t(x,u)-c_t(x',u')|
\le
L_x|x-x'|+L_u|u-u'|
\end{equation}
for all $(x,u),(x',u')\in\mathcal{D}_\alpha$.
At every $(x,u)\in\mathcal{D}_\alpha$, we also assume access to a cost subgradient
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-cost-subgradient}
\boldsymbol{\zeta}_t(x,u)
=
\bigl(\zeta_t^x(x,u),\zeta_t^u(x,u)\bigr)
\in\partial c_t(x,u)
\end{equation}
satisfying
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model-subgradient-bounds}
|\zeta_t^x(x,u)|\le L_x,
\qquad
|\zeta_t^u(x,u)|\le L_u.
\end{equation}
These subgradient bounds hold, for example, when $c_t$ has a convex
extension to an open neighborhood of $\mathcal{D}_\alpha$ satisfying
\eqref{eq:model-lipschitz}; see
\cite[Thm.~9.13]{rockafellar1998variational}.

We use $x_t$ and $u_t$ without parameter arguments for the trajectory generated by
the online controller. Its cumulative cost is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-cumulative-cost}
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}
\triangleq
\sum_{t=1}^T c_t(x_t,u_t).
\end{equation}
The superscript $\mathrm{on}$ stands for online.


Section~\ref{sec:sdac} defines a class of SDAC policies characterized by a
parameter $\mathbf{m}$ in a parameter set $\mathcal{M}_H$.
For a fixed $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, the corresponding \emph{SDAC-policy
trajectory} is the state--action
sequence $\{(x_t(\mathbf{m}),u_t(\mathbf{m}))\}_{t=1}^T$ obtained by applying
the SDAC policy with parameter $\mathbf{m}$ at every time, starting from
$x_1(\mathbf{m})=0$.
Its cumulative cost is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-sdac-cumulative-cost}
J_T(\mathbf{m})
\triangleq
\sum_{t=1}^T c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{m}),u_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr).
\end{equation}
The online policy in \eqref{eq:def-cumulative-cost} and the fixed SDAC policy in
\eqref{eq:def-sdac-cumulative-cost} are evaluated on the same arrival sequence
$\{w_t\}_{t=1}^T$ and cost function sequence $\{c_t\}_{t=1}^T$.
For a fixed positive integer $H$, the regret against the best fixed
SDAC policy is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-regret}
\RegSDAC{T}(H)
\triangleq
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}
-\min_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H}J_T(\mathbf{m}).
\end{equation}
Our objective is to satisfy \eqref{eq:model-feasibility} at every time and achieve
sublinear regret.

\section{Simplex Disturbance-Action Control (SDAC)}
\label{sec:sdac}

\subsection{From Disturbance-Action Control to SDAC}
DAC uses a finite linear filter of past disturbances. Under standard conditions, this
parameterization yields convex surrogate costs and efficient online updates. In
our scalar storage model, DAC has the following form:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dac-param}
u_t = \sum_{i=1}^{H} \theta_i w_{t-i},
\end{equation}
where $H$ is a positive integer (the memory horizon) and
$\boldsymbol{\theta}=(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_H)^\top\in\mathbb{R}^H$ is the vector
of filter coefficients.

For our constrained model, an unrestricted filter does not ensure feasibility: its
action can exceed $\alpha x_t$ or become negative.
We therefore introduce \emph{Simplex Disturbance-Action Control} (SDAC), which uses a
simplex-constrained filter tailored to the storage dynamics. This restriction makes
every fixed SDAC policy feasible while keeping surrogate costs convex and online
updates efficient.


\subsection{Policies and Structural Properties}
Fix a memory horizon $H\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}$ and define the subprobability simplex
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-simplex}
\mathcal{M}_H
\triangleq
\left\{
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{R}^H:
m_i\ge0,\quad
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\le1
\right\}.
\end{equation}
The inequalities $m_i\ge0$ hold for every $i\in[H]$.
We write $\|\mathbf{m}\|_1=\sum_{i=1}^H|m_i|$ for the $\ell_1$ norm.
With the convention $w_\tau\equiv0$ for $\tau\le0$, each
$\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$ defines an SDAC policy through
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-sdac-policy}
u_t(\mathbf{m})
\;\triangleq\;
\sum_{i=1}^{H} m_i\alpha^i w_{t-i}.
\end{equation}
Here, $m_i\alpha^i w_s$ is the amount from arrival $w_s$ used $i$ periods later.

The following state decomposition yields two structural properties: every
fixed SDAC policy satisfies the feasibility constraint
\eqref{eq:model-feasibility}, and its induced cost is convex and Lipschitz in
$\mathbf{m}$. Omitted proofs appear in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-sdac-proofs}.

\begin{lemma}[State decomposition under SDAC]
\label{lem:sdac-state-decomp}
Fix a positive integer $H$.
For $t\in[T]$ and $i\in[H]$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-arrival-sums}
\begin{aligned}
r_t(i)
&\triangleq
\sum_{j=1}^{i}\alpha^{j-1}w_{t-j},
\\
r_t
&\triangleq
\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}\alpha^{j-1}w_{t-j},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where the empty sum defining $r_1$ is zero. Then, for every
$\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$ and $t\in[T]$, the SDAC-policy state associated
with $\mathbf{m}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-state-decomp}
x_t(\mathbf{m})
=
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i r_t(i)
+\bigl(1-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\bigr)r_t.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

By \eqref{eq:sdac-state-decomp} and \eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy}, the state and
action of an SDAC policy can be written together as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-affine-pair}
\begin{aligned}
x_t(\mathbf{m})&=r_t+\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x,\mathbf{m}\rangle,\\
u_t(\mathbf{m})&=\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}\rangle,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where the coefficient vectors in $\mathbb{R}^H$ are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-phi-u}
\begin{aligned}
\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x
&\triangleq\bigl(r_t(1)-r_t,\ldots,r_t(H)-r_t\bigr)^\top,\\
\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u
&\triangleq\bigl(\alpha w_{t-1},\alpha^2 w_{t-2},\ldots,\alpha^H w_{t-H}\bigr)^\top.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The superscripts $x$ and $u$ identify the coefficients for the state and action,
respectively. Both vectors and $r_t$ depend only on past arrivals and $\alpha$,
so they are independent of $\mathbf{m}$. Thus, both the state and action are
affine in $\mathbf{m}$.

\begin{lemma}[Feasibility of SDAC policies]
\label{lem:sdac-feasibility}
For every $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, the fixed SDAC policy
indexed by $\mathbf{m}$ is feasible:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-policy-feasibility}
0\le u_t(\mathbf{m})\le \alpha x_t(\mathbf{m})
\qquad \text{for every }t\in[T].
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $t\in[T]$. By \eqref{eq:def-arrival-sums} and $w_\tau\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-arrival-available}
\alpha r_t(i)-\alpha^i w_{t-i}
=\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha^j w_{t-j}
\ge0,\qquad i\in[H].
\end{equation}
Using \eqref{eq:def-simplex}, $r_t\ge0$ from \eqref{eq:def-arrival-sums},
and \eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy},
\begin{align*}
0\le u_t(\mathbf{m})
&=\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\alpha^i w_{t-i} \\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:sdac-arrival-available}}{\le}
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\alpha r_t(i) \\
&\le\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\alpha r_t(i)
+\alpha\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\right)r_t \\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:sdac-state-decomp}}{=}\alpha x_t(\mathbf{m}).
\end{align*}
This proves \eqref{eq:sdac-policy-feasibility}.
\end{proof}


\begin{lemma}[Convexity and Lipschitz continuity of the surrogate cost]
\label{lem:sdac-surrogate}
For $t\in[T]$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-surrogate-cost}
\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
\triangleq
c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{m}),u_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr),
\qquad
\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-surrogate-G}
G_\alpha
\triangleq
\alpha\left(\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}+L_u\right).
\end{equation}
Then $\ell_t$ is convex on $\mathcal{M}_H$ and, for all
$\mathbf{m},\mathbf{m}'\in\mathcal{M}_H$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-lipschitz}
|\ell_t(\mathbf{m})-\ell_t(\mathbf{m}')|
\le
G_\alpha\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\|_1.
\end{equation}
For $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, use \eqref{eq:model-cost-subgradient} to define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-subgradient}
\begin{aligned}
(\zeta_t^x,\zeta_t^u)
&=
\boldsymbol{\zeta}_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{m}),u_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr), \\
\mathbf{g}_t
&\triangleq
\zeta_t^x\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x
+\zeta_t^u\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{align}
\mathbf{g}_t&\in\partial\ell_t(\mathbf{m}),
\label{eq:surr-subgradient-membership}\\
\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty&\le G_\alpha.
\label{eq:surr-subgradient-bound}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\subsection{Approximation of Infinite-Memory SDAC Policies}
\label{subsec:infinite-memory-sdac}

Define the infinite-memory parameter domain
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-infinite-simplex}
\mathcal{M}_\infty
\triangleq
\left\{
\mathbf{m}=(m_i)_{i\ge1}:m_i\ge0,\quad
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}m_i\le1
\right\}.
\end{equation}
Each $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_\infty$ defines an infinite-memory SDAC policy
through
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-infinite-sdac-policy}
u_t(\mathbf{m})
\triangleq
\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}m_i\alpha^i w_{t-i}.
\end{equation}
The state $x_t(\mathbf{m})$ follows \eqref{eq:model-dynamics} with initial
condition \eqref{eq:model-init}.
We use \eqref{eq:def-sdac-cumulative-cost} to define $J_T(\mathbf{m})$ for
$\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_\infty$ as well.
After extending vectors in $\mathcal{M}_H$ by zeros, SDAC policies with horizon $H$
are exactly the infinite-memory SDAC policies with $m_i=0$ for every $i>H$.

\begin{lemma}[Approximation of infinite-memory SDAC policies]
\label{lem:sdac-truncation-approx}
Every infinite-memory SDAC policy is feasible for every arrival sequence with
entries in $[0,1]$. Fix $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_\infty$ and an integer $H\ge1$,
and define its truncation by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-sdac-truncation}
\mathbf{m}_H(\mathbf{m})\triangleq(m_1,\ldots,m_H)^\top.
\end{equation}
Then $\mathbf{m}_H(\mathbf{m})\in\mathcal{M}_H$. If each $c_t$ satisfies
\eqref{eq:model-lipschitz}, then, for every such arrival sequence,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-truncation-cost-gap}
\begin{aligned}
&\left|J_T\bigl(\mathbf{m}_H(\mathbf{m})\bigr)-J_T(\mathbf{m})\right| \\
&\qquad\le
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)\alpha^{H+1}T.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

A \emph{fixed-fraction policy}, parameterized by $k\in[0,\alpha]$, chooses
$u_t=kx_t$ at every time. We denote its trajectory by
$\{(x_t(k),u_t(k))\}_{t=1}^T$, where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-fraction-trajectory}
\begin{aligned}
u_t(k)&=kx_t(k),\qquad x_1(k)=0,\\
x_{t+1}(k)&=(\alpha-k)x_t(k)+w_t.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Since $\alpha-k\ge0$ and $w_t\ge0$, induction gives $x_t(k)\ge0$.
Thus, $0\le u_t(k)=kx_t(k)\le\alpha x_t(k)$, so every such policy is feasible.

\begin{corollary}[Fixed-fraction policies as infinite-memory SDAC policies]
\label{cor:fraction-as-sdac}
For $k\in[0,\alpha]$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-fraction-parameter}
m_i(k)=\frac{k}{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{k}{\alpha}\right)^{i-1},
\qquad i\ge1,
\end{equation}
where $0^0=1$.
Then $\mathbf{m}(k)\in\mathcal{M}_\infty$ and, for every arrival sequence with
entries in $[0,1]$, the infinite-memory SDAC policy indexed by $\mathbf{m}(k)$
coincides with the feasible fixed-fraction policy $u_t(k)=kx_t(k)$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fraction-sdac-trajectory}
\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{m}(k)),u_t(\mathbf{m}(k))\bigr)
=\bigl(x_t(k),u_t(k)\bigr),
\qquad t\in[T].
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\section{Online SDAC Controller}
\label{sec:online-algorithm}

The controller maintains a parameter $\mathbf{m}_t\in\mathcal{M}_H$ and updates
it by OMD \cite{hazan2016introduction} with step size $\eta>0$ and unnormalized
negentropy:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-negentropy}
\Psi(\mathbf{m}) \triangleq \sum_{i=1}^H\bigl(m_i\log m_i-m_i\bigr), \qquad
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^H,
\end{equation}
where $0\log0\triangleq0$. For $\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^H$ and
$\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$, the associated Bregman divergence is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-bregman}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{v})
\triangleq
\sum_{i=1}^H\Bigl(m_i\log\tfrac{m_i}{v_i}-m_i+v_i\Bigr).
\end{equation}
Initialize the online parameter as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-init}
\mathbf{m}_1\triangleq\frac{1}{H+1}\mathbf{1}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Action selection}
At time $t$, the controller uses $\mathbf{m}_t$, initialized by
\eqref{eq:alg-init} for $t=1$ and produced by the OMD update at time $t-1$
for $t\ge2$. The feasibility guarantee for an SDAC policy uses the same parameter at every
time. When $\mathbf{m}_t$ varies, the
nominal action $\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\rangle$ can exceed
$\alpha x_t$. The controller therefore selects
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-feasible-action}
u_t
\triangleq
\min\!\left\{
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\rangle,
\alpha x_t
\right\}.
\end{equation}
The state then evolves according to \eqref{eq:model-dynamics}.
Since $\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u$ and $\mathbf{m}_t$ are componentwise nonnegative,
$\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\rangle\ge0$. If $x_t\ge0$, then
\eqref{eq:alg-feasible-action} gives $0\le u_t\le\alpha x_t$, and
\eqref{eq:model-dynamics} gives $x_{t+1}\ge w_t\ge0$. Since $x_1=0$, induction
shows that the online trajectory is feasible.

Equation \eqref{eq:availability-clipped-action} shows that the $D_\Psi$-projection of
$\mathbf{m}_t$ onto the parameters compatible with $x_t$ induces exactly the
clipped action $u_t$.
\paragraph{Surrogate cost and OMD update}
After $w_t$ and $c_t$ are revealed, the controller evaluates the subgradient
$\mathbf{g}_t$ in \eqref{eq:surr-subgradient} at $\mathbf{m}_t$ and sets
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-omd-argmin}
\mathbf{m}_{t+1}
\triangleq
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H}
\left\{
\eta\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}\rangle
+D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_t)
\right\}.
\end{equation}
Lemma~\ref{lem:omd-closed-form} gives a two-step implementation. First, take the
coordinatewise multiplicative step
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-omd-mult}
\widetilde{m}_{t+1,i}
\triangleq
m_{t,i}\exp\!\bigl(-\eta g_{t,i}\bigr),
\qquad
i\in[H].
\end{equation}
Next, project onto $\mathcal{M}_H$ by rescaling if needed:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alg-omd-proj}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{m}_{t+1}
&=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}) \\
&=\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}}
{\max\{1,\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}\|_1\}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}


\section{Regret Upper Bounds}
\label{sec:regret-analysis}
The next theorem bounds the regret of the online SDAC controller against the
best fixed SDAC policy. Proofs appear in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-regret-proofs}.

\begin{theorem}[Regret against fixed SDAC policies]
\label{thm:regret-bound}
Suppose the costs satisfy the assumptions of Section~\ref{sec:model-setup}.
For any step size $\eta>0$, the controller in
Section~\ref{sec:online-algorithm}, initialized by \eqref{eq:alg-init}, satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:regret-theorem-bound}
\RegSDAC{T}(H)
\le
\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta}+\eta T\Gamma_\alpha,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-Gamma-alpha}
\Gamma_\alpha
\triangleq
\frac{G_\alpha^2}{2}
+
\frac{\alpha G_\alpha(L_x+\alpha L_u)}{(1-\alpha)^2},
\end{equation}
and $G_\alpha=\alpha\bigl(\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}+L_u\bigr)$ as in
\eqref{eq:def-surrogate-G}.

Choosing
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-optimal-step-size}
\eta^*=\sqrt{\frac{\log(H+1)}{T\Gamma_\alpha}}
\end{equation}
gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:regret-optimized-bound}
\RegSDAC{T}(H)\le 2\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha T\log(H+1)}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The proof combines the OMD bound for the surrogate costs
(Lemma~\ref{lem:omd-surrogate-regret}) with the following bound on the
difference between realized and surrogate costs.
\begin{lemma}[Stability under online parameter updates]
\label{lem:contractive-deviation}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound}, the online SDAC
controller satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stability-sum-bound}
\sum_{t=1}^T \Bigl(c_t(x_t,u_t)-\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)\Bigr)
\le
\frac{\alpha\eta T G_\alpha(L_x+\alpha L_u)}{(1-\alpha)^2}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}


\begin{corollary}[No-regret against infinite-memory SDAC policies]
\label{cor:regret-vs-infinite-sdac}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound}, run the online SDAC
controller with step size $\eta^*$ and memory horizon
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-memory-horizon}
H=H_T
\triangleq
\max\!\left\{1,\left\lceil\frac{\log T}{\log(1/\alpha)}\right\rceil\right\},
\end{equation}
where $\eta^*$ is given by \eqref{eq:def-optimal-step-size} with $H=H_T$.
Define the regret against the best fixed
infinite-memory SDAC policy by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-infinite-regret}
\RegSDAC{T}(\infty)
\triangleq
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}-\inf_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_\infty}J_T(\mathbf{m}).
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:infinite-sdac-regret-bound}
\begin{aligned}
\RegSDAC{T}(\infty)
&\le
2\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha T\log(H_T+1)}\\
&\quad+
\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)\alpha^{H_T+1}T.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For fixed $\alpha,L_x,L_u$, the right-hand side is
$O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log\log T}\right)=o(T)$.
The same bound holds against the best fixed-fraction policy, since
Corollary~\ref{cor:fraction-as-sdac} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fraction-regret-comparison}
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}
-\min_{k\in[0,\alpha]}\sum_{t=1}^T c_t\bigl(x_t(k),u_t(k)\bigr)
\le\RegSDAC{T}(\infty).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\section{Minimax Regret Lower Bound}
\label{sec:regret-lower-bound}

We now show that, with $H,\alpha,L_x,L_u$ fixed, no causal feasible
controller can guarantee worst-case expected regret growing more slowly
than $\sqrt{T}$. Thus, the upper bound in
Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound} has the best possible dependence on $T$,
up to a constant factor. The lower bound applies to every causal feasible
controller; only the hindsight benchmark is restricted to SDAC policies.

\begin{theorem}[SDAC regret lower bound]
\label{thm:sdac-lower-bound}
Fix $\alpha\in(0,1)$, $L_x,L_u>0$, and an integer $H\ge1$.
Assume the dynamics, initial state, and feedback of
Section~\ref{sec:model-setup}, and let the integer horizon satisfy
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-lower-horizon}
T\ge2\left\lceil\frac{\log 2}{\log(1/\alpha)}\right\rceil+2.
\end{equation}
For every causal feasible online controller $\mathcal{A}$, possibly
randomized, there is a deterministic sequence of arrivals and costs,
fixed before the interaction, that satisfies the model assumptions with
constants $L_x,L_u$ and gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-lower-simplified}
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\!\left[\RegSDAC{T}(H)\right]
\ge\frac{G_\alpha}{8}\sqrt{T},
\end{equation}
where $G_\alpha=\alpha(L_x/(1-\alpha)+L_u)$ as in
\eqref{eq:def-surrogate-G}.
The expectation is over the controller's internal randomization.
The sequence may depend on $\mathcal{A}$, but not on its realized random
choices.
\end{theorem}

The upper bound~\eqref{eq:regret-optimized-bound} is
$2\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha T\log(H+1)}$. For fixed $H,\alpha,L_x,L_u$,
both bounds therefore scale as $\sqrt{T}$. The upper bound contains a
factor $\sqrt{\log(H+1)}$, whereas the lower bound is independent of $H$.
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-lower-proofs} proves the theorem and also gives
a lower bound valid for every $T\ge2$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-lower-finite-horizon}).

\begin{remark}[Large-$\alpha$ regime]
\label{rem:large-alpha-regime}
For fixed $L_x,L_u>0$, as $\alpha\uparrow1$, both $G_\alpha$ and the
right-hand side of \eqref{eq:sdac-lower-horizon} are of order
$(1-\alpha)^{-1}$. Under this horizon condition, the lower bound is
$\Omega((1-\alpha)^{-1}\sqrt{T})$, while the upper bound is
$O((1-\alpha)^{-3/2}\sqrt{T\log(H+1)})$.
Thus, these bounds differ by factors of $\sqrt{\log(H+1)}$ in memory
dependence and $(1-\alpha)^{-1/2}$ in retention dependence.
Subsequent work closes these gaps for the same policy class
\cite{asgari2026cumulative}.

This comparison applies under the stated horizon condition. For fixed $T$,
the right-hand side of the general bound~\eqref{eq:sdac-lower-exact}
remains finite as $\alpha\uparrow1$.
The calculations are given in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-lower-proofs}.
\end{remark}

\paragraph*{Lower-bound construction}
Set $w_t=1$ and compare the fixed SDAC parameters
$\mathbf{m}^{(0)}=\mathbf{0}$ and
$\mathbf{m}^{(1)}=\mathbf{e}_1=(1,0,\ldots,0)$.
The first policy never uses resource; the second uses each arrival one
period later, after storage loss. For $t=2,\ldots,T$, their trajectories are
\[
\begin{aligned}
u_t(\mathbf{m}^{(0)})&=0,&
u_t(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})&=\alpha,\\
x_t(\mathbf{m}^{(0)})&=\frac{1-\alpha^{t-1}}{1-\alpha},&
x_t(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})&=1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Both parameters belong to $\mathcal{M}_H$ for every $H\ge1$, which
explains why the lower bound does not depend on $H$.

Choose costs $c_t(x,u)=\xi_t(L_xx-L_uu)$, where the signs $\xi_t$ are
independent, equally likely to be $1$ or $-1$, and independent of the
controller's randomization. Causality gives every online controller zero
expected cost. Each fixed comparison policy also has zero expected cost,
but choosing the better of the two in hindsight gives an advantage.
Their cumulative cost difference is the weighted sum
$\sum_{t=2}^T\xi_t\beta_t$ in \eqref{eq:lower-cost-difference}, with
$\beta_t$ defined in \eqref{eq:sdac-lower-beta}.
Khintchine's inequality \cite{szarek1976best} bounds the expected absolute
value of this sum from below. Averaging over the signs then gives a
deterministic sequence with the stated expected regret.

\section{Discussion and Extensions}
\label{sec:extensions}

We discuss three extensions. In each case, the feasibility, convexity, and
stability arguments apply with the stated changes to the policy domain and
Lipschitz constants. We omit the resulting regret bounds, whose form is unchanged
up to these constants and, in the vector settings, the chosen product norm.

\paragraph{Retention $\alpha\ge 1$}
Here we extend the model beyond the assumption $\alpha\in(0,1)$.
The case $\alpha=1$ has no storage loss, while $\alpha>1$ represents growth
between decisions. Fix $k$ with $\alpha-1<k<\alpha$, so $k>0$, and define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:extension-retention-transform}
\begin{aligned}
\bar\alpha&\triangleq\alpha-k\in(0,1),\\
v_t&\triangleq u_t-kx_t,\qquad 0\le v_t\le\bar\alpha x_t.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:extension-retention-transform} into
\eqref{eq:model-dynamics} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:extension-transformed-dynamics}
\begin{aligned}
x_{t+1}&=\alpha x_t-(kx_t+v_t)+w_t\\
&=(\alpha-k)x_t-v_t+w_t
=\bar\alpha x_t-v_t+w_t.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Thus, $v_t$ is the control action for a storage system with retention
coefficient $\bar\alpha\in(0,1)$. For $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:extension-fixed-policy}
\begin{aligned}
v_t(\mathbf{m})&=\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\bar\alpha^i w_{t-i},\\
u_t(\mathbf{m})&=kx_t(\mathbf{m})+v_t(\mathbf{m}),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $x_t(\mathbf{m})$ follows \eqref{eq:extension-transformed-dynamics}
with initial condition \eqref{eq:model-init}.
Equation~\eqref{eq:sdac-policy-feasibility}, with $\alpha$ replaced by
$\bar\alpha$, gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:extension-policy-feasibility}
0\le v_t(\mathbf{m})\le\bar\alpha x_t(\mathbf{m}).
\end{equation}
For $\mathbf{m}_t\in\mathcal{M}_H$, the online controller instead selects
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:extension-online-policy}
\begin{aligned}
v_t&=\min\!\left\{\sum_{i=1}^H m_{t,i}\bar\alpha^i w_{t-i},\,
\bar\alpha x_t\right\},\\
u_t&=kx_t+v_t.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Equations \eqref{eq:extension-policy-feasibility} and
\eqref{eq:extension-online-policy} give $0\le v_t\le\bar\alpha x_t$
for the fixed and online policies, respectively.
Using $k>0$ and \eqref{eq:extension-retention-transform},
\[
\begin{aligned}
0\le kx_t\le u_t&=kx_t+v_t\\
&\le(k+\bar\alpha)x_t\\
&=\alpha x_t,
\end{aligned}
\]
so the original feasibility constraint \eqref{eq:model-feasibility} is satisfied.
Equation~\eqref{eq:model-state-bound}, with $\alpha$ replaced by
$\bar\alpha\in(0,1)$, applies to \eqref{eq:extension-transformed-dynamics}
and gives $0\le x_t\le1/(1-\bar\alpha)$.
Assume that the original costs $c_t(x,u)$ are convex and satisfy the
conditions \eqref{eq:model-lipschitz}, \eqref{eq:model-cost-subgradient},
and \eqref{eq:model-subgradient-bounds} on
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:extension-cost-domain}
0\le x\le\frac{1}{1-\bar\alpha},
\qquad
kx\le u\le\alpha x.
\end{equation}
The cost in the variables $(x,v)$ is $c_t(x,kx+v)$, which is convex because
$(x,v)\mapsto(x,kx+v)$ is affine. For two feasible pairs $(x,v)$ and $(x',v')$,
\eqref{eq:model-lipschitz} on \eqref{eq:extension-cost-domain} and the
triangle inequality give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:extension-cost-lipschitz}
\begin{aligned}
&|c_t(x,kx+v)-c_t(x',kx'+v')|\\
&\quad\le L_x|x-x'|+L_u|k(x-x')+v-v'|\\
&\quad\le L_x|x-x'|+kL_u|x-x'|+L_u|v-v'|\\
&\quad=(L_x+kL_u)|x-x'|+L_u|v-v'|.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:extension-cost-subgradient}
(\zeta_t^x,\zeta_t^u)=\boldsymbol{\zeta}_t(x,kx+v).
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:model-cost-subgradient} and
\eqref{eq:extension-cost-subgradient},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:extension-subgradient-inequality}
\begin{aligned}
&c_t(x',kx'+v')-c_t(x,kx+v)\\
&\quad\ge\zeta_t^x(x'-x)+\zeta_t^u\bigl(k(x'-x)+v'-v\bigr)\\
&\quad=(\zeta_t^x+k\zeta_t^u)(x'-x)+\zeta_t^u(v'-v).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Thus, \eqref{eq:extension-subgradient-inequality} shows that
$(\zeta_t^x+k\zeta_t^u,\zeta_t^u)$ is a subgradient of the transformed cost.
The triangle inequality and \eqref{eq:model-subgradient-bounds} give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:extension-subgradient-bounds}
\begin{aligned}
|\zeta_t^x+k\zeta_t^u|
&\le|\zeta_t^x|+k|\zeta_t^u|\le L_x+kL_u,\\
|\zeta_t^u|&\le L_u.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The online controller uses the same OMD update with surrogate cost
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:extension-surrogate-cost}
\mathbf{m}\mapsto
c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{m}),kx_t(\mathbf{m})+v_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr).
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:extension-cost-lipschitz} and
\eqref{eq:extension-subgradient-bounds}, the feasibility and regret
upper-bound analysis applies to \eqref{eq:extension-surrogate-cost} with $\alpha$
replaced by $\bar\alpha$ and $L_x$ replaced by $L_x+kL_u$, while $L_u$ is
unchanged.
Both the online controller and the comparators use the same fixed $k$.
Each comparator uses \eqref{eq:extension-fixed-policy}.
The substitution \eqref{eq:extension-retention-transform} preserves the cost
at every time, so it also preserves
cumulative costs and regret against this class. Selecting $k$, either in
advance or online, is left for future work.

\paragraph{Vector actions and multi-task allocation}
Consider a single storage state $x_t$ with a vector action
$\mathbf{u}_t\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^n$ that allocates resources across $n$ tasks,
subject to $\sum_{a=1}^n u_{t,a}\le\alpha x_t$. The dynamics and initial state are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:extension-vector-dynamics}
x_{t+1}=\alpha x_t-\sum_{a=1}^n u_{t,a}+w_t,\qquad x_1=0,
\end{equation}
and the cost
$c_t(x,\mathbf{u})$ is convex in $(x,\mathbf{u})$. Replace
$\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$ with a matrix
$\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^{n\times H}$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:extension-matrix-policy}
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{a=1}^n\sum_{i=1}^H M_{a,i}&\le1,\\
u_{t,a}(\mathbf{M})&=\sum_{i=1}^H M_{a,i}\alpha^i w_{t-i}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
By nonnegativity and \eqref{eq:extension-matrix-policy},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:extension-matrix-aggregation}
\begin{aligned}
m_i&\triangleq\sum_{a=1}^n M_{a,i}\ge0,\qquad i\in[H],\\
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i
&=\sum_{a=1}^n\sum_{i=1}^H M_{a,i}\le1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Thus, $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$ by \eqref{eq:def-simplex}.
Summing the actions in \eqref{eq:extension-matrix-policy} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:extension-total-action}
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{a=1}^n u_{t,a}(\mathbf{M})
&=\sum_{i=1}^H\left(\sum_{a=1}^n M_{a,i}\right)\alpha^i w_{t-i}\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:extension-matrix-aggregation}}{=}
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\alpha^i w_{t-i}\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy}}{=}u_t(\mathbf{m}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:extension-total-action}, \eqref{eq:extension-vector-dynamics},
\eqref{eq:model-dynamics}, and \eqref{eq:model-init}, the two state
trajectories have the same total action and initial state. Hence,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:extension-matrix-state}
x_t(\mathbf{M})=x_t(\mathbf{m}).
\end{equation}
Therefore,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{a=1}^n u_{t,a}(\mathbf{M})
&\overset{\eqref{eq:extension-total-action}}{=}u_t(\mathbf{m})\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:sdac-policy-feasibility}}{\le}\alpha x_t(\mathbf{m})\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:extension-matrix-state}}{=}\alpha x_t(\mathbf{M}).
\end{aligned}
\]
By \eqref{eq:surr-affine-pair}, \eqref{eq:extension-matrix-aggregation},
and \eqref{eq:extension-matrix-state}, the state is affine in $\mathbf{M}$.
The vector action is affine by \eqref{eq:extension-matrix-policy}, so
$c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{M}),\mathbf{u}_t(\mathbf{M})\bigr)$ is convex in $\mathbf{M}$. For the
online SDAC controller, a nominal vector whose entries sum to more than
$\alpha x_t$ can be scaled proportionally so that its entries remain nonnegative
and sum to $\alpha x_t$. Under an $\ell_1$ Lipschitz bound in the action, the size of this
correction equals the excess total action, and the stability proof proceeds with
the corresponding matrix norm. OMD then runs over the matrix subprobability
simplex in \eqref{eq:extension-matrix-policy}.

\paragraph{Multiple storage states with diagonal dynamics}
Suppose the state is $\mathbf{x}_t\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^d$ and evolves according to
$\mathbf{x}_{t+1}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_t-\mathbf{u}_t+\mathbf{w}_t$, where
$\mathbf{A}=\operatorname{diag}(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_d)$,
$\alpha_s\in(0,1)$ for $s\in[d]$, the arrivals satisfy
$\mathbf{w}_t\in[0,1]^d$, and the per-storage constraints are
$0\le u_{t,s}\le\alpha_s x_{t,s}$. Since $\mathbf{A}$ is diagonal,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:extension-diagonal-dynamics}
x_{t+1,s}=\alpha_s x_{t,s}-u_{t,s}+w_{t,s},\qquad s\in[d].
\end{equation}
In \eqref{eq:extension-diagonal-dynamics}, the next state of each storage
system depends only on its own current state, action, and arrival.
Assign one SDAC parameter in $\mathcal{M}_H$ to each storage state and use
\eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy} with $\alpha_s^i$ in coordinate $s$. The dynamics
and feasibility constraints decouple by storage state, and the SDAC parameter domain
is a product of subprobability simplices. With \eqref{eq:model-init} in each
coordinate, \eqref{eq:model-state-bound}, with $\alpha$ replaced by $\alpha_s$,
gives $x_{t,s}\le1/(1-\alpha_s)$
for every $s\in[d]$. The online SDAC controller clips
each nominal action to $\alpha_s x_{t,s}$. If the cost is jointly convex and
satisfies compatible Lipschitz and bounded-subgradient assumptions on the resulting bounded product
domain, the surrogate cost is jointly convex in all SDAC parameters and OMD applies
on the product set. With a sum of negentropy regularizers, the OMD minimization
separates by storage state, although each subgradient may depend on all storage states
and actions. Cross-coupled storage systems (nondiagonal $\mathbf{A}$) require
further analysis.


\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied adversarial online control of a storage system under a state-dependent
action constraint. We introduced SDAC policies, which are feasible by
construction, and developed an online SDAC controller with
$O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log(H+1)}\right)$ regret against the best fixed SDAC policy.
The SDAC approximation results further give
$O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log\log T}\right)$ regret against the best fixed
infinite-memory SDAC policy for a suitable choice $H=\Theta(\log T)$.
Under the horizon condition \eqref{eq:sdac-lower-horizon}, the
lower bound gives
$\Omega((1-\alpha)^{-1}\sqrt{T})$ regret as $\alpha\uparrow1$.
For the bounds proved here, a factor of $\sqrt{\log(H+1)}$ remains between
the upper and lower bounds
in their dependence on $H$, together with a factor of $(1-\alpha)^{-1/2}$
in their dependence on $1-\alpha$. Subsequent work closes these gaps for
the same policy class \cite{asgari2026cumulative}. The model applies directly to
energy-harvesting battery management and related settings in which storage limits
must be respected in real time.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-state-decomp}]
By \eqref{eq:model-init}, \eqref{eq:def-arrival-sums}, and $w_\tau=0$
for $\tau\le0$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-state-initial}
x_1(\mathbf{m})=r_1(i)=r_1=0,\qquad i\in[H].
\end{equation}
For $1\le t<T$ and $i\in[H]$, shifting the summation index in
\eqref{eq:def-arrival-sums} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-arrival-recursions}
\begin{aligned}
r_{t+1}
&=\sum_{j=1}^{t}\alpha^{j-1}w_{t+1-j}
 =w_t+\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}\alpha^j w_{t-j} \\
&=w_t+\alpha r_t, \\
r_{t+1}(i)
&=w_t+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha^j w_{t-j} \\
&=w_t+\alpha r_t(i)-\alpha^i w_{t-i}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The sums in \eqref{eq:sdac-arrival-recursions} are empty when their upper
limit is zero.

Suppose \eqref{eq:sdac-state-decomp} holds at $1\le t<T$. Substituting it
and \eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy} into \eqref{eq:model-dynamics} gives
\begin{align*}
x_{t+1}(\mathbf{m})
&=\sum_{i=1}^H m_i
\bigl(\alpha r_t(i)-\alpha^i w_{t-i}\bigr) \\
&\quad
+\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\right)\alpha r_t+w_t \\
&=\sum_{i=1}^H m_i
\bigl(\alpha r_t(i)-\alpha^i w_{t-i}+w_t\bigr)\\
&\quad
+\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\right)(\alpha r_t+w_t)\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:sdac-arrival-recursions}}{=}
\sum_{i=1}^H m_i r_{t+1}(i)\\
&\quad
+\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\right)r_{t+1}.
\end{align*}
Together with \eqref{eq:sdac-state-initial}, this proves
\eqref{eq:sdac-state-decomp} by induction.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-surrogate}]
Fix $t\in[T]$. By \eqref{eq:surr-affine-pair} and
\eqref{eq:def-surrogate-cost}, $\ell_t$ is the composition of the convex
function $c_t$ with an affine map, so it is convex.
Equations \eqref{eq:sdac-policy-feasibility} and \eqref{eq:model-state-bound} place
every SDAC-policy state--action pair in $\mathcal{D}_\alpha$.

By \eqref{eq:def-arrival-sums} and \eqref{eq:def-phi-u}, using
$0\le w_\tau\le1$ and $0<\alpha<1$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-coefficient-norms}
\begin{aligned}
\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x\|_\infty
&=\max_{i\in[H]}\bigl(r_t-r_t(i)\bigr)\\
&=\max_{i\in[H]}\sum_{j=i+1}^{t-1}\alpha^{j-1}w_{t-j}\\
&\le\max_{i\in[H]}\sum_{j=i+1}^{\infty}\alpha^{j-1}\\
&=\max_{i\in[H]}\frac{\alpha^i}{1-\alpha}
=\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha},\\
\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u\|_\infty
&=\max_{i\in[H]}\alpha^i w_{t-i}\\
&\le\max_{i\in[H]}\alpha^i=\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The finite sum in \eqref{eq:surr-coefficient-norms} is empty when
$i\ge t-1$, since $w_\tau=0$ for $\tau\le0$.

Fix $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, and let $(\zeta_t^x,\zeta_t^u)$ and
$\mathbf{g}_t$ be defined by \eqref{eq:surr-subgradient}. For every
$\mathbf{m}'\in\mathcal{M}_H$, \eqref{eq:model-cost-subgradient} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-subgradient-inequality}
\begin{aligned}
&\ell_t(\mathbf{m}')-\ell_t(\mathbf{m})\\
&\quad\ge\zeta_t^x\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{m}')-x_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr)\\
&\qquad\quad+\zeta_t^u\bigl(u_t(\mathbf{m}')-u_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr)\\
&\quad\overset{\eqref{eq:surr-affine-pair}}{=}\bigl\langle
\zeta_t^x\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x+\zeta_t^u\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,
\mathbf{m}'-\mathbf{m}\bigr\rangle\\
&\quad\overset{\eqref{eq:surr-subgradient}}{=}
\langle\mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}'-\mathbf{m}\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Thus, \eqref{eq:surr-subgradient-inequality}
proves \eqref{eq:surr-subgradient-membership}.

By the triangle inequality,
\begin{align*}
\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty
&\le
|\zeta_t^x|\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x\|_\infty
+|\zeta_t^u|\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u\|_\infty \\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:model-subgradient-bounds}}{\le}
L_x\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^x\|_\infty
+L_u\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u\|_\infty\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:surr-coefficient-norms}}{\le}
\frac{\alpha L_x}{1-\alpha}+\alpha L_u \\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:def-surrogate-G}}{=}G_\alpha.
\end{align*}
This proves \eqref{eq:surr-subgradient-bound}.

By \eqref{eq:surr-subgradient-inequality}, H\"older's inequality, and
\eqref{eq:surr-subgradient-bound},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surr-one-sided-cost-bound}
\begin{aligned}
\ell_t(\mathbf{m})-\ell_t(\mathbf{m}')
&\le\langle\mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\rangle\\
&\le\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\|_1\\
&\le G_\alpha\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}'\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Applying \eqref{eq:surr-one-sided-cost-bound} in both orders of
$\mathbf{m},\mathbf{m}'$ proves \eqref{eq:surr-lipschitz}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-truncation-approx}]
By \eqref{eq:def-sdac-truncation} and \eqref{eq:def-infinite-simplex},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-truncation-membership}
\begin{aligned}
m_i&\ge0,\qquad i\in[H],\\
\|\mathbf{m}_H(\mathbf{m})\|_1
&=\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\le\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}m_i\le1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Hence, $\mathbf{m}_H(\mathbf{m})\in\mathcal{M}_H$ by
\eqref{eq:def-simplex}.

Take $H=T$ in \eqref{eq:sdac-truncation-membership}. Since $w_\tau=0$
for $\tau\le0$, \eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy} and
\eqref{eq:def-infinite-sdac-policy} give, for $t\in[T]$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-infinite-truncation-match}
\begin{aligned}
u_t\bigl(\mathbf{m}_T(\mathbf{m})\bigr)
&=\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}m_i\alpha^i w_{t-i}\\
&=u_t(\mathbf{m}),\\
x_t\bigl(\mathbf{m}_T(\mathbf{m})\bigr)&=x_t(\mathbf{m}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The state equality in \eqref{eq:sdac-infinite-truncation-match} follows by
induction using \eqref{eq:model-init} and \eqref{eq:model-dynamics}.
By \eqref{eq:sdac-infinite-truncation-match} and
\eqref{eq:sdac-policy-feasibility}, the infinite-memory policy is feasible.

For any $H\ge1$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-truncation-errors}
\begin{aligned}
e_t^u&\triangleq
u_t(\mathbf{m})-u_t\bigl(\mathbf{m}_H(\mathbf{m})\bigr),\\
e_t^x&\triangleq
x_t\bigl(\mathbf{m}_H(\mathbf{m})\bigr)-x_t(\mathbf{m}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy} and \eqref{eq:def-infinite-sdac-policy},
using $0\le w_\tau\le1$ and $\alpha^i\le\alpha^{H+1}$ for $i>H$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-truncation-action-error}
\begin{aligned}
0\le e_t^u
&=\sum_{i=H+1}^{t-1}\alpha^i m_i w_{t-i}\\
&\le\sum_{i=H+1}^{t-1}\alpha^i m_i\\
&\le\alpha^{H+1}\sum_{i=H+1}^{t-1}m_i\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:def-infinite-simplex}}{\le}\alpha^{H+1}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The sums in \eqref{eq:sdac-truncation-action-error} are empty when $t\le H+1$.

Subtracting \eqref{eq:model-dynamics} for the two policies and using
\eqref{eq:model-init} and \eqref{eq:sdac-truncation-errors} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-truncation-error-recursion}
e_1^x=0,\qquad e_{t+1}^x=\alpha e_t^x+e_t^u.
\end{equation}
Iterating \eqref{eq:sdac-truncation-error-recursion}, with $0<\alpha<1$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-truncation-state-error}
\begin{aligned}
0\le e_t^x
&=\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\alpha^{t-1-s}e_s^u\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:sdac-truncation-action-error}}{\le}
\alpha^{H+1}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\alpha^{t-1-s}\\
&=\alpha^{H+1}\sum_{j=0}^{t-2}\alpha^j\\
&\le\alpha^{H+1}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\alpha^j
=\frac{\alpha^{H+1}}{1-\alpha}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The finite sums in \eqref{eq:sdac-truncation-state-error} are empty at $t=1$.

Feasibility and \eqref{eq:model-state-bound} place both state--action pairs
in $\mathcal{D}_\alpha$. By \eqref{eq:model-lipschitz} and the nonnegative
errors in \eqref{eq:sdac-truncation-action-error} and
\eqref{eq:sdac-truncation-state-error},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-truncation-step-cost}
\begin{aligned}
&\Bigl|c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{m}_H(\mathbf{m})),
u_t(\mathbf{m}_H(\mathbf{m}))\bigr)\\
&\qquad-c_t\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{m}),u_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr)\Bigr|\\
&\quad\le L_xe_t^x+L_ue_t^u\\
&\quad\le\frac{L_x\alpha^{H+1}}{1-\alpha}+L_u\alpha^{H+1}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Summing \eqref{eq:sdac-truncation-step-cost} and applying the triangle
inequality to \eqref{eq:def-sdac-cumulative-cost} proves
\eqref{eq:sdac-truncation-cost-gap}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:fraction-as-sdac}]
Since $0\le k/\alpha\le1$, \eqref{eq:def-fraction-parameter} gives
$m_i(k)\ge0$ for every $i\ge1$ and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fraction-parameter-mass}
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i=1}^{H}m_i(k)
&=\sum_{i=1}^{H}\left[
\left(1-\frac{k}{\alpha}\right)^{i-1}
-\left(1-\frac{k}{\alpha}\right)^i\right]\\
&=1-\left(1-\frac{k}{\alpha}\right)^H\le1,\\
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}m_i(k)
&=\lim_{H\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{H}m_i(k)\le1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Thus, \eqref{eq:fraction-parameter-mass} and
\eqref{eq:def-infinite-simplex} give $\mathbf{m}(k)\in\mathcal{M}_\infty$.

Iterating the fixed-fraction recursion \eqref{eq:def-fraction-trajectory}
gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fraction-state-sum}
x_t(k)=\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}(\alpha-k)^{i-1}w_{t-i}.
\end{equation}
For every $i\ge1$, \eqref{eq:def-fraction-parameter} and $\alpha>0$ give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fraction-weight-identity}
\begin{aligned}
\alpha^i m_i(k)
&=\alpha^i\frac{k}{\alpha}
\left(\frac{\alpha-k}{\alpha}\right)^{i-1}\\
&=k(\alpha-k)^{i-1}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:def-fraction-trajectory}, \eqref{eq:fraction-state-sum},
and \eqref{eq:fraction-weight-identity},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fraction-action-match}
\begin{aligned}
u_t(k)&=kx_t(k)\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}k(\alpha-k)^{i-1}w_{t-i}\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\alpha^i m_i(k)w_{t-i}\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:def-infinite-sdac-policy}}{=}
u_t\bigl(\mathbf{m}(k)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Induction using \eqref{eq:model-init}, \eqref{eq:model-dynamics}, and
\eqref{eq:fraction-action-match} proves \eqref{eq:fraction-sdac-trajectory}.
\end{proof}

\section{Proofs for the Online SDAC Controller}
\label{sec:appendix-algorithm-proofs}

The OMD results for unnormalized negentropy and its Bregman divergence on the
probability simplex are standard \cite{hazan2016introduction}. We prove the
corresponding forms for the subprobability simplex $\mathcal{M}_H$.

\begin{lemma}[Strong convexity of negentropy on the subprobability simplex]
\label{lem:subprob-negentropy-strong-convexity}
For every $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$ and
$\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{M}_H\cap\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:subprob-negentropy-strong-convexity}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{v})
\ge
\frac12\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{v}\|_1^2.
\end{equation}
Thus, $\Psi$ is $1$-strongly convex with respect to $\|\cdot\|_1$ on
$\mathcal{M}_H$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First suppose $\mathbf{m}>0$ coordinatewise. For $s\in[0,1]$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:negentropy-segment}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{d}&\triangleq\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{v},\\
\mathbf{z}_s&\triangleq\mathbf{v}+s\mathbf{d}\\
&=(1-s)\mathbf{v}+s\mathbf{m}
\in\mathcal{M}_H\cap\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:def-negentropy},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:negentropy-hessian}
\nabla^2\Psi(\mathbf{z}_s)
=\operatorname{diag}(1/z_{s,1},\ldots,1/z_{s,H}).
\end{equation}
Cauchy--Schwarz and \eqref{eq:def-simplex} give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:negentropy-hessian-bound}
\begin{aligned}
\|\mathbf{d}\|_1^2
&=\left(\sum_{i=1}^H
\frac{|d_i|}{\sqrt{z_{s,i}}}\sqrt{z_{s,i}}\right)^2 \\
&\le
\left(\sum_{i=1}^H\frac{d_i^2}{z_{s,i}}\right)
\left(\sum_{i=1}^H z_{s,i}\right) \\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:def-simplex}}{\le}
\sum_{i=1}^H\frac{d_i^2}{z_{s,i}}\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:negentropy-hessian}}{=}
\mathbf{d}^\top\nabla^2\Psi(\mathbf{z}_s)\mathbf{d}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
By Taylor's formula along \eqref{eq:negentropy-segment} and
\eqref{eq:def-bregman},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:negentropy-interior-bound}
\begin{aligned}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{v})
&=\int_0^1(1-s)
\mathbf{d}^\top\nabla^2\Psi(\mathbf{z}_s)\mathbf{d}\,ds \\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:negentropy-hessian-bound}}{\ge}
\|\mathbf{d}\|_1^2\int_0^1(1-s)\,ds
\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:negentropy-segment}}{=}
\frac12\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{v}\|_1^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

For any $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$ and $0<\varepsilon<1$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:negentropy-approximation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{m}^{\varepsilon}
&\triangleq(1-\varepsilon)\mathbf{m}+\varepsilon\mathbf{v}\\
&\in\mathcal{M}_H\cap\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:def-bregman} and $0\log0=0$, the divergence is continuous
in its first argument. Therefore,
\begin{align*}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{v})
&=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}^{\varepsilon}\|\mathbf{v})\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:negentropy-interior-bound}}{\ge}
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
\frac12\|\mathbf{m}^{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{v}\|_1^2\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:negentropy-approximation}}{=}
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
\frac12(1-\varepsilon)^2\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{v}\|_1^2\\
&=\frac12\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{v}\|_1^2.\qedhere
\end{align*}
\end{proof}


\begin{lemma}[Action clipping as a Bregman projection]
\label{lem:availability-projection}
For $t\in[T]$, define the set of parameters compatible with the current state by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:availability-set}
\mathcal{B}_t
\triangleq
\left\{
\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H:
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}\rangle
\le \alpha x_t
\right\}.
\end{equation}
Let $\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}$ be the Bregman projection of
$\mathbf{m}_t$ onto $\mathcal{B}_t$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:availability-projection}
\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}
\in
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{B}_t}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_t).
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:availability-clipped-action}
\left\langle
\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}
\right\rangle
=
\min\!\left\{
\left\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\right\rangle,
\alpha x_t
\right\}
=u_t.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:alg-init}, \eqref{eq:alg-omd-mult}, and
\eqref{eq:alg-omd-proj}, every coordinate of $\mathbf{m}_t$ is positive.
By \eqref{eq:model-init}, \eqref{eq:model-dynamics}, and
\eqref{eq:alg-feasible-action}, induction gives $x_t\ge0$.
Thus, $\mathbf{0}\in\mathcal{B}_t$ by \eqref{eq:availability-set}.
The set $\mathcal{B}_t$ is compact and convex, and
$D_\Psi(\cdot\|\mathbf{m}_t)$ is continuous and convex by
\eqref{eq:def-bregman}. Hence, \eqref{eq:availability-projection} has a
minimizer. By \eqref{eq:subprob-negentropy-strong-convexity} and
\eqref{eq:def-bregman}, for every $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:availability-divergence-bound}
\begin{aligned}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_t)
&\ge\frac12\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1^2\ge0,\\
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t\|\mathbf{m}_t)&=0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
If $\mathbf{m}_t\in\mathcal{B}_t$, then
\eqref{eq:availability-projection} and
\eqref{eq:availability-divergence-bound} give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:availability-unclipped-parameter}
\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}=\mathbf{m}_t.
\end{equation}

Otherwise, suppose for contradiction that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:availability-slack}
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\rangle
<\alpha x_t
<\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\rangle.
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:availability-slack},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:availability-delta}
\delta\triangleq
\frac{\alpha x_t-
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\rangle}
{\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\rangle-
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\rangle}
\in(0,1).
\end{equation}
Convexity of $\mathcal{M}_H$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:availability-interpolation}
\mathbf{m}_\delta
\triangleq
(1-\delta)\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}+\delta\mathbf{m}_t
\in\mathcal{M}_H.
\end{equation}
Using \eqref{eq:availability-interpolation} and
\eqref{eq:availability-delta},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:availability-interpolated-action}
\begin{aligned}
&\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_\delta\rangle \\
&=
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\rangle
\\
&\quad+\delta\left(
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t\rangle-
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\rangle
\right)\\
&=\alpha x_t.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Thus, \eqref{eq:availability-set} and
\eqref{eq:availability-interpolated-action} give
$\mathbf{m}_\delta\in\mathcal{B}_t$.
By \eqref{eq:availability-slack} and
\eqref{eq:availability-divergence-bound},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:availability-positive-divergence}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\|\mathbf{m}_t)
\ge\frac12\|\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1^2>0.
\end{equation}
Convexity in the first argument and
\eqref{eq:availability-interpolation} give
\begin{align*}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_\delta\|\mathbf{m}_t)
&\le
(1-\delta)D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\|\mathbf{m}_t)
\\
&\quad+\delta D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t\|\mathbf{m}_t) \\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:availability-divergence-bound}}{=}
(1-\delta)D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\|\mathbf{m}_t)
\\
&\overset{\substack{\eqref{eq:availability-delta}\\
\eqref{eq:availability-positive-divergence}}}{<}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\|\mathbf{m}_t),
\end{align*}
contrary to \eqref{eq:availability-projection}. Hence, when
$\mathbf{m}_t\notin\mathcal{B}_t$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:availability-active-constraint}
\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_t^u,\mathbf{m}_t^{\mathrm{exec}}\rangle
=\alpha x_t.
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{eq:availability-unclipped-parameter} and
\eqref{eq:availability-active-constraint} with \eqref{eq:alg-feasible-action}
proves \eqref{eq:availability-clipped-action}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Closed-form solution of the OMD update]
\label{lem:omd-closed-form}
Fix $\mathbf{m}_t\in\mathcal{M}_H\cap\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H$, $\eta>0$, and
$\mathbf{g}_t\in\mathbb{R}^H$. Define $\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}$ by
\eqref{eq:alg-omd-mult}. The unique solution of
\eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin} is given by \eqref{eq:alg-omd-proj} and satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-interior-update}
\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\in\mathcal{M}_H\cap\mathbb{R}_{>0}^H.
\end{equation}
Thus, the iterates initialized by \eqref{eq:alg-init} remain strictly positive.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:alg-omd-mult}, $\widetilde{m}_{t+1,i}>0$ for every $i\in[H]$.
For $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, \eqref{eq:def-bregman} and
\eqref{eq:alg-omd-mult} give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-objective-reduction}
\begin{aligned}
&\eta\langle\mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}\rangle+D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_t) \\
&=\sum_{i=1}^H\left(
m_i\log\frac{m_i}{\widetilde{m}_{t+1,i}}-m_i+m_{t,i}
\right) \\
&=D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1})
+\sum_{i=1}^H(m_{t,i}-\widetilde{m}_{t+1,i}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Terms with $m_i=0$ use $0\log0=0$ throughout.
The term $\sum_{i=1}^H(m_{t,i}-\widetilde{m}_{t+1,i})$ in
\eqref{eq:omd-objective-reduction} is independent of $\mathbf{m}$.

Define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-candidate}
\mathbf{m}^\star
\triangleq
\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}}
{\max\{1,\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}\|_1\}}.
\end{equation}
Since $\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}>0$ coordinatewise,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-candidate-domain}
\begin{aligned}
m_i^\star&>0,\qquad i\in[H],\\
\|\mathbf{m}^\star\|_1
&=\frac{\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}\|_1}
{\max\{1,\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}\|_1\}}
\le1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For $i\in[H]$, \eqref{eq:omd-candidate} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-candidate-log-ratio}
\log\frac{m_i^\star}{\widetilde{m}_{t+1,i}}
=-\log\max\{1,\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}\|_1\}.
\end{equation}
If $\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}\|_1\le1$, then
$\log\max\{1,\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}\|_1\}=0$; otherwise,
\eqref{eq:omd-candidate-domain} gives $\|\mathbf{m}^\star\|_1=1$. Thus,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-complementarity}
\bigl(1-\|\mathbf{m}^\star\|_1\bigr)
\log\max\{1,\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}\|_1\}=0.
\end{equation}

For $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, expand \eqref{eq:def-bregman}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-projection-gap}
\begin{aligned}
&D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1})
-D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}^\star\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}) \\
&=D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}^\star)
\\
&\quad+\sum_{i=1}^H(m_i-m_i^\star)
 \log\frac{m_i^\star}{\widetilde{m}_{t+1,i}}\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:omd-candidate-log-ratio}}{=}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}^\star)
\\
&\quad+\bigl(\|\mathbf{m}^\star\|_1-\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\bigr)
\log\max\{1,\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}\|_1\}\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:omd-complementarity}}{=}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}^\star)
\\
&\quad+\bigl(1-\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\bigr)
\log\max\{1,\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}\|_1\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
In \eqref{eq:omd-projection-gap}, $1-\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\ge0$ by
\eqref{eq:def-simplex}, and $\log\max\{1,\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1}\|_1\}\ge0$.
Using \eqref{eq:omd-candidate-domain},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-projection-gap-bound}
\begin{aligned}
&D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1})
-D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}^\star\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t+1})\\
&\quad\overset{\eqref{eq:omd-projection-gap}}{\ge}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}^\star)\\
&\quad\overset{\eqref{eq:subprob-negentropy-strong-convexity}}{\ge}
\frac12\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}^\star\|_1^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Equations \eqref{eq:omd-objective-reduction} and
\eqref{eq:omd-projection-gap-bound} give the unique minimizer
$\mathbf{m}_{t+1}=\mathbf{m}^\star$ in \eqref{eq:alg-omd-proj}.
Equation \eqref{eq:omd-candidate-domain} proves \eqref{eq:omd-interior-update};
induction from \eqref{eq:alg-init} gives positivity of every iterate.
\end{proof}

\section{Proofs for the Regret Upper Bounds}
\label{sec:appendix-regret-proofs}

\begin{lemma}[One-step OMD movement bound]
\label{lem:omd-move}
The OMD update \eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin} with step size $\eta>0$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-movement-bound}
\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1
\le \eta\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty
\le \eta G_\alpha.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:alg-init} and \eqref{eq:omd-interior-update}, both iterates
are positive. First-order optimality in \eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin}, using
\eqref{eq:def-bregman}, gives, for every $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-first-order-condition}
\begin{aligned}
&\eta\langle\mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}\rangle\\
&\quad\le\sum_{i=1}^H(m_i-m_{t+1,i})
\log\frac{m_{t+1,i}}{m_{t,i}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Apply \eqref{eq:subprob-negentropy-strong-convexity} in both directions,
then \eqref{eq:omd-first-order-condition} with $\mathbf{m}=\mathbf{m}_t$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-movement-square}
\begin{aligned}
&\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1^2 \\
&\quad\le
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\|\mathbf{m}_t)+D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_t\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}) \\
&\quad\overset{\eqref{eq:def-bregman}}{=}
\sum_{i=1}^H(m_{t+1,i}-m_{t,i})
\log\frac{m_{t+1,i}}{m_{t,i}}\\
&\quad\overset{\eqref{eq:omd-first-order-condition}}{\le}
\eta\langle\mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\rangle \\
&\quad\overset{\text{H\"older}}{\le}
\eta\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
If $\mathbf{m}_{t+1}=\mathbf{m}_t$, \eqref{eq:omd-movement-bound} holds
with zero left-hand side. Otherwise, divide \eqref{eq:omd-movement-square}
by $\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1$ and apply
\eqref{eq:surr-subgradient-bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[OMD regret for the surrogate costs]
\label{lem:omd-surrogate-regret}
For every $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$, the update \eqref{eq:alg-omd-argmin} satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-surrogate-regret}
\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)
-\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
\le
\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_1)}{\eta}
+
\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{t=1}^T\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty^2.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:surr-subgradient-membership},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-convexity-step}
\begin{aligned}
\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
&\le
\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}\rangle \\
&=
\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\rangle \\
&\quad+
\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:omd-first-order-condition} and \eqref{eq:def-bregman},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-three-point-step}
\begin{aligned}
&\eta\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}\rangle \\
&\quad\le\sum_{i=1}^H(m_i-m_{t+1,i})
\log\frac{m_{t+1,i}}{m_{t,i}}\\
&\quad=
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_t)
-D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}) \\
&\qquad\quad
-D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\|\mathbf{m}_t).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Equation \eqref{eq:omd-three-point-step} also holds when $m_i=0$, with
$0\log0=0$.
Substituting \eqref{eq:omd-three-point-step} into
\eqref{eq:omd-convexity-step} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-one-step-regret}
\begin{aligned}
\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-\ell_t(\mathbf{m})
&\le
\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_t)}{\eta}
-\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1})}{\eta} \\
&\quad+
\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\rangle
\\
&\quad
-\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\|\mathbf{m}_t)}{\eta}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Since $\eta>0$, H\"older's inequality and
\eqref{eq:subprob-negentropy-strong-convexity} give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-young-step}
\begin{aligned}
&\langle \mathbf{g}_t,\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\rangle
-\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\|\mathbf{m}_t)}{\eta} \\
&\qquad\overset{\text{H\"older}}{\le}
\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1\\
&\qquad\quad-\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\|\mathbf{m}_t)}{\eta}\\
&\qquad\overset{\eqref{eq:subprob-negentropy-strong-convexity}}{\le}
\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1\\
&\qquad\quad-\frac{\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1^2}{2\eta} \\
&\qquad=\frac{\eta}{2}\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty^2\\
&\qquad\quad-\frac{\bigl(\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1
-\eta\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty\bigr)^2}{2\eta}\\
&\qquad\overset{\eta>0}{\le}
\frac{\eta}{2}\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:omd-young-step} into
\eqref{eq:omd-one-step-regret} and summing over $t$ gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{t=1}^T\bigl(\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-\ell_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr)\\
&\quad\le\frac{1}{\eta}\sum_{t=1}^T
\bigl(D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_t)
-D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1})\bigr)\\
&\qquad\quad+
\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{t=1}^T\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty^2\\
&\quad=\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_1)}{\eta}
-\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_{T+1})}{\eta}\\
&\qquad\quad+
\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{t=1}^T\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty^2\\
&\quad\overset{\eqref{eq:subprob-negentropy-strong-convexity}}{\le}
\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_1)}{\eta}+
\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{t=1}^T\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:omd-surrogate-regret}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:contractive-deviation}]
Set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-state-deviation}
d_t\triangleq|x_t-x_t(\mathbf{m}_t)|,\qquad
d_1\overset{\eqref{eq:model-init}}{=}0.
\end{equation}
For $z,z'\in\mathbb{R}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:positive-part-bounds}
|[z]_+-[z']_+|\le|z-z'|,\qquad [z]_+\le|z|.
\end{equation}
For $1\le t<T$, \eqref{eq:surr-affine-pair}, \eqref{eq:model-dynamics},
\eqref{eq:alg-feasible-action}, and \eqref{eq:sdac-policy-feasibility}
give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:deviation-positive-part-dynamics}
\begin{aligned}
x_{t+1}
&=[\alpha x_t-u_t(\mathbf{m}_t)]_++w_t,\\
x_{t+1}(\mathbf{m}_t)
&=[\alpha x_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-u_t(\mathbf{m}_t)]_++w_t.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Equations \eqref{eq:deviation-positive-part-dynamics} and
\eqref{eq:positive-part-bounds} give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:deviation-fixed-parameter}
\begin{aligned}
&|x_{t+1}-x_{t+1}(\mathbf{m}_t)|\\
&\quad=\bigl|[\alpha x_t-u_t(\mathbf{m}_t)]_+\\
&\qquad\quad-[\alpha x_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-u_t(\mathbf{m}_t)]_+\bigr|\\
&\quad\overset{\eqref{eq:positive-part-bounds}}{\le}
|\alpha x_t-\alpha x_t(\mathbf{m}_t)|
\overset{\eqref{eq:def-state-deviation}}{=}\alpha d_t.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The triangle inequality and \eqref{eq:def-state-deviation} give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:deviation-recursion}
\begin{aligned}
d_{t+1}
&\le|x_{t+1}-x_{t+1}(\mathbf{m}_t)|\\
&\quad+|x_{t+1}(\mathbf{m}_t)-x_{t+1}(\mathbf{m}_{t+1})|\\
&\overset{\substack{\eqref{eq:deviation-fixed-parameter}\\
\eqref{eq:surr-affine-pair}}}{\le}\alpha d_t+
|\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}_{t+1}^x,
\mathbf{m}_t-\mathbf{m}_{t+1}\rangle|\\
&\overset{\text{H\"older}}{\le}\alpha d_t+
\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_{t+1}^x\|_\infty
\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:surr-coefficient-norms}}{\le}\alpha d_t+
\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:def-state-deviation}, $d_1=0$ and $d_T\ge0$. Summing
\eqref{eq:deviation-recursion} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:deviation-summed-recursion}
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{t=1}^T d_t
&=\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}d_{t+1}\\
&\le\alpha\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}d_t+
\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}
\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1\\
&\overset{d_T\ge0}{\le}\alpha\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_t+
\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}
\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Rearranging \eqref{eq:deviation-summed-recursion}, since $1-\alpha>0$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:deviation-sum}
\sum_{t=1}^T d_t
\le\frac{\alpha}{(1-\alpha)^2}
\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1.
\end{equation}
For $T=1$, \eqref{eq:def-state-deviation} makes both sides of
\eqref{eq:deviation-sum} zero.

For $t\in[T]$, use \eqref{eq:surr-affine-pair},
\eqref{eq:alg-feasible-action}, monotonicity of $[\cdot]_+$, and
\eqref{eq:positive-part-bounds}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:online-clipping-gap}
\begin{aligned}
0\le u_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-u_t
&=[u_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-\alpha x_t]_+\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:sdac-policy-feasibility}}{\le}
[\alpha x_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-\alpha x_t]_+\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:positive-part-bounds}}{\le}
\alpha|x_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-x_t|\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:def-state-deviation}}{=}\alpha d_t.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Both state--action pairs are feasible, so \eqref{eq:model-state-bound}
places them in $\mathcal{D}_\alpha$. By \eqref{eq:def-surrogate-cost} and
\eqref{eq:model-lipschitz},
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{t=1}^T\bigl(c_t(x_t,u_t)-\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)\bigr)\\
&\quad\le
\sum_{t=1}^T\bigl(L_xd_t+L_u|u_t-u_t(\mathbf{m}_t)|\bigr)\\
&\quad\overset{\eqref{eq:online-clipping-gap}}{\le}
(L_x+\alpha L_u)\sum_{t=1}^T d_t\\
&\quad\overset{\eqref{eq:deviation-sum}}{\le}
\frac{\alpha(L_x+\alpha L_u)}{(1-\alpha)^2}
\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\|\mathbf{m}_{t+1}-\mathbf{m}_t\|_1\\
&\quad\overset{\eqref{eq:omd-movement-bound}}{\le}
\frac{\alpha\eta(T-1)G_\alpha(L_x+\alpha L_u)}{(1-\alpha)^2}\\
&\quad\overset{T-1\le T}{\le}
\frac{\alpha\eta T G_\alpha(L_x+\alpha L_u)}{(1-\alpha)^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:stability-sum-bound}.
\end{proof}


\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-bound}]
Fix $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$. Since $H\ge1$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-initial-log-bound}
\log(H+1)
=\int_1^{H+1}\frac{dz}{z}
\ge\int_1^{H+1}\frac{dz}{H+1}
=\frac{H}{H+1}.
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:alg-init} and \eqref{eq:def-bregman}, with $0\log0=0$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-initial-divergence}
\begin{aligned}
D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_1)
&=\sum_{i=1}^H m_i\log m_i \\
&\quad+\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\log(H+1)-\|\mathbf{m}\|_1
+\frac{H}{H+1}\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:def-simplex}}{\le}
\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\log(H+1)\\
&\quad+(1-\|\mathbf{m}\|_1)\frac{H}{H+1}
-\frac{\|\mathbf{m}\|_1}{H+1}\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:omd-initial-log-bound}}{\le}
\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\log(H+1)\\
&\quad+(1-\|\mathbf{m}\|_1)\log(H+1)\\
&=\log(H+1).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The inequalities in \eqref{eq:omd-initial-divergence} use
$m_i\in[0,1]$ and $\|\mathbf{m}\|_1\in[0,1]$ from \eqref{eq:def-simplex}.

By \eqref{eq:omd-surrogate-regret}, \eqref{eq:omd-initial-divergence}, and
\eqref{eq:surr-subgradient-bound},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omd-uniform-surrogate-regret}
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{t=1}^T\bigl(\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-\ell_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr)\\
&\quad\le\frac{D_\Psi(\mathbf{m}\|\mathbf{m}_1)}{\eta}
+\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{t=1}^T\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_\infty^2\\
&\quad\le\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta}+\frac{\eta T G_\alpha^2}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Using \eqref{eq:def-cumulative-cost}, \eqref{eq:def-sdac-cumulative-cost},
and \eqref{eq:def-surrogate-cost},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:regret-fixed-comparator}
\begin{aligned}
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}-J_T(\mathbf{m})
&=\sum_{t=1}^T\bigl(c_t(x_t,u_t)-\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)\bigr) \\
&\quad+\sum_{t=1}^T
\bigl(\ell_t(\mathbf{m}_t)-\ell_t(\mathbf{m})\bigr) \\
&\overset{\substack{\eqref{eq:stability-sum-bound}\\
\eqref{eq:omd-uniform-surrogate-regret}}}{\le}
\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta}
+\frac{\eta T G_\alpha^2}{2} \\
&\quad+\frac{\alpha\eta T G_\alpha(L_x+\alpha L_u)}
{(1-\alpha)^2} \\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:def-Gamma-alpha}}{=}
\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta}+\eta T\Gamma_\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Taking the maximum over $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_H$ in
\eqref{eq:regret-fixed-comparator} and using \eqref{eq:def-regret} proves
\eqref{eq:regret-theorem-bound}.
Since $H,T\ge1$ and $\Gamma_\alpha>0$, \eqref{eq:def-optimal-step-size} gives
$\eta^*>0$ and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:regret-balanced-terms}
\frac{\log(H+1)}{\eta^*}
=\eta^*T\Gamma_\alpha
=\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha T\log(H+1)}.
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:regret-balanced-terms} into
\eqref{eq:regret-theorem-bound} proves \eqref{eq:regret-optimized-bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:regret-vs-infinite-sdac}]
Fix $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_\infty$. By
\eqref{eq:sdac-truncation-membership} and \eqref{eq:def-simplex},
$\mathbf{m}_{H_T}(\mathbf{m})\in\mathcal{M}_{H_T}$.
Hence,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:infinite-comparator-bound}
\begin{aligned}
J_T^{\mathrm{on}}-J_T(\mathbf{m})
&=J_T^{\mathrm{on}}-J_T\bigl(\mathbf{m}_{H_T}(\mathbf{m})\bigr)\\
&\quad+J_T\bigl(\mathbf{m}_{H_T}(\mathbf{m})\bigr)-J_T(\mathbf{m})\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:def-regret}}{\le}\RegSDAC{T}(H_T)\\
&\quad+J_T\bigl(\mathbf{m}_{H_T}(\mathbf{m})\bigr)-J_T(\mathbf{m})\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:sdac-truncation-cost-gap}}{\le}\RegSDAC{T}(H_T)\\
&\quad+\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)\alpha^{H_T+1}T.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Taking the supremum over $\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_\infty$ in
\eqref{eq:infinite-comparator-bound}, then using
\eqref{eq:def-infinite-regret} and \eqref{eq:regret-optimized-bound} with
$H=H_T$, proves
\eqref{eq:infinite-sdac-regret-bound}.

Since $T\ge1$ and $0<\alpha<1$, \eqref{eq:def-memory-horizon} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:memory-horizon-rounding}
\frac{\log T}{\log(1/\alpha)}
\le H_T
\le 1+\frac{\log T}{\log(1/\alpha)}.
\end{equation}
In particular, $H_T=1$ when $T=1$. Since $0<\alpha<1$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:memory-truncation-tail}
\begin{aligned}
\alpha^{H_T+1}T
&\overset{\eqref{eq:memory-horizon-rounding}}{\le}
\alpha^{1+\log T/\log(1/\alpha)}T\\
&=\alpha\exp\!\left(
\frac{\log\alpha}{\log(1/\alpha)}\log T\right)T \\
&=\alpha e^{-\log T}T=\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:memory-horizon-rounding} and monotonicity of $\log$, for fixed
$\alpha$ as $T\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:memory-log-growth}
\begin{aligned}
\log(H_T+1)
&\le\log\!\left(2+\frac{\log T}{\log(1/\alpha)}\right)\\
&=O(\log\log T).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For fixed $\alpha,L_x,L_u$, \eqref{eq:memory-truncation-tail} and
\eqref{eq:memory-log-growth} bound the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:infinite-sdac-regret-bound} by
$O\!\left(\sqrt{T\log\log T}\right)=o(T)$.

For every $k\in[0,\alpha]$, Corollary~\ref{cor:fraction-as-sdac} gives
$\mathbf{m}(k)\in\mathcal{M}_\infty$.
Using \eqref{eq:fraction-sdac-trajectory} and
\eqref{eq:def-sdac-cumulative-cost},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fraction-comparator-cost}
\begin{aligned}
&\inf_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_\infty}J_T(\mathbf{m})\\
&\quad\le\min_{k\in[0,\alpha]}J_T(\mathbf{m}(k))\\
&\quad=\min_{k\in[0,\alpha]}\sum_{t=1}^T c_t\bigl(x_t(k),u_t(k)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Subtracting \eqref{eq:fraction-comparator-cost} from $J_T^{\mathrm{on}}$
and using \eqref{eq:def-infinite-regret} proves
\eqref{eq:fraction-regret-comparison}.
\end{proof}

\section{Proofs for the Regret Lower Bound}
\label{sec:appendix-lower-proofs}

\begin{lemma}[Lower bound for every $T\ge2$]
\label{lem:sdac-lower-finite-horizon}
Fix $\alpha\in(0,1)$, $L_x,L_u>0$, and integers $H\ge1$ and $T\ge2$.
For every causal feasible online controller
$\mathcal{A}$, possibly randomized, there is a deterministic oblivious sequence
of arrivals and costs satisfying the assumptions of
Section~\ref{sec:model-setup} such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-lower-exact}
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\!\left[\RegSDAC{T}(H)\right]
\ge
\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}
\sqrt{\sum_{t=2}^T \beta_t^2},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sdac-lower-beta}
\beta_t
\triangleq
\alpha\left(
L_u+\frac{L_x(1-\alpha^{t-2})}{1-\alpha}
\right),
\qquad t=2,\ldots,T.
\end{equation}
The expectation is over the internal randomization of $\mathcal{A}$.
The sequence may depend on $\mathcal{A}$, but not on its realized random choices.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-lower-finite-horizon}]
We use the random-sign argument of Hazan, Agarwal, and Kale
\cite[Sec.~2.3, footnote~3]{hazan2007logarithmic}; see also
\cite[Sec.~3.2, Thm.~3.2]{hazan2016introduction}.
Kumar, Dean, and Kleinberg \cite[App.~D, Thm.~D.2]{kumar2023unbounded}
use a related construction with signs assigned to blocks for discounted
memory. Here, each time has an independent sign, and the SDAC policies
are evaluated on their own feasible storage trajectories.

Fix a causal feasible controller $\mathcal{A}$. Let
$\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_T$ be independent Rademacher random variables, independent
of the controller's randomization. Set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-random-instance}
w_t=1,\qquad c_t(x,u)=\xi_t(L_xx-L_uu),\qquad t\in[T].
\end{equation}
Each cost is affine with gradient $\xi_t(L_x,-L_u)$, so it is convex and
satisfies \eqref{eq:model-subgradient-bounds} because $|\xi_t|=1$.
The triangle inequality verifies \eqref{eq:model-lipschitz}:
\[
\begin{aligned}
|c_t(x,u)-c_t(x',u')|
&\overset{\eqref{eq:lower-random-instance}}{=}
|L_x(x-x')-L_u(u-u')|\\
&\le L_x|x-x'|+L_u|u-u'|.
\end{aligned}
\]

Consider the two fixed SDAC parameters
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-comparator-parameters}
\mathbf{m}^{(0)}=\mathbf{0},\qquad
\mathbf{m}^{(1)}=\mathbf{e}_1=(1,0,\ldots,0)\in\mathcal{M}_H.
\end{equation}
For $t=2,\ldots,T$, \eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy}, \eqref{eq:model-init},
\eqref{eq:model-dynamics}, and \eqref{eq:lower-random-instance} give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-comparator-trajectories}
\begin{aligned}
u_t(\mathbf{m}^{(0)})&=0,\qquad
u_t(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})=\alpha,\\
x_t(\mathbf{m}^{(0)})
&=\sum_{s=0}^{t-2}\alpha^s
=\frac{1-\alpha^{t-1}}{1-\alpha},\\
x_t(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})&=1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The state formula for $\mathbf{m}^{(1)}$ in
\eqref{eq:lower-comparator-trajectories} follows by induction from
$x_2(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})=1$ and $\alpha\cdot1-\alpha+1=1$.
Subtracting the states in \eqref{eq:lower-comparator-trajectories} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-state-difference}
\begin{aligned}
x_t(\mathbf{m}^{(0)})-x_t(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})
&=\frac{1-\alpha^{t-1}}{1-\alpha}-1\\
&=\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha^{t-2})}{1-\alpha}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:model-init}, \eqref{eq:def-sdac-policy}, and
\eqref{eq:lower-random-instance}, both policies incur zero cost at $t=1$.
Thus, \eqref{eq:def-sdac-cumulative-cost}, \eqref{eq:lower-random-instance},
and the actions in \eqref{eq:lower-comparator-trajectories} give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-cost-difference}
\begin{aligned}
&J_T(\mathbf{m}^{(0)})-J_T(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})\\
&\quad=\sum_{t=2}^T\xi_t
\left[L_x\bigl(x_t(\mathbf{m}^{(0)})-x_t(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})\bigr)
+\alpha L_u\right]\\
&\quad\overset{\eqref{eq:lower-state-difference}}{=}
\sum_{t=2}^T\xi_t
\left(\frac{\alpha L_x(1-\alpha^{t-2})}{1-\alpha}+\alpha L_u\right)\\
&\quad\overset{\eqref{eq:sdac-lower-beta}}{=}
\sum_{t=2}^T\xi_t\beta_t.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

By causality, $(x_t,u_t)$ depends only on past signs and the controller's
randomization, so it is independent of $\xi_t$. By
\eqref{eq:def-cumulative-cost}, \eqref{eq:lower-random-instance}, and
$\mathbb{E}[\xi_t]=0$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-online-zero-mean}
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi},\mathcal{A}}[J_T^{\mathrm{on}}]\\
&\quad=\sum_{t=1}^T
\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi},\mathcal{A}}
\bigl[\xi_t(L_xx_t-L_uu_t)\bigr]\\
&\quad=\sum_{t=1}^T
\mathbb{E}[\xi_t]\,
\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi},\mathcal{A}}[L_xx_t-L_uu_t]
=0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The trajectories in \eqref{eq:lower-comparator-trajectories} are
deterministic. Hence, for $j\in\{0,1\}$,
\eqref{eq:def-sdac-cumulative-cost} and \eqref{eq:lower-random-instance} give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-sdac-zero-mean}
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}[J_T(\mathbf{m}^{(j)})]\\
&\quad=\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbb{E}[\xi_t]
\bigl(L_xx_t(\mathbf{m}^{(j)})-L_uu_t(\mathbf{m}^{(j)})\bigr)
=0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Since both parameters in \eqref{eq:lower-comparator-parameters} belong to
$\mathcal{M}_H$, \eqref{eq:def-regret} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-two-policy-regret}
\begin{aligned}
\RegSDAC{T}(H)
&\ge J_T^{\mathrm{on}}
-\min\{J_T(\mathbf{m}^{(0)}),J_T(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})\}\\
&=J_T^{\mathrm{on}}
-\frac{J_T(\mathbf{m}^{(0)})+J_T(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})}{2}\\
&\quad+\frac12
\left|J_T(\mathbf{m}^{(0)})-J_T(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})\right|.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The $p=1$ Khintchine inequality \cite{szarek1976best} applies to the
deterministic coefficients $\beta_2,\ldots,\beta_T$ and independent signs:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-khintchine}
\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}
\left|\sum_{t=2}^T\xi_t\beta_t\right|
\ge\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\sqrt{\sum_{t=2}^T\beta_t^2}.
\end{equation}
Taking expectations in \eqref{eq:lower-two-policy-regret} and using
\eqref{eq:lower-online-zero-mean} and \eqref{eq:lower-sdac-zero-mean} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-sign-average}
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi},\mathcal{A}}[\RegSDAC{T}(H)]\\
&\quad\ge\frac12\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}
\left|J_T(\mathbf{m}^{(0)})-J_T(\mathbf{m}^{(1)})\right|\\
&\quad\overset{\eqref{eq:lower-cost-difference}}{=}
\frac12\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}
\left|\sum_{t=2}^T\xi_t\beta_t\right|\\
&\quad\overset{\eqref{eq:lower-khintchine}}{\ge}\frac{1}{2\sqrt2}
\sqrt{\sum_{t=2}^T\beta_t^2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The expectation in \eqref{eq:lower-sign-average} averages over the $2^T$
sign sequences. Therefore, some $\boldsymbol{\xi}^\star$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-fixed-sign-bound}
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\!\left[
\RegSDAC{T}(H)\mid\boldsymbol{\xi}=\boldsymbol{\xi}^\star\right]\\
&\quad\ge
\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi},\mathcal{A}}[\RegSDAC{T}(H)]\\
&\quad\overset{\eqref{eq:lower-sign-average}}{\ge}
\frac{1}{2\sqrt2}\sqrt{\sum_{t=2}^T\beta_t^2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Fix $\boldsymbol{\xi}^\star$ in \eqref{eq:lower-random-instance} before the
interaction. This gives a deterministic oblivious sequence, and
\eqref{eq:lower-fixed-sign-bound} proves \eqref{eq:sdac-lower-exact}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:sdac-lower-bound}]
Since $0<\alpha<1$ and $\log\alpha=-\log(1/\alpha)$, for
$t=\left\lceil\log 2/\log(1/\alpha)\right\rceil+2,\ldots,T$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-decay-half}
\begin{aligned}
\alpha^{t-2}
&\le
\alpha^{\lceil\log 2/\log(1/\alpha)\rceil}\\
&\le
\alpha^{\log 2/\log(1/\alpha)}
=e^{-\log2}=\frac12.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:sdac-lower-beta} and $L_x,L_u>0$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-beta-half}
\begin{aligned}
\beta_t
&\overset{\eqref{eq:lower-decay-half}}{\ge}
\alpha\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{2(1-\alpha)}\right)\\
&\ge
\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(L_u+\frac{L_x}{1-\alpha}\right)
\overset{\eqref{eq:def-surrogate-G}}{=}\frac{G_\alpha}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The number of indices in \eqref{eq:lower-decay-half} satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-index-count}
T-\left\lceil\frac{\log 2}{\log(1/\alpha)}\right\rceil-1
\overset{\eqref{eq:sdac-lower-horizon}}{\ge}\frac{T}{2}.
\end{equation}
Discarding nonnegative squares and applying \eqref{eq:lower-beta-half},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower-beta-square-sum}
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{t=2}^T\beta_t^2
&\ge\sum_{t=\lceil\log 2/\log(1/\alpha)\rceil+2}^T\beta_t^2\\
&\ge\left(T-\left\lceil\frac{\log 2}{\log(1/\alpha)}\right\rceil-1\right)
\frac{G_\alpha^2}{4}\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:lower-index-count}}{\ge}
\frac{TG_\alpha^2}{8}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For the sequence supplied by Lemma~\ref{lem:sdac-lower-finite-horizon},
$G_\alpha>0$ and \eqref{eq:lower-beta-square-sum} yield
\eqref{eq:sdac-lower-simplified}:
\begin{align*}
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\!\left[\RegSDAC{T}(H)\right]
&\overset{\eqref{eq:sdac-lower-exact}}{\ge}
\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{\sum_{t=2}^T\beta_t^2}\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:lower-beta-square-sum}}{\ge}
\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{\frac{TG_\alpha^2}{8}}\\
&=\frac{G_\alpha}{8}\sqrt{T}.\qedhere
\end{align*}
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Justification of Remark~\ref{rem:large-alpha-regime}]
For fixed $L_x,L_u>0$, \eqref{eq:def-surrogate-G} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:large-alpha-G}
(1-\alpha)G_\alpha
=\alpha\bigl(L_x+(1-\alpha)L_u\bigr)
\longrightarrow L_x
\end{equation}
as $\alpha\uparrow1$. Since $1\le z^{-1}\le\alpha^{-1}$ for
$z\in[\alpha,1]$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:large-alpha-log}
\begin{aligned}
1\le\frac{\log(1/\alpha)}{1-\alpha}
&=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\int_\alpha^1\frac{dz}{z}\\
&\le\frac{1}{\alpha}\longrightarrow1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:large-alpha-G} and \eqref{eq:large-alpha-log}, both
$G_\alpha$ and the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:sdac-lower-horizon}
are of order $(1-\alpha)^{-1}$; taking a ceiling changes a number by
less than one. Under \eqref{eq:sdac-lower-horizon},
\eqref{eq:sdac-lower-simplified} and \eqref{eq:large-alpha-G} imply
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:large-alpha-lower}
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\!\left[\RegSDAC{T}(H)\right]
=
\Omega\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{T}}{1-\alpha}\right).
\end{equation}
For the upper bound, \eqref{eq:def-Gamma-alpha} and
\eqref{eq:large-alpha-G} give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:large-alpha-Gamma}
\begin{aligned}
(1-\alpha)^3\Gamma_\alpha
&=\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\bigl((1-\alpha)G_\alpha\bigr)^2 \\
&\quad+\alpha\bigl((1-\alpha)G_\alpha\bigr)
(L_x+\alpha L_u) \\
&\longrightarrow L_x(L_x+L_u)>0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:large-alpha-Gamma},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:large-alpha-upper}
\sqrt{\Gamma_\alpha}
=\Theta\!\left((1-\alpha)^{-3/2}\right)
\quad\text{as }\alpha\uparrow1.
\end{equation}
Together with \eqref{eq:regret-optimized-bound}, this gives the upper
bound stated in Remark~\ref{rem:large-alpha-regime}.

For fixed $T$ and $t=2,\ldots,T$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:large-alpha-fixed-horizon}
\frac{1-\alpha^{t-2}}{1-\alpha}
=\sum_{s=0}^{t-3}\alpha^s\longrightarrow t-2
\qquad\text{as }\alpha\uparrow1,
\end{equation}
where the sum is empty for $t=2$. Equations
\eqref{eq:large-alpha-fixed-horizon} and \eqref{eq:sdac-lower-beta}
give $\beta_t\to L_u+L_x(t-2)$ for every $t=2,\ldots,T$.
Since this is a finite set of indices, the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:sdac-lower-exact} remains finite.
\end{proof}
